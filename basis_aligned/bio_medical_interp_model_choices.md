# Where Exact-Mechanistic Model Extraction Can Have Real Medical Impact: A Ranked Field Map

## TL;DR
- The single highest-impact fit is **1-D physiological signals — ECG first, then EEG and voice/audio** — because small models are genuinely SOTA-competitive, large public multi-site datasets exist, and the field has documented, expert-validated cases where interpreting a model revealed a *new* biomarker (the 2026 *Nature* ECG sudden-cardiac-death marker; the PLN-mutation ECG features). This is the cleanest place for your "fold → cross-site generalize → biomarker hypothesis" loop.
- The three strongest existence proofs that the loop *works* are: (1) retinal fundus → systemic factors (Poplin et al. 2018, age/sex/smoking/blood pressure "not previously thought to be quantifiable"); (2) ECG deep-learning biomarker discovery (sudden cardiac death, *Nature* 2026; PLN carriers, 2021); and (3) regulatory-genomics motif syntax (BPNet, Avsec et al. 2021, CRISPR-validated Nanog helical periodicity). All three are small-to-modest models on modest-dimensional inputs — i.e., foldable-scale.
- **De-prioritize** gigapixel whole-slide-image prognostics, chest CT, mammography, and any task dominated by billion-parameter foundation models or where the discriminative feature is already fully known (e.g., diabetic-retinopathy lesion grading). Also heed your own finding: for pure confounder/shortcut detection, occlusion beats you — pitch this as *biomarker discovery*, not bias auditing.

## Key Findings
- **Small models are competitive exactly where you need them to be.** ECG: a 9-layer residual CNN (Ribeiro et al., *Nature Communications* 2020, 11:1760) trained on >2 million labeled exams from the CODE study "outperform[ed] cardiology resident medical doctors in recognizing 6 types of abnormalities in 12-lead ECG recordings, with F1 scores above 80% and specificity over 99%," tested against 827 tracings annotated by three cardiologists. Regulatory genomics: BPNet is a ~10-convolution-layer, 64-filter CNN on 1-kb inputs (~10⁵ parameters). Colorectal histology tiles: Kather et al. (*PLOS Medicine* 2019, e1002730) reached "nine-class accuracy of >94%" on the CRC-VAL-HE-7K external set of 224×224 H&E patches, with later systems (Color-CADx) reporting up to 99.3%. These are all foldable-scale tasks. Conversely, WSI-level prognostics and CT increasingly rely on gigapixel inputs and foundation models — a poor fit for your hard constraint.
- **"What did the model learn" is an explicitly stated goal in several fields.** Regulatory genomics states a "critical need for a general method that can identify cis-regulatory motif syntax"; retinal imaging researchers explicitly frame deep learning as a tool for "discovery of novel associations through unbiased modelling"; ECG groups explicitly aim to "discover complex features … [that] play a pathophysiological role in relatively unknown diseases."
- **Saliency is known to be unreliable in exactly these medical settings.** Arun et al. (*Radiology: Artificial Intelligence* 2021; medRxiv 2020) found that "all eight saliency map techniques failed at least one of the criteria and were inferior in performance compared with localization networks. For pneumothorax segmentation, the AUPRC ranged from 0.024 to 0.224, while a U-Net achieved a significantly superior AUPRC of 0.404 (P < .005)" — across Gradients, SmoothGrad, Integrated Gradients, Smooth IG, GradCAM, XRAI, Guided-backprop and Guided GradCAM on the SIIM-ACR Pneumothorax and RSNA Pneumonia datasets. Adebayo et al.'s "Sanity Checks for Saliency Maps" independently showed many maps are insensitive to model/label randomization. This is the gap your exact rendering fills.
- **Exactness ≠ truth is a real, documented risk.** Retinal age/sex prediction generalizes across ethnicities but degrades out-of-distribution (Rim et al.: internal R² 0.74–0.92 for age vs external 0.36–0.63). This is precisely why your cross-site generalization filter is essential and should be marketed as the core of the method, not an afterthought.

## Details

### Ranked shortlist of best-fit medical settings

**1. ECG (12-lead and single-lead) — arrhythmia, structural disease, and prognostic biomarker discovery.** *Top pick.*
- *Task / why it fits:* 1-D, low-dimensional, small models are literally SOTA. The scientific question is increasingly "what morphology does the model use," not just accuracy — the field has explicitly moved into biomarker discovery. Scores 5/5/5/5/5 on your criteria.
- *Datasets:* PTB-XL (21,837 records from 18,885 patients, 12-lead, 10 s, 71 SCP-ECG statements; open on PhysioNet). CODE / CODE-test (>2 million tracings; CODE-test = 827 tracings with gold-standard labels where two cardiologists agreed). Georgia G12EC (~10,344), Chapman-Shaoxing (10,646), Ningbo (40,258). Strongly multi-site and multi-country.
- *Small-model evidence:* Ribeiro et al. residual CNN, 9 conv layers, F1 > 0.80, specificity > 0.99, beating residents.
- *Existence proofs:* (a) *Nature* 2026 (Lingman et al., 655:210–218) — deep learning on a Swedish region's ECGs linked to death certificates isolated a 2.2% high-risk group with a 7.0% annual sudden-cardiac-death rate (higher than the 4.6% rate among those with reduced LVEF); 86.1% of high-risk patients were not flagged by LVEF; externally validated in a US health system and a Taiwanese registry; the model was paired with a generative model to *visualize a previously undescribed, easily-visible waveform biomarker* and propose a mechanism. (b) *Circulation: Arrhythmia & Electrophysiology* 2021 (PLN p.Arg14del carriers): DNN c-statistic 0.95 (95% CI 0.91–0.99), which "identified novel PLN-specific ECG features (e.g., increased PR-duration)" that significantly improved a logistic regression baseline (P<0.001).
- *Difficulty vs impact:* Low difficulty, very high impact. **Start here.**

**2. Regulatory genomics (TF binding / chromatin accessibility from DNA sequence) — motif and syntax discovery.**
- *Task / why it fits:* The entire point is "what sequence features did the model learn." De novo motif/syntax discovery is the explicitly stated field goal ("Our model represents a powerful general approach to uncover the motifs and syntax of cis-regulatory sequences"). Small CNNs are standard. 5/5/4/4/5.
- *Datasets:* ENCODE/Roadmap (Basset: DNase-seq across 164 cell types = 125 ENCODE + 39 Roadmap). ChIP-seq/ChIP-nexus (BPNet: raw data GEO GSE137193; processed data Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.3371215; trained model on Kipoi as "BPNet-OSKN"; also Stowers repository LIBPB-1456). DESSO on 690 ENCODE ChIP-seq datasets. ENCODE data are effectively open (note: the BPNet paper text is under a standard Springer Nature licence, but the underlying data and code are open).
- *Small-model evidence:* BPNet = 64 filters/layer, a 25-bp first conv layer + 9 dilated conv layers, 1-kb one-hot input, receptive field ±1034 bp (~10⁵ parameters — small; the paper does not state an exact count). Basset is similarly small; DeepSEA is larger (~50M params) and less foldable-friendly.
- *Existence proof (arguably the strongest in all of biomedicine):* BPNet (Avsec et al., *Nature Genetics* 2021, 53:354–366) found that "Nanog preferentially binds with helical periodicity, and TFs often cooperate in a directional manner, which we validate using CRISPR-induced point mutations." Motifs were "preferentially spaced at ∼10.5 bp periodicity"; "Sox2 enhances Nanog binding, but not vice versa"; the ~10.5-bp pattern "had not been discovered previously." Validation used CRISPR/Cas9 two-base substitutions (Sox2 TTG→AGG at chr10:85,539,634–643; Nanog TGA→GGC at chr10:85,539,756–765) across three independently derived clones per motif, followed by ChIP-nexus. Interpretation used DeepLIFT contribution scores + TF-MoDISco (which produced a novel contribution-weight-matrix motif representation) — precisely the *approximate* attribution pipeline your exact rendering would replace or complement.
- *Difficulty vs impact:* Medium difficulty (1-D over a 4-letter alphabet; your foldable architecture needs adapting to sequence convolutions), very high scientific impact, with a mature wet-lab validation culture.

**3. Retinal fundus imaging — predicting systemic/demographic factors (discovery mode, NOT DR grading).**
- *Task / why it fits:* The canonical existence proof for model-driven feature discovery. Modest-resolution images; the question is genuinely "what in the retina encodes age/sex/blood pressure." 5/5/3–4/4/4.
- *Datasets:* UK Biobank (~84,743 fundus photos used in one sex-prediction study), EyePACS (88,702 images; resolutions from 433×289 to 5184×3456), Messidor-2 (1,748), APTOS 2019 (3,662), Qatar Biobank (3,000 subjects). Multi-cohort/ethnicity data exists (SEED: Chinese/Indian/Malay). EyePACS/APTOS are under Kaggle competition terms; UK Biobank requires application.
- *Small-model evidence:* Sex-from-fundus with AutoML/MobileNet-class models reaches AUROC 0.93 (internal), 0.84 accuracy (external); the underlying sex signal (AUC 0.97) is strong enough that smaller nets replicate it.
- *Existence proof:* Poplin et al. (*Nature Biomedical Engineering* 2018, 2:158–164): "trained on data from 284,335 patients and validated on two independent datasets of 12,026 and 999 patients, we predicted cardiovascular risk factors not previously thought to be present or quantifiable in retinal images, such as age (mean absolute error within 3.26 years), gender (AUC = 0.97), smoking status (AUC = 0.71), systolic blood pressure (mean absolute error within 11.23 mmHg) and major adverse cardiac events (AUC = 0.70)." Sex prediction was "previously inconceivable to those who spent their careers looking at retinas."
- *Caveat / why not #1:* Standard fundus resolution exceeds histology tiles, so you must downsample; and the true feature (for sex) is still unknown — a *feature* for your discovery pitch, but generalization is imperfect (external R² drops sharply), reinforcing the need for your cross-site filter.
- *Difficulty vs impact:* Medium difficulty, high impact.

**4. EEG — seizure detection/prediction and sleep staging (mechanism/feature interpretability).**
- *Task / why it fits:* 1-D multichannel, small models competitive, trust is an acknowledged bottleneck, and interpretability is an active sub-field. 4/4/5/4/4.
- *Datasets:* TUH EEG Seizure Corpus (TUSZ, largest public), CHB-MIT (16 patients, 85 seizures), Sleep-EDF, and multi-site sleep cohorts (U-Sleep trained on 15,660 recordings across 16 studies).
- *Small-model evidence:* A shallow 1-D-CNN+transformer seizure model reached AUC 0.93; interpretable XGBoost sleep staging hits 87.0% accuracy (κ 0.81) competitive with deep nets; TinySleepNet is explicitly tiny.
- *Existence proof:* Weaker than ECG — mostly "interpretable model" papers rather than a landmark validated novel-feature discovery. This is unclaimed territory (opportunity) but also a maturity risk.
- *Difficulty vs impact:* Medium difficulty, high impact; less mature discovery narrative.

**5. Voice / audio biomarkers — Parkinson's and other neuro/respiratory disease.**
- *Task / why it fits:* 1-D signal (or small spectrogram), small models competitive, and the acoustic biomarkers driving prediction are of direct clinical interest. 4/4/4/3/4.
- *Datasets:* Parkinson's Voice/telemonitoring datasets (UCI, mPower); relatively small and sometimes single-site — the multi-site criterion is the weak point.
- *Small-model evidence:* Ensembles/shallow models reach 95–98% accuracy (LightGBM/RF at 98.01%, ROC-AUC 0.9914 on the Parkinson's Voice Disorder Dataset); CNN on log-mel spectrograms competitive. XAI work already names biomarkers (spread2, PPE, RPDE).
- *Existence proof:* Emerging (composite-spectrogram "novel acoustic biomarker" work) but not yet a landmark validated discovery.
- *Difficulty vs impact:* Low–medium difficulty; impact moderate; multi-site coverage is the concern.

**6. Cell microscopy / morphological profiling (Cell Painting) — drug mechanism-of-action.**
- *Task / why it fits:* The scientific question is explicitly "what morphological feature corresponds to this mechanism." Multi-institution data exists at scale. 5/4/3/5/4.
- *Datasets:* JUMP-CP cpg0016 (>140,000 chemical + genetic perturbations, multi-center, public); BBBC datasets. Strongly multi-site — batch effects are the central challenge, which is *good* for your generalization filter.
- *Small-model evidence:* Mixed — single-cell crops are small and foldable-friendly, but SOTA is trending to Swin transformers (cell-vision fusion at 69.79% accuracy). Documented that supervised extractors "cheat" on background pixels (Grad-CAMO), so exact rendering has clear value.
- *Difficulty vs impact:* Medium–high difficulty (batch effects, need for single-cell crops), high impact for target ID / MoA.

**7. Dermoscopy — skin lesion classification (as a controlled feature-audit + discovery testbed).**
- *Task / why it fits:* Small images, large public multi-site data (ISIC), and a famous shortcut literature (surgical ink markings inflate melanoma probability scores — JAMA Dermatology 2019; ISIC color-calibration patches only in benign images). 3/3/4/5/3.
- *Caveat:* Much of the obvious value here is *shortcut detection*, where you concede occlusion wins. Pitch only the *positive* discovery angle (what genuine dermoscopic texture separates melanoma from nevus). Lower priority.

### The 2–3 strongest existence proofs across all medical domains
1. **Retinal fundus → systemic factors (Poplin et al., *Nat Biomed Eng* 2018).** The textbook case: a model surfaced signals (sex, smoking, blood pressure) clinicians did not know were encoded, externally validated on 12,026 + 999 patients. Proves interpreting a vision model can generate real, novel biomarker hypotheses.
2. **ECG sudden-cardiac-death biomarker (Lingman et al., *Nature* 2026).** A deep model + generative visualization revealed a previously undescribed, easily-visible waveform biomarker that beats LVEF (7.0% vs 4.6% annual SCD rate; 86.1% missed by LVEF), externally validated in three countries, with a mechanistic hypothesis. This is the closest published analogue to your entire loop: extract → visualize exact feature → validate across sites → mechanism.
3. **BPNet regulatory-genomics motif syntax (Avsec et al., *Nat Genet* 2021).** Interpreting a small CNN discovered Nanog ~10.5-bp helical periodicity and directional Sox2→Nanog cooperativity, *CRISPR-validated*. The gold standard for "interpretation → wet-lab-confirmed new biology" — and on a foldable-scale model.

### Where the field relies on unreliable saliency (your improvement opportunity)
Chest X-ray, dermoscopy, retinal, and histology model papers overwhelmingly rationalize predictions with Grad-CAM/saliency. Arun et al. quantified that these fail trustworthiness criteria in medical imaging (AUPRC 0.024–0.224 vs 0.404 for a U-Net); Adebayo et al. showed insensitivity to model/label randomization. An exact, zero-approximation feature rendering is a categorical improvement over heatmaps wherever a small model is competitive.

## Recommendations
- **Stage 1 (now, ~1 quarter): ECG on PTB-XL.** Train a foldable 1-D model for a well-characterized task (e.g., bundle-branch block or atrial fibrillation) where the ground-truth morphology is known, to *validate that fold recovers the known feature exactly*. Benchmark against the Ribeiro CNN. Success threshold: extracted filters reproduce textbook ECG criteria and accuracy is within ~2–3 F1 points of the reference CNN. If fold cannot recover known ECG features, stop and reassess before attempting discovery tasks.
- **Stage 2 (next, ~1–2 quarters): ECG discovery + cross-site filter.** Move to a prognostic/labeled task with multi-country data (CODE, Chapman-Shaoxing, Georgia). Extract candidate features, then use cross-dataset generalization to separate true from spurious. Benchmark: a candidate that survives ≥2 independent cohorts and is not attributable to lead/device metadata becomes a biomarker hypothesis. This mirrors the *Nature* 2026 pipeline.
- **Stage 3 (parallel, highest scientific ceiling): regulatory genomics (Basset/BPNet-style).** Highest discovery payoff and a ready CRISPR validation culture. Requires adapting your architecture to sequence convolutions over a 4-letter alphabet. Benchmark: recover known JASPAR motifs exactly, then search for novel syntax; validate survivors against held-out ChIP data before claiming discovery.
- **Stage 4 (optional, high visibility): retinal sex/systemic-factor rendering.** Downsample fundus images and attempt to render the exact retinal pattern encoding sex — a famously unknown feature. Threshold: the pattern must replicate across ≥2 ethnic cohorts (SEED subgroups, UK Biobank) to count.
- **Positioning:** Market as *biomarker/mechanism discovery with a built-in truth filter (cross-site generalization)*, not as bias auditing. Concede shortcut detection to occlusion up front; it strengthens credibility.
- **What would change these rankings:** If your foldable architecture proves hard to train competitively on 1-D signals, drop to retinal/histology small-image tasks where you already have an end-to-end validation. If a collaborator brings wet-lab CRISPR capacity, elevate genomics to #1.

## Caveats
- **Exactness ≠ truth remains the central epistemic risk.** An exactly-rendered feature can still be a cohort-specific artifact (stain, camera, device, site). Your cross-site generalization step is load-bearing, not optional. Retinal external-validation degradation (age R² 0.74–0.92 internal → 0.36–0.63 external) is the cautionary datum.
- **Small-model competitiveness is task-specific, not domain-specific.** Within a "good" domain (e.g., histology), tile-level texture classification suits small models but slide-level prognosis does not. Choose tasks, not domains.
- **Some headline "discoveries" are secondary reporting or preprints.** The *Nature* 2026 ECG paper and the 2021 *Nature Genetics* BPNet paper are peer-reviewed; several voice/EEG "novel biomarker" claims are preprints (medRxiv) or single-cohort and should be treated as suggestive, not established.
- **Confounder detection is explicitly out of scope for your differentiator** — you tested this and occlusion wins. Do not build the pitch around it.
- **Licensing varies:** PTB-XL and CODE-test (PhysioNet/Zenodo, open), NCT-CRC-HE-100K (CC-BY on Zenodo/Kaggle), and ENCODE (open) are broadly usable; EyePACS/APTOS carry Kaggle competition terms; UK Biobank and JUMP-CP require application/registration. Confirm terms before training.
- Non-medical domains (materials science, remote sensing) are out of scope per the brief and were not assessed.
