First, the method question

The SVD(WX) clustering is real structure — but it is not where the good circuits are

You asked about the SVD(W·X) clustering method. To be exact about what it is: for a layer whose MLP output is an exact sum of per-unit terms, we compute each hidden unit's exact contribution, project the removed vectors onto the top singular directions of the layer's realized output (the SVD of W·X over real text), correlate those damages across thousands of tokens, and hierarchically cluster the units. It recovers genuine structure: clustering the same units on two independent halves of the data agrees at ARI 0.58 versus ≈0 for a shuffle — the unit groups are stable and reproducible, not noise.

But when we ask the only question that matters — is any cluster causally necessary? — the method runs into a wall we hit again and again. The cleanest, most stable cluster in the whole model (mlp17's newline group, split-half ARI 0.778) carries −16% of its layer's causal effect on the newline prediction — negligible, and less than a random set of the same size. mlp0's article cluster, when mean-ablated, moves the article decision by 0.015, barely above a random-unit control. The computation is diffuse over units and redundant: no small group of neurons is the circuit.

The honest verdict on clustering SVD(W·X) clustering answers "which units vary together" (reproducibly), not "which units compute the behaviour" (no cluster does, alone). It is a good display of a layer's internal covariance and a poor circuit finder. So the circuits below were not found by clustering — they were found by tracing behaviours causally from the input embedding to the output, and they are a categorically stronger object.

Every one of the three circuits below turned out to share one shape, which is the headline result of this whole phase:

The most convincing single test is the three-way fanout. A period can be followed by a line break, a capitalized new sentence, or a lowercase continuation. The embedding bigram fires identically at every period — P(newline)=0.42, P(capital)=0.10 regardless of what comes next. The full network flips its bet by context:

The two outcomes anti-correlate: the blocks trade off "end the paragraph" against "start a sentence" using context the embedding cannot see. That flip is the circuit's whole job, and the context-blind bigram never makes it.

  • embeddingThe ./!/? token vectors carry a fixed lean toward "\n" in the output head — a memorised association, not a computation.
  • attn 0–2Read the surrounding context and decide whether this period ends a structural unit; 80% of the newline-vs-continuation routing lives here.
  • block 17Suppresses newline as a high-frequency function token (see Circuit 3); removing it raises P(newline) by ~21 points.

Correction, stated plainly An earlier activation-based writeup grouped prepositions with be-verbs as "reach for the indefinite article." The causal test says the opposite: prepositions pull toward "the," not "a/an." Only be-verbs favour the indefinite. The mistake was reading the direction off activations; the intervention reversed it. This is corrected in the main report too.

Path split. Ablating front attention shrinks the spread between these contexts from 0.144 to 0.105 — attention carries the a/an-vs-the choice. Ablating the front MLP collapses all article probability to near zero — the MLP carries the magnitude ("an article is coming at all"). Two paths, two jobs.

  • embeddingPreceding-word identity carries the raw lean (be→a/an, prep/punct→the), visible even with all blocks skipped.
  • attn 0–2Routes the definite/indefinite choice by context — the part that is actually grammar, not lookup.
  • mlp 0–2Sets the overall article magnitude; without it the model does not commit to predicting an article.
  • block 17Calibrates "the" down as one of the most frequent tokens in the language.

This is why block 17 appears in the other two circuits as the final stage: the same layer that trims newline and "the" is running one frequency correction across the whole vocabulary. A textbook last-layer calibrator, isolated and measured.

And it is the one part of this model you can actually turn. The correction lives on a single rank-1 direction — remove it and the calibration is gone, remove a random direction and nothing happens — and that direction is stable across disjoint data (cosine 0.98). Scale the model's projection onto it and you get a clean frequency/diversity dial: at the default, the twenty commonest tokens hold 29% of the probability mass; dial it down and that rises to 42%, dial it up and it falls to 13%. It is the exception that frames the rule — everything else in this model (routing, word choice, even its memory of whether it is inside a quote) is distributed and offers no such knob: readable in places, editable almost nowhere.

So — what quality of thing is this?

Honest assessment

What is solid. Three behaviours are traced from the input embedding to the output with a causal test behind every stage, controls and nulls throughout, and every measurement reproduced on held-out text. We can point to where an association is stored, where the decision is made, and where it is tempered — and say how many nats each is worth. The trigger→route→calibrate shape recurs across all three, which is itself a load-bearing finding, not a coincidence of one example.

What is genuinely limited — and one place the limit just moved. Below the block level the model mostly resists component naming: computation is diffuse and redundant, so single-unit, single-cluster, and single-head ablations are defeated every time — the induction copy operation, chased to its heads, turned out spread across nearly the whole attention stack (ablating the 16 heads that most cleanly point at the copy source removes only 19% of it). Clustering components does not isolate anything here. But conditioning on the behaviour instead does: the last layer's frequency calibration isolates to a single rank-1 direction — removing that one direction destroys the calibration (103% of its benefit gone) while removing a random direction does nothing (0–2%). So the right question is not "which component" (redundancy hides that) but "which behaviour-conditioned direction, tested by removal" — and that reaches rank-1. Finally, the embedding "trigger," read on its own, is a poor predictor — worse than uniform overall — so it is a relative lean, not a standalone lookup table; the network does the real work.

Net: these are real circuits with quantified, causally-verified components and honestly-marked resolution limits — a stronger object than the stable-but-inert unit clusters the clustering method produced, and the right level of description for a model this redundant.
