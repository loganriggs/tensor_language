Reading bilin18

What we found inside a 546M-parameter bilinear transformer: six layers read end to end, a causal graph with every edge tested by intervention, a depth map that says where the model compresses and where it refuses to, and a weights-only formula that predicted almost all of it. Including every correction it took to get here — the ledger below runs to thirteen.

The model and the method

bilin18 is an 18-layer, 546M-parameter language model whose MLPs are purely bilinear — each one computes Down((Lx)·(Rx)), no activation function — and whose attention multiplies two separate score matrices with no softmax. The absence of nonlinearities beyond multiplication is what makes it readable: every layer is a third-order tensor, every output coefficient is an exact quadratic form, and sums split exactly, so decompositions that are approximations elsewhere are identities here.

Each layer went through the same loop. Find the causal directions by order-averaged attribution (each direction's damage averaged over all removal orders, so nothing hides behind redundancy and the parts must sum to the whole). Split each direction's activation exactly by writer — embedding, each attention block, each MLP. Name it two ways and keep only names that beat a permutation null. Replace it with the smallest surrogate that holds the model's loss, against a random-direction control. Then state the result as a variable graph and test each edge by transplanting values between inputs. Every claim below survived that loop, and the ones that didn't are reported with the same prominence.

The depth map

The single most useful picture. The ends of the network are individually heavy; the middle looks free one layer at a time — and is not. A fair, order-averaged attribution over the fourteen middle layers (green) shows two layers doing most of the middle's work, a long redundant tail, and one layer whose fair share is negative.

Layer 1 is the most important unit in the model (5.65 nats). Layers 2 and 3 carry 64% of the middle. Layer 4's negative share was the strangest number in the program until a coalition sweep oriented it: its cost flips sign exactly when layers 2–3 are deleted — layer 4 is a reader of layers 2–3, and on an unwritten bus its computation misfires. The tail (5–15) contributes 3–7% each, understated three-to-five-fold by one-at-a-time ablation.

unifying result These profiles turned out to be one measurement in disguise. For each layer, ask how much of its total deletion cost is captured by individual direction-bands plus pairwise band interactions: layers 1, 2, 3 score 24 / 39 / 57% — most of their effect exists only in third-order and higher combinations — while layers 0, 16, 17 score 67 / 99 / 100%. Every layer that compresses is interaction-shallow; every layer that resists is interaction-deep, in the registered ordinal prediction's exact order. Compressibility and interaction shallowness are the same property seen from two sides — which is this report's best one-line answer to what "understandable" means for a bilinear stack.

Two further regularities fall out. Compressibility tracks mechanism class: squared context summaries compress to one projection, token-identity features need more of their form, accumulated high-rank computation refuses entirely. And the distribution-robust part of every compressible leader is exactly its low-rank core: on out-of-distribution text the discarded parameters are neutral at best and harmful at worst — layer 16's replacement literally beats the intact model there, an effect that vanishes on training-like data (tested on a fresh fineweb sample).

The causal graph

The program's end state is not a list of features but a graph whose edges were each moved by hand: transplant a variable's value from one input into another and check the downstream behaviour matches transplanting its low-level realizer.

The z → c₀ abstraction inside layer 1 is honest about its grade: transplanted across documents it reproduces 68% of the true effect (against a 13% shuffled control) versus 92% on-distribution — the leak is localised to cross-document transport, exactly where the coefficient fit degrades. Partial abstractions with located leaks beat clean stories.

Structure verifies, token stories don't

Every time a claim of the form "direction X detects token-thing Y" met an actual intervention, it lost. Injecting layout characters into prose moves the supposed register-detector no more than injecting random words. Steering the syntax axis moves determiner probabilities less than it moves everything else. Meanwhile every structural claim — which head computes a variable, which layer feeds which, what gain an edge has, how few numbers replace a layer — survived intervention, three for three.

The reliable currency of this model's mechanisms is directions, edges, and gains — not token stories. The exact writer decompositions that suggested the token stories were not wrong as mathematics; they decompose covariance on the natural distribution, and covariance across documents is still correlational. An exact decomposition is not an intervention.

Pure weight algebra also ranks the responsible attention head first (5× margin) and predicted both the direction and ranking of the layer-4 coupling before any model was run. Consequence adopted: weight-side candidates first; model evaluations only to verify and to test edges. The expensive discovery phase of this program was, in retrospect, one Gaussian-equivalent computation away from the weights.

Edges are operators, and half a calculus works

The graph's edges began as scalars — a gain per edge — and scalars don't compose: knowing L0 feeds attention and attention feeds L1 predicted nothing about which head carries a given signal, because composition needs each edge's subspace signature. Upgrading to operator signatures produced a half-working calculus, scored blind wherever possible:

resolved The deepest structural question — whether the middle's dense interaction hides independent or mutually-exclusive sub-structure — resolved into a two-part answer, corrected twice along the way by its own controls. In support, the density is real everywhere: every downstream form and attention head couples essentially the same broad set of layer-1 direction pairs (one magnitude envelope, unsigned overlaps 0.6–0.8, at whole-model, per-reader, and per-form resolution). In functionals, the opposite: the signed quadratic forms the readers actually compute are nearly orthogonal — cosines 0.09–0.16 within readers, across readers, and across heads. The model reads its hardest layer through an overcomplete family of nearly-independent measurements on one shared substrate — and deleting direction-bands destroys every measurement over a region at once, which is exactly why the middle read as inseparable holism. The compression program re-aims accordingly: functional coordinates, not direction coordinates.

Two further structural facts came free. Edges are filters, not pipes: the same L0→L1 route passes the punctuation axis at unit gain, a second direction at 1.6σ, and the number axis at 0.23σ. And mediation itself is non-additive — single-head kill fractions sum past 100% on one edge and spread thinly on another — so "the" carrier is sometimes a category error, which is exactly what the failed blind test measured.

The closing statement: the quadratic stack is locally transparent and globally opaque to static intervention, with the transition inside a single layer. Deep coefficients respond to collective state, not injectable directions — the same per-layer diffusion that produces the composition law and the interchange leak. Long-range control would need input-dependent injection, a different intervention class; that boundary is measured, not assumed.

Composition has a law

Installing verified replacements together is where "did we compress it correctly" gets its final answer, and this model answered with structure. Three surrogates installed jointly cost 1.48× the sum of installing them alone — and the entire excess sits on the one pair of layers linked by a verified edge (the L16→L17 syntax bus; the unlinked pairs contribute +0.0005, zero within noise). The causal graph predicted, in advance and from a different kind of experiment, exactly where composition would fail.

refined A late-arc accounting correction sharpened these numbers: freezing the final norm's per-token gain to its no-damage values shows L17's raw span damage is 82% norm-mediated (the final rms_norm re-scaling a suddenly quieter residual; content-level cost +0.089, not +0.490) while L16's is mostly content (35% mediated) — and the interaction excess grows to +0.205 at content level, where it is ~90% the size of both individual content damages combined. The composition law was understated by the gain channel, not created by it. The full decomposition, measured stepwise: loss curvature alone (convex CE on superposing logit perturbations) would produce +0.46 of excess; the network's joint response destructively interferes and cancels about two-thirds of that; the remaining +0.16 content-level excess is carried ~21% by L17's quadratic and the rest by attention-and-upstream interaction. Compensation — the model actively attenuating compound damage — appears three separate times in this arc, and the product-law constant was always the residue of that cancellation war. The law's final scope, measured on a grid of damage shapes: it holds within a fixed damage family (where it was validated out-of-sample), fails across families (R² 0.12–0.47, as the bilinear-form refinement predicted), carries a content-level constant of ~13 rather than the norm-inflated 23, and inverts outright when one damage is small — the compensation machinery can make joint damage cheaper than the sum.

Three registered attempts to remove the interaction then failed in a row — refitting downstream-of-upstream closed 21%, raising the form rank closed 0%, widening the output span closed 30% — and the failures were the finding. Across all eight measured configurations the excess follows a product law:

excess ≈ 23 · d₁₆ · d₁₇   (c = 22.9 ± 2.4; 9% mean error)

A quadratic reader of summed errors produces a cross-term proportional to the product of the error magnitudes — the architecture's signature, now in the composition algebra. Every fidelity knob shrinks a factor; none removes the coupling, which is why no single fix could work. Tested out of sample at two never-measured corners with predictions registered first: 13% and 23% error, inside the registered 25% bar.

The practical rule for compressing this model: joint ≈ Σ dᵢ + c·Σ dᵢdⱼ over graph-linked pairs. The causal graph names which pairs interact; the law prices the interaction; compression budgeting becomes arithmetic.

refined A second linked pair (the 3→4 edge, probed with a grid of damage shapes) showed the scalar constant is per-damage-family, not per-edge: coupling strength varies ~5× with which directions are damaged. The full law is a bilinear form in the two damage profiles — the scalar version is its rank-1 shadow, valid when damage shape is held fixed, as it was in the validated 16→17 sweeps. The unlinked-pair control meanwhile confirmed the graph at its cleanest: no edge, no interaction, eighteen cells, excess ≤ 0.0013.

What's still open

The 2→3→4 chain got its abstraction treatment, and the answer is the anticipated negative, now measured: the 3→4 edge has no small variable set — eight weights-derived coupling directions carry 21% of the full-edge effect (registered bar: half), and the coupling operator beats the writer's own loudest directions by only ~8%. The chain's content is genuinely high-rank. The syntax bus, meanwhile, has no upstream supplier: no layer 5–15 moves L16's bus span more than a random span (ratios ≤1.15) — at ten layers' range every influence saturates into the same diffuse ~0.2σ floor, so the L16→L17 relay is local structure assembled from unstructured supply. The bus content itself is old, linearly exposed, and lexical-headed: the stream entering L16 predicts it at R² 0.979 with a purely linear map, two-thirds of its variance is already determined at L2 — and over half comes from the current token's embedding alone, so "syntax bus" overreads; it is a lexical channel with contextual refinement. A new instrument re-drew the depth map: linear predictability of each layer's write is lowest in the middle (L6–10, R² 0.52–0.62) and near-perfect at the end (L16: 0.97, L17: 0.95). corrected But causal tests split that map in two — measured twice, after an instrument bug (stand-ins fit on post-mix inputs, applied pre-mix) was caught and the whole set re-measured on a consistent protocol: the middle's nonlinear variance is largely loss-irrelevant — replacing a middle layer's MLP with its 55–60% linear map costs only ~0.03 nats — while replacing L2's map costs 0.109, three times the median. Functionally necessary nonlinearity is front-loaded; the middle's incompressibility is about representation, not about the nonlinearity of its map. The resolved tension: the strongest interaction (16→17) sits between the two most linear layers because it lives mostly in L17's 5% nonlinear residue — replacing L17 with its linear stand-in kills 79% of the interaction excess while leaving L17's own function intact (a +0.030 residue survives, routed elsewhere). The same residue cushions damage on the same channel: one quadratic skin, two faces. And the constructive version: all thirteen layers 5–17 can be replaced by sequentially-refit linear maps for +1.56 nats total. The completed front map crowns the story: linearization cost is unimodal with its peak at L1 (+0.282, declining monotonically to +0.054 at L4) — the layer this program found hardest to read by intervention is independently the model's most functionally nonlinear by replacement. The model, in one sentence: five genuinely nonlinear front layers, a nearly-linear thirteen-layer pipe, and one quadratic interaction skin at the end. Inside L1 itself the story splits spectrally: the top-48 interface every reader consumes is nearly linearly generable (12% of the layer's replacement cost at 59% of its energy), the deep tail beyond rank 512 is inert, and the functionally necessary nonlinearity concentrates in a mezzanine band of mid-variance directions (ranks ~50–500) that none of the reading experiments — all conducted on the top-48 — ever touched. The loud output is linear; the hard computation is quiet. The attention-pattern census closed the last unprofiled component family with a triple-negative: patterns are weakly positional (only L2 hosts a positional hub), not at all lexical (key-token identity explains zero, at every depth), and therefore genuinely contextual — attention is the one component of this model that is not token-headed. The division of labor in one line: the stream remembers the token, attention reads the context, the front MLPs do the nonlinear work, and the output end amplifies and stabilizes. And one structural motif spans both component families, surviving its covariance-matched null: shared watch-list, private combinations — a layer's nine heads watch substantially the same few stream directions (within-layer filter alignment 0.71 vs 0.35 matched null) and combine them into per-head score forms, exactly as the model's MLP readers share L1's coordinates while computing private functionals. The MLP watch-list is model-wide; the attention watch-list is per-layer. And the two component types keep to themselves: attention's and the MLP's watch-lists are separate institutions at every layer (alignment at or below the matched null), their write channels are separate too (5/5 layers, mild active avoidance), and their quadratic codes are mutually unreadable. Concretely: the residual stream is the 1152 numbers per token that every layer adds to and reads from, and at any layer the attention heads are sensitive to ~8 directions of it while the MLP is sensitive to a different ~8 (overlap at or below the covariance-matched random level); their outputs likewise deposit into different ~8-dim direction sets (overlap below random); and each type's quadratic reading code cannot express the other's. Same vector, different apertures in and different deposits out — with the model's famous diffuseness living entirely within each type. corrected And the end-to-end test overturned the "two channels" reading: downstream attention's read directions align far more with upstream MLP writes (0.62–0.68) than with upstream attention writes (0.20–0.43). The flow is a relay: MLPs write into the stream, attention reads MLP-written content and transports it across positions as values (the cargo result), and MLPs consume it. Locally separated apertures; cross-type hand-offs end to end. That diffuseness now has its finest-grain measurement: the gauge-invariant unit-mass spectrum is maximally flat — 98–99% of each layer's 4608 hidden units are effectively active — so this model has no important neurons at the weight level at all. (A gauge audit against the balanced-form spec confirmed every published statistic here is gauge-invariant; the model itself is not balanced — uniform defect ≈ 0.35 — so raw per-matrix weight readings would be ~2× contaminated and are avoided by standing rule.) The tail layers 5–15 have fair shares but no profiles. The syntax bus has two verified stations and unknown origin. And the strongest tool found here — the weights-plus-S formula — has now made its first blind predictions: on four never-profiled tail layers, with the success bar registered in advance, it located the measured causal leader's subspace in three of four (energies 0.89–0.97 against a ~0.007 random baseline; at one layer its single top eigenvector is the leader, cosine 0.92). The fourth is an honest miss (0.40) that flagged itself with a poor top-vector match. Two caveats travel with the hits: the tail layers' attributions are noisy at the cheap permutation budget used, and one tail layer's top output span improves shifted-corpus loss when deleted — the truncation-as-regularisation pattern appearing unprompted. The blind protocol for edges has now run, and its answer reshapes the tail: adjacent tail edges carry no alignment structure at all (write-to-reader alignment ratios 0.81–1.18, indistinguishable from isotropic), and measured edge strength is instead predicted by pure dilution — the writer's share of the residual stream, which declines perfectly monotonically with depth (0.252 → 0.040, Spearman +0.79 against intervention-measured effects). The tail routes by arithmetic, not by aiming: each layer speaks at roughly constant volume into an accumulating stream. The tail now has its profiles too: the blind formula's spans are causal at 8 of 11 tail layers, all with small effects (≤0.025 nats), as the dilution law predicts — and at five of eleven layers an 8-dim span exists whose deletion improves home-corpus CE (best −0.016), so truncation-as-regularization is endemic to the tail, not a distribution-shift curiosity. Those spans replicate on disjoint data (−0.021 at the strongest) — yet pruning exactly them plus a finetune still loses to an identically-finetuned intact control (+0.011 nats). Four candidate classes, four refutations, the last selected by the hypothesis's own success criterion: deletion benefit is a property of the frozen operating point, not spare capacity. Nothing found in this model is a removable constraint. The program's instruments have meanwhile been assembled into a benchmark draft (BENCHMARK.md in the repo): a semantic track scored causally (explanations must predict ablation outcomes, not just activation correlations) and a structural track scored on a measured fidelity-vs-complexity Pareto — replacement programs from a typed ladder (constant → low-rank refit linear → full component; the compact-quadratic rung is measured dead), with reference points from +1.81 nats at 0.07M stand-in parameters to +1.26 at 15.9M, and complexity counted at the balanced gauge point so it cannot be gamed by rescaling. The benchmark's transfer split rests on a finding of its own: the sibling model bilin12 obeys all three structural laws (dilution, the L1 nonlinearity crown, matched filters), and ablation fingerprints on shared text show the two independently trained models assign token-level causal responsibility to attention components at the same fractional depths — bilin12's components best-match bilin18 at exactly their own depth fraction (0.06/0.17/0.50, three for three), with analogous components correlating at 0.34 against a 0.05 non-analog floor. The full fingerprint atlas (36 components, every layer's MLP span and attention) is mutually distinguishable (median 0.07), depth-smooth (35 of 36 components' nearest same-type fingerprint is an adjacent layer's — causal responsibility varies smoothly along the stack), and type-marked; edges in the benchmark carry functional types (coordinates, summary statistics like norms, low-rank maps, or opaque) priced by description length, with both-direction audits whose sign distinguishes energy-dependence from content. The atlas arc's capstone: pooled, unlabeled fingerprints from the family's models self-organize onto one causal depth coordinate — the joint embedding tracks fractional depth at 0.85 for the bilinear pair and 0.79 with the cross-family third model included (nulls ~0.05–0.30); layer order alone is recoverable from fingerprints at 0.9. MLP causal placement follows depth fraction universally across all three architectures; the cross-family model's attention is displaced from its nominal fractions on both instruments tried, but the displacement's direction flips between instruments and is withdrawn as unresolved (ledger #13). One hypothesis was tested and refuted on three candidate classes: that the residual stream constrains the model — that pruning connections plus a short finetune could beat the intact model. Spans flagged by deletion benefit (+0.026 nats vs an identically-finetuned control), the foreign-code reader L11 (+0.033), and the product-law interchange edge (+0.067) all proved load-bearing, with the most structurally implicated connection the most load-bearing. The "deletion helps" observations were distribution-shift artifacts; nothing tested frees capacity when removed.
