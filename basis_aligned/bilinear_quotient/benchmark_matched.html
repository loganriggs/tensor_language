How much of each module do we understand — with the right stand-in?

The earlier per-module score used one generic stand-in for everything and made most of the model look opaque. Score each module instead with the stand-in matched to what it computes — a local window of the layer's own residual for attention, a token table plus that window for front MLPs, a low-rank bilinear map for the readout — and most of the model resolves: the front grammar machine (attention and MLPs), the front+gatherer attention, and the readout all reach ~0.8–0.95, and layer 4 — long the worst MLP — certifies at 0.82 once scored in its own rotating coordinates (held-out). What is left has now been split in two: the content computation's read interface is about eight stable features (every consumer of the content reads them; filtering the content down to the eight costs only 10–15% of removing it entirely), while the genuinely high-dimensional remainder is construction scratch — material the middle works through but never ships. The frontier is no longer "high-rank content"; it is "simulating a construction process whose output is eight numbers."

What each band is — and why the hold-outs hold out

Front + gatherer attention (L0–5): local-window routing of the residual, ~0.66–0.95. With the correct instrument — a window of the layer's own residual input, not the raw token embedding (§1054 corrects §1047) — attention layers 0–4 land at 0.82–0.95 and layer 5 at 0.68. A broad causal-mean pool does not beat the window, so the loss-bearing part of the attention is local routing; the broad content-pooling lives in the value- residual / MLP path, not the attention output. Front MLPs (L0–1): grammar, ~90% (mlp1, the dominant writer, 0.95). Middle attention (L6–11): partial (0.5–0.68), with modest stakes; deep attention (L12–17): low recovery but near-zero stakes (removing a layer costs ≤0.02 nats), so effectively understood by negligible contribution. Deep-middle MLPs (5–14): the re-scoped frontier. Exactly bilinear and structured — they multiply the accumulated content by itself — and their read interface is now known to be ~8 stable features (the content API; the dense remainder is construction scratch, read by nothing). Stand-ins here top out near 0.4–0.6 because simulating the construction still needs the high-rank scratch; reading the result does not. The same eight features recur across the model family (7/8 in a different architecture, 5/8 at a different width). Readout (L15–17): ~90%. Near-linear / low-rank bilinear reads. The whole-model gap is the construction process, not the representation.

A second, causal certificate: context locality (added later)

The bars above score stand-in fidelity — can a simple map reproduce what the module computes. A later program certified an independent, fully causal property: context locality — run the real weights on truncated context and pay the price in prediction loss. Results, all with registered predictions and wrong-text controls: where every one of the 162 attention heads looks is computable from the weights over the last ~128 tokens — replace all patterns at once and the model loses 0.014 nats (control: 1.51); every MLP, all 18, recomputes from its position's last ≤64 tokens (0.002–0.014 each); a joint stack of twelve such reductions runs at ≈0.03–0.04 nats total (~1% of loss, audited on fresh data). The one genuinely long-range channel is attention's value pooling — worth ~0.07 nats at a 128-token horizon on natural prose, carried redundantly by layers 5–8, and worth ~30× more on repetitive text where the induction machinery engages. Both certificates hold, with near-identical constants, in an independently trained sibling model. So the frontier statement sharpens: modules are locally computable and largely stand-in-able; what resists is simulating the construction — a process that must be run, though only ever on nearby context.

A third kind of row: two attention heads replaced by a computation you can read (added later)

The copy circuit's front end — the two "matcher" heads that look for earlier copies of the current token — now has a working stand-in built entirely from legible parts: look up the raw embedding codes of the current token and the candidate source, push them through the heads' own query/key weight pipelines (no forward passes of the model), map the resulting score through one fitted 2-parameter affine function, and write the output onto one direction of the residual stream (the certified match-evidence axis). Held-out recovery: 0.92 of the two heads' 2.0-nat function on the repeat regime — within 3 points of transplanting the true axis values (0.95), 17 points above a "matching is happening" level-only baseline, with a shuffled-score control landing exactly at that baseline. The general-context version (max score over all far candidate sources, no offset knowledge) was then run and generalizes: 97.4% recovery on a repeat period it was never fitted on. The reduction was then extended to the whole four-head front end: add the found source's successor token's layer-0 value code through the fetcher heads' projection slices, scaled by two measured signed constants, and 0.78 of the full four-head function returns — everything computed from the weight matrices, the embedding table, and four scalars. (Flipping the two signs sends recovery to −111%: the circuit's sign conventions are semantic content.) Remaining scope boundary: the verbatim-repeat regime, where copying binds. This is the strongest per-module row on the board: not a fitted imitation of the module's outputs, but the module's own criterion, read out of its weights, driving one named variable.
