How much of each module do we understand — with the right stand-in?

The earlier per-module score used one generic stand-in for everything and made most of the model look opaque. Score each module instead with the stand-in matched to what it computes — a local window of the layer's own residual for attention, a token table plus that window for front MLPs, a low-rank bilinear map for the readout — and most of the model resolves: the front grammar machine (attention and MLPs), the front+gatherer attention, and the readout all reach ~0.8–0.95, and layer 4 — long the worst MLP — certifies at 0.82 once scored in its own rotating coordinates (held-out). What is left has now been split in two: the content computation's read interface is about eight stable features (every consumer of the content reads them; filtering the content down to the eight costs only 10–15% of removing it entirely), while the genuinely high-dimensional remainder is construction scratch — material the middle works through but never ships. The frontier is no longer "high-rank content"; it is "simulating a construction process whose output is eight numbers."

What each band is — and why the hold-outs hold out

Front + gatherer attention (L0–5): local-window routing of the residual, ~0.66–0.95. With the correct instrument — a window of the layer's own residual input, not the raw token embedding (§1054 corrects §1047) — attention layers 0–4 land at 0.82–0.95 and layer 5 at 0.68. A broad causal-mean pool does not beat the window, so the loss-bearing part of the attention is local routing; the broad content-pooling lives in the value- residual / MLP path, not the attention output. Front MLPs (L0–1): grammar, ~90% (mlp1, the dominant writer, 0.95). Middle attention (L6–11): partial (0.5–0.68), with modest stakes; deep attention (L12–17): low recovery but near-zero stakes (removing a layer costs ≤0.02 nats), so effectively understood by negligible contribution. Deep-middle MLPs (5–14): the re-scoped frontier. Exactly bilinear and structured — they multiply the accumulated content by itself — and their read interface is now known to be ~8 stable features (the content API; the dense remainder is construction scratch, read by nothing). Stand-ins here top out near 0.4–0.6 because simulating the construction still needs the high-rank scratch; reading the result does not. The same eight features recur across the model family (7/8 in a different architecture, 5/8 at a different width). Readout (L15–17): ~90%. Near-linear / low-rank bilinear reads. The whole-model gap is the construction process, not the representation.
