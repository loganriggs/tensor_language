How much of each module do we understand — with the right stand-in?

The earlier per-module score used one generic stand-in for everything and made most of the model look opaque. Score each module instead with the stand-in matched to what it computes — a local token window for front attention, a token table plus that window for front MLPs, a low-rank bilinear map for the readout — and the front grammar machine and the readout resolve to ~90%. What is left is not a mystery but two named mechanisms bounded by real properties: broad content pooling, and a genuinely high-dimensional content computation.

What each band is — and why the hold-outs hold out

Front (L0–1): grammar machine, ~90%. Local-window attention writing part-of-speech; token+window MLPs (mlp1, the dominant writer, reaches 0.95). Transition (L2–4): partial — local structure giving way to pooling. Content gatherers (attn3–5) & broad middle attention (6–14): bounded — these are broad recency-weighted pools of the value residual (bag-of-words content), a named mechanism a local window cannot reconstruct. Deep-middle MLPs (5–14): the frontier. Exactly bilinear, and structured — they multiply the pooled content by itself (content × content) — but that content is genuinely high-dimensional (you need almost the full content subspace, confirmed three independent ways), so a bounded stand-in tops out near 0.4–0.6. Readout (L15–17): ~90%. Near-linear / low-rank bilinear reads of the built residual; the readout attention is essentially inert. The remaining whole-model gap is that high-rank content — located and named, not an "irreducible ceiling."
