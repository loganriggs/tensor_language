Three Circuits, One Variable

Three of the best-certified circuits in an 18-layer, 546M-parameter language model — what each one computes, where it physically lives, why the model needs it, and the single intermediate variable all three turn out to consume. Every claim below is causal: established by removing, scrambling, or window-restricting the component and measuring the damage, always against controls.

Units and methods in one breath. Damage is measured in nats of cross-entropy (prediction loss); this model's total is ≈3.3 nats/token, so 0.9 nats from one component is enormous and 0.01 is small. A read-mask blinds a head beyond a distance window without touching its output machinery; an ablation zeroes its output; a scramble feeds it wrong-token information at the right scale. Heads are named layer.head (e.g. 3.8 = layer 3, head 8).

The variable first: block-0's per-token value code

At layer 0, attention computes a value vector for every token — a static, per-token code (the same token always gets the same vector). This architecture has an unusual plumbing feature: every later layer's attention mixes its own values with these layer-0 values, half-and-half (v = ½·v + ½·v₁, the "value-residual"). So block-0's token code is broadcast through the entire network — it rides inside every attention value at every layer.

The demonstration that all three circuits below drink from this one pipe (§1236): scramble the code — swap which position's vector belongs to which token, keeping magnitudes right — separately along its two routes:

One corruption of the broadcast kills copying (nearly the whole long-range budget) and content (with a 2.6× tilt toward content words over grammar) at once, while the same corruption of the local write kills neither. Identity-permutation control is exact; combined scramble ≥ each single. One variable, one distribution channel, three consumers.

Circuit 1 — the identity matcher

"have I seen this token before — and where?"
WhatA long-range equality test. For each position, two heads look back across hundreds of tokens for earlier copies of the current token. On text whose second half repeats its first, they park 30–33% of all their long-range attention on the single position exactly one period back — the query token's own earlier occurrence. This is not a repeat-only mode: on ordinary prose the same heads put ~10% of their far attention on self-match positions, 6× their layer-mates' baseline. The criterion — token identity — is never switched off (§1215, §1219). WhereHeads 2.5 and 3.8, front of the network. The localization is extreme: window-restrict only head 3.8's reads and 1.04 nats of repeat-text performance vanishes, while its eight layer-mates together account for 0.004 (§1206–07). Upstream, layers 0–1's attention (attn0/attn1) build the per-position identity representations being compared — zero their writes at the source half and the matchers' characteristic reads collapse 58–71% (§1228). One auxiliary, 3.1, only helps while 3.8 is alive — blind the main matcher and 3.1's reads turn actively harmful (§1213). WhyMatching is step one of copying/induction — the model's single most profitable long-range behavior. With the matchers working, verbatim-repeated text costs 0.36 nats/token against 3.3 on fresh prose: repetition is nearly free. What the match produces is a where — a location downstream circuits can exploit. Notably, this model pays for matching with dedicated long-range reads; its softmax-attention sibling does the same match locally inside its keys and needs no matcher heads at all. Which design a model uses is decided by its attention score function — both cost the same total (§1217–18).

Evidence: §1204–1207 (localization), §1215 (offset signature), §1219 (prose criterion), §1213 (auxiliary), §1228–1230 (builders). Uses the shared variable as: the substance compared — match keys and queries are built from stream content descending from the broadcast token code.

Circuit 2 — the successor fetcher

"…then what came next?"
WhatThe retrieval step. Where the matchers attend a token's earlier copy (offset 128 on period-128 repeat text), these heads attend one position after it — offset 127, the successor — and deliver its value as the prediction's raw material. The two signatures are cleanly split: matchers peak at 128 with ~1% at 127; fetchers peak at 127 (§1215). WhereHeads 8.3 and 8.4, mid-stack — a genuinely redundant pair: each alone costs 0.10–0.17 when read-masked, the two jointly 0.54 (87% of their layer) — one covers for the other (§1207). A third fetcher, 5.5 (the head the old head-atlas called "the induction head"), has the same offset-127 signature but is fully backed up by the pair: masking its range costs 0.086 (§1205, §1215). The four named heads together carry 69% of everything all 162 heads do at long range when copying binds; a distributed tail carries the rest — the honest split is 59/41 (§1209). WhyFetching turns a match into a prediction: "the last time X appeared, Y followed" is the entire algorithm of induction, and induction is where this architecture's one long-range channel earns most of its 3.2-nat repeat-regime value — a price that is identical across three architecturally different siblings (3.200 / 3.206 / 3.231 nats), because what repetition is worth is set by the text, not the machine (§1212). The payload the fetchers deliver is value vectors — which, by the plumbing above, are half block-0 token code: corrupt the broadcast and fetching collapses even though every pattern is intact (§1236).

Evidence: §1205–1210 (stations and redundancy), §1215 (signature), §1209 (necessity/sufficiency), §1212 (family constant), §1236 (payload). Uses the shared variable as: the payload delivered — the fetched value is λ·v₁-laden.

Circuit 3 — the content pool

"what is this text about?"
WhatThe model's topic sense. Attention in the middle layers accumulates a bag of nearby tokens' block-0 codes into the residual stream; by layers 6–14 this bag has become a high-dimensional content representation whose readable core is ~8 stable, human-nameable features (a livestock topic, a political-register axis, a partitive construction…) read as a package by everything downstream (§1076, §1113–15). Patching a real passage's content pattern into another text transports its topic — 62× the random-direction effect (§1059–60). WhereUnlike circuits 1 and 2, this one has no star heads: it is a crowd. The carrying band is layers 5–9 (read-masking the band costs 0.067 nats on prose while every single layer in it costs ≤0.009 — §1186–87), and an exhaustive screen of all 162 heads found that the best nameable 12-head core carries only 43% of the prose pooling budget; the other half lives in ~150 heads at ≤0.002 each, whose value is synergistic — random subsets are worth more per head the more of them you take (§1222–23). Tellingly, the top of that 12-head core is circuits 1 and 2's stations doing double duty (~22% of prose pooling). WhyContent is the hard 77% of language-model loss (grammar is the easy 23%). The pooled bag supplies every deep MLP's working material — the deep layers' computation is literally context×context multiplication on it (§1041) — and the readout consumes its 8-feature core at the end. It is also the reason the model needs any long-range reading on ordinary prose at all: the entire non-copy long-range budget (~0.07 nats at a 128-token horizon) is this pooling (§1180–81). And its substance is the same broadcast code: scramble v₁ and content-word prediction degrades 2.6× more than grammar (§1236), matching the old result that deleting the value-residual costs +3.3 nats, content-tilted (§FINDINGS 4a).

Evidence: §1049–1076 (the manifold and its origin), §1113–1115 (skeleton/API), §1186–87 (the crowd), §1222–23 (core vs tail), §1236 (substance). Uses the shared variable as: the bag contents — the pool is an aggregate of broadcast token codes.

Postscript: the criterion read straight off the weights — with a minus sign

After this page was first drafted, the matching criterion was pushed all the way down to the weight matrices (§1237–1240). Three facts, in the order they fell:

1. It's computable with zero forward passes. Feed raw token embedding codes through heads 2.5 and 3.8's actual query/key pipelines and score all pairs: same-token pairs separate from different-token pairs perfectly (AUC discriminability 1.00; their sixteen layer-mates average 0.00). "Is this the same token?" is a fact about the weights and the embedding table alone.

2. The algebra is a mirrored signed conjunction. This architecture's attention score is a product of two bilinear forms, and it has no softmax, so scores keep their sign. At same-token pairs, each matcher runs one positive-scoring branch and one negative-scoring branch — head 2.5 as (+0.74, −0.78), head 3.8 as (−0.80, +0.81), mirror twins — so the product is a large, consistently negative match flag. In the live model the matchers really do deliver −1× the matched token's value (in-vivo mean signed pattern −0.30 and −0.42, 99–100% sign-consistent). Even the fetchers split by sign: 8.4 delivers +successor, 8.3 −successor, each 100% consistent.

3. The minus sign is load-bearing. Flip one matcher's pattern sign — touch nothing else — and copying degrades more than if the head were blinded outright (1.43 vs 1.08 nats); flip both matchers and the damage (7.81) exceeds twice the value of the entire long-range system. Wrong-signed evidence is the worst input this program has ever fed the model — downstream circuits trust the convention completely. (Flipping a low-value head as control: 0.0004.)

How the three compose — and one circuit that pointedly doesn't join

The composition is a supply chain: block-0 writes a token code → the value-residual broadcasts it everywhere → the matchers compare it across distance (circuit 1) → the fetchers deliver it from the matched site (circuit 2) → the crowd pools it into topic (circuit 3) — with the deep MLPs and readout consuming the last two products. Circuits 1+2 dominate when text repeats (3.2 nats at stake); circuit 3 dominates on fresh prose (0.07 nats at stake but feeding the hard 77% of prediction). Because all three are downstream of one variable, a single scramble of that variable degrades all of them at once — which is exactly what §1236 shows, and why the value-residual is the most protected channel in the architecture (it is also the most register-robust one, §1079–1112).

The contrast that proves the sharing is real rather than "everything depends on everything": the model's single most expensive head, 5.7 (0.92 nats), belongs to a circuit that uses none of this. An MLP manufactures a fixed vector at position 0; head 5.7 fetches it for 99.8% of queries; the vector is the stream's baseline — replace the whole head with that one constant and 98.5% of its function survives. No token code, no matching, no pooling: a bias-delivery circuit, whose entire "information need" was later shown to be two bits of the current token's frequency class (§1234). Sharing the variable is a property of these three circuits, not of the network at large.
