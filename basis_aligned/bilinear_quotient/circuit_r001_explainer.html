The Line-Break Circuit

One census circuit from the bilin18 model, explained end to end — components, what it fires on, and what it does to the loss, with mechanically chosen examples.

What it is

In list-formatted text — numbered sight-seeing entries, hotel listings, headed sections — the model constantly has to decide whether the current line ends here. This circuit is a piece of that decision. The census found it as a set of 184 positions (tag r.0.0.1) whose loss depends, far more than anywhere else, on the same small piece of machinery: four bundles of output directions in the first and fourth MLP layers — directions 12–16 and 48–64 of mlp0's output variation on this context family, and directions 16–24 and 24–32 of mlp3's. Nearly every member position is one where the next token is a layout newline (or a heading marker), inside entries like 26Barrafina⏎⏎27Barrafina….

The causal test

The test is the same operation the census used to find it: run the model normally, but delete those four direction-bundles from the two MLPs' outputs (project them out at every position), and measure how the per-position loss changes. If the circuit story is right, positions inside the neighborhood should move a lot and everything else should barely move. That holds:

+1.01mean ΔCE, 96 members that use the directions
−2.73mean ΔCE, 88 members the directions oppose
+0.44same document family, non-members
+0.13everywhere else

The two-signed split is not a bug — it is a certified property of these circuits (sign-mixedness). The direction-bundles push a line-break expectation into the stream. At half the member positions the document does break, so deleting the push hurts (up to +5.2 nats on a single token). At the other half the layout does something else — the entry continues, or a heading follows — and there the same push was fighting the right answer, so deleting it helps (down to −6.8 nats). The circuit is a policy, and like any policy it is sometimes wrong; the census clusters by who is affected, not by whether the effect is good.

Two things the examples show honestly. First, base losses of 5–26 nats: these are positions the model finds genuinely hard — ambiguous layout choices — which is exactly where a dedicated policy earns its keep. Second, the sign structure is visible even among the top five: the Westminster Abbey row improves by 6.8 nats under ablation, because there the break-push was wrong.

What was registered, and what failed

Before running, two bars were registered: (a) member damage ≥ 4× the same-document non-member damage, and (b) at least 4 of the 5 random members hurt when ablated. Both failed — because both assumed the circuit only ever helps. The measured structure is a two-signed policy whose effect size concentrates on the neighborhood: mean |ΔCE| of 2.7 nats on member positions, 1.5 on non-member positions in the same documents, 0.35 in the rest of the corpus — 7.7× the corpus background, but only 1.8× the same-document neighbors, so what really separates members from their neighbors is the signed structure above, not raw effect size. That is a more specific picture than the registered story — but the registered story as written was wrong, and stays on the record.

Scope caveats. These 184 positions come from the census fit window, which is dominated by one travel-guide book — the family's corpus-generality was established separately (the place-suffix-then-newline mechanism certified at 7.7–7.9× precision lift on held-out triggers, in sibling circuits r.3.0/r.8.0). Circuit tags are relative to one tree build; the same physical circuit gets different tags in different rebuilds, so identity across builds goes through member overlap, not names. None of the ten examples lands in the top-8 registered trigger pairs of those siblings — this leaf is the grid-reference and venue-name end of the same layout family, not the pure place-suffix end.

Why this one matters

This circuit is a clean instance of the program's central finding about what census circuits are: causally-selective dependence neighborhoods. It has a nameable job (line-break policy in listings), specific early-layer machinery (mlp0/mlp3 direction-bundles), large causal footprint concentrated on its neighborhood (7.7× the corpus background), and a two-signed action that any honest description has to include. It is also one vote for the compositional picture: the machinery lives in layers 0 and 3 — contextual layout information is computed early and read late.
