bilin18 · variable-level understanding benchmark

How much of the model do we understand — component by component?

Each of the 18 layers has two components: an attention block and an MLP. The benchmark replaces one component at a time with a stand-in built only from named variables — the current token, a running topic vector, and the previous token — and asks how much of that component's own contribution to the loss the stand-in recovers (1 = fully explained, 0 = no better than deleting it, negative = the stand-in is worse than deleting it).

The per-component graph

Read left to right through depth. Grammar is written and understood at the front; the content machine in the middle sits at or below zero. Attention 5, the content-pooling layer, shows the largest negative here — but a later, cleaner re-scoring (§1039) found that its −4.84 is an artifact of this stand-in family (the topic-subspace term is off-distribution for a pooled output); with a plain per-token or bag-of-words stand-in it scores near zero. attn 5 is mechanistically well understood — it is the recency-weighted content-word pool over the value residual — just not reconstructible by these simple stand-ins.

The headline number over time

What actually changed is rigor, not the score: the in-sample 0.81 was memorization; the honest held-out number is a draw-sensitive band around a third of the model.
