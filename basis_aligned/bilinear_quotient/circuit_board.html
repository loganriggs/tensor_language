How to read the numbers

Every quantity on this page reduces to one measurement: how much worse the model predicts the next token when a piece of it is silenced. Definitions in the order you'll meet them.

nat
The unit of prediction loss. A prediction's cross-entropy is −ln p(actual next token). A damage of +0.7 nats means the model's probability on the correct token was roughly halved (e0.7 ≈ 2); +2.3 nats means cut to a tenth. All numbers are averages over held-out FineWeb text the model never fit on.
mean-ablation
How a component is "silenced": its output at every position is replaced by its average output over 24 unrelated text rows. The component still emits something of the right size — it just carries no information about the current input.
target / elsewhere
Positions where the capability fires vs. everywhere else. Target masks are computed mechanically from the token stream — e.g. comparative targets = positions whose next token is "than" with a comparative word 2–20 tokens back. The first 64 tokens of each row are excluded (burn-in).
damage (dCE)
CE(ablated) − CE(intact) at a position or averaged over a mask. The per-example numbers below are exactly this, per position.
concentration
target damage ÷ elsewhere damage for the same ablation. 45.9 means silencing the component hurts its targets ~46× more than everything else — the signature of a specialist rather than a generalist.
jitter control
The same damage measured at target positions shifted +2 tokens — same local text, wrong position. Catches "the component just matters near this kind of text." Must be ≤1.5× before a screen verdict is issued.
random control
The same damage at a count-matched random position set. Catches "the component matters everywhere and the targets are just positions." Same ≤1.5× bar. A screen with a dirty control gets no verdict — it reruns at more data.
gap & recovery
For extractions: gap = target CE with every attention head mean-ablated minus target CE of the full model. Recovery of an arm = (CE(all-ablated) − CE(arm)) ÷ gap: 0 = all attention dead, 1 = full model. "0.714 recovery" means the extracted kit restores 71.4% of what attention as a whole was worth at those targets.
route
The baseline every extraction keeps. bilin18 mixes each head's values with block-0's values (v = ½v + ½v₁). The route arm keeps that block-0 broadcast — nearly free to describe, since it's a per-layer scalar times values the extraction keeps anyway — and replaces only each head's fresh values with their mean. It alone recovers 33–44% of any capability measured so far, so extraction claims are always quoted above it.
band
A contiguous run of attention layers, written like a02 = attention layers 0–2, L0–5 = layers 0 through 5.
gate
A position mask inside which a band runs live (outside it's routed). Gates used so far are pure token properties — "comparative token here", "question clause open" — so they cost ~zero bits to describe. Twice now, a gated band has beaten the same band fully live: under a partial extraction, extra live outputs can be interference.
selectivity
Of a removal: damage at targets vs. elsewhere when the specialist is deleted from the extraction. "26× surgical" = removing 8.1 costs 0.361 nats at comparative targets and 0.014 elsewhere.
owner / crowd / pair
The three structural forms found so far. Owner: one head carries the capability (8.1 = 100.7% of its layer's comparative effect). Crowd: many heads share it, none necessary (question's layer-4 state: top head 32%). Pair: two heads split one job additively (17.2+17.3, joint = sum of solos within 2.6%).
G3 / G1 / G2
The three goals a circuit must pass to close. G3 weights-read: predict the behavior from the weight matrices alone, then certify the prediction on natural text. G1 extract: rebuild the capability from a small itemized kit (route + gates + specialist) and measure its recovery. G2 remove: delete the specialist and show only the capability degrades.
kit
The extracted circuit as a parts list: everything kept live while all other attention is mean-replaced, with each part's description cost. Costs are qualitative for now: ~free = per-layer scalars and masks computable from the token stream; weights kept = actual head/band parameters retained. The recovery ladders below add parts one at a time, so each row's increment is what that part alone buys on top of everything above it.

The closer, across the family

The flagship universality result (§1373–78): three independently trained sibling models — spanning two attention score functions — all build the closer as one surgical head at matched relative depth, with layer-mates that are net interference at its targets. Every bet registered before its run; five clean sweeps.

Shares above 100% mean keeping only the owner beats the intact layer — the other heads are net noise at its targets. The training problem, not the architecture, determines that a closer exists, that it is one head, and where it lives; the score function modulates only its strength (sqrd12's softened quote cell is exactly where its whole closer is weaker). §1373–78.

The two-tier bill: one 22-head commons (~19.5M params, twice-vetted — the union of two independent drop-cost rankings with both bands' dead weight removed) + 6 unique specialist heads (~5.3M) + zero-bit token-computed gates + the ~free route ≈ 25M params — 4.6% of the model — for 66–87% of four capability families. At the margin a new capability costs 1–5 heads. Six "width inversions" along the way taught the design rule: past the necessary depth, more live heads subtract — and one sharp corollary, never gate a constant (gating the sink head cost 0.44 nats of boundary seam). Ledger: §1366–68.

About the examples below: every example is real held-out FineWeb text. Illustrative = the 5 targets where silencing the circuit's specialist hurt most (selection criterion disclosed: sorted by dCE). Random = 5 targets drawn with a fixed seed, damage as it fell. The spread between the two groups is honest information: it shows how evenly the circuit's load is distributed over its targets.

Induction — the extraction that taught the grain G1 PARTIAL

The oldest and largest extraction target: in-context copying, worth ~5 nats at identity-supported targets. Its ladder is the program's clearest record of what description actually buys the loss back — each rung is a different answer to "what is a circuit?", and the jump between rungs 2 and 3 changed the whole program's accounting from head-count to description length.

The rung 2→3 jump (+49 points for ~zero added description) is why every later extraction starts from the route: the crowd's contribution to copying is carried by the block-0 value broadcast, which costs almost nothing to keep. Honest caveat that comes with it: the same broadcast carries the model's general content pool, so "elsewhere" recovers 68% too — the route is a shared substrate, not a circuit part. The remaining 21% gap is the open frontier; two attempts to replace the kept heads with printable code both failed.
