# Symmetric quadratic block least squares — 2026-09-20 16:26 UTC

Primary-literature follow-up to failed full quadratic variable projection: compare alternating exact linear block solves with Adam/Muon on three planted quadratic targets. Widths4/3/4 exactly accommodate coordinate-sparse, bilinear-CP, and sparse-Tucker teachers. Three paired initializations;500 ALS sweeps or1200 gradient steps at0.05. Different per-step costs are recorded, so this is an algorithm/compute comparison, not equal-iteration fairness.

Optimize the symmetric polynomial tensor, not an arbitrary unsymmetrized representative. Given C and B, canonical quadratic coefficients are linear in A; likewise for B and C. Build the small exact design matrices and solve by SVD least squares, avoiding normal equations. Normalize rows of A/B and compensate C, preserving function. Verify objective monotonicity within1e-8 squared relative tolerance; save factors and histories. No global-convergence or sparse-basis guarantee is assumed.

References motivating the algorithm choice: O'Leary and Rust, variable projection (https://www.cs.umd.edu/users/oleary/software/varpro.pdf); Minster et al., stable CP-ALS with QR/SVD (https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10855); Hu et al., CP-ALS convergence under orthogonal/incoherent assumptions (https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14037). The last paper's ordinary-CP guarantees are not transferred to our symmetrized, potentially redundant parametrization. Native design matrices would be too large to form; a successful toy control would motivate matrix-free block solves, not this dense implementation at full width.
