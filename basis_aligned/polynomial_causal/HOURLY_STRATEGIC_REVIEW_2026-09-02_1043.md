## Circuit interpretation targets

A useful circuit account must eventually provide all of the following:

1. specify what information is read, what operation is performed, what is written, and what later computation uses
   that write;
2. group parts of different heads or MLPs when downstream computation treats them as one variable, and split one
   native module when its parts serve different tasks;
3. predict held-out documents, circuit families, and shifted inputs;
4. extract an executable subcomputation or a precise interface that reproduces the target effect;
5. selectively remove, swap, or edit the target while preserving unrelated behavior;
6. explain how shared and task-specific pieces compose and are reused; and
7. identify the same units across fitting restarts, data splits, and legal gauges, or define them by downstream
   operational equivalence.

The full goal is a smaller transparent tensor program that is predictive, composable, and manipulable. Literal
storage and compute are eventual prices. Rank, quantization, reconstruction, variance explained, or CE alone do not
discover a circuit and do not satisfy the goal.
