## Exact theorem mapping

Rota's incidence-algebra Möbius inversion on a finite poset applies exactly to the Boolean lattice of source subsets (G.-C. Rota, “On the Foundations of Combinatorial Theory I. Theory of Möbius Functions,” 1964, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00536294). The mapping is:

- poset element $S$ is an intervention subset;
- the zeta transform is $F(S)=\sum_{T\subseteq S}\mu(T)$ after anchoring $F(\varnothing)$;
- inversion gives $\mu(T)=\sum_{S\subseteq T}(-1)^{|T|-|S|}F(S)$;
- all $2^6$ values uniquely determine all $2^6$ coefficients in $O(6\,2^6)$ arithmetic per scalar cell via the fast subset transform.

The assumptions—finite complete lattice and one deterministic value for every subset—are met after exact instrument closure. The theorem is indifferent to neural-network nonlinearity and therefore exactly solves interaction accounting. It does not guarantee that $Y$ or $Z$ is semantic, stable across corpora, identifiable under alternate operational partitions, sufficient outside the fixed downstream interface, or selectively removable without collateral damage. Those are empirical circuit conditions, not violations of the inversion theorem.

Shapley–Taylor interaction indices (Sundararajan, Dhamdhere, and Agarwal, 2020, primary manuscript: https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05622) provide a different weighted attribution when only interactions up to a chosen order are reported. That object is not needed here: all 64 corners are intentionally observed, signed high-order terms are scientifically meaningful through normalization and the tail, and weighting them into an attribution would obscure the registered parent-child closure. It is a possible summary only after identification, not a replacement for the exact lattice.

Tensor-rank, Tucker, tensor-train, and polynomial-decomposition algorithms do not exactly solve the current identification problem because they require choosing a coefficient tensor and approximation norm whose coordinates are not yet semantic. They could price or compress an already identified response family, but would not establish selective causal transfer or stable grouping.
