## Circuit interpretation targets

A useful decomposition must eventually provide all seven kinds of evidence:

1. **Computational specification:** what information is read, what operation is performed, what is written, and which later computation uses it.
2. **Grouping across modules and splitting within modules:** merge head/MLP pieces when downstream computation treats them as one variable, and split a native module when its pieces do different jobs.
3. **Held-out and out-of-distribution prediction:** predict activations and behavioral effects on unseen examples and shifted task variants.
4. **Extraction or sufficiency:** an isolated executable circuit, or a precisely defined interface plus background, reproduces the target computation or signed causal effect.
5. **Selective manipulation:** removing, swapping, or editing the circuit changes the intended behavior while preserving unrelated behaviors, with redundancy and interactions measured explicitly.
6. **Composition and reuse:** shared pieces support multiple tasks or modules, and their joint behavior is predictable.
7. **Stable identification:** the units survive data splits, optimization seeds, and plausible gauges, or are defined operationally by downstream equivalence.

The full program goal remains a smaller transparent tensor program that is predictive on fresh and shifted text, composable when replacements are installed together, manipulable by registered edits, and simpler in literal storage, compute, edges, states, and program description. Lower cross-entropy or lower rank alone does not meet these circuit targets.

## Confound audit

- **Local morphology:** complete-subject donors, absolute coordinated-plural alignment, reverse transfers, and matched plural controls must all survive; local head-noun number is insufficient.
- **Disconnected interventions:** every eligible site needs a finite full-state causal ceiling. A fitted projector that changes an activation but not the answer is not a circuit.
- **Multiple mediators and nonlinear composition:** marginal necessity does not partition an effect. The two-site neutralization and ordered H-to-Q reset/rescue arms must be evaluated as written rather than added arithmetically.
- **Frame mixing and gauge:** a low-dimensional rotated frame is only a probe until held-out interchange and downstream-reader behavior identify it. The spectral direction remains a nonselective comparison, not the semantic answer.
- **Leakage and post-selection:** discovery chooses sites/seeds; locked validation decides transfer. Family-only fits cannot see cross-syntax donors, validation cannot choose a replacement seed, and all coordinated-subject failures remain in the denominator.
- **Precision and optimizer health:** exact dtype, deterministic QR signs, finite gradients, absence of model gradients, seed stability, and rank-two/four falsifiers remain hard gates.
- **Physical-price errors:** a logical step can contain several graph batches. Call counts, tokens, retained bytes, deadline checks, and the separate worst-shape allocator receipt must be reconstructed independently before execution.
- **Publication and reproducibility:** run-unique create-only artifacts are required because older result names have been overwritten by reruns. No success may rely on an untracked mutable receipt.
- **Frontier objective mismatch:** the adopted 0.25 scale shows that local reconstruction and end-to-end loss can disagree. Task 14 avoids mistaking this for circuit evidence by judging counterfactual transfer and selectivity, not reconstruction quality.
