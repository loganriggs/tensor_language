### 1. *-algebra block decomposition of the task's Q-family (the math FOR the
### gauge-aware fallback) — EXECUTED THIS REVIEW (instrument shipped)
**Object.** The exact identity of rung 469: for downstream direction g,
g·ΔMLP = ⟨Q(g), S(x)⟩ with Q(g) = sym(Left^T diag(Down^T g) Right), an
1152×1152 symmetric matrix per direction. A task defines a FAMILY
{Q(g_c)} over circuit/reader directions c.
**Theorem (Artin–Wedderburn; algorithmic: Murota–Kanno–Kojima–Kojima 2010,
Maehara–Murota 2011 error-controlled).** The matrix *-algebra generated by a
family of symmetric matrices admits a canonical simultaneous
block-diagonalization; the blocks are found from the eigenspaces of a generic
element of the COMMUTANT {X : [X, Q(g_c)] = 0 ∀c}, and are unique up to
orthogonal transforms WITHIN blocks. This is exactly a gauge quotient: the
decomposition is invariant under the within-block gauge group, so any claim
made at block grain automatically satisfies the two-coordinate invariance
clause — frame-relativity is quotiented out by construction, not checked
empirically. It is also task-conditioned cross-module structure in the 00:47
sense: shared block patterns across {Q_m8, Q_m9, Q_m12} families = pieces of
different MLPs implementing the same downstream computation.
**Assumptions that may fail.** (a) The task family may generate the full
algebra (trivial commutant = only identity) — then NO block structure exists
at any within-block gauge, a clean strong null; (b) approximate blocks need
the error-controlled variant (eigen-gap of the commutant operator), not exact
nullspaces; (c) 1152² operator is Lanczos-heavy — restrict to the task
response subspace first (which is itself the program's learned-geometry
theme).
**Measurable consequence beyond reconstruction.** Block sizes = executable
cost of the compiled bilinear form; block-respecting projectors = selective
removal handles that commute with the mechanism (removal of one block
provably cannot leak into another through Q); cross-MLP shared blocks =
composable circuit pieces.
**Cheapest falsifier.** CPU: build {Q(g_c)} for already-opened reader

### 3. Stability selection over matching-pursuit supports (guards 478's
### successors)
**Object.** The gate supports 478's matching pursuit selects (≤32 terms).
**Statement (Meinshausen–Bühlmann 2010).** Greedy selection (Mallat–Zhang
MP; Tropp's OMP conditions rarely verifiable here) is notoriously
support-unstable under resampling even when fit quality is stable. Stability
selection — refit on complementary halves, keep terms selected with
frequency ≥ π — bounds expected false selections. A removal rung that trusts
an unstable support inherits its variance.
