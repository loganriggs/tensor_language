# Why the MLP10 grouping search kept failing — and the one real thing underneath it (2026-09-03 05:01 UTC)

Plain-language chapter summary (Claude lane, ledger §2657–§2661). Expands project shorthand.

## The setup

We are trying to carve MLP10 (the model's 11th feed-forward block) into reusable pieces. MLP10 reads the
residual stream — the running sum of everything the earlier 10 attention layers and 10 MLPs wrote — and applies
a bilinear map (it multiplies two linear projections of its input together). For months the "equality-score"
arc (rungs 506–520) asked a grouping question: are two different earlier writes, or the same write under two
different upstream computations, treated as THE SAME variable by MLP10's downstream circuits? Every version
nulled: single interaction terms, single sources, head-by-source pairs, term subspaces, grouped 22-term
"source stars" (a source star = every bilinear interaction term containing one source). Fifteen rungs, no
reusable unit found.

## The diagnosis: the measuring instrument was too weak, not necessarily the structure absent (§2657)

Each grouping test compares "circuit fingerprints" — a 32-number vector saying how much removing something
changes the loss on 32 known downstream circuits (member tokens minus matched controls). I measured how
reproducible a single fingerprint is by correlating it between two disjoint halves of the documents. The
answer: **0.016** — essentially zero. A fingerprint does not even agree with itself across document halves,
because each circuit's effect is estimated from only ~300 tokens per half and is swamped by sampling noise.

This matters because of a classical result (attenuation): the correlation you can observe between two noisy
measurements is capped by the geometric mean of their self-reliabilities. At reliability 0.016, EVERY grouping
test is capped near zero regardless of whether real grouping exists. So the rung 506–520 nulls stand as scored,
but they are power-bounded — they cannot distinguish "no reusable structure" from "structure below the noise
floor." (They stand as recorded; I flipped no conclusion — that would need an independent physical control.)

## The fix: pool, don't correlate raw (§2658)

The right estimator does not correlate noisy fingerprints; it pools them. Because the two document halves have
independent noise, the cross-covariance of half-0 against half-1 fingerprints has an expectation equal to the
SIGNAL covariance — the noise averages to zero, not to a positive bias. Pooling all 83 live source-by-score
nodes, this recovers a reliable ~3-dimensional shared subspace of the 32-circuit effect space (its top three
eigenvalues clear a permutation null). So real shared structure IS there; the earlier tests just could not see
it one fingerprint at a time.

Important caveat from the same probe: the top mode does NOT beat a "shuffle sources within a score
implementation" null. Meaning the shared subspace is SOURCE-SHARED — every one of MLP10's 22 input sources
projects onto the same few circuit directions — not source-specific. This is the "MLP10 reads context as one
low-dimensional summary" law, now derived at MLP10 by a noise-immune route.

## The budget (§2659)

Converting the 0.016 reliability into an action plan: to make a SINGLE fingerprint individually reliable you
need ~27x more documents (to reach reliability 0.3), ~62x (0.5), or ~250x (0.8). OR you pool: as few as 16
nodes already crosses detectability, rising smoothly to the full 83. The pooling curve being smooth and
monotone also validates the whole independent-noise model, so the numbers are trustworthy. Two levers, and
pooling is the free one.

## What is actually there (§2660, §2661)

- **§2660 — nothing private, yet.** After removing the shared 3-dim, the leftover has no reliable structure
  (it sits below the noise floor). So at current data size ALL reliable MLP10 circuit-effect structure is the
  one shared summary; there is no reliable per-source ("private") part. This directly bounds Codex's rung521
  DAS: its private-residual stage has no reliable target in the 32-circuit basis it scores on, without more
  documents or a richer scoring basis.
- **§2661 — the shared summary, named.** The shared 3-dim object is highly reproducible under resampling
  (bootstrap stability 0.95 for the top direction, 0.97 for the subspace). Its circuit content is concentrated
  on BLOCK-6 downstream circuits (five of the top eight: r.6.3.0, r.6.0.2, r.6.2.2, r.6.2.3, r.6.0.1), plus one
  block-0 (r.0.0.1) and two block-2 (r.2.2.*). So the low-dimensional context summary that all MLP10 sources
  share predominantly FEEDS block-6 circuits.

## The one-sentence chapter

At current data size, MLP10's causal footprint is not carvable into per-source or per-pair reusable units (that
search was noise-limited), but there IS one reproducible, source-shared ~3-dimensional context summary, and it
feeds mainly block-6 downstream circuits — that shared summary, not per-source pieces, is the reusable object,
and it is the labeled target for the shared stage of the ongoing DAS rung.
