# Plain-English MLP0 interaction-head update — 2026-09-01 15:18 UTC

## Goal and result first

Our full goal is to replace bilin18 with a smaller tensor program that still predicts new text, still works when
several replacements are installed together, and still responds correctly to controlled edits. For MLP0, we are
also trying to explain the complete computation as token-only, token-by-context, and context-only parts.

The latest substantial result refines the token-by-context part:

> MLP0's token×context interaction has one dominant attention0 carrier—layer 0, head 3—but it is not a two-head
> mechanism. Head 3 is supported by a reproducible distributed tail across the other heads.

Here “head 3” is zero-indexed: it is the fourth of attention0's nine heads. It was already the most important
attention0 head under an older whole-head removal test. The new result shows that it is also the largest carrier of
MLP0's isolated token×context computation.

## Terms and computational definitions

### Attention head

Attention0 has nine parallel heads. For a destination position `i`, head `h` computes a weighted sum of value
vectors from allowed source positions `j` in the prefix. In simplified notation,

`y_h(i) = sum_j attention_score_h(i,j) * value_h(j)`.

In bilin18, an attention score is the product of two query/key dot products, with a causal mask preventing access to
future positions. Each `y_h` has 128 coordinates. The model concatenates the nine 128-coordinate head outputs and
applies one 1,152-by-1,152 output matrix. Splitting that matrix into nine column blocks gives the exact head writes

`a_h = O_h y_h`,

and the full attention0 write is `a = sum_h a_h`, apart from a tiny BF16 arithmetic remainder.

### BF16 arithmetic remainder

BF16, or bfloat16, stores floating-point numbers with fewer precision bits than float32. A single matrix
multiplication and nine separately computed partial matrix multiplications can therefore round in slightly different
ways. We define

`a_eps = deployed_attention_write - sum_h a_h`.

This remainder has only **2.75e-6 of the squared energy** of the full attention write. We keep its MLP0 interaction
term separate and never label it as a semantic head feature. Its measured next-token cross-entropy effect is about
**-0.00030 nat**, far inside the preregistered 0.002-nat ceiling.

### Centered token×context interaction

Let `e` be the current token vector and `a` the attention0 context write. MLP0's bilinear map is

`B(u,v) = Down((Left u) * (Right v))`,

where `*` is coordinate-by-coordinate multiplication. After subtracting the fitting-set mean token and mean context,
write the deviations as `delta_e` and `delta_a`. Let `g_bar` be the fitting-set mean of the squared scalar
normalization gain. The fixed-mean-gain token×context term found in the preceding experiment is

`I = g_bar * [B(delta_e, delta_a) + B(delta_a, delta_e)]`.

Because this expression is linear in `delta_a`, and `delta_a` is the sum of the centered head writes, it splits
exactly:

`I = I_0 + I_1 + ... + I_8 + I_eps`.

This is why the head decomposition is not a fitted probe or a choice of basis. It follows directly from the model's
weights and the additivity of attention heads at the output projection.

### Cross-entropy benefit

Cross-entropy is the model's average negative log-probability for the actual next token. Lower is better. A reported
benefit of `+0.10 nat` means that adding the component lowered cross-entropy by 0.10. It does not mean 10% accuracy.

### Singleton and removal tests

Later computation is nonlinear, so one measurement is not enough to call a head important. We used both boundaries:

- `singleton benefit = CE(no semantic interaction heads) - CE(only head h)`;
- `removal benefit = CE(all heads except h) - CE(all heads)`.

The singleton score asks whether a head is sufficient when starting from no semantic interaction. The removal score
asks whether it is necessary when all other heads are available. A head can have a large singleton score and a small
removal score when other heads redundantly reproduce part of its service.

For compact comparison we also report

`endpoint average = (singleton benefit + removal benefit) / 2`.

This average is not a Shapley value; it samples the two physically clearest endpoints rather than every possible
head subset.

### Spearman correlation

Spearman correlation compares two rankings. A value of 1 means exactly the same order, 0 means no monotone ordering,
and -1 means the reverse order. The head ranking reproduced across fitting and held-out documents at **0.933**. The
new isolated-interaction ranking correlates **0.900** with the older whole-attention-head removal ranking.

## What we physically tested

We retained MLP0's token main effect, context main effect, normalization modulation, bias, and exact numerical
normalization residual. Only the nine semantic interaction-head terms changed. On both 96 fitting and 96 disjoint
held-out documents, we ran 21 configurations:

- all nine semantic heads;
- zero interaction, including the numerical head-sum remainder;
- the numerical remainder alone;
- each of nine heads alone;
- all heads except each of the nine heads.

The first run deliberately failed its exact-control bar. The zero-interaction arm summed ten float tensors and then
converted them to BF16, while the parent experiment subtracted one already-computed interaction tensor. That changed
held-out cross-entropy by 1.04e-4, above the frozen 1e-6 equality bar. We preserved that failed receipt, changed only
the zero-interaction control to subtract the identical parent tensor, and reran without changing rows, semantic arms,
or thresholds.

The repaired run reproduces both parent boundaries exactly: maximum cross-entropy difference **0.0**. The nine head
terms plus numerical remainder reconstruct `I` at relative mean-squared error **2.24e-18**. All intervention and call
checks were live, so the registered instrument predicate passed and the strong null did not fire.

Formally, if `G` is the set of all nine heads, then

`alone percentage(h) = 100 * [CE(no heads) - CE({h})] / [CE(no heads) - CE(G)]`,

`removal percentage(h) = 100 * [CE(G without h) - CE(G)] / [CE(no heads) - CE(G)]`.

So the user's reading is exactly right: the left column includes only head `h`; the right column includes every head

loses only about 33%. This is evidence that other heads can redundantly cover part of head 3's service. The alone
percentages should not be summed: because heads can overlap, compensate for one another, or interact downstream,
their separate benefits need not add to 100%. In fact, the nine singleton percentages sum to **190.2%**, while the
nine leave-one-out percentages sum to only **40.1%**. That wide boundary gap is quantitative evidence of overlap or
nonlinear compensation, but it still does not identify which particular pairs produce it.

### What “endpoint average” meant

The preregistration needed one number per head to rank the heads without choosing only the empty or only the full
boundary. It used

`endpoint average(h) = [alone benefit(h) + removal benefit(h)] / 2`.

For head 3 this is `(79.3% + 32.9%)/2 = 56.1%`, or `0.0704 nat`. This average is **not another intervention**, is
not a Shapley value, and does not allocate interactions. It is merely the arithmetic mean of the two displayed
boundary measurements. The figure now emphasizes the two physical measurements instead of that summary.

### Are interactions between heads separated here?

No—not at the downstream behavioral level. The 21 configurations contain every singleton and every leave-one-out
arm, but not every pair or every larger subset. They therefore show that redundancy or interaction exists, but they
cannot say whether the important term is specifically head 3 with head 8, head 3 with head 7, or a higher-order
combination.

There is one useful exact distinction:

- **Inside the isolated MLP0 `I` branch**, the head terms are algebraically additive. Each term is
  `I_h = g_bar * [B(delta_token, delta_head_h) + B(delta_head_h, delta_token)]`, so
  `I = sum_h I_h + I_epsilon`. There is no `head3 * head8` product and no `head3 * head3` term inside this branch.
- **After that branch is inserted into the rest of the model**, later bilinear layers, normalization, the output
  probability calculation, and cross-entropy are nonlinear. Those downstream operations can make the behavioral
  benefit of two heads non-additive. The large difference between head 3's 79.3% singleton result and 32.9% removal
  result is a boundary-level symptom of that redundancy/nonlinearity, not a decomposition of it.
- Head-by-head quadratic terms such as `B(delta_head_3, delta_head_8)` belong to the separate context-main branch
  `C`, not to token-by-context branch `I`; rung402 deliberately kept `C` fixed.

To isolate a particular downstream pair, define `V(S) = CE(no heads) - CE(S)` for a subset of heads. The pair term
from an empty background would be

`pair_effect(h,k) = V({h,k}) - V({h}) - V({k})`.

A negative value would mean diminishing returns or redundancy for that pair from the empty boundary; a positive
value would mean synergy. This would still be only one background, which is why a full Shapley allocation averages
each head's marginal effect over every possible background subset.

A background-independent allocation would require all `2^9 = 512` head subsets, followed by a complete Möbius or
Shapley calculation. The present 21-arm result did not do that, so it should not be read as having assigned pairwise
or higher-order interactions to individual heads.

## Five follow-up points: branch importance, decompositions, head gauge, and document count

### 1. How important is each `T/C/I/S` branch for cross-entropy?

They are not all around one nat. The complete 16-arm factorial lets us report three different questions. The fairest
single allocation is the Shapley value: for each branch, average the change in cross-entropy when that branch is added
over every possible background made from the other three branches. On the held-out SELECT documents, the complete
MLP0 grammar supplies `3.5211 nat` relative to retaining only the fixed constant and numerical residual.

```text
Shapley share of the complete 3.5211-nat branch benefit
(bar length is approximately 2.5 percentage points)

I  █████████████████  43.7%   1.5375 nat
T  █████████████████  42.6%   1.4983 nat
C  █████               11.9%   0.4177 nat
S  █                    1.9%   0.0675 nat
```

The fitting and held-out values, plus the literal leave-one-out cost, are:

| branch | computational meaning | Shapley FIT | Shapley SELECT | SELECT share | remove it while the other three remain |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `I` | centered token×context bilinear term | 1.5008 | **1.5375** | **43.7%** | **0.1255 nat** |
| `T` | centered token main effect | 1.4703 | **1.4983** | **42.6%** | **0.0817 nat** |
| `C` | centered context main effect, including context×context terms | 0.3506 | 0.4177 | 11.9% | 0.0543 nat |
| `S` | change caused by the example-specific normalization gain | 0.0543 | 0.0675 | 1.9% | 0.0008 nat |

The Shapley column and removal column answer different questions. `I` alone recovers 3.3759 nat and `T` alone
recovers 3.3438 nat from the empty boundary—about 96% and 95% of the complete benefit—yet removing either from the
full set costs only 0.1255 or 0.0817 nat. They overlap strongly. The exact branch-level Möbius calculation confirms
this: the empty-background `T×I` term is **-3.2227 nat**, where the negative sign means strong diminishing returns or
redundancy. Other pair terms are also negative: `T×C=-1.0888`, `C×I=-1.1207`, `T×S=-0.4744`, `I×S=-0.4235`, and
`C×S=-0.3539` nat. Higher-order terms partly restore those losses.

So the practical priority is not “all four equally.” `T` and `I` are co-primary, `C` is meaningful but secondary,
and `S` is the first branch to deprioritize. We should not delete `S` merely from this attribution: the rank-448
audit could still reveal that a small native role is where the approximation makes a disproportionate error.

### 2. Could different branches use different decompositions?

Yes. The four branches have different mathematical structure and therefore suggest different compressed objects:

- `T` is a token-conditioned quadratic function. Exhaustive token inputs can support an exact-token bypass plus a
  smaller residual code, or a decomposition of the folded token quadratic tensor.
- `I` is linear in context for a fixed token and linear in token for a fixed context. It naturally supports a
  bilinear or tensor decomposition weighted by the joint token/context distribution.
- `C` is quadratic in the context write and contains both within-head and cross-head context products. A symmetric
  quadratic-form or low-rank context-subspace decomposition is more natural than a token code.
- `S` is a scalar gain difference multiplying the complete quadratic numerator. A small scalar-conditioned correction
  may suffice; giving it a large independent tensor program would be hard to justify from its current CE share.

The branch-resolved rank-448 audit tests which of these specialized objects is actually needed. It does not assume
in advance that four separate programs are best.

### 3. Could a more global decomposition still be better?

Also yes. The large negative branch-level interaction terms show that the branches are redundant in downstream
prediction. Four independent compressors could duplicate directions that a joint program shares, and their errors
could compose nonlinearly. The existing rank-448 program is already a global shared-input decomposition: one
448-coordinate code feeds both sides of the complete MLP0 product.

The right use of the branch audit is therefore diagnostic. If one branch explains most rank-448 damage, add a
branch-aware constraint or bypass to a global program. If errors are spread evenly but cancel jointly, retain a
global objective and include the complete downstream loss or a joint causal metric. Branches generate hypotheses
and falsifiers; they do not force the final storage layout.

### 4. Are attention heads gauge-dependent, and should we decompose shared subspaces instead?

There is a provable gauge freedom inside each head. If `y_h` is a head's 128-coordinate value output and `O_h` is
its output-projection block, then for any invertible 128-by-128 matrix `G_h`,

`y'_h = G_h y_h` and `O'_h = O_h G_h^(-1)`

give exactly the same residual-stream write: `O'_h y'_h = O_h y_h`. Query/key coordinates have the analogous
change-of-basis gauge `q'=Gq`, `k'=G^(-T)k`, which preserves `q^T k`. Head labels can also be permuted. Thus the
coordinates inside a head are not uniquely interpretable.

There is an important limit: arbitrary mixing between heads is not generally a gauge because different heads use
different attention weights. Permutation is always legal; a general cross-head rotation is legal only in special
degenerate cases. Moreover, the output-projected write `a_h=O_h y_h` used in rung402 is invariant to the within-head
value/output gauge above. The head-level result is therefore more stable than an interpretation of raw value
coordinates, even though “head 3” as a label is permutable.

A more basis-independent next object is a residual-stream subspace jointly written by multiple heads. One concrete
construction is:

1. collect centered output-projected head writes `delta_a_h` on fitting documents;
2. use the exact token-dependent linear operator
   `L_e(u)=g_bar*[B(delta_e,u)+B(u,delta_e)]`, so `I_h=L_e(delta_a_h)`;
3. form an averaged Gram operator such as `E[L_e^T W L_e]`, where `W` is either output energy or a downstream
   cross-entropy/Fisher metric;
4. diagonalize that operator or fit held-out reduced-rank regression, then measure how strongly every head writes to
   each residual-stream direction;
5. physically remove or retain those directions and compare against ordinary PCA, shuffled subspaces, and the
   original head partition on disjoint documents.

This can discover one subspace jointly supplied by heads 3, 8, and 7 without treating the head labels as the desired
basis. It should advance only if the rank-448 audit says interaction preservation is the limiting problem; otherwise
we would be decomposing the wrong branch.

### 5. Should these experiments use more documents?

The present exact grammar and rank-448 audit use **96 FIT and 96 disjoint SELECT documents**, scoring 192 next-token
positions per document. That is 18,432 scored positions per role, or 36,864 total, before repeating them across all
factorial arms. The current rank-448 receipt keeps this population frozen; changing it after registration would
change the test.

The user's throughput intuition is mostly right at the project level: recent heavy GPU rungs have a median runtime
around two minutes, while writing, auditing, and choosing the next experiment has often created longer gaps. During
a factorial run, however, evaluation itself is compute-bound and cost scales approximately linearly with both the
number of documents and number of arms. A 32-arm audit on twice as many documents takes roughly twice as much
evaluation time.

For consequential confirmations, the better policy is:

- use the frozen 96+96 split for discovery and exact instrument validation;
- report document-level variability or bootstrap confidence intervals;
- if the winning branch is close, unstable, or changes the next program, confirm on a larger untouched population
  while keeping the original reference moments and compact map fixed;
- enlarge evaluation documents rather than fitting documents first, so more data tests transport instead of
  silently changing the decomposition.

Given the low GPU-to-thinking-time ratio, a larger confirmation is worth doing when it can reverse a branch choice.
It is not necessary to multiply every exploratory factorial by the same factor regardless of its margin.

The preregistered sparse-carrier prediction used the endpoint-average ranking and required its top two values to
account for at least 65% of the positive endpoint-average total on both document splits. They account for **62.2%
on FIT and 60.5% on SELECT**, so that prediction failed. This 60.5% is a percentage of the sum of positive ranking
scores, whereas the percentages in the figure are fractions of the complete 0.1255-nat interaction effect; their
denominators are different. We do not lower the threshold or call the result two-head sparse. The other predictions held:
the computation and controls were exact, head 3 was the predicted winner, its ranking matched the old head map, and
an individually material head existed at both endpoints.

## Interpretation

The old whole-head assay said attention0 looked extremely concentrated: its top two heads supplied 88.3% of summed
positive one-head removal cost. That test changed every downstream use of a head's attention write at once.

The new assay changes only the head's contribution to MLP0's centered token×context interaction. At this cleaner
port, the top-two share falls to about 61%. The old test therefore overstated how concentrated this particular MLP0
mechanism is. The best current description is:

`token×context interaction = dominant local head 3 + stable distributed support from heads 8, 7, 6, 0, and 4 + smaller tail`.

This is more informative than saying “continuous context.” It tells us that the largest contextual service entering
MLP0 is closely tied to one known local-window head, while several other heads provide weaker and partly redundant
context routes. It is still not a complete semantic description of what head 3 selects from the prefix.

## Why source-position decomposition does not come next

Our frozen decision rule allowed source-position expansion only if the head attribution was sparse enough. The 65%
bar failed, so enumerating positions inside head 3 would explain only the dominant branch while prematurely ignoring
the distributed tail. That would produce a tidy but incomplete story.

The next experiment instead connects this mechanism to an already validated smaller MLP0 program.

## Completed computation: audit the rank-448 MLP0 projection by branch

The existing rank-448 context projection replaces the two 1,152-dimensional inputs to MLP0's `Left` and `Right`
maps by one shared 448-dimensional code:

`c = Encoder z`,

`Left z approximately equals Left_small c`,

`Right z approximately equals Right_small c`.

It then keeps the native 4,608 coordinatewise products and native `Down` map. Its literal MLP0 program stores
9,954,432 values instead of 15,926,400, saving 5,971,968 values at this layer. “Rank 448” therefore means 448 shared
input coordinates, not 448 hidden product units and not 448 attention states.

We now have exact native `T/C/I/S` branches, so we asked a much sharper compression question: when the rank-448
program makes an error, is that error concentrated in the token main effect, context main effect, token×context
interaction, or normalization modulation? The physical audit evaluated all 32 subsets of five error factors:
`T`, `C`, `I`, `S`, and an auxiliary factor `A` containing the changed constant, explicit normalization residual,
and final BF16 rounding closure. `EMPTY` was the native MLP0 and `FULL` was the unchanged rank-448 program.

On held-out SELECT documents, the rank-448 program added only **0.00564 nat** of cross-entropy above native, but its
Shapley allocation was not even across branches:

```text
rank-448 SELECT loss allocation; positive means this factor adds loss

I  +0.00581 nat   token×context interaction
T  +0.00190 nat   token main effect
C  +0.00006 nat   context main effect
S  -0.00201 nat   normalization-gain modulation
A  -0.00012 nat   constant/residual/rounding closure
   ------------
    0.00564 nat   complete compact-program loss
```

The entries can be negative because a Shapley value averages a factor's marginal loss over many backgrounds; here
`S` and `A` partly compensate for errors from the positive factors. The fitting documents gave the same named order
`I > T > C > S`, with Spearman rank correlation `1.0`. The arithmetic identities were at about `3e-13`, native and
compact BF16 endpoint states were bit-exact, the parent native cross-entropies were reproduced exactly, every
intervention arm was live, and the fixed program retained `90.1111%` of its fitting energy. This is therefore not a
rounding artifact or a failed reconstruction.

The registered prediction that `T` would be the largest positive error failed. Instead, `I` supplied **74.8% of the
sum of positive named Shapley loss on SELECT**. That joins the mechanism and compression results: `I` is both the
largest native causal branch and the part the covariance-trained rank-448 code preserves least well. The next
compressor hypothesis is consequently a **global interaction-weighted projection at the same rank and literal
price**. “Global” matters: the result motivates weighting the shared code toward interaction-relevant residual-stream
directions; it does not imply storing four separate branch programs or choosing coordinates by head labels.

Because this conclusion would change the next program, we ran a larger frozen confirmation on 384 distinct held-out
source documents, split into four fixed 96-document waves. It scored 73,728 next-token positions while holding the
rank-448 map, branch definitions, 32 arms, and fitting reference fixed.

### Larger-document result: interaction leads when pooled, but not stably enough to optimize alone

The exact instrument and stability controls passed. Pooled rank-448 loss was `0.00711 nat`; every wave was positive
and ranged from `0.00573` to `0.00861 nat`. Auxiliary rounding/closure never dominated, the interaction Shapley range
was only `0.00236 nat`, and the pooled named order remained `I > T > C > S`, with rank correlation essentially `1.0`
against the earlier SELECT result.

However, the registered interaction-dominance prediction failed:

| population | `I` loss | `T` loss | larger branch |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| wave 0, 96 documents | 0.00591 | 0.00234 | `I` |
| wave 1, 96 documents | 0.00357 | 0.00366 | `T` |
| wave 2, 96 documents | 0.00593 | 0.00354 | `I` |
| wave 3, 96 documents | 0.00375 | 0.00383 | `T` |
| pooled 384 documents | **0.00479** | **0.00334** | `I`, but only by 0.00145 |

The preregistration required `I` to lead in every wave, beat `T` by at least `0.0015 nat` pooled, and supply at least
60% of pooled positive named loss. It instead led in two of four waves, beat `T` by `0.001448 nat`, and supplied
**54.4%**. Two waves with `I <= T` triggered the registered strong null. The margin miss of about `0.000052 nat` is
small, but the two wave reversals make the substantive conclusion clear without changing any threshold.

This does **not** retract the native branch result: in the exact native grammar, `I` and `T` remain co-primary and
both are far larger than `C` and `S`. It corrects the narrower compression claim. The fixed rank-448 program has a
joint `T/I` preservation problem whose balance varies across documents; the earlier 96-document result made it look
more purely interaction-led than it is. Therefore an interaction-only metric is not licensed. The safer next screen
is a global equal-rank objective that jointly preserves token-main and token×context outputs, with three explicit
comparators: the existing covariance projection, an interaction-only weighting, and a token-only weighting. A method
that improves only the pooled mean while sacrificing two document waves should fail.

### Global branch-sensitive subspaces: better branch reconstruction did not improve prediction

We then tested that global screen at exactly the same rank and price. Instead of treating heads as coordinates, it
formed 1,152-by-1,152 sensitivity matrices from the exact derivatives of `T` and `I` with respect to the MLP input.
The candidate bases were the top 448 directions for token sensitivity, interaction sensitivity, and their
equal-weight sum. All retained one shared code and the same legal computation; a random 448-dimensional subspace was
the negative control.

After two preserved scoring-path failures, the final run reproduced every baseline wave **exactly**. The valid result
was:

| rank-448 basis | pooled CE damage | exact T relative MSE | exact I relative MSE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| existing covariance | **0.007107** | 0.09459 | 0.11545 |
| token-derivative | 0.007280 | **0.08805** | 0.11582 |
| interaction-derivative | **0.007106** | 0.08967 | **0.11266** |
| joint T+I derivative | 0.007357 | 0.08813 | 0.11288 |
| random | 0.195632 | 1.07135 | 0.91954 |

“Relative MSE” here is squared branch-output error divided by squared native branch-output size; lower is better.
The specialists did what their construction intended: token error fell by 6.9% and interaction error by 2.4%.
The joint geometric-mean branch error fell by 4.56%, just short of the registered 5% bar. But those cleaner local
approximations did not lower cross-entropy. Joint T+I was worse overall and improved only waves 2 and 3; wave changes
relative to covariance were `-0.000401`, `-0.001286`, `+0.000524`, and `+0.000162 nat`, where positive means the
joint basis was better. Predictions B/C failed, D held, and the strong null fired.

The justified conclusion is narrower than “no possible rank-448 input subspace can work.” Five tested bases cannot
prove a universal statement over the whole Grassmann manifold. What they do show is that the exact first-order
T/I sensitivity objectives tested here improve their intended Euclidean branch errors without improving the actual
next-token objective. Smarter weighting of the same local branch MSE is therefore not currently licensed.

There is also a useful mathematical equivalence. Let `W=[L;R]`, let `C` be input covariance, and let `h` be the
gradient of a probed branch output with respect to the stacked `L/R` projections. Then the input gradient is
`g=W^T h`, so

`C^(1/2) E[g g^T] C^(1/2) = (S^(1/2) W C^(1/2))^T (S^(1/2) W C^(1/2))`,

where `S=E[h h^T]`. Thus rung 405 is already a **two-sided weighted low-rank approximation** for the particular
token/interaction sensitivity weight `S`; taking its top eigenvectors is the weighted Eckart--Young solution. Merely
renaming another token/interaction Gram as an “output-side WLRA” would not be a genuinely different experiment.
A genuinely different weight would need new information—for example the downstream cross-entropy gradient rather
than an isotropic output probe—and must include a shuffled-eigenbasis control. Direct nonlinear fitting or a small,
observable document/token state are the other distinct routes left by this null.
