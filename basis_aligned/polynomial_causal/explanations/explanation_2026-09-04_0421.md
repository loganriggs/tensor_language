# Circuit research update — 2026-09-04 04:21 UTC

## The goal and the current standard

The goal is to replace the 546-million-parameter bilin18 transformer with a smaller explicit tensor program whose
parts correspond to computations. A convincing circuit must eventually state what information is read, what operation
is performed, what is written, and which later computations use it. It must also predict held-out and shifted examples,
work when combined with other extracted parts, and support targeted removal or interchange without damaging unrelated
behavior.

This update is unusually correction-heavy. That is useful: several fast experiments produced attractive numbers, but
their generated examples and train/test separation were not valid enough to support circuit claims. The raw files are
preserved while the conclusions are explicitly retracted. The current work is making the reusable system strict enough
that later throughput does not trade away evidential quality.

## 1. Why 768 eigenvectors were retained

There was no natural cutoff at 768.

For normalized inputs $x_l$ to the late MLPs, we averaged their centered covariance matrices,

$$
C=\frac{1}{|L|}\sum_{l\in L}\mathbb E[(x_l-\mu_l)(x_l-\mu_l)^\top],
$$

sorted the eigenvectors of $C$ from greatest to least variance, and tested preset widths

$$
k\in\{128,256,384,512,768,896,1024,1088\}.
$$

The held-out error changed smoothly rather than showing an eigenvalue gap or causal elbow. In one seven-MLP
intervention, retaining 256, 512, and 768 directions added approximately 0.397, 0.192, and 0.084 nat of cross-entropy.
We continued with 768 because it keeps two thirds of the 1152-dimensional residual stream, leaves a substantial
384-dimensional complement, and gives a measurable but still usable intervention. It was an experimental resolution,
not evidence for exactly 768 semantic variables. The late-width line is now closed because it described variance and
compression but did not identify, split, or group behavioral circuits.

## 2. The last valid new circuit-level result

For numbered-list continuation, attention block 8 writes a particular contribution $T$ at the final position. We
carried that exact contribution separately and stopped selected later components from reading it. On held-out SELECT
examples:

- removing every path from $T$ damaged the correct-answer margin by 2.121 logits;
- removing its reads by later attention and MLP components caused 1.914 logits of damage, or 90.3% of the total;
- removing only its direct path to the final readout caused 0.218 logits of damage, or 10.3%; and
- preventing MLP8 and MLP10 jointly from reading it caused 0.701 logits of damage, only 36.6% of all downstream-read
  damage.

“Margin” here means the correct answer logit minus the competing answer logit. A positive damage means the intervention
reduced that margin.

This shows that later components compute most of the numbered-list effect from $T$; $T$ is not already the complete
answer sent directly to the logits. MLP8 is the largest single reader, but the effect is not concentrated in MLP8 and
MLP10. Their joint intervention also differed from the sum of their individual effects by 0.120 logit, so interactions
cannot be inferred by adding single-component ablations.

The same intervention helped a repeated-label copying control. Therefore this remains a refined but nonselective
circuit: it identifies a real write-to-reader path, but not a part that can yet be removed without collateral effects.

## 3. Why the 16-behavior battery result was retracted

A shared circuit battery is still the right scaling strategy. The invalid version ran quickly—54 GPU-seconds for 16
behaviors—but speed did not make its evidence valid.

The generator used by that run had seven independent problems:

1. It created only FIT, SELECT, and TEST data, with no out-of-distribution phase.
2. Its random seeds used Python's process-randomized `hash()` function, so another process could generate different
   supposedly frozen examples.
3. Its A1, A2, answer-preserving, and control examples were generated independently. They did not share a group ID tying
   together four transformations of the same underlying situation.
4. It tokenized prompts and answers separately. For byte-pair tokenizers, the tokenization of a continuation can change
   when it is joined to the prompt, especially after whitespace.
5. It opened FIT, SELECT, and TEST in one run rather than requiring a passing receipt from each earlier phase.
6. Its result did not contain a task-authority hash, protocol hash, physical call schedule, or exact per-phase receipt.
7. The preregistration was amended at 04:05 after the registered managed run had already started at 04:03:46, and after
   earlier smoke and capability scans had exposed outcomes and changed the behavior bank.

Several individual transformations were also wrong: weekday and month values were reused across supposedly disjoint
splits, some A2 transformations merely appended one term and changed length and position together, and several controls
did not have distinct answers and foils.

Consequently, statements such as “attention 8 writes for 10 of 16 behaviors” and “the same MLP8–11 readers implement six
successor surfaces” are hypotheses from a diagnostic screen. They do not update any circuit record or adoption claim.

## 4. Two later runs inherited that invalid selection

Two short experiments then used the five behaviors and reader choices selected by the invalid battery.

One contains a useful exact mathematical identity. If an MLP is bilinear and receives RMS-normalized input, then along
an intervention $x-tW$ its output, after subtracting bias, is

$$
M(\operatorname{RMSNorm}(x-tW))-b
=d\,\frac{Q(x)-tB(x,W)+t^2Q(W)}
{\lVert x\rVert^2-2t\langle x,W\rangle+t^2\lVert W\rVert^2},
$$

where $Q$ is the MLP's quadratic map, $B$ is its polarized cross term, and $d=1152$ is the residual width. This theorem
follows directly from the weights and normalization; it does not depend on those five tasks. The numerical run matched
the formula to about $8.3\times10^{-7}$ on its sampled rows, which is a useful implementation check.

However, its reported cross-term fractions, normalization contribution, linearization error, and similarity across
five behaviors inherit the invalid task and reader selection. They are not held-out circuit evidence. Also, describing
the response as “six numbers” was misleading: $Q(x)$, $B(x,W)$, and $Q(W)$ are 1152-dimensional vectors and depend on
the row's $x$ and $W$. The identity is compact as a function of the scalar $t$, but it is not yet a compact reusable
reader implementation across inputs.

The other run applied a Möbius transform to all subsets of four selected readers. For a measured damage function $v(S)$
over reader subsets $S$, the pair interaction is

$$
m(\{i,j\})=v(\{i,j\})-v(\{i\})-v(\{j\})+v(\varnothing).
$$

That is the correct way to measure whether two removals interact. But the chosen behaviors and top-four readers came
from the invalid battery, so the reported “two-of-four” and cross-behavior redundancy claims are retracted. A future
version must fix one common reader set before looking at the interaction results and must use valid phased task data.

## 5. The first strict reusable task adapter

The first repaired adapter targets positional list retrieval:

```text
Items: red, blue, green, black. Item 2:  ->  blue
```

Its abstract computation is $f(v,q)=v_q$: return the value at queried index $q$. Each generated situation produces four
linked comparisons:

- **A1:** change only the queried index; the answer changes.
- **A2:** keep the query fixed and swap the selected value with one distractor; the answer changes.
- **P:** replace one unqueried value with a novel value; the answer stays fixed.
- **C:** place the same target at two positions and change the query between them; the internal index changes but the
  answer stays fixed, with another distinct value retained as a foil.

FIT, SELECT, and TEST contain four-item lists. The out-of-distribution phase contains six-item lists. All four phases use
different payload vocabularies. The default authority contains

$$
4\text{ phases}\times24\text{ situations}\times4\text{ transformations}=384\text{ rows}.
$$

“Authority” means the complete frozen set of generated examples that later model calls are allowed to use. Its SHA-256
digest is `16307b8bb9273d56f7c3d09cd629fca78fa1db7f110278e959b6ee301cfb7571`.

For every prompt $p$ and answer $a$, the adapter checks the tokenizer jointly: the tokens for $p$ must be an exact prefix
of the tokens for $p+a$, followed by exactly one answer token. Base and donor prompts must have the same token length so
an internal state at position $j$ refers to the same semantic location in both. Random seeds are derived from SHA-256,
and the validator recomputes every row identity and the precise A1/A2/P/C edit semantics. The adapter plus shared
integration tests currently pass 14 CPU tests.

This licenses only a future capability-only FIT run. It does not yet show that the model performs the behavior, and it
does not authorize circuit localization or opening SELECT, TEST, or OOD.

## 6. R593: a valid instrument failure, not a selector/content result

R593 asks whether an induction head can be decomposed below the native head boundary into an equality-based selector
and copied content whose controlled interchange predicts the answer. It kept the original scientific examples,
hypotheses, and $10^{-5}$ reconstruction threshold while repairing two invalid checks inherited from R592.

Its first managed launch failed before Python started because one command-line argument exceeded Linux's per-argument
limit. The repaired launcher put the exact source bytes in an anonymous, immutable memory file and passed only a
672-byte argument list. Independent tests verified the source hash, complete seal mask, corruption failures, descriptor
closure, and all unchanged scientific inputs. After a final storage check, this exact version was dispatched once
through the managed runner.

The immutable launcher worked. All 54 endpoint batches in FIT completed. The first directed intervention then failed
the registered instrument check. The published diagnostic reported

$$
\max\lvert\Delta_{\mathrm{saved}}-\Delta_{\mathrm{planned}}\rvert
=2.288818359375\times10^{-5}>10^{-5}.
$$

The program therefore published an invalid diagnostic and stopped. SELECT, final test, and out-of-distribution data
were never opened; no scientific predicate was scored; there were no backward passes or weight updates.

The completed independent audit found that this published number was not the physical planned-versus-realized error.
The saved array contained the planned change plus a synthetic $2\times10^{-5}$ failure marker. Float32 rounding turned
that marker into the reported $2.2888\times10^{-5}$ maximum. There were 55 markers: 32 at layer 5, 7 at layer 7, and
16 at the joint layer-8 transaction.

The audit reconstructed the actual float32 additions from the receipt-bound native states and planned changes. All 32
layer-5 rows really did fail the frozen absolute-$10^{-5}$ predicate, with physical errors as large as
$6.103515625\times10^{-5}$. At a representative coordinate,

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{\mathrm{before}}&=1447.919189453125,\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{planned}}&=75.30926513671875,\\
x_{\mathrm{after}}&=1523.228515625,\\
(x_{\mathrm{after}}-x_{\mathrm{before}})-\Delta_{\mathrm{planned}}
&=6.103515625\times10^{-5}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is half of one float32 spacing unit at the modified value. Thus the frozen check was applied as written and the
invalid terminal stands, but the absolute threshold asks float32 arithmetic to reproduce an abstract change more
accurately than the local numerical spacing permits. The saved array was also mislabeled: it records marked planned
changes, not the actual realized changes.

This does not license simply increasing the tolerance. A valid prospective instrument would save the before, planned,
and after arrays; compare the after-state bits against an independently rounded float32 target; and report the realized
transaction-level change together with its local float32 spacing. Because two same-layer sites are applied in one
transaction, it must not invent a per-site attribution for the rounding residue. The post-execution audit is complete,
and no rerun is authorized. The exact receipt and reconstruction checks are in
[the R593 post-execution audit](../INDUCTION_CENTERED_FIXED_GEOMETRY_RUNG593_POSTEXECUTION_AUDIT.md).

## 7. Current direction

The highest-information route is now:

1. Compile a capability-only managed invocation for the strict positional-list adapter. FIT must pass before writer
   localization exists; SELECT, TEST, and OOD remain separate unopened phases.
2. If capability holds, identify candidate writers and downstream readers on FIT, freeze them, and measure interchange,
   necessity, sufficiency, and joint reader interactions on later phases.
3. Add the next adapters by reusing this validator: repaired subject–verb agreement, one grouped ordered-successor
   family, quote closing under an exact grammar, and sentence-boundary case routing. Ambiguous HTML/fence/list tasks stay
   blocked until a parser supplies a unique authored continuation.
4. Compare tasks using held-out intervention profiles. Merge components across native attention heads or MLPs only when
   downstream computation treats them as the same variable; split a native component when its parts have different
   behavioral and interaction profiles.

This preserves the intended 20/80 gain: one strict generator/compiler/intervention system reused across many behaviors,
while refusing to count fast but leaky screens as high-quality circuits.
