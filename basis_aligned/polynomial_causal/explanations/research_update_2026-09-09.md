## The high-level rundown

**We now have several concrete pieces of a circuit explanation and reliable
ways to execute and edit them. We do not yet have the smaller, reusable
model decomposition that meets your full success criterion.**

The day unfolded in five main steps:

1. **We checked that our reconstruction and intervention tools reproduce the
   actual computation.** They do. The initial attempt to discover sharing by
   looking for repeated factors or a smaller linear state found no usable
   reduction in the trained example. We kept the tools and changed the search.
2. **We distinguished genuine multi-step lookup from copying earlier answers.**
   One small model's apparent longer-step ability mostly came from answer
   history. A second existing checkpoint could compose facts before seeing
   any previous answers, so it became the useful target for studying composition.
3. **We found a concrete endpoint message used in composed lookup.** An earlier
   fact supplies an endpoint to a later matching fact. Swapping this message
   selectively changes the intended answer, including simultaneous swaps on
   registered held-out and shifted cases. This is today's strongest positive
   circuit-interface result. Its routing and background still use native weights.
4. **We tested whether these pieces combine into a clean, broadly reusable
   lookup algorithm.** Several plausible simplifications failed. Record order,
   entity identity, contextual routing, and interactions between writes matter
   to the model's full outputs. Exact algebra helped rule out specific simple
   organizations and explain why some apparently clean task results were misleading.
5. **We returned to full bilin18 and investigated an actual shared computation.**
   A token-value producer feeds several attention heads. Its outputs alone
   cannot replace their contextual values. The latest tests locate a two-command
   interaction inside the native reads, including a substantial joint-routing
   term that a simpler crossed-command explanation misses.

The practical gain is a more precise account of **what is computed, where it
is reused, and which proposed simplifications fail**. The unresolved part is
turning that account into a shorter independently specified program whose
new-input behavior and interventions remain faithful.

## First, the models and the measurements

Most afternoon experiments used a small associative-lookup model. A document
contains facts such as `a→b`, `b→c`, and `c→d`; a query asks for the result of
following one, two, or three links. If the facts define a function `F`, a
three-hop query asks for `F(F(F(a)))`. A *hop* is one function application.

We compared two existing 400,640-parameter checkpoints: the pilot model with
attention/MLP/attention, and a stronger model with four attention layers.
An **MLP**, or multilayer perceptron, is a learned transformation applied at
one token position. These models have a 29-token vocabulary and 128-dimensional
internal states. They are controlled research targets, not miniature evidence
that a claim has already been verified in full bilin18.

The late-evening experiments use **actual bilin18**: 18 layers, 545,902,902
parameters, 1,152-dimensional states, nine attention heads per layer, and
50,304 output tokens. A head is one parallel attention computation. `L9H1`
means layer 9, head 1, counting from zero.

Several measurements recur below:

- A **logit** is the model's numerical score for one possible next token.
  Applying softmax converts all logits into a probability distribution.
  Full-output fidelity compares every token score or probability, not just
  whether the most likely answer is correct.
- **KL divergence** measures how much a candidate distribution differs from
  the native model: `KL(p||q)=sum_j p_j log(p_j/q_j)`. Zero means agreement;
  larger values mean more distributional damage. We report natural-log units,
  called nats. Several screens require mean KL ≤ .001.
- **RMS**, root mean square, measures vector size: square the entries, average,
  then take the square root. Relative RMS error divides the error's RMS by the
  reference effect's RMS. A 1% limit is a full-vector prediction requirement.
- **IID** means new examples generated by the same distribution. **OOD**, out
  of distribution, means a specified shift—for example shorter function cycles
  or changed record layouts. Passing one such shift does not establish all OOD
  generalization. A cycle returns to its starting entity after several links.
- An **intervention** changes a specified internal contribution and recomputes
  the affected computation. A swap asks whether replacing a proposed variable
  transfers its predicted meaning. A numerical replay check asks whether our
  implementation matches the same edit in the original model.

## 1. The reconstruction pilot gave us a trustworthy executor

The [pilot](bilinear_reconstruction_pilot_report.md) implemented direct and
recurrent forms of product attention. Direct execution revisits earlier
sources; recurrent execution accumulates their key–key–value products and
contracts the current query against that accumulated state. Associativity
makes these two ways to evaluate the same attention expression.

We explicitly retained normalization, position-dependent rotations, residual
connections, shared values where present, and output transformations. We also
checked removals, swaps, and joint edits. The trained small-model FP64
comparison agreed within about `3.7e-13` in full logits on the registered
populations and edit arms. FP64 means 64-bit floating-point arithmetic; the
small residual error is numerical, not an exact-real proof of the full network.

The discovery attempt asked whether local update functions shared a smaller
linear span, or whether trained multiplicative factors were exact duplicates.
The registered 128-function bank needed all 128 numerical directions; the
inspected MLP factors had no exact duplicates. Those particular reduction
proposals therefore failed. This does not exclude compact nonlinear structure.

We also built planted examples where sharing was known in advance. They exposed
an important distinction: **two consumers can share a calculation while needing
separate editable histories**. If a source contribution is removed for only
one consumer, a single shared state may no longer reproduce both consumers.
That distinction now informs the native intervention tests.

The executor is useful infrastructure. Merely rewriting the original attention
recurrence did not discover a new semantic circuit or reduce its opaque weights.

## 2. We found why the first model seemed to handle longer chains

In the [answer-history study](contextual_answer_history_circuit.md), the first
checkpoint answered repeated higher-hop queries almost perfectly but answered
new ones poorly. Earlier query answers were already present in the input.

On fresh random-cycle documents, repeated hop-2/3 queries were 100% correct;
novel queries were only 6.28% correct. Preventing the final attention layer from
reading the earlier matching answers reduced repeated-query accuracy to 6.50%.
Removing equally many wrong-entity answers had almost no effect under the
registered control. Short-cycle OOD tests supported the same source mechanism.

This explains a real behavior: retrieval of a previously supplied answer.
It did not provide a good target for discovering a new multi-step algorithm.

We therefore checked the existing four-attention checkpoint. In a small
first-query-only test it answered all registered two- and three-hop queries
correctly without earlier answers. Larger subsequent panels gave lower
three-hop accuracy—90.6% IID and 81.3% short-cycle OOD—so the initial 100%
figure should not be treated as universal capability. The
[composition dossier](cold_query_composition_reconstruction.md) retains both.

## 3. The main positive result: joining two facts into an endpoint message

For facts `u→v` and `v→w`, a **join** matches their common middle entity `v`
and combines them into a two-edge relationship `u→w`.

Serialization matters because causal attention reads earlier positions. The
experiments found that the useful combined information is written at the
later of the two relevant records. Counterbalancing all six orders of a
three-edge chain supported that source prediction. Removing the predicted
suffix-join source lowered correct-answer probability by .856–.978 across
the registered groups; removing the other chain sources had at most .019
absolute mean effect. Keeping only that source was still insufficient for
strict full-output fidelity.

Further interventions localized directional writers to layer 2, especially
H1 and H2. Breaking the shared middle label greatly reduced both their
joining scores and their causal effects; changing both matched labels
restored the relationship. This is evidence for a matching operation, with
contextual details still present.

The most explicit successful field is described in the
[endpoint dossier](forward_endpoint_field_circuit.md). When `v→w` appears
before `u→v`, L2H1 transports a contribution derived from `w` into the later
record. Its exact form is

```text
endpoint_map(w) = O V embedding(w) / 8
message(t,s)    = routing_score(t,s) × normalization_gain(s) × endpoint_map(w)
```

`embedding(w)` is the learned vector for token `w`. `V` transforms it into a
value; `O` maps that value back into the state used by later computation.
The source position is `s`, and the receiving position is `t`. The factor
`1/8` comes from this checkpoint's intervening residual mixtures. The routing
score and normalization gain come from the live contextual model.

The same endpoint map is used by multiple consumers. Exchanging endpoints
between two independent joins retargeted all 64 registered three-hop query
cases across 32 IID/OOD worlds. Single swaps had little effect on the other
query, and both swaps worked together. An independently exported executor
agreed with the corresponding native edits to roughly `1e-13` in full logits.

This is meaningful progress on extraction, manipulation, and reuse. Its limits
are equally material:

- Those successful swaps used fixed record layouts. A broader random-layout
  proposal covering both two- and three-hop consumers failed: the purported
  endpoint field barely controlled two-hop answers. Its predeclared three-hop
  results remained useful, but did not turn the combined proposal into a pass.
- Simultaneously edited disjoint source writes can compose cleanly when one
  final attention layer remains and its query is fixed. Writes into the same
  source state need not obey that additive law.
- The export retains 387,968 arbitrary constants after an ordinary final-readout
  matrix fold. That fold accounts for the 12,672-constant reduction from the
  checkpoint. The discovered endpoint formula has not removed additional
  native coefficients or explained all routing and background computation.

## 4. Why the clean graph algorithm was not enough

A tempting whole-head replacement was: detect matching middle entities, write
joined endpoints, and reuse that rule across both orientations. We built a
physically smaller version. After repairing a missing-calibration-cell problem,
the valid test failed badly: mean full-token KL was roughly 36–83 on its held
populations. It was smaller and behaviorally unusable. Even retaining exact
native scores wherever the proposed match rule allowed them, while deleting
the other sources, failed strict fidelity.

Two further findings explain why a perfect graph-walk story is insufficient
for faithful reverse engineering:

**Entity names affect the native distributions.** Renaming entities while
preserving the graph sometimes changes answers, and often changes confidence.
A perfectly label-equivariant predictor—one that merely renames its outputs
when input labels change—cannot match both native distributions arbitrarily
well. For an aligned pair `p,q`, any common prediction `r` satisfies

```text
[KL(p||r) + KL(q||r)]/2 = JS(p,q) + KL((p+q)/2 || r) ≥ JS(p,q).
```

Here **JS**, Jensen–Shannon divergence, is the average divergence of each
distribution from their mean. This gives an unavoidable error floor without
fitting a candidate. The measured query-KL floors were .01439 IID and .006391
OOD, above .001. The bound concerns this symmetry requirement and these query
panels, not every smaller possible program. See the
[entity-symmetry explanation](entity_symmetry_fidelity_bound.md).

**Record order also affects outputs.** Reordering facts preserves `F` but
changes native query distributions. Corresponding three-hop paired KL floors
were .0052–.0197. A faithful program therefore needs some positional dependence.
The first query-layer state does have an exact reusable split,

```text
query_state = document_summary(document, hop) + local_part(entity, hop).
```

Four document summaries can be reused across 24 entity queries. But asking
those summaries to update themselves under arbitrary record permutations
without rereading the document imposed a much stronger condition. The exact
stored-coefficient calculation required the full 1,058-dimensional centered
key/value incidence space for that linear interface. An incidence description
records which identity occurs in each key/value position. This closes that
particular linear summary-transport proposal; it does not rule out nonlinear
summaries. Details are in [summary transport](summary_record_transport.md).

We also traced specific wrong answers to direct or previously joined content
at the wrong binding source. The initial selected-error analysis was strong,
but a fresh complete-cohort confirmation repaired only 5/11 OOD errors,
missing its 50% bar. We retained that failure rather than redefine the
population around repairable errors.

## 5. Exact response algebra exposed interactions we cannot omit

A **normalization gain** rescales a state according to its RMS. This couples
contributions: changing one component alters the scale applied to the others.
But normalization was not the only source of interaction.

For a source state `x=z+αd`, where `d` is a join write and `α` scales it, each
of the final reader's two key scores and its value is linear in `x` before
normalization. Multiplying those three linear expressions gives

```text
read(α) = gain(α)^3 × (C0 + α C1 + α² C2 + α³ C3).
```

The `C` coefficients are output vectors computed from the native source and
reader. We compiled this exact curve and matched physical source edits at
five positive, zero, and negative scales. Keeping only the proposed leading
terms failed: forward linear-only errors were about 7%; backward
quadratic-only errors were about 53–65%, against a 1% bar. The full curve is
an exact response tool, not a simpler independently learned circuit.

When two joins write into the same binding, retaining their separate complete
curves and one shared normalization denominator still fails to predict their
joint effect. Products involving both writes are essential. A particular
origin-key × endpoint-value interaction was close to selective, but missed
its fixed IID threshold: .4806 mean target probability loss versus .5 required.
Its OOD result passed; the nomination as a whole did not.

We then asked whether a coordinate change could make a fixed query interface
three independent squared channels, split it into smaller common blocks, or
make every reader share one linear multiplicative gate. Exact coefficient
witnesses rejected those specific forms. In plain terms, changing coordinates
did not separate that coupled operation in the proposed ways. These are
local restrictions, not a theorem that circuits cannot be found.

See [the join-read explanation](join_write_response_curve.md) for the curve,
interaction tests, and precise scope of the algebraic certificates.

## 6. The latest result on full bilin18: shared values, contextual readers

Full bilin18 explicitly reuses a value computed at the first layer. A later
head receives

```text
effective_value = (1 − λ) × contextual_value + λ × first_value.
```

The first value depends only on its source token. The contextual value depends
on the state computed at that later layer. `λ` is learned and can be negative;
this is not necessarily an average of the two values.

We tested the fixed L9H1/H4, L11H3, L15H5 consumers on 32 four-cell worlds:
128 sequences containing independently varied temporal and is/was commands.
These worlds were already opened in earlier work. Their historical FIT/HOLDOUT
labels do not provide fresh OOD evidence for these new hypotheses.

Removing contextual values and keeping only shared first values failed:
mean full-token KL was .0487/.0424. Removing only shared values also exceeded
the .001 fidelity bar, at .00150/.00141. A four-arm experiment—keep both,
remove either, remove both—showed that their signed command effects generally
align rather than oppose one another. Negative `λ` did not imply an opposing
behavioral role. Their joint deletion effect also differed from the sum of
single effects by about 10–12% in full-logit RMS.

We next decomposed the full shared-removal response `E(a,b)` over two binary
command bits into four **command modes**:

```text
E(a,b) = M00 + (−1)^a M10 + (−1)^b M01 + (−1)^(a+b) M11.
Muv    = one quarter of the signed sum over the four command cells.
```

`M00` is the average response; `M10` and `M01` describe the separate command
contrasts; `M11` is their interaction. This is an exact four-cell change of
coordinates, not a model fitted to future inputs. Orthogonality means the
omitted modes give the smallest possible RMS error for a restricted response.
Any command-independent response has at least about **37% relative full-logit
error** on these worlds. Small changes in one answer-versus-foil margin had
hidden broader command dependence.

An exact token-count calculation then established that the first-value
producer has **zero mixed command mode** at all 795 aligned source positions:
each position satisfies `count00 + count11 = count01 + count10` for every token.
Any token-only map preserves that identity. The interaction must arise later.

The GPU experiment completed at **23:06 UTC** locates a material interaction
already in the shared reads. Write `S=P U`, where `P` is the signed routing
pattern and `U` is the source payload after the fixed output projection.
The exact mixed read is

```text
S11 = P11 U00 + P01 U10 + P10 U01 + P00 U11.
```

Products here sum over source positions. `U11=0`, so the final term vanishes.
`P11 U00` is a joint-command router reading average payload. The two crossed
terms combine routing for one command with payload for the other.

| Later-query measurement, fixed four heads | FIT | HOLDOUT |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mixed read RMS / complete shared read RMS | 3.25% | 3.58% |
| Relative error if only crossed terms are retained | 77.8% | 75.0% |

Thus the mixed read is material under the registered 1% rule, and joint
routing cannot be omitted under the registered crossed-only hypothesis.
Different-layer writes remain separate entries in these pooled norms; they
are not summed into an invented single intervention. The earlier temporal
query has zero mixed read, as expected because it cannot see the future command.

The computation matches native reads to maximum absolute error `2.14e-5`
and relative RMS `1.33e-7`; the four-term algebra closes to `1.42e-14`.
The managed run took 1.88 seconds, with 32 full forwards and 192 extra local
attention contractions. Fourteen capture/algebra controls passed.
[Primary result](../BILIN18_ROUTED_SHARED_COMMAND_INTERACTION_V1_RESULT.json).

This locates an operation we must explain. It does not yet prove that this
read interaction accounts for the entire final-output interaction, supplies
a reduced state interface, or generalizes to new language constructions.
All 545,902,902 native parameters remain required by the prototype.

## Where this leaves your four properties

| Requirement | Today's strongest evidence | Main unfinished part |
|---|---|---|
| OOD prediction | Specified shifted-data tests support answer-history and restricted endpoint-message mechanisms. | Broader endpoint reuse failed; the new full-model command studies use opened data. |
| Extraction | Independent token-to-output execution and exact implementation of several edited paths. | A smaller explained program with native routing/background replaced or explicitly simplified. |
| Removal and interchange | Selective endpoint swaps; source-specific history cuts; native-corresponding component interventions. | A broadly valid abstraction of the full circuit, including redundancy and overlapping writes. |
| Composition and reuse | Shared endpoint computation, simultaneous disjoint swaps, exact joint-response algebra. | Compact rules that predict coupled joint use on fresh inputs while reducing independent specification. |

Your appended criterion additionally requires reduced structural description,
including all adapters and remaining arbitrary coefficients. **That requirement
is still unmet.** An exported model, a saved response curve, or a successful
local intervention is not enough to claim the finished decomposition.

The current bottleneck is contextual routing and its interactions with payloads.
The evidence supports keeping those interactions explicit. Non-additivity
itself is compatible with compositionality: a circuit can compose correctly by
computing the joint term. The next research decision is how to explain that
term with a reusable operation whose inputs and cost can be stated, then test
its causal role and new-input prediction. The next registered experiment
removes only the joint-command part of the residual state just before layer9,
while retaining the average and separate-command parts. It asks whether one
upstream computation supplies the interaction to all four readers, or whether
later operations regenerate it. The intervention primitive is implemented;
this next native-model experiment has not yet run.
