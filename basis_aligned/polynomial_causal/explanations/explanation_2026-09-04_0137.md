# What a late MLP actually reads: the gated linear read and the fading tail (2026-09-04 01:37 UTC; revised 01:50 UTC with §2792/§2793)

Plain-language chapter (Claude lane, ledger §2777–§2790), continuing the "below the block" chapter of 00:33. Sign convention
(§2135): every loss number is CE ADDED ABOVE THE REAL MODEL on held-out documents — LOWER IS BETTER. Baseline 3.0322;
"cost" always means how much worse the model gets when the named piece of its input is taken away.

## Where we were

At 00:33 the picture was: the late blocks (8–17) can be given a shared 1024-dim frame for .037, but a 768-dim frame costs
.125 for the MLPs alone. That extra ~300 dimensions — "the tail" — is nearly isotropic (no dominant directions), is written
mostly by the late blocks themselves (.071 of the .125 is late-origin, .034 early-origin), and is read by the MLPs, not by
attention (attention's tail use is .015). The question this chapter answers: WHAT is the MLP doing with the tail, and WHERE
does the tail come from, block by block?

## Finding 1 — the read is a linear read gated by the core (§2780–§2785, §2788)

A bilinear MLP computes Down[(Left x) ∘ (Right x)]. Split the input exactly into core c (the 768 frame) and tail t. Then the
output splits exactly into three pieces: core×core, core×tail (two cross terms), tail×tail. Measured:

- Drop only tail×tail: .0087 of the .125. Drop the cross terms too: the whole .125. So 83% of what the tail buys is the CROSS
  term — the tail enters LINEARLY, multiplied by a core-dependent gate. In calculus terms the cross term is exactly the MLP's
  Jacobian at c, applied to t: J(c)·t.
- The gate needs the whole core (§2782): replace the gate by its constant mean and the cost is .130 — no better than dropping
  the tail entirely. The read is a token-conditioned linear map, not a fixed one.
- It is dense over the hidden units (§2781): no small set of hidden units carries it.
- The model is calibrated to it (§2788): scale the cross term by α and the loss is a parabola with its minimum at α = 1
  (costs .125 / .040 / .009 / .034 / .140 at α = 0 / .5 / 1 / 1.5 / 2; curvature ≈ .12 nat per unit α²). The model wants
  exactly the read it has, not more of it.
- The output of the read lands back inside the core frame (§2785, .87 of it recovered by projecting the output to the core).
- The two largest tail directions are worthless (§2786, dropping them costs .0017): they are accumulation drift (the mean
  direction of the late writes), not content. The content is spread across the ~300 small directions.

So a late MLP is: a quadratic on the 768-dim core, PLUS a core-gated linear read of a ~300-dim isotropic side channel, whose
result is written back into the core. Attention takes a small sip of the same channel, late through its query-side pattern
(§2787: .011 of the .015 is pattern, Q > K), early symmetrically through pattern and value alike (§2789).

## Finding 2 — where the tail comes from: a fading accumulation (§2777, §2790)

The residual stream at a late block is exactly the sum of one λ-weighted contribution per earlier block (the architecture
mixes the stream with a scalar λ at every block entry). §2790 removed, from what each late MLP reads, the tail written by
blocks at a given DISTANCE behind it:

| tail removed from the reader's input | cost | share of the .0711 late-origin total |
|---|---|---|
| the block's own attention write | .0023 | 3% |
| the last two blocks' writes | .0302 | 43% |
| the last five blocks' writes | .0526 | 74% |
| everything more than five blocks back | .0069 | 10% |

And the cost share tracks the ENERGY share (43% cost vs 49% energy for the last two blocks; within .06 everywhere): the read
is not selective by recency, it simply reads what is there. The tail channel is therefore neither a short wire (my "banded"
null was not met) nor a flat bus (my "spread" prediction that the far field carries ≥ 25% was FALSE — it carries 10%, the
null "≤ 10%" met by .003): each late MLP effectively reads the last three or four blocks' writes.

**Correction (§2792, resolved by §2793).** The first version of this chapter read the per-distance profile as "each write
fades by ×0.8 per block, half-life ≈ 3 blocks, because of λ". That was wrong on the mechanism and too steep on the number. The
checkpoint's λ is ≈ 1 in the late blocks (0.88–1.41), so writes are not architecturally faded at all. §2793 held the WRITER
fixed and measured how its tail write decays in each later reader's input: the profile is the sum of two things. (i) Later
late blocks simply write more tail — block 16's writes carry about five times the tail energy of block 8's (rank correlation
with depth .97). (ii) With the writer fixed, there IS a real fade of ×0.87 per block (half-life ≈ 5 blocks, not 3), and it is
normalisation, not λ: the residual stream's size quadruples across blocks 8–17 (rms 433 → 1742), every reader rms-normalises
its input, so an old write occupies a shrinking share of what the reader sees. The formula λ²·(‖x_{l−1}‖/‖x_l‖)² predicts the
measured retention to within 8% in log. §2793 also found the tail is read COHERENTLY: no single late block's tail is worth more
than 8% of the .0711 total, and the ten single-block removals sum to only half the joint removal — the writes are correlated
(each late MLP re-writes ~30% of what it read back into the tail, §2791), so the channel behaves like one bus with memory
rather than ten independent wires.

One structural surprise, preserved: removing the recent and the older writes together costs 35% more than the two removals
add up to (.74 sub-additivity), while removing attention-written and MLP-written tail separately WAS additive (§2778). The
interaction lives inside a writer kind, across time — the tail×tail quadratic piece (the .0087) couples recent and older
writes of the same kind.

## What this is and is not

It is a description of the program's read, made exact where the architecture allows (the input split and the writer
decomposition are identities, not fits). It is NOT a compression: nothing here installs into the §312 frontier (norm-2304 at
+2.6735), and the strict explained fraction is unchanged (5.348% / 10.923% / 4.727 nat / 0 of 68). Five predictions were
wrong on the way (§2786 b/c/e, §2787 b/c/e, §2789 b/c, §2790 c) and stand in the ledger as written.

## What it decides next

If the read J(c)·t is really a token-gated linear map, the natural next question is whether the gate's dependence on c is
low-rank — a low-rank J(c) = J₀ + Σ a_r(c) J_r would be the first candidate in this lineage that REDUCES parameters rather
than describing them. That is an exact weight computation (the family {J(c)} has at most 768 generators), registered as
late_tail_read_operator_rank_probe and running now. My prior, from §2673/§2675's high-rank token operators and §2782's
whole-core gate, is that it is high-rank in every block — in which case the route closes exactly, and the honest smaller
program remains attention compression plus a faithful, high-rank MLP.
