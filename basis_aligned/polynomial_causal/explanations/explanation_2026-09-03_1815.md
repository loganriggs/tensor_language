# Robust-circuit update — 2026-09-03 18:15 UTC

## Goal and current strategy

The goal is to turn bilin18 into a smaller, predictive, and manipulable computational description. A useful circuit
must do more than reconstruct activations or use fewer dimensions. It should:

1. predict behavior on held-out and meaningfully shifted inputs;
2. identify a concrete computation in activations and, eventually, weights;
3. support an intervention that changes the intended behavior without changing unrelated behavior;
4. explain interactions and reuse across heads, MLPs, and tasks;
5. remain meaningful under harmless changes of internal basis.

The current campaign is deliberately spending more time on counterfactual validity. Hundreds of examples are useful
only if each group asks a clear causal question and all results are stored under one canonical circuit identity.

## What closed since the 16:52 update

### The induction dataset was almost, but not fully, supported by the model

The induction task separated two variables: which earlier source matches the final query, and which payload follows
that source. R554 tested whether the unmodified model gave the correct payload in every combination and under
meaning-preserving edits.

The model passed all four selector-by-payload combinations and the selected-match necessity test. It failed one
predeclared SELECT control: after an irrelevant source edit, only 6 of 9 groups retained the required positive margin,
and the bootstrap lower mean margin was $-0.00580$ rather than greater than zero. Because the rule required every cell,
the result is a null. The planned component search was not run.

R557 separately verified the exact intervention semantics with a planted equality-and-copy computation. It passed
3,240 discrete checks. This proves that the proposed score and payload swaps mean what we say they mean; it does not
prove that bilin18 implements them at any particular site.

### A linear pending-opener subspace was not selective

R556 fit a projector $QQ^\top$ at the 128-dimensional output of layer 13, head 8:

$$
h_{\mathrm{patched}}=h+(h_{\mathrm{donor}}-h)QQ^\top.
$$

Dimensions 2, 4, 8, and 16 reproduced every answer-changing target cell across all three seeds. But every seed at
every one of those dimensions changed at least one answer-preserving control too much. This is a useful distinction:
the head contains directions that can steer the answer, but the tested linear subspaces do not isolate the
pending-opener variable. Increasing the dimension would not address that failure.

### The exact attention factor points to payload, but one source term is still too broad

R560 stopped treating a head as the smallest unit. For the semantic pending-opener position, it used the exact
attention contribution

$$
c=s(q,k)\,W_Ov,
$$

where $s(q,k)$ is the product of the two query-key scores and $W_Ov$ is that source token's value after the head's
output projection. It transplanted the score, the projected value, or both.

The projected value alone recovered 96.5--96.9% of the full-head effect for direct delimiter changes and
126.4--126.6% for completed-then-reopened examples. The score alone recovered only 1.0--2.1%. This is strong evidence
that delimiter identity is carried mainly by the value branch, while routing is comparatively generic.

The candidate still failed selectivity. A punctuation edit produced a full-vocabulary root-mean-square logit change
of 29.8--30.1% of the full-head effect, above the frozen 25% maximum. Moving the value from the adjacent wrong source
also exceeded its 25% bound. Therefore a single semantic source contribution is not yet the complete circuit. The
next pending-opener hypothesis should test a small semantic source region or downstream-equivalent collection, not a
wider hidden subspace.

The run also exposed a metadata error in 108 completed-then-reopened rows: the donor prompt and answer were correct,
but the stored donor-variable label repeated the base label. Source positions were therefore derived from the actual
closer-to-opener mapping before any model output was opened. A first implementation then crashed after FIT because of
an extra dictionary level; it was recorded as invalid, repaired without changing the experiment, and independently
audited in R561.

## The new increment/successor authority

The old increment result used only forty examples of the form `1, 2 -> 3`. It suggested that layer-8 heads move digit
identity and MLPs 8--14 turn it into the next digit, but it could not distinguish a reusable `+1` computation from
copying a number or continuing one fixed format.

R563 now provides 160 semantic groups and 1,120 paired examples. Each group contains:

- a digit-written `+1` state change;
- a number-word `+1` state change;
- a digit-to-number-word `+1` change;
- an edit that breaks only the middle of an otherwise coherent sequence;
- a surface rewrite that leaves the numbers and answer fixed;
- a repeated-number/copy control;
- a step-two control.

FIT, SELECT, FINAL_TEST, and OOD contain 64, 32, 32, and 32 complete groups. Their content words, prompt leads, and
starting-number pools are disjoint. All seven variants of a semantic group remain in the same split. All 1,120
oriented prompt pairs are unique and every answer endpoint is one GPT-2 token.

Manual review caught and rejected an initial construction that contained labels such as `[digits]` and `[step two]`.
Those labels would have revealed the experimental condition to the model. No model output had been opened. The
canonical R563 version uses natural prompts and explicitly permits the same endpoint prompt to appear in several
derived rows of one semantic group.

R564 was the next experiment. For answer $a$ and the set $N$ of eligible digit and number-word answers, it
measures

$$
m(x,a)=z_a(x)-\max_{b\in N,\ b\ne a}z_b(x).
$$

Every target and control endpoint must choose the correct numeric candidate in at least 75% of groups with a positive
bootstrap lower mean margin. Breaking the middle number must reduce the coherent answer margin in at least 65% of
groups. FIT opens first; SELECT opens only after a complete FIT pass. FINAL_TEST and OOD remain closed. Only a passing
result can reopen the old L8H7/L8H3 and MLP8--14 localization hypothesis.

### R564 result

R564 stopped at FIT, as required. Every digit, number-word, and cross-format `+1` endpoint had 0/64 accuracy against
the full numeric candidate set. Mean margins were negative by 1.37--2.62 logits. The repeated-number control did pass:
the two surface versions selected the repeated value in 84.4% and 85.9% of groups with positive bootstrap support.
Thus the prompt family elicited numeric continuation, but not the proposed `+1` or `+2` operation. Breaking the middle
number reduced the expected-answer margin in only 1/64 groups and made it worse on average.

R565 recomputed all 896 saved endpoint margins, 12 opened cells, 64 paired necessity effects, hashes, and the
20-forward ledger without loading the model. SELECT, FINAL_TEST, and OOD correctly remained unopened. This is a
dataset-capability null, so it forbids localizing the old component hypothesis on R563. The next step is an explicitly
exploratory development screen for natural formats that the model already understands, followed by a new freeze on
unseen groups and values; the thresholds and failed families will not be edited after the fact.

### Development-only format screen

R566 then compared six natural prompt forms under `+1`, copy, and `+2`. Its 432 prompts are permanently excluded from
confirmatory splits. The result split the original idea into two plausible behaviors. Two- and three-line numbered
lists were 100% accurate on `+1` but 0% on `+2`, suggesting a list-index successor rather than general arithmetic.
Comma-separated digits were 100% on `+1` and copy and 62.5% on `+2`. Comma-separated number words were 100% on `+1`
and copy but only 20.8% on `+2`. A prose number-word format reached only 54.2% on `+1`.

R567 therefore starts from two explicit hypotheses: numbered-list index successor, and comma-separated numeric-
sequence continuation. It will freeze unseen groups for each and use copy and `+2` as specificity controls. This is a
substantive circuit split: the old L8H7/L8H3/MLP8--14 story may apply to list labels without explaining the more general
digit/word sequence behavior.

## How results are organized to prevent duplicate work

Every task-defined circuit has one versioned JSON dossier under `bilinear_quotient/circuits/`. It records the active
causal claim, all counterfactual families, split hashes, candidate sites, every held or failed experiment, and the
exact next missing step.

The generated `CIRCUIT_CAMPAIGN_QUEUE.md` is the working view. It currently distinguishes five task-defined circuit
records from 70 legacy census candidates, which are not counted as established circuits. It also lists open
experiments, blockers, evidence gaps, and the canonical record path. The experiment index rejects unexplained repeats
of the same protocol or exact execution. This means future work begins from a dossier and a missing evidence cell,
not from remembering a prose filename.
