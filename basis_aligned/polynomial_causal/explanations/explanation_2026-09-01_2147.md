# Why the nearly free sub-500M repair failed — 2026-09-01 21:47 UTC

The proposed 29,968-byte repair did **not** work, and this changes our explanation of the failure.

## What was changed

The candidate program has three compressed families:

- rank-64 query/key maps stored with two-byte FP16 numbers;
- generated MLP0 and MLP4 programs stored with two-byte BF16 numbers;
- a small MLP16 quadratic program containing 14,984 numbers.

“Quadratic” means its output depends on squared linear measurements. Computationally, for input `x`, it evaluates

`sum_r output_r * sum_k value_rk * (vector_rk dot x)^2 + constant`.

Rung414 stored those 14,984 numbers in two-byte BF16. It behaved well on average but changed one position's
cross-entropy by `0.11764` relative to the FP32-parent program, above the frozen `0.10` limit. The proposed repair
kept only those 14,984 numbers in four-byte FP32. Everything else remained exactly the same storage family.

The resulting artifact contains **495,847,230 numbers and 991,724,428 bytes**. That is only 29,968 bytes—about
`0.003%`—larger than rung414.

## The result

Most aggregate measures still look excellent:

- cross-entropy damage: `+0.05297` nat;
- retained circuit checks: 17 of 62;
- composition ratio versus the additive prediction: `1.034`;
- normalized 62-circuit effect-vector cosine: `0.99996`;
- new WikiText mean/p95/max damage: `0.0653 / 0.1120 / 0.1614` nat.

But the measurement the repair was designed to fix barely moved. The worst per-position difference became
`0.11647`, not the registered `<=0.05`. Prediction B therefore fails. The signed intervention gate was conditional
on a full pass, so it was not run and the mixed-precision artifact is not adopted.

## What we learned instead

The earlier explanation blamed rounding the small quadratic program itself. Direct comparison now shows that changing
those coefficients from BF16 to FP32 changes positions by only `0.00124` on average and at most `0.02234`. It explains
only `3.9%` of the total squared deviation from the parent. In fact, that small difference partly cancels the larger
one: the overall root-mean-square difference becomes slightly worse after restoring FP32.

So the correct explanation is more interesting. The quadratic MLP16 replacement amplifies small perturbations made
earlier by source, query/key, and MLP0/4 storage rounding. The problem is not primarily the precision of its own
coefficients; it is nonlinear composition. A component can be individually stable, and a later component can be
individually accurate, while their combination creates rare large errors.

This is also a concrete lesson for learning our own definition of simplicity. A bytes-only objective would strongly
prefer this repair: it is nearly free and all the average metrics pass. A useful learned simplicity rule must instead
predict the consequences we care about, including tail behavior and composition with other replacements. This failed
case should be an adversarial teaching example, while its circuit family remains unavailable to whatever sealed
confirmation set we eventually choose.

The adopted byte frontier therefore remains **511,758,646 numbers / 1,023,517,292 bytes**, with 43 of 62 circuit
checks. The smaller 495,847,230-number program remains adopted only with its honest FP32-heavy
**1,867,449,228-byte** storage. There will be no precision sweep or relaxed position threshold.
