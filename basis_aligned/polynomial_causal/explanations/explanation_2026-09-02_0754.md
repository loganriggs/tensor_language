# Update since 06:10: three failed grouping bases, then a new attention decomposition — 2026-09-02 07:54 UTC

## Goal

We want an executable account of bilin18 whose parts correspond to computations rather than automatically treating
an attention head, an MLP, or one weight coordinate as a circuit. A useful part should tell us what information it
reads, what operation it performs, what it writes, and which later computations use the write. It should predict
unseen and shifted inputs, support extraction or selective removal, preserve unrelated behavior, and reveal reuse or
composition when those are actually present. Fewer parameters or lower rank is useful only after it helps achieve
those tests.

The 06:10 update ended after complete MLP8, MLP9, and MLP12 writes failed to behave like one shared downstream
variable. Since then, we tested three progressively less coordinate-dependent ways of splitting those MLPs.

## 1. Rung 476: the old task-selected product groups made downstream grouping worse

Each of these MLPs has4,608 exact multiplicative products. Rung467 had previously selected a subset in each MLP using
four code-context effects. Rung476 removed those frozen subsets and measured a62-number response profile: one signed
CE-loss effect for each curated downstream behavioral circuit.

The implementation check was exact: selected subset plus its complement reproduced the complete-MLP intervention
with0.0 nat error. The negative result is therefore about the proposed groups, not a broken decomposition.

Under the native matcher, the best selected-group pair had37.3% response cosine after controlling for ordinary token
difficulty, versus49.0% for the corresponding complete MLPs. Under the transplanted matcher, the best selected pair
reached41.4%, versus57.3% for its complete parents, while a matched random group reached69.2%. The old four-context
selection isolates a real code-specific correction, but it does not isolate the cross-MLP downstream commonality.

## 2. Rungs 477/477b: individual native products have almost no stable behavioral identity

For MLP `m`, product number `j`, and one downstream circuit, the measured first-order response was

`- <CE gradient at the MLP write, Down[:,j] × (product_source[j] - product_absent[j])>`.

The angle brackets are a dot product over the1,152 residual-stream coordinates. This number estimates the CE effect
of removing that one equality-induced product contribution. For each product we averaged it on circuit members and
subtracted a matched control average. The resulting32-number vector says which discovery circuits later computation
uses that product for. We required that vector to agree across the two matcher sources and two document halves.

Only3 of4,608 products in MLP8 and4 of4,608 in each of MLP9 and MLP12 survived. That is0.065%,0.087%, and0.087%.
There were zero stable cross-MLP product matches.

The first run also revealed a real data-accounting bug: the four-document batch containing rows248--251 crossed the
pre-registered row250 boundary, but two rows were initially assigned to the wrong half. Rung477b split that batch by
row. Counts became correct, the sum over halves reproduced the original response at`1.83e-17` relative squared error,
and the scientific result remained3/4/4 stable products with zero cross-MLP matches. We use only the corrected tensor
for later fitting while preserving the original run as instrument-invalid.

## 3. Rung 478: sparse weighted mixtures interpolate one view but do not transfer

An individual product might be the wrong basis even if a weighted mixture is meaningful. For each MLP pair, rung478
defined a common32-circuit target halfway between the two complete-MLP response directions. It then fit two kinds of
weighted product mixtures separately in each MLP:

- nonnegative weights, corresponding to partial removal of real product contributions; and
- signed weights, which may remove some contributions and add others.

The fit used only native-matcher documents0:250. It stopped as soon as the mixture reached at least95% target cosine
and at most20% scale-adjusted relative error; it did not choose a rank or tune a number of products.

All six pair-by-weight-sign candidates fit. They needed only6--8 products and achieved96.5--98.3% cross-MLP cosine
on the fitting view. The best candidate, signed MLP8+MLP12, then fell to50.6% under the other matcher on the same
documents,15.1% on the other document half under the native matcher, and5.9% on that half under the transplant. Its
minimum was below the33.9% 95th percentile of controls where cross-MLP circuit alignment was deliberately destroyed.

This is unusually clear: the desired direction is easy to interpolate in32 dimensions, but the fitted mixture is not
a reusable computation. We therefore closed sparse weighted mixtures of the native products rather than increasing
their support or changing a sparsity penalty.

## 4. Rung 479: a coordinate-free common-block test also fails

The third test removed the product-coordinate dependence. For an MLP input `x` and downstream CE gradient `g`, the
exact quadratic part read downstream can be written

`g^T MLP(x) = x^T Q(g) x`,

where

`Q(g) = sym(Left^T diag(Down^T g) Right)`.

`sym(M)` means `(M + M^T)/2`. Thus `Q(g)` is a symmetric matrix describing which pairs of input directions matter to
that downstream reader. We restricted it to one fixed32-dimensional subspace learned from equality-induced input
state changes on native documents0:250.

A genuine common two-block structure would mean there is a non-constant matrix `C` satisfying approximately
`C Q = Q C` for every circuit-specific reader matrix from both MLPs. In ordinary terms, all readers would preserve
the same two input subspaces. The eigenvectors of `C` define the two projectors, and rotating coordinates inside
either subspace does not change those projectors. This is why the test is insensitive to the internal coordinate
choice that affected the product experiments.

Before model outcomes were opened, an independent synthetic calibration measured the test on planted blocks and
structureless matrices. It produced zero false block detections in32 controls. Planted blocks with3% noise left at
most2.9% of their matrix size outside the blocks; forced splits of structureless matrices left at least12.4% at the
5th percentile. We therefore replaced the preliminary guessed bars by a3% fitting bar before running rung479.

The actual result was far from a block:

- the best real non-constant commutation error was`.03685`, while the 5th percentile after independently rotating one
  MLP was`.03581`; real structure did not beat the destroyed-alignment controls at all;
- the forced real split left34.2% median reader-matrix size outside the blocks on the fitting view, versus the3% bar;
- held-out-view medians were34.2--37.5%; and
- the fitted state subspace's squared overlap with the other views was71.0%,52.1%, and48.6%, failing on the other
  document half.

One forced block had48.5% minimum circuit-profile cosine, above its raw35.9% control percentile but below the70% bar
and below the required15-point control margin. This is weak shared variation, not an identified block.

## 5. What is and is not ruled out

We can now reject three specific explanations for shared internal coordinates among MLP8, MLP9, and MLP12:

1. individual native product coordinates;
2. sparse weighted mixtures containing at most32 native products; and
3. a shared two-block reader algebra inside the registered32-dimensional equality-state subspace.

This does **not** prove that no possible decomposition exists, and it does not mean the three MLPs compute identical
functions in different coordinates. The exact query-position interventions show that all three participate in the
same equality-correction behavior, while the grouping tests say later computation does not treat the tested internal
pieces as one stable variable. The safest current description is “three distinct causal components cooperating on
one behavior,” not “one representation copied three times.”

No odd-root validation circuit or documents500:1000 were opened in this chapter, because no proposal earned exact
validation. No parameter, rank, or CE-compression claim was made.

## 6. The new active direction: let downstream circuits choose the basis inside attention0

We have now started rung480. It deliberately avoids duplicating the old Q/K weight SAE, sparse token vocabulary,
Tucker decomposition, and failed direct bilinear generator.

The starting object is the independently replicated rung424/425 continuous attention0 block. For a realized causal
edge it uses six coordinates for score branch1, six for score branch2, and32 for the head-by-output payload. Its exact
fitted computation is a sum of1,152 trilinear contributions:

`score1_coordinate[i] × score2_coordinate[j] × payload_coordinate[k] × fixed_output[i,j,k]`.

Those coordinates can rotate internally, so rung480 will not assume the current axes are circuits. On the32 even-root
discovery circuits, it will calculate the member-minus-control downstream CE response of every contribution. For each
of the three modes, it will use this response tensor to define one leading projector: the direction later computation
distinguishes most strongly after accounting for the other two modes.

The test then asks whether those projectors survive document halves, matcher source, and an independently refit block
after removing harmless rotations. A proposed “slab”—one projector in one mode while keeping all coordinates in the
other two—must have a stable32-circuit response, beat activation-only and circuit-label controls, survive omission of
whole circuit families, and differ from its complement. Only then will we perform exact removal on the reserved odd
families.

This is a decomposition by downstream use rather than by head, raw weight row, rank, or variance. The full frozen
protocol is already committed. If it fails, the continuous attention block remains a strong predictive interface but
not a semantically identified internal circuit, and the active line switches to the independent MLP0 token-only,
token-by-context, and context-only decomposition.
