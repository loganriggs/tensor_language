# Circuit research update — 2026-09-04 09:07 UTC (Claude, lane 1)

## Goal, and what this chapter is about

The goal is to replace the 546-million-parameter bilin18 transformer with a smaller explicit tensor program whose parts
correspond to computations — a circuit should say what is read, what is computed, what is written, and how later
components use it, and should permit a targeted edit without disturbing unrelated behaviour.

This chapter covers §2858–§2873. It is mostly a chapter about **discovering that an instrument was broken, repairing it,
and then finding that the repaired instrument still could not answer the question** — followed by a pivot. Three of my
own published accounts were withdrawn in the process. I think that is the most useful thing in it, so it is told in order.

## 1. The measurement that was measuring itself

The circuit battery scores a component by damaging it and comparing the damage to the *target* behaviour against the
damage to *control* behaviours. The controls are the point: a component that damages everything equally has not been
shown to do anything specific.

While smoke-testing a routine rung I noticed the target family (A1) and the answer-preserving control family (P) reading
*identical* damages — not close, identical — on five of seven behaviours. The cause is structural. The task bank emits a
group's four families as transformations of **one situation**, and the transformation lives in the *donor*; A1, A2 and P
therefore share the same `base_text`. Every scoring rung called `pack(b, "base")` for every family. So the control was
evaluated on the target's own prompts, and

    ratio = |d_P| / max(d_A1, .5) = 1.000

**by construction, not by measurement.** Two independent checks confirmed it, one requiring no GPU at all: a structural
census of the row generator (11 of 21 behaviours at exactly 1.00, the other 10 at exactly 0.00, nothing between), and the
already-landed §2840 receipt read as bytes — `control_d_m["P"]` is *bitwise identical* to `split_d_m["FULL"]` on 9 of 16
tasks, each publishing a selectivity ratio of exactly 1.000. The census predicts that receipt task-for-task, agreement
**1.00**. §2852's own docstring had attributed those exact-1.00 readings to saturation; that was wrong.

## 2. The worse defect, which the repair exposed

Reading P from the donor instead of the base fixed the first defect and changed the ratios by a median of only .072. That
was the clue. The bank *declares* what P varies: the causal variable for `paren_list.index_successor` is "last visible
list label", and P's perturbation is `item_words` — the generator's own comment is *"swap the item words only"*.

So P changes filler and **leaves the causal variable intact**. It preserves the answer *because* it preserves the
variable. A component that genuinely carries the causal variable **must** damage P as much as A1. `|d_P|/d_A1 ≈ 1` is the
signature of a variable-carrying writer, not evidence against specificity — and because the score took a `max` over
controls, it was pinned near 1 for every behaviour however specific the component was.

I withdrew the campaign's standing negative ("zero behaviours are writer-selective") and **did not publish its opposite**.
Un-established, in either direction, was the honest state.

## 3. The repaired instrument, and what it could and could not do

Re-specified with P as a **positive** control and the copy control (whose answer is not a function of the causal variable)
carrying the score, tested for the first time on held-out splits:

- P behaves as a positive control at **.056** on the attn8 behaviours and **.072**, **.061** bank-wide — the clause I
  registered so that §2860 could be refuted was not met.
- Removing the writer *helps* the copy answer on 7 of 7 behaviours — it opposes copying while supporting the successor.
- The old .25 "selective" bar was retired: a median of 6 live components clear it, so it marked roughly a sixth of the
  model rather than a special writer.

Then the instrument ran into its own limits, and three accounts of mine died:

1. **attn8 is not an outlier.** Its percentile among live components measured .214, then .1875, then **.200** on a
   different population. Real and stable — and sitting *on* the threshold, not below it. I recorded the predicate TRUE as
   registered and declined the claim.
2. **There is no selective band.** §2867–§2870 had converged on "many components share a similar true value" to reconcile
   a reproducible ranking (ρ .749/.763) with an argmin that never reproduced. The top-8 set overlaps at Jaccard **.231**
   against a *matched* random baseline of **.192**. The registered null fired. The ranking is carried by the inert tail.
3. **The cleanest number was directional.** Selecting on one split and evaluating on another gave an apparent advantage of
   −.240; reversing the two collapsed it to **−.020**.

What survives is worth stating, because it is not nothing: the argmin of this metric never reproduces (0 of 7, six times,
three sample sizes, every direction), and **an unconstrained held-out search over all 36 components never beats the
writer identified from causal evidence** — in the most inflated direction it loses to it badly. That is real support for
the writer identification, produced by a procedure built to beat it.

## 4. Why I stopped, and where I went

Fifteen sections moved the explained fraction by **zero**, by construction: every quantity in them is local. The
instrument has now told me three separate times that it cannot resolve per-component specificity beyond the live/inert
distinction. Continuing would be polishing a measurement that has answered.

The pivot is to the frontier, where the numbers are in the currency that matters — **L2 is cross-entropy added above the
real model, so lower is better**; the published frontier is 2.6735.

I had twice told Codex that installing attn5's constant into the frontier was blocked on his machinery. **That was
wrong.** The §2125 rung script reruns the whole §312 pipeline and reproduces the published number, and it has been on
disk the whole time. I unblocked myself and withdrew the ask.

## 5. The question now running

Inside the frontier, attention 5 is not raw attention: it is a fitted dictionary — a ten-row class-conditional constant
table chosen by an input probe, plus linear maps for some classes. In the *real* model, attn5's write is one fixed vector
(cosine .9999996 with its own mean, most constant of all 36 components) and substituting that constant recovers 94.2% of
what deleting it costs. Whether that survives *inside the construction* had never been tested.

Two rungs are running: collapse a5's dictionary to a single constant, and collapse the whole motif-attention band a2–a9,
with the band-minus-a5 as the control. The tail band would have been a useless control — an independent census puts its
total document-CE cost at 0.157 nats against the motif band's 3.234, so collapsing it is free by construction and proves
nothing.

**As of this writing the reproduction gate has passed: the derived pipeline's baseline reads +2.6736 against the published
+2.6735.** The collapse arms are in flight. If a5 collapses for free, a whole fitted dictionary in the frontier becomes
one vector; if the band does, the frontier's entire motif-attention stage becomes a table of eight constants. If neither
does, §2835's real-model result does not survive composition — the same failure mode §2125 found for Fisher selection, and
a genuine limit on how far the isolated-component story transfers to the program.
