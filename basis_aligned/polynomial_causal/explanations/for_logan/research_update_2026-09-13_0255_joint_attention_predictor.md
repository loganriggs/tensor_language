# Research update: a compact predictor of a circuit interaction

**13 September 2026, 02:55 UTC.** Covers completed work since [the 02:00 update](research_update_2026-09-13_0200_executable_interaction_path.md), including the new-context confirmation completed at 02:46 and a scope audit performed while preparing this report. Layer and head numbers are zero-based: head17.2 is the third head in the final attention layer.

## 1. High-level overview

**We found a small, explicit formula that predicts a particular interaction's behavioral effect on new city names and sentence constructions.** It uses three attention source sums, requires no fitted coefficients, and has **3.5–5.2% effect error on 96 new prefixes**. All 95 nonzero effect signs match; the remaining reference effect and prediction are both zero at the measured precision.

This is a useful development for your interaction-path idea. The computation becomes simpler when we follow the actual joint attention product and distinguish where interactions are generated. We did not need another difficult CP or sparse-Tucker optimization to obtain this result.

There is an important boundary to that success: **the formula predicts one conditional contribution, not the entire circuit or even the entire interaction between the original two components.** It still receives inputs generated by several native model executions. A new audit makes that distinction quantitative: using it alone to predict the original full interaction would leave **72–81% relative error**. That is compatible with accurately predicting this smaller contribution.

The main events were:

1. **Confirmed that MLP9 is only part of the explanation.** Its response formula was accurate, but even correcting the exact native MLP9 interaction left most of the final behavioral interaction.
2. **Located an important later contribution in the final block.** State interventions showed that the interaction is distributed and cannot be attributed by simply subtracting layerwise norms.
3. **Separated the final block's attention and bilinear-MLP contributions.** The attention contribution was substantial for regional spelling; the MLP-only explanation failed.
4. **Traced the attention contribution to known head17.2.** This reconnects the path to a previously studied module. It does not yet prove that the old extracted component inside that head is the same computation.
5. **Used the mathematical review to derive a joint QK/value expansion.** It exposed terms that separate QK analyses would miss and led directly to the three-sum approximation.
6. **Froze that approximation and tested new inputs.** The positive result survived a separate comparison against the native head's measured write.

My assessment: this is stronger evidence for equation-guided interaction decomposition and limited predictive extraction. The unsolved part is generating the required inputs with a smaller, reusable program, and accounting for the remaining interaction.

## 2. What was the interaction we were trying to explain?

The previous report defined a small child computation spanning MLP7, attention8, MLP8 and head9. It interacts contextual readings with token-derived values, then writes along a fixed direction at head9. That child, \(C\), sits inside a larger known component, \(P\). We define the remainder by

$$
P=C+R.
$$

Removing child and remainder together reproduces parent removal exactly at their write interface. But their final effects do not add exactly, because the remaining model is nonlinear.

Use subscripts \(N,C,R,P\) for the native model and the three removal conditions. If \(Y\) is a final measured outcome, the nonadditivity is

$$
I_{\mathrm{total}}=Y_P-Y_C-Y_R+Y_N.
$$

For regional experiments, \(Y\) is a spelling-token score margin. For newline controls, it can be the next-token cross-entropy: negative log probability measured in nats. A positive CE change means damage; absolute CE change measures disturbance in either direction.

This interval primarily asked **where the nonadditivity comes from and how to compute it**, rather than searching for another independent spelling component.

## 3. Why we moved from MLP9 to the final block

The previous report's MLP9 formula accurately predicted its local response, yet removing that predicted interaction left roughly 85–90% of regional nonadditivity. A reasonable objection was that numerical error in the formula might be amplified downstream.

We therefore repeated the correction using the **actual native mixed state**, independently of the formula. The conclusion survived: more than half the final interaction remained in every regional and FineWeb group. Formula-versus-native correction differences were only 0.025–0.046% of regional interaction. Two FineWeb groups missed the strict 1% precision criterion, at 5.49% and 1.67%; those misses remain recorded. They did not change the mediation verdict.

Next, we reset the parent-removal trajectory at different block boundaries to the additive reference

$$
\bar h_\ell=h_{C,\ell}+h_{R,\ell}-h_{N,\ell}.
$$

This removes accumulated mixed state at that boundary and lets the actual remaining network run. There was no common early boundary through block12 that cut the remaining interaction below half in all regional groups. The curves were also nonmonotone: removing an interaction can uncover another contribution or remove a cancellation.

The final block stood out. Moving the reset from after block16 to after block17 reduced the remaining regional interaction norm from **37–79% to 8–26%** across groups.

That does not uniquely assign those differences to a circuit on the ordinary parent-removal trajectory. The principled local object is instead

$$
G_{17}=F_{17}(\bar h_{16})-\bar h_{17},
$$

where \(F_{17}\) is the actual last block, with the shared initial-state input held as in the native runs. It measures the mixed state that this block generates when given the additive incoming state. We evaluate its effect against the explicitly specified background \(\bar h_{17}\).

## 4. What folding through the last bilinear layer revealed

Split the last block at its MLP input. Let \(z_A\) be the pre-MLP state obtained by running attention on \(\bar h_{16}\), and let

$$
\bar z=z_C+z_R-z_N,
\qquad g(z)=z+\operatorname{MLP}_{17}(\operatorname{RMSNorm}(z)).
$$

Then the exact local split is

$$
G_{17}=
\underbrace{g(\bar z)-\bar h_{17}}_{G_{\mathrm{MLP}}}
+\underbrace{g(z_A)-g(\bar z)}_{G_{\mathrm{attention}}}.
$$

The first term is the MLP's mixed response on the additive pre-MLP input. The second is the attention-generated mixed input, including how the MLP processes it.

MLP-only prediction missed the full local regional effect by **53–84%**. The attention branch's aligned contribution was roughly **51–80%**, depending on construction. These alignment measures are projections of outcome vectors, not independent percentages of a semantic circuit. Separately measured branch effects added within 0.12–0.19% on the regional panels. FineWeb showed a different balance, with the MLP contribution larger.

We then expanded the attention branch exactly through the bilinear MLP. Define

$$
B(z)=D[(Lz)\odot(Rz)],\qquad
\rho(z)=\frac{\|z\|^2}{1152}+\epsilon.
$$

Here \(L,R\) are \(4608\times1152\), \(D\) is \(1152\times4608\), \(\odot\) means elementwise multiplication, and \(\epsilon\) is the actual model's RMS epsilon. For \(v=z_A-\bar z\), putting \(z=\bar z\),

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(z+v)-g(z)={}&v\\
&+\frac{D[(Lz)\odot(Rv)+(Lv)\odot(Rz)]}{\rho(z+v)}\\
&+\frac{D[(Lv)\odot(Rv)]}{\rho(z+v)}\\
&+\left(\frac{\rho(z)}{\rho(z+v)}-1\right)\frac{B(z)}{\rho(z)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

These are the direct write, its interaction with the existing state, its square, and the normalization correction. The output bias cancels. No denominator is frozen and no optimizer is involved.

The direct attention write alone predicted this branch's regional effect within **3.5–9.5%**. Keeping its cross term and normalization correction reduced error to **0.064–0.135%**. The square term added little at the tested intervention strength. It cannot be assumed negligible for arbitrary edits.

The analogous FineWeb effects are often only a few millionths of a nat. Relative prediction errors can therefore be large despite very small absolute discrepancies; this is not uniform high-accuracy validation across all behaviors.

## 5. The joint attention decomposition—and why the math cycle helped

The direct attention mixed write was concentrated in **known head17.2**: its regional effect differed from the full attention-layer effect by only **1.25–1.62%**. Its performance was much worse on the newline controls, so the single-head conclusion is specific to this regional interaction.

For one final query position and \(T\) causal source positions, that head computes

$$
a_s=\frac{q_{1,t}^Tk_{1,s}}{128},\qquad
b_s=\frac{q_{2,t}^Tk_{2,s}}{128},\qquad
H(a,b,V)=\sum_{s\leq t}a_sb_sV_s.
$$

The two score arrays \(a,b\) each have shape \(T\). Values \(V\) have shape \(T\times128\). The result is a 128-vector, mapped to the 1,152-dimensional residual stream by the head's output matrix. Scores include the actual normalization and positional rotations. Values include the actual current/first-layer mixture. There is no softmax.

The key point agrees with your correction about QK1 and QK2: **their product is the computation**. Changing one factor under the child edit and another under the remainder edit produces an interaction even if neither factor has an interaction by itself.

The 02:29 mathematical review turned this observation into an exact executable expansion. For any of the three inputs \(x\in\{a,b,V\}\), define

$$
c_x=x_C-x_N,\qquad r_x=x_R-x_N,\qquad
\bar x=x_N+c_x+r_x,\qquad e_x=x_A-\bar x.
$$

Here **\(A\) means running the last block on the additive incoming state**, not the original joint parent-removal trajectory. The quantity \(e_x\) measures the nonadditivity already present when this input reaches the multiplication. It can arise from the query/key computations and normalization.

The desired local head interaction is

$$
I_H=H(a_A,b_A,V_A)-H(a_C,b_C,V_C)
-H(a_R,b_R,V_R)+H(a_N,b_N,V_N).
$$

It separates exactly into products mixing the child and remainder changes, plus products containing an inherited nonadditive input \(e_x\). There are 12 terms of the first kind and seven grouped terms of the second kind.

The math review included a planted counterexample: change only score1 under one edit and only score2 under the other. Every input's own mixed change is zero, but the joint attention interaction is nonzero. The new implementation recovered it to floating-point precision. An analysis of the inputs independently would have missed the whole effect.

On native data, retaining only the 12 cross-edit products missed regional effects by **32–37%**. The inherited terms were needed. The full expansion reproduced the native local effect within **0.020–0.043%** on the regional development panels. Separate cross and inherited effects added closely and largely reinforced one another.

**This math cycle helped directly:** it supplied the right decomposition, an executable falsifier, and the structure of the approximation tested next. It did not merely suggest another factorization family.

## 6. How 19 groups became three source sums

On the development panel, the larger terms involved changed values multiplied by score changes, and the inherited nonadditivity of either score. We retained these groups and dropped the score-score terms with unchanged native values, the value-only inherited term, and terms involving multiple inherited changes.

For clarity, write the native scores as \(a,b\). Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
w_C&=r_a(b+c_b)+(a+c_a)r_b+r_ar_b,\\
w_R&=c_a(b+r_b)+(a+r_a)c_b+c_ac_b.
\end{aligned}
$$

All products here are elementwise over source positions. The approximation is

$$
\widehat I_H=
\sum_s (w_C)_s(c_V)_s
+\sum_s(w_R)_s(r_V)_s
+\sum_s(e_a\bar b+\bar a e_b)_s\bar V_s.
$$

Each sum contracts a scalar weight per source with a 128-dimensional value. That is what **three contractions** means. It introduces no learned coefficients. The regrouping is exact for the selected terms; omitting the other terms is the approximation.

This selection used development examples, so its initial 2.9–4.7% error and 96/96 sign agreement were not independent confirmation. We froze the formula before scoring the next panel.

The behavioral test projects \(\widehat I_H\) through the real head output matrix, adds the resulting write to the specified final-state background, and uses the actual final RMS normalization, unembedding and score soft-cap. **It does not run that write through the last MLP in this particular compact-predictor test.** The earlier MLP response experiment is a separate result. Keeping these targets distinct prevents combining their error bars into an untested end-to-end claim.

## 7. Fresh confirmation and the scope audit

The new panel contained 96 prefixes: four constructions, city pairs London/Boston and Manchester/Chicago, the existing six spelling endpoints, and both city directions. There were also 24 old replay anchors. Full prefixes were checked for duplicates and paired cue confounds before scoring; no model-score filtering or coefficient refitting was used.

The city pairs are new to this local path test, not necessarily globally unstudied tokens. This is lexical/construction generalization, not a new-corpus OOD test.

The following errors compare directly against the **independent native head write**, rather than only our algebraic reference:

| New construction | Conditional head-effect error | Nonzero signs matched | Native regional pairs correct |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Reader reply | 5.20% | 24/24 | 12/12 |
| Grew up | 3.96% | 24/24 | 12/12 |
| Return home | 3.64% | 24/24 | 12/12 |
| Exact spelling | 3.53% | 23/23 | 12/12 |

The remaining effect in the last row was zero in both reference and prediction. Error means the Euclidean norm of prediction minus reference across the group, divided by the reference norm—not the fraction of incorrect examples or a percentage of the model's total score.

The mean absolute predicted margin changes were only **0.000194–0.000717**. Predicting these small effects accurately is useful, but their size matters when judging coverage.

I performed an additional CPU audit for this report: what if we treated the same prediction as an explanation of larger targets, without fitting a scale?

| Construction | Error against full local block17 generation | Error against original child/remainder nonadditivity |
|---|---:|---:|

| Grew up | 42.7% | 77.9% |
| Return home | 27.0% | 71.9% |
| Exact spelling | 22.1% | 74.0% |

A zero prediction would have 100% relative error. The compact term is informative, but substantially incomplete for these broader targets. Because the interventions use different backgrounds, these are predictive comparisons, not unique percentages of causal responsibility.

## 8. What this means for the four properties and the next work

| Desired property | New evidence | Remaining gap |
|---|---|---|
| OOD prediction | A fixed conditional formula predicts new cities/constructions with small local-effect error. | Existing endpoints; no new-corpus test or autonomous prediction from tokens. |
| Extraction | Explicit three-sum executable local interaction, using actual weights and a declared interface. | Four native input conditions, native background and readout are still supplied. |
| Selective removal | Builds on the previously validated removable child and its preservation controls; new work explains part of its interaction. | No new general selectivity claim for the compact head17.2 term. |
| Composition/reuse | Exact joint-product bookkeeping and prediction of a finite interaction, preserving both QK factors. | Full behavioral composition remains incomplete; reuse of the older within-head component is untested. |

The strongest red-team concerns are therefore real but specific: conditional inputs may hide much of the computation; development selection may overstate simplicity; small effects need absolute-error checks; and recurrence of a known head does not identify the same component. The executed counterchecks address several of these: new unscored prefixes, independent native-write references, explicit total-interaction errors, and exact numerical controls. They support the local result while leaving the larger extraction problem open.

The best next structural question is whether the required inputs can reuse the previously extracted source computation inside head17.2. We should check its existing dossier and actual weight subspaces before building another component. **That weight-interface check has not been completed in this reporting interval.** Afterward, the useful direction is to explain the remaining interaction and reduce supplied native inputs, rather than repeatedly improving a small local error on the same examples.

There were no new CP/LL1 optimizer convergence results in this interval. Most progress came from exact expansion, cached analysis and native interventions. The three-group development screen used 480 final readouts and no transformer forwards, taking 0.63 seconds of experiment-body execution. Fresh confirmation used 600 forwards plus 480 readouts and took 7.54 seconds. These timings exclude queue wait and human/agent implementation time; they are not whole-model speedup claims. The mathematical review's decision to exploit the existing product was therefore good computational ROI here.
