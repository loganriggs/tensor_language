# Explanation update since 16:01 — 2026-09-02 17:14 UTC

## Goal

We are trying to replace the model with a smaller, executable tensor program whose parts correspond to actual
computations. A useful part should say what information is read, how it is combined, what is written, and what later
computations use it. Parts should be grouped across attention heads or MLPs when they are operationally the same and
split within a head or MLP when they do different work. The resulting circuits should predict held-out and shifted
text, compose with one another, support selective removal or replacement, remain stable across data splits and
allowed changes of internal basis, and eventually have a literal storage and compute advantage.

This is why lower rank, fewer stored numbers, quantization, reconstruction error, or average CE alone are not the
target. They can price a circuit after we identify one; they do not tell us what the circuit computes.

The [16:01 explanation](explanation_2026-09-02_1601.md) gives the full account from 02:19 through the start of
rung495. This file explains everything that changed after that point.

## 1. The simple quadratic response rule failed

The 16:01 update reported that an occurrence-specific monotone curve predicted large MLP interventions better than
ordinary addition, although it failed at half strength and partly relied on saturation outside its fitted range.
A separate test asked whether the model architecture supplied a cleaner formula.

For each occurrence, it measured the effect `y(lambda)` when an MLP change was scaled by `lambda`. It solved

`y(lambda) = a*lambda + b*lambda^2`

exactly from the measured half-strength and full-strength effects, then predicted effects at 1.5x and a new 2x
strength. No parameters were fitted across examples.

This failed decisively. Relative to the ordinary additive prediction, the quadratic prediction had:

- 1.26–1.53 times as much error at half strength;
- 2.10–2.98 times as much error at 1.5x strength; and
- 3.56–4.14 times as much error at the new 2x strength.

The sign of the quadratic term usually indicated saturation, so that qualitative observation was real. But its
magnitude was almost as large as the linear term and the complete 18-layer suffix has important higher-order effects.
Two measured points therefore do not determine the response curve. We are not fitting a higher-degree polynomial:
that would continue describing an occurrence-specific curve rather than grouping or extracting a circuit.

## 2. What rung495 actually computed

Attention1 has nine heads. Each head uses two separate query-key scores and one value/output path. For a query
position `i` and earlier source position `j`, its raw residual-stream contribution has the form

`score1[h,i,j] * score2[h,i,j] * output_value[h,j]`.

For each exact MLP0 branch removal—token-only `T`, context-only `C`, token-by-context interaction `I`, and the small
normalization correction `S`—the experiment captured score1, score2, and output/value in both the normal and
branch-absent runs. It evaluated all eight choices of taking those three quantities from one run or the other.
Inclusion–exclusion then separated seven finite changes per head:

`score1`, `score2`, `output`, `score1×score2`, `score1×output`, `score2×output`, and the three-way interaction.

There are nine heads times seven changes, or 63 raw attention-write pieces. Their sum is exactly the complete change
in attention1's raw residual write.

For each of the 62 existing downstream circuits, the experiment differentiated that circuit's CE with respect to
the complete raw attention1 write, through the model's real RMS normalization, MLP1, residual path, and all later
layers. The number assigned to piece `p` was

`downstream response of p = sum over tokens and residual coordinates of gradient * p`.

Stacking these signed responses over the 62 circuits and four MLP0 branches gives each piece a downstream-use
fingerprint. Two pieces from different heads would be a candidate shared computation if their fingerprints pointed
in nearly the same direction on documents0:250, remained similar without reselection on documents250:500, and beat
circuit-label and token-position controls. Cosine similarity is reported as a percentage: 100% means proportional
signed effects on every measured circuit, 0% means no linear relation, and a negative value means opposite effects.

This was a first-order screen. Even a pass would have required a later finite two-way replacement through the real
normalization and suffix.

## 3. The first run exposed a numerical bug and did not count

The preregistration required the eight-arm algebra and inclusion–exclusion calculation in float32. The first source
accidentally kept those operations in BF16, the model's deployed dtype. Inclusion–exclusion subtracts several large,
nearly cancelling terms, so BF16 rounding was amplified:

- branch-absent endpoint error: `4.85e-6` relative squared;
- 63-piece sum error: `7.83e-3`; and
- summed downstream-response error: `1.44e-5`.

The registered limits were `1e-10`, `1e-10`, and `1e-9`. Prediction A—the instrument check—was false. Therefore the
first receipt was preserved but none of its scientific outcomes licensed a conclusion or successor.

Rung495b repaired only this arithmetic. It recomputed factors, arms, inclusion–exclusion terms, and gradient
contractions in float32, following the already established attention-factor implementation. The actual model
forward, raw-write gradient, normalization, MLP1, and suffix remained BF16. Data, pair selection, controls,
thresholds, and conditional validation were unchanged.

The repair passed:

- normal and branch-absent endpoint errors: `2.71e-14` and `3.07e-14`;
- 63-piece sum error: `6.29e-12`;
- summed downstream-response error: `3.71e-13`; and
- exactly 125 prefix captures, 500 branch-absent forwards, and 28,364 backward calls.

This makes the second receipt a valid scientific test.

## 4. No complete below-head piece was shared strongly enough across heads

The best material pair was the output/value-only contribution from head0 and the output/value-only contribution from
head8. It was a mutual nearest match and beat shuffled circuit labels, but its similarity was much too weak and did
not confirm:

![Best cross-head attention-piece similarity](assets/attention1_piece_similarity_rung495.svg)

The raw fingerprint cosine was 55.7% on the selection half, below the required 90%, and fell to 34.7% on the fixed
confirmation half, below 80%. After subtracting the response shared by all four MLP0 branches, it was 54.5% and
31.6%. The corresponding best-scaled residual errors were 83.0% and 93.8%, where lower is better. This is not a
near-miss: the two pieces have some nonrandom overlap, but downstream circuits do not treat them as the same
variable and the relation is not stable across documents.

Because the discovery condition failed, the position-control pass and the 30 validation circuit tags on
documents500:1000 were correctly left unopened.

The within-head split condition also failed. Head8's `score1×output` and `score1×score2×output` pieces had cosines
about -79.7% and -70.9% across the two halves. The negative sign looks dramatic, but cosine ignores magnitude: these
pieces are largely the same one-dimensional effect with opposite signs. After allowing the best signed scale, their
residual difference was only 60.4–70.5%, below the 85%/80% required to call them distinct downstream functions.

The supported conclusion is deliberately narrow:

> When each attention1 head is split into complete score1, score2, output/value, and their interaction changes, none
> of the resulting 63 pieces is used similarly enough across heads to identify a shared downstream variable under
> the current 32-circuit discovery test.

This does not show that attention heads are the correct basis. It specifically motivates a finer split inside each
query-key score.

## 5. The next experiment: separate query and key sides

A score is a dot product between a query-side vector and a key-side vector. Two heads can read the same query feature
but combine it with different key features, or share a key feature while producing different outputs. Rung495 kept
each whole query-key score together, so it could miss exactly this structure.

Rung496 therefore treats each head as five factors:

`Q1, K1, Q2, K2, V`.

It evaluates all 32 normal-versus-branch-absent combinations. Inclusion–exclusion gives every interaction among the
five factors. Each interaction is then divided equally among the factors participating in it. For example, a
`Q1×K1×V` interaction contributes one third to Q1, one third to K1, and one third to V. This is the Shapley
allocation: the average marginal contribution of a factor over every possible order in which the five factors could
be changed.

The five allocated contributions sum exactly to the complete attention-write change. But this allocation is a
mathematical convention, not a claim about how the model represents the computation. To ensure the convention does
not create an artificial match, every candidate must also agree in two direct views:

- **factor first:** change this Q or K factor while all four partners remain branch-absent;
- **factor last:** change this factor after all four partners are already normal.

The primary bank contains 36 sides: Q1, Q2, K1, and K2 from nine heads. Query sides can match query sides across
heads; key sides can match key sides. The private 128-dimensional coordinates are not compared. Every candidate is a
complete raw residual-write contribution, so compatible rotations of a head's private Q/K coordinates leave the
measurement unchanged.

A pass requires the same cross-head side pair to survive:

1. Shapley-average, factor-first, and factor-last views;
2. raw and branch-mean-removed downstream fingerprints;
3. selection and fixed confirmation documents;
4. shuffled circuit labels and shifted token positions;
5. the 30 held-out circuit tags and documents500:1000; and
6. a check that the matching side is more similar than the heads' opposite Q/K sides or complete outputs.

Even then it is only a candidate. The next required test would physically replace the selected input side and ask
whether the intended circuit effects are preserved while unrelated circuits remain unchanged.

The [rung496 preregistration](../ATTENTION1_QUERY_KEY_DOWNSTREAM_SHAPLEY_RUNG496_PREREGISTRATION.md) is frozen. Its
exact five-factor computation, allocation, endpoint controls, parent checks, and three CPU tests are implemented and
passing. Downstream collection and scoring are now being implemented; no rung496 model outcome has opened.
