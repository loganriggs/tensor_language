# Better sparse encoding improves the full folded factorization

**Latest:** [09:47 first overcomplete result](#update0947).

**11 September, 09:40 UTC.** Changing only how we select sparse reader
coefficients improved full unembedding–MLP coefficient capture from **29.91%
to 33.21%**, with the same dictionary, output weights and program size.
This is a weight-only result: no text, new feature learning or behavioral fit.
It confirms that the old encoder obscured some useful reconstruction, but
does not establish interpretable circuits or settle the remaining error.

The overcomplete dictionary experiment is still running under its original
protocol. Its eventual result must be separated from this encoder issue.
The small frozen FineWeb diagnostic remains queued afterward. No million-token
discovery sweep has been added.

## What was wrong with the support choice

Lasso selects sparse coefficients by balancing reader reconstruction against
an L1 penalty, the sum of coefficient magnitudes. With normalized reader x and
dictionary B, it solves

$$
\min_z\;\tfrac12\|x-zB\|_2^2+\lambda\|z\|_1.
$$

The old encoder then took the 128 largest coefficient magnitudes and refitted
their values without the penalty. But if Lasso produced fewer than 128 nonzero
coefficients, the extra slots came from tied zeros. Their selection could
depend on dictionary row order rather than explanatory value. After the
unpenalized refit, these arbitrarily selected features could become active.

We tested this with one four-dimensional vector and the same orthogonal
dictionary under all 24 permutations of its feature names. Lasso selected
one feature; the encoder was required to keep two. The raw Lasso reconstruction
was identical under every permutation, but the refitted two-feature capture
ranged from **73.12% to 86.02%**. An exact two-feature support search remained
invariant. This is a demonstrated inference issue, not a change in features.
[Permutation control](../../LASSO_PADDING_PERMUTATION_V1_CONTROL.json).

## The replacement selection rule

Keep Lasso's nonzero features, up to the fixed budget. When slots remain,
add the feature that gives the largest exact one-step least-squares reduction
in residual error. This is orthogonal least squares (OLS), using the same
selection algebra as our existing OLS implementation. It is a greedy rule,
not a guarantee of the globally best sparse support.

If S is the current selected span, let r be the unexplained part of x and let
v_j be candidate feature b_j after projecting away its part already in S.
Its possible improvement is

$$
\Delta_j=\frac{(r^\top v_j)^2}{\|v_j\|_2^2}.
$$

Choose the largest improvement, update the residual and repeat. The
implementation retains projection factors, avoiding a full Gram-matrix update
at every addition. It matches the existing OLS routine on a separate random
control and fixes the permutation example without changing its two-term price.
[Implementation control](../../LASSO_OLS_COMPLETION_V1_CONTROL.json).

On 64 previously inspected native reader weights from one frozen nonorthogonal
dictionary, with 128 features per reader:

| Encoding | Reader reconstruction capture |
|---|---:|
| Existing Lasso(.05), top128, least squares |42.28%|
| Earlier dimension-scaled Lasso penalty |45.52%|
| Lasso(.05), OLS completion, least squares |47.49%|

Sixty-three readers needed extra slots; 63/64 improved over the old encoder.
All registered numerical and improvement checks passed. These are diagnostic
weights already used in earlier analysis, not a fresh validation sample.
[Subset result](../../NATIVE_LASSO_OLS_COMPLETION_V1_AUDIT.json).

## The complete folded-tensor check

We then encoded all 9,216 Left/Right reader weights with this rule, keeping
the learned basis, native product pairings, Down and bias fixed. Of those
readers, 7,025 required support completion. The run took 84.13 CPU seconds,
including 75.59 seconds for encoding; no competing GPU job was launched.

Full folded coefficient capture rose **29.9058% to 33.2108%**, an improvement
of **3.3049 percentage points** at unchanged size. Here capture means

$$
1-\frac{\|T-\widehat T\|_F^2}{\|T\|_F^2},
$$

where T includes every unembedding token's symmetric quadratic interaction
matrix. Exact implicit tensor contractions include downstream products and
cancellations. They do not assume the reader-level gain transfers automatically.
Parent-score replay error was 4.44e-16; sparse-program versus dense reconstructed
reader execution differed by 2.82e-15. All three registered predictions passed.

On the historical reader split, fitting-reader capture is 58.67% and the
previously held-out portion is 47.61%, versus 43.79% before re-encoding. That
47.61% is only slightly above the earlier PCA reader score of 47.54%; it is not
strong new evidence of weight generalization. The encoder was developed after
examining part of that split. Full-tensor reconstruction is an exact diagnostic,
but the split is no longer fresh method-selection validation.
[Full result and saved program](../../FULL_OBLIQUE_OLS_COMPLETION_V1_RESULT.json).

The second dictionary start replicated the same procedure: full capture rose
from **29.8454% to 33.1620%**; all numerical, gain and replication bars passed.
However, the two complete recoded functions have cosine similarity **0.4235**,
missing the registered 0.9 stability threshold. This comparison retains native
Down in both programs; it is not directly the earlier comparison after Down
refitting. The encoding gain is reproducible, while stable identification is
still unsupported. [Second-start result](../../FULL_OBLIQUE_OLS_COMPLETION_S937_V1_RESULT.json).

## What the live overcomplete fit is doing

The hourly review also examined a frozen intermediate snapshot of the live
overcomplete fit. Its initialization uses 2,304 sampled training readers as
atoms. A unit reader that is itself an atom has a special advantage: its
single-example Lasso optimum is already attainable before dictionary training.
For unit-ball atoms and 0<lambda<1,

$$
\tfrac12\|x-zB\|_2^2+\lambda\|z\|_1
\ge \lambda-\tfrac12\lambda^2.
$$

Equality is attained by using the matching atom with coefficient 1-lambda.
This follows from the triangle inequality; the numerical control verifies the
equality and optimality conditions. It can anchor features to sampled readers,
although moving an atom can improve many other readers and lower the joint loss.

At snapshot iteration81, each atom's original reader supplied 60.42% of squared
code energy overall. The median effective contribution count was 2.33 readers,
while the median number of nonzero uses was 88. The distinction is between
contribution size and merely being used. Features had also moved: median cosine
to their initial directions was 0.8906, missing our >=0.9 prediction. These are
intermediate code statistics, not additive function energy or a final verdict
on feature discovery. The original sampled-reader group had lost some of its
initial optimality, so these are not unchanged point copies.

A reporting correction is explicit: this snapshot fell between logged
objective checks, but the first audit incorrectly marked the missing replay
as passed. Its original instrument verdict is unverified. A separate algebraic
calculation on the exact frozen snapshot agrees to 5.55e-17; it verifies the
computed objective without inventing a missing historical measurement.
[Snapshot](../../L1_READER_ANCHOR_V1_AUDIT.json),
[correction and independent check](../../L1_READER_ANCHOR_REPLAY_V1_AUDIT.json).

The live experiment and its original encoder remain unchanged, preserving the
registered comparison. Its outcome will need separate judgments about joint
optimization, feature discovery and encoding. The new support-completion result
is one concrete reason to red-team a negative outcome rather than concluding
that the weights lack structure. None of these results yet proves the four
circuit properties. [Hourly review](../../HOURLY_STRATEGIC_REVIEW_2026-09-11_0922.md).

## 09:47: first overcomplete result, optimization unfinished

The first overcomplete start finished its allocated 1802 seconds. The exact
conditional coding and dictionary steps were solving their own problems, but
joint stationarity remained 6.24e-4 against the 1e-5 threshold. This is an
**unfinished optimization**, not a converged structural null. The second
start is now running under the unchanged protocol.

| First-start dictionary | Historical held-out reader capture | Full folded coefficient capture |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sampled native-reader dictionary before fitting |45.92%|38.68%|
| Learned overcomplete dictionary |46.86%|43.77%|

The full coefficient gain is 5.09 percentage points against its own initial
baseline. The historical held-out reader gain is only 0.94 percentage points,
below the registered two-point bar. Consequently the campaign's all-start
convergence and reader-gain requirements cannot pass unchanged, even though
its second start is still useful for the remaining comparisons.

This dictionary has 2304 features rather than 1152: **9,142,272 matrix
coefficients versus 7,815,168** for the complete dictionary. Its 43.77% score
is therefore not a matched-capacity improvement over the 33.21% OLS result.
“Before fitting” means before this dictionary optimization; those initial atoms
were themselves sampled from the trained model's weights.

The first result uses the original top128 encoder, whose padding limitation
is now documented. It has not yet been re-encoded with OLS completion. Numerical
execution checks passed, but global optimization, stable identification and
behavioral prediction remain unresolved.
