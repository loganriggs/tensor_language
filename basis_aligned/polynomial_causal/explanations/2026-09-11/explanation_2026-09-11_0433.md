## The methods actually used

Here a reader is a linear combination of input coordinates, a product multiplies
two reader values, and a writer distributes a product's output across tokens.
After folding the full unembedding into the last bilinear layer, each token has
a quadratic matrix. The native weights already represent all these matrices with
4,608 shared products. Discovery seeks better groups, shared readers and reusable
subcomputations inside that exact representation.

ALS/GN trials did not converge. Penalty changes the objective. |

## Latest result: coupled source selection

We keep the 17 value-source readers obtained by folding one compact
unembedding/MLP17 component through attention17's OV maps. For each head, we select
17 source directions inside its 256-dimensional joint key-weight span. MLP16
produces polynomial functions read by both families.

“Producer sharing” is the mean squared principal cosine between their normalized
polynomial function spaces. It measures overlap between computations under a
weight-coefficient inner product. “QK touch” is the fraction of joint routing
numerator coefficient energy involving at least one selected source direction,
including interactions with unselected directions. Neither is explained behavior.

## What the optimization computes

Let $G$ be MLP16's producer-function Gram matrix, and $B$ the fixed OV reader
matrix. A Gram matrix records pairwise inner products of polynomial coefficient
matrices. Define

$$
M=GB^\top(BGB^\top)^{-1}BG,
\qquad
J(E)=\frac1{17}\operatorname{tr}
\left[(E^\top GE)^{-1}E^\top ME\right].
$$

Here the columns of $E$ are candidate key readers. The inverse is on the
supported function space; these native cases retained all 17 dimensions. Let
$S$ measure joint QK source influence, averaged over discovery distances after
normalizing each distance by its own numerator energy. The fitted objective is

$$
\Phi(E)=\frac12\frac{J(E)}{J_{\max}}
+\frac12\frac{\operatorname{tr}(E^\top SE)}{I_{\max}},
\qquad E^\top E=I.
$$

Both maxima are computed within the head's joint key span. This normalization
prevents equal numerical weights from silently giving one term much more scale.
We optimize the subspace on a Grassmann manifold: rotating its internal basis
does not change the object. The influence term is a surrogate for actual QK
touch, which is why equal normalized weights did not guarantee the 80% target.
Query position is 511; even source positions determine discovery statistics,
and odd source positions supply the original validation-distance test.

For orthonormal original and fitted frames $A,F$, compute

$$
A^\top F=U\operatorname{diag}(c)V^\top,
\qquad
E_{\rm mid}=(AU+FV)\operatorname{diag}\bigl((2+2c)^{-1/2}\bigr).
$$

This aligns principal directions before taking their midpoint; averaging raw
frame entries would depend on an arbitrary basis. Orthogonality and projector
identities were checked to $6.9\times10^{-15}$.

## Executed next check: can the shared producer function be computed simply?

For the leading canonical pair of unit functions $u,v$ in each head, with
coefficient cosine $c$, the explicit common and difference functions are

$$
f_+=\frac{u+v}{\sqrt{2(1+c)}},\qquad
f_-=\frac{u-v}{\sqrt{2(1-c)}}.
$$

Each original function uses both terms. A cosine below one does not make them
identical. We formed their actual MLP16 quadratic matrices and computed their
best possible approximation with $k$ unrestricted real products.

A single real product has at most one positive and one negative eigenvalue.
A sum of $k$ therefore has at most $k$ of each. Conversely, any symmetric matrix
with this inertia can be written with $k$ products by pairing its positive and
negative eigenmodes. For nonnegative eigenvalue magnitudes $p_i,n_i$, sorted
separately in descending order, the optimal squared coefficient capture is

$$
C_k(Q)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(p_i^2+n_i^2)}{\|Q\|_F^2}.
$$

The spectral lower bound follows by aligning the approximation's eigenvectors
with the target and retaining the largest allowed eigenvalues of each sign.
It is attained constructively: for positive and negative eigenvectors $a_i,b_i$,

$$
\bigl(\sqrt{p_i}a_i+\sqrt{n_i}b_i\bigr)^\top x
\;\bigl(\sqrt{p_i}a_i-\sqrt{n_i}b_i\bigr)^\top x
=p_i(a_i^\top x)^2-n_i(b_i^\top x)^2.
$$

This is our scalar extension of the existing exact one-product update, not a
new iterative solver. It concerns a quadratic coefficient metric, not the
hard joint problem of sharing products across many outputs.

The 18-function CPU audit took **4.70 seconds**. Exact norms, eigenpair residuals
and reconstructed 16-product captures agree within $4.4\times10^{-15}$.
The mean optimal 16-product capture is **18.20% for common functions versus
14.19% for differences**. Ninety percent requires a median **256 products
versus 259**. Both small/simple-common-function predictions missed.

The strongest methodological explanation is now selection: the leading
canonical pair maximizes alignment, not arithmetic simplicity. This calculation
rules out an optimizer failure for approximating these *fixed scalar functions*;
it does not rule out simpler combinations elsewhere in their 17-dimensional
spans, other unembedding components, shared intermediate computations, or
structure concentrated on natural states. A structural negative for those
broader possibilities remains unsupported. Literal storage for $k$ independently
specified products is $2\times1152\times k$ input coefficients per scalar
function; reuse could change that price. Residual, bias, RMS and earlier-state
dependencies remain explicit background.

## Assumptions and remaining bottlenecks

The dangerous assumption is that “simple” must mean a tiny global output rank.
Many local reusable computations may have high global rank. Orthogonal readers,
disjoint clusters and one common block partition can also hide overlapping
structure. Antipodal quadratic symmetry alone forces none of those simplifications.
Sparse token usage likewise does not guarantee cheap input functions, as both
the dictionary and today's producer audit demonstrate.

Coefficient error weights formal input directions without their natural-state
frequency. That is an explicit weights-first baseline, not a claim about
behavioral relevance. Validation should later measure where a frozen factorization
works or fails, without silently refitting it to the validation corpus.

The dense token-by-input-by-input tensor would require about 267 GB in FP32.
Exact implicit contractions avoid materializing it. Conditioning and cancellation
have been real optimizer problems; local stationarity, independent starts,
function/subspace agreement and exact restricted optima provide different checks.
The good GPU is useful for contractions and large fits. Small compressed spectral
and manifold problems can be faster on CPU, as today's timings show.

Remaining weight-only coverage includes convergent overlapping block fits,
hierarchical signed output sharing with explicit exceptions, and jointly selecting
shared functions for both reuse and simple implementation. Existing converged
congruence screens should not be repeated as if untried. The next selection
criterion needs to address this simplicity gap, rather than spending another
cycle sweeping the same alignment/routing weight or expanding the text panel.

or overlapping block decompositions.

For orthonormal quadratic matrices$H_i$and unit coefficients$c$, the objective is

$$
Q(c)=\sum_i c_iH_i,\qquad
F(c)=\max(\lambda_{\max}(Q),0)^2+
\min(\lambda_{\min}(Q),0)^2,\qquad \|c\|_2=1.
$$

The numerator is the optimal one-real-product coefficient capture; the
quadratic has unit Frobenius norm. The gradient comes from the two extreme
eigenvectors. Their gaps are recorded to detect nonsmooth ties. CPU finite
differences agree within7.9e-11; the native function and spectral checks agree
within6.7e-15. The associated QK and OV functions are also recovered explicitly,
so losing similarity cannot masquerade as finding a shared simple computation.

An analytic bound supplies a stronger but narrow conclusion. For

$$
S=\sum_iH_i^2,
$$

vector Cauchy–Schwarz gives$Q(c)^2\preceq S$. A sum of$k$real products has rank
at most$2k$, hence its captured coefficient energy cannot exceed

$$
\min\left(1,\sum_{j=1}^{2k}\lambda_j^{\downarrow}(S)\right).
$$

The one-product bounds range10.99–20.56%across heads. Thus **no combination in
any of these fixed spans can have50%of its coefficient energy in one product**,
even if a better optimizer exists. The16-product bound is1and says nothing.
This bound does not prove global optimality of the returned fits or rule out the
registered improvement threshold; those are separate claims.
