# Weights-first factorization: methods, assumptions and bottlenecks

The weights-first search is not exhausted. We have tested several representations
and repaired real optimization failures, but too much of the campaign has concerned
small global decompositions of the same tensor. More data fitting would not repair
that coverage gap. FineWeb is the training corpus according to the user; use it for
in-distribution validation. Pile is a corpus-shift check. A fit adapted on Pile is
not an untouched-model OOD check for that fitted object.

The current priority is to find structure from weights, freeze candidates, and
then validate on data. No new activation-, CE-, or Fisher-guided discovery is queued.
The existing million-token panel does not need expansion for this stage.

## The object and the meaning of a factor

For normalized MLP17 input $x$, let $L,R$ be its two input maps, $D$ its output
map and $U$ the entire unembedding. The quadratic contribution before subsequent
normalization and tanh is

$$
f_v(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{4608}(UD)_{vj}(l_j^\top x)(r_j^\top x)
=x^\top Q_vx,
$$

$$
Q_v=\frac12\sum_j(UD)_{vj}(l_jr_j^\top+r_jl_j^\top).
$$

Thus the native weights already provide an exact shared 4,608-product
representation. Discovery asks whether these products can be reorganized into
simpler, reusable groups, readers or output patterns. A factor need not be one
token's private object: its vocabulary writer says which tokens share it, with
signed strengths. Bias and residual contributions remain explicit; $f$ alone is
not the final logits.

Most current fits minimize squared coefficient error

$$
\frac{\sum_v\|Q_v-\widehat Q_v\|_F^2}{\sum_v\|Q_v\|_F^2}.
$$

“Captured energy” means one minus this error. It is not accuracy, explained
behavior, or natural activation variance. All vocabulary rows enter the metric.
Centered-output experiments explicitly separate the vocabulary-common function;
that function must be retained before the actual final tanh.

ALS/GN runs remain unconverged. |
| 256 signed squares: a common list of squared readers with signed writers | L-BFGS and a scale-corrected polishing stage | Locally converged, 9.65% capture; more parameters than the 128-product model. |
| Overlapping multi-output blocks: several outputs reuse a small dense quadratic subsystem | Exact conditional writer solves, within-block QR coordinates and manifold conjugate gradient | 16 blocks, each with 16 readers and four quadratic outputs; 8.63% capture, still unconverged. Blocks may overlap. |
| Sparse multiplication graph in a shared input frame | Alternating edge selection and conjugate gradient constrained to orthogonal readers | Two locally converged starts capture 3.67% and 3.29% of the centered tensor; their functions differ. |
| Output groups with concentrated factor usage | Exact rank-128 output factorization followed by raw and equal-token varimax rotations | Both rotations converged locally. Top four loadings hold about 32–33% of retained loading energy. Rotating preserves the original rank-128 tensor approximation. |

| Shared structure after folding through attention output/value maps | Exact implicit contractions and a routing-mode eigendecomposition | Useful source-sharing identities, but few routing modes do not explain most of this coefficient tensor. This is a separate, deeper folded object. |

These rows use different capacities, penalties and sometimes different output
metrics. They are not a fair leaderboard. Primary numerical details are in the
[campaign](../2026-09-10/unsupervised_structure_campaign.md) and
[preceding attention-fold update](explanation_2026-09-11_0005.md).

Variable projection means solving the linear output-writer problem for each
candidate set of nonlinear readers. Alternating least squares updates one factor
group at a time; Gauss–Newton uses joint local curvature through implicit linear
solves. Manifold optimization keeps constraints such as orthogonal readers valid
at every step. These are project implementations of established algorithms,
not a demonstrated state-of-the-art benchmark. Relevant primary references:
[variable projection](https://www.cs.umd.edu/users/oleary/software/varpro.pdf),
[Gauss–Newton versus ALS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12331), and
[Tensorlab block-term methods](https://www.tensorlab.com/doc/btd.html).

## Assumptions that can hide structure

**A few global factors may be the wrong target.** Even allowing each factor an
arbitrary quadratic input function, the exact output-unfolding optimum captures
only 34.05% of full coefficient energy at 128 output factors. At 512 it captures
73.79%, and at 1,024 it captures 97.55%. This is an exact bound for that output-rank
restriction and metric, not a bound on arbitrary shared arithmetic programs.
It argues for testing many local groups and reader reuse rather than requiring
one tiny global bottleneck. [Spectrum](../../FULLU_OUTPUT_FUNCTIONS_V1_AUDIT.json).

**Orthogonality and exclusive groups are extra restrictions.** Useful readers
may overlap. A word can participate in multiple computations, so disjoint
clusters or a single tree may be less appropriate than overlapping signed
groups with explicit remainders. The overlapping block family relaxes some of
this restriction but has not yet converged. Output varimax is only a rotation
inside an already truncated space; it cannot recover structure discarded first.

**Antipodal symmetry does not imply low rank.** Every homogeneous quadratic
satisfies $f(-x)=f(x)$, including dense full-rank forms. Products and signed squares
both preserve this symmetry. Indeed,

$$
(a^\top x)(b^\top x)
=\frac14\left[((a+b)^\top x)^2-((a-b)^\top x)^2\right].
$$

Therefore a product can be replaced by two signed squares. Their differences
at a fixed budget involve parameter sharing, optimization and regularization;
they are not completely unrelated representational families. Nonnegative-only
output coefficients would wrongly exclude general indefinite quadratic forms.

**Coefficient geometry is a chosen metric.** It weights all formal input
directions, including directions rarely reached by the model. It is a sensible
weights-first baseline, but cannot establish that a coefficient tail is behaviorally
irrelevant. Weight-only alternatives include separate common/contrast accounting,
balanced output-group objectives and upstream-coupled structure. A change of
coordinates must carry its metric along unless the metric change is intentional.

**The cancellation penalty changes the question.** It repaired a pathological
fit whose separate component energies were about 14,265 times its total energy.
But it can also discourage legitimate cancellation and depends on how terms are
split. Local convergence of the penalized fit is not convergence of the original
unpenalized problem or evidence of a global optimum.

**A stable function need not have unique individual factors.** Basis rotations
inside a shared block can preserve the computation. Compare whole block functions
and reader subspaces before declaring that factors disappeared across restarts.
Statistical independence and non-Gaussian assumptions are also not justified
merely because tensor entries came from trained weights.

## Bottlenecks and the next methods

The dense $50,304\times1,152\times1,152$ tensor has about 66.76 billion entries,
roughly 267 GB in FP32. We do not materialize it: exact native-factor contractions
and small Gram matrices compute objectives and spectra. The GPU is useful and
working; recent native contractions take seconds, while nonlinear solver chunks
take roughly one to nine minutes. Solver conditioning, implementation time and
incomplete representation coverage are more immediate limits than needing a
larger text corpus. Local disk currently has only about 15 MB free, restricting
large new checkpoints even though GPU memory is a separate resource.

The substantial unexhausted directions are joint approximate block structure
of the entire quadratic family; overlapping or hierarchical output groups with
explicit corrections; and coupled reader/producer decompositions across layers.
Appropriate methods include structured nonlinear least squares for block terms,
group-sparse or hierarchical factor models, and coupled factorization with shared
parameters. Each needs a stated assumption, capacity-matched controls and converged
independent starts before a negative receives a structural interpretation. Merely
running another optimizer on the same 128-product model does not cover them.

A constructive baseline has now begun: select shared terms from the exact native
product dictionary and refit their writers exactly. At each step, orthogonal
least squares selects the candidate giving the largest remaining coefficient-error
reduction across outputs. For residualized feature Gram $G$ and target-feature
cross matrix $C$, its gain is

$$
\Delta_j=\frac{\|C_{:j}\|_2^2}{G_{jj}}.
$$

This tests native-term reuse and supplies weight-informed initializations without
first solving a nonlinear reader search. It is greedy, restricted to the native
dictionary, and not globally optimal. A CPU control matches every selected term
against exhaustive one-step refits and its predicted gains within $4.1\times10^{-14}$.
The native capacity curve has not run. This is OLS, not correlation-only OMP;
no sparse-recovery theorem is claimed for these correlated trained weights.

## Latest QK result, kept separate

The joint QK1×QK2 test is complete. Heads17.2/17.3 do not share a globally
proportional normalized routing row on the tested formal continuous inputs.
The median two-source routing sine is 0.698. The original self-position row
incorrectly treated query and key as independent slots; an explicit correction
now tests the tied-input quartic and replays the native formula within
$1.6\times10^{-15}$. The distinct-source counterexample is unaffected.

This rejects only global proportional routing for that pair and those tests.
It does not reject shared input subspaces, shared subterms, or agreement on the
two behaviors of interest. In particular, it is not the promised full answer
to how those tasks divide a head's joint product space.
