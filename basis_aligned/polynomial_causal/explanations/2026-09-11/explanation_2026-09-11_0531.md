# Weights-first factorization: methods, assumptions, and bottlenecks

The research priority is to discover structure from the weights, freeze the
candidate, and then validate it on FineWeb. Pile is a separate corpus-shift/OOD
check because the model was trained on FineWeb. More data-guided discovery is
deferred until the weight-only avenues have been properly tested. Running more
tokens does not fix an inadequate representation or an unconverged optimizer.

We have tried several distinct structural models. However, the latest sequence
of block experiments mostly changed the solver for the same model. That is
optimization work, not additional independent evidence against structure.
The search has not exhausted either the structural assumptions or their
capacity and initialization choices. Nor should the existing implementation
be described as a comprehensive comparison of state-of-the-art methods.

The latest ten-minute block continuation finished: numerical checks passed,
but convergence and the registered improvement target failed. A new exact
weight-only audit then ruled out one specific explanation for the stall:
the block's writer/core factors were already balanced almost optimally.
Neither result demonstrates absence of circuits.

## The mathematical object

Let $x$ be the normalized input to the last bilinear layer. Its quadratic
contribution, after applying the entire unembedding $U$, is

$$
q_v(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{4608}(UD)_{vj}(l_j^\top x)(r_j^\top x)
       =x^\top T_vx,
\qquad
T_v=\sum_j(UD)_{vj}\operatorname{sym}(l_jr_j^\top).
$$

Here $v$ indexes an output token, $D$ writes products into the residual stream,
and $\operatorname{sym}(A)=(A+A^\top)/2$. All token matrices $T_v$ are fitted
jointly, so a factor can be reused across tokens. There is no need to first fit
an independent decomposition for each token and then match factors afterward.

The full tensor has $50304\times1152\times1152$ entries, about 267 GB in float32.
We use its existing product representation and implicit contractions instead
of materializing it. The folded quadratic contribution is not the complete
final logit: residual addition, biases, final RMS normalization and the output
$30\tanh(\cdot/30)$ remain explicit background operations.

## What has actually been used

| Structural assumption | Computation and optimizer used | Current limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Shared simple products | Fit $q_v(x)\approx\sum_k a_{vk}(p_k^\top x)(s_k^\top x)$. Solve output writers exactly conditional on readers; optimize readers with Adam/L-BFGS, then test alternating least squares and joint Gauss–Newton repairs. | One penalized 128-product fit locally converged; ALS and Gauss–Newton continuations did not. Penalty, capacity and initialization coverage remain incomplete. |
| Signed squares | Fit sums of $a_{vk}(p_k^\top x)^2$, with signed coefficients. L-BFGS plus normalization/stationarity repair. | The 256-square fit locally converged. Products and signed squares are related representations, not wholly independent hypotheses. |
| Sparse interaction graph | Learn a shared orthonormal input frame and retain a sparse set of pairwise products. Use constrained conjugate-gradient optimization and support updates. | Two starts converged to different fitted functions. A single orthogonal coordinate frame may be too restrictive. |
| Overlapping multi-output blocks | Each block reads a small input subspace and contains several dense quadratic functions with distinct output writers. Exact conditional writer solves; raw L-BFGS, custom and library manifold conjugate gradient, then exact-Hessian trust regions. | The current 16-block, 16-reader, 4-output model remains unconverged. Orthogonality within a dense block fixes coordinates; blocks may overlap. Fixed block sizes and output count are substantive restrictions. |

## Assumptions that deserve scrutiny

**Fixed small output rank can impose a ceiling before optimization starts.**
The current blocks have at most 64 output directions. An existing exact
relaxation says even arbitrary quadratic functions with that output rank can
capture at most 23.84% of full-U coefficient energy. Our block fit captures
8.63%, so this ceiling does not explain the whole shortfall, but it prevents
that configuration from achieving near-complete reconstruction at any optimum.
It is not a bound on other arithmetic programs or on behavioral usefulness.
[Spectrum receipt](../../FULLU_OUTPUT_FUNCTIONS_V1_AUDIT.json).

**Coefficient error is a chosen weights-only metric.** It measures agreement
between quadratic coefficients over all output rows, not agreement on the
distribution of model states. Large common-output components can dominate it.
Full and centered fits answer different questions; subtracting the common
component is a diagnostic, not automatically a legal final-logit simplification.
This motivates reporting several weight-space views before introducing data.

**Sparsity and independence are hypotheses.** Tokens may share overlapping
functions rather than belong to disjoint clusters. Functions may share some
readers but differ in other readers and writers. Dense block cores allow more
internal interactions than one-product factors, while independent-block tests
assume stronger separability. A failure in one family should redirect these
assumptions, not close the whole search.

**Antipodal symmetry does not force semantic clusters.** The homogeneous
quadratic branch satisfies $q(-x)=q(x)$. That symmetry allows signed-square
representations but does not guarantee a small number of factors, positive
coefficients, or a unique grouping of tokens.

**Regularization is not the same as counting computations.** Our component
energy penalty discourages large terms, but splitting one term into multiple
identical smaller terms can reduce it. Comparisons across capacities must keep
that bias and literal program cost visible.

## Latest optimization result and the new penalty audit

The faster trust-region implementation made 388 outer iterations and 18,698
Hessian-vector evaluations in 600.49 fit seconds. Capture increased from
8.62875% to 8.63438%. Its gradient norm was $1.23\times10^{-6}$ against
$10^{-7}$; scaled stationarity was $2.16\times10^{-4}$ against $10^{-4}$.
The loss plateau test passed, but the gradient tests did not. Therefore it is
still unconverged. Conditional writer solves had condition number 15.03,
so that small linear solve is not evidently ill-conditioned.
[Native receipt](../../BLOCK_TRUST_REGION_THIN_V1_RESULT.json).

We previously removed an unnecessarily large intermediate from the constrained
Hessian calculation. The measured 14.7–44.3× improvement applies to that host
kernel, not the complete fit. The remaining 18,698 curvature evaluations show
why an apparently small number of outer iterations can still be expensive.

For the new CPU audit, write one block as $A=FC^\top$, where $F=UW$ contains
four output writers and $C$ contains its four quadratic cores in an orthonormal
coefficient basis. The penalty is

$$
P(F,C)=\sum_{j=1}^{4}\|f_j\|^2\|c_j\|^2,
\qquad
\min_{FC^\top=A}P(F,C)=\frac{\|A\|_*^2}{4}.
$$

The nuclear norm $\|A\|_*$ is the sum of singular values. The lower bound
follows from the triangle inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz. A singular-value
factorization followed by a normalized four-by-four Hadamard rotation attains
it: that rotation gives every factor column the same norm. Thin QR and a
four-by-four SVD perform this calculation without constructing the large $A$.
This is a direct derivation for our fixed four-output block. Related
matrix-factorization literature studies this same column-energy penalty and
its dependence on factor count.
[Cavazza et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.05092),
[factor-count pathology](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.03487).

The native audit preserved every block function within $2.03\times10^{-15}$
and attained the bound within $1.55\times10^{-15}$. But it removed only
$7.36\times10^{-10}$ of the penalty, far below the preregistered 1% threshold.
The blocks were already effectively balanced. This rules out that particular
cheap repair; it does not establish full convergence or identifiable factors.
The audit used no text, no GPU and no checkpoint mutation, and took 1.11 seconds.
[Audit](../../BLOCK_WRITER_CORE_GAUGE_V1_AUDIT.json).

## Where stronger methods would change the search

The most useful expansion is adaptive, overlapping structure with matched
costs, rather than repeatedly extending the same fixed-size fit. Hierarchical
iteratively reweighted least squares for block-term model selection is a
concrete literature candidate: it jointly shrinks groups and columns to select
block counts and ranks. Its published model must be mapped to our symmetric,
multi-output cores; its convergence and recovery claims cannot simply be
transferred to this problem.
[Rontogiannis, Kofidis and Giampouras](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09759).

Other unresolved comparisons include overlapping token hierarchies, mixtures
of simple products and richer blocks, and independent solver/restart coverage
at matched parameter budgets. The immediate penalty audit has been executed;
these broader extensions are candidates, not completed experiments or queued
jobs. No additional identical block chunk or data-guided fit was submitted.
The four behavioral properties still require frozen-candidate validation.
