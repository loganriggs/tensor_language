# Converged small-block fits, and a compact full-rank alternative

Both native runs of the new small-block representation converged locally, but
they captured only about **5.94% of coefficient energy**, below the earlier
8.63% block fit. This is a completed test of a different capacity allocation,
not another unfinished run or evidence that structure is absent.

The mathematical follow-up identifies a shared restriction in these small
models: they read only a small subspace of the input. We derived an18.76%
ceiling for the new representation and then implemented a compact alternative
that permits full input and output rank. It uses sequences of small linear
mixing operations, often called butterfly-style transforms.

That alternative passes exact execution controls. On a small planted tensor,
two fitting starts recovered it almost exactly; a third settled at a small
nonzero error. An exact-curvature audit did not find a useful saddle-escape
direction at that point. **This alternative has not been fitted to the native
model yet.** No text was used in any of these discovery experiments.

## What the native experiment tested

Each of23blocks reads four linear combinations of the1152-dimensional input.
It exposes all ten quadratic interactions among those four values: four
squares and six distinct cross-products. Each interaction has its own output
writer. Input subspaces may overlap across blocks.

The model uses370944learned coefficients, slightly fewer than377344in the
previous16-block/16-reader/4-output model. Its block-energy penalty differs
from the previous factor-energy penalty, so penalized objectives are not
directly comparable. Raw coefficient reconstruction is reported separately.
Both models retain the native unembedding, bias, normalization and background.

Output writers were solved exactly conditional on the input subspaces. The
subspaces were optimized using standard Grassmann trust regions with exact
Hessian-vector products. Here a Grassmann point denotes a subspace: rotating
the coordinates inside it does not change the available quadratic functions.
Two independent starts sampled native product readers using only weight energy.

## Why the input-space restriction matters

Write the full folded quadratic family as token-indexed symmetric matrices
$T_v$, and let

$$
S=\sum_v T_v^2.
$$

The23four-reader blocks collectively use at most92input directions. For their
joint input projector P, every fitted quadratic lies inside that subspace on
both input axes. Therefore its best possible captured coefficient energy obeys

$$
\sum_v\|PT_vP\|_F^2
\le\operatorname{tr}(PS)
\le\sum_{i=1}^{92}\lambda_i(S),
$$

where the eigenvalues are ordered largest first. Dividing by native coefficient
energy gives **18.7601% full** and **17.3403% centered**. This reuses an existing
saved spectrum; it did not require another GPU capture. Numerical eigen-residual
checks passed, but this is not an interval-arithmetic certificate.

The ceiling still permits improvement beyond5.94%; it does not explain away
the gap as an achieved optimum. It does show that this family cannot reconstruct
most weights. In the same unrestricted inside-subspace relaxation, at least352
input directions are necessary for50%full coefficient capture. These bounds
do not apply to behavioral usefulness, distribution-weighted errors, or every
compact arithmetic program. [Bound audit](../../FULL_QUADRATIC_INPUT_CEILING_V1_AUDIT.json).

## A different assumption: simple full-rank linear computations

A matrix can have full rank while being cheap to describe and apply. Instead
of learning every entry independently, learn a sequence of stages, each mixing
small groups of coordinates. The outputs of one stage are reused by the next.
Butterfly factorization provides an established learnable parameterization of
fast linear transforms; its success on structured transforms is not a theorem
that this model's weights have that structure.
[Dao et al., Learning Fast Algorithms for Linear Transforms](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/dao19a.html).

Our explicit mixed-radix construction uses the factorization
$1152=2^7\cdot3^2$: seven stages of two-coordinate mixing and two stages of
three-coordinate mixing. Each stage has independently learned small matrices
on a fixed wiring pattern. One full1152-by1152map then needs

$$
1152(7\cdot2+2\cdot3)=23040
$$

coefficients. Three such maps define a bilinear branch

$$
q_b(x)=B_b\big[(A_bx)\odot(C_bx)\big].
$$

Four branches retain4608products, matching the native product count, but use
276480coefficients for their linear maps instead of15925248independent dense
matrix entries. This counts a proposed bilinear component, not the whole
model; native unembedding, biases and background remain charged. Fixed wiring
and execution code are explicit. The construction allows full mode ranks,
so the92-reader ceiling does not apply.

The new assumption is strong and different: the relevant linear maps must
admit short compositions of small mixing operations. Coordinate ordering,
wiring flexibility, initialization and conditioning may all matter. Full rank
removes one restriction; it does not guarantee a good native approximation.

## What the construction and fitting controls actually established

A24-dimensional planted construction used648coefficients instead of1728dense
factor coefficients. All three tensor unfoldings had full rank24. Its direct
execution matched the explicitly constructed quadratic tensor within
$4.44\times10^{-16}$, and parameter gradients matched within
$5.98\times10^{-16}$. This demonstrates compact full-rank representability,
not learned native structure. [Construction control](../../MIXED_RADIX_BILINEAR_V1_CONTROL.json).

We then fitted a separate eight-dimensional planted tensor using SciPy's
trust-region nonlinear least-squares solver and exact automatic Jacobians.
The target and three initializations used the same known wiring with random
near-identity stages. Initial relative errors were0.77–0.82; this is a restricted
local recovery test, not arbitrary-start or unknown-wiring recovery.

Two starts reached relative Frobenius errors below $9\times10^{-9}$. The third
stopped at1.1313%relative error with a small gradient, so the all-start recovery
prediction failed. Its unexplained *squared* coefficient-energy fraction is
only0.0128%, but it is still a nonzero stationary solution despite a known exact
solution. [Planted fit](../../MIXED_RADIX_PLANTED_V1_RESULT.json).

The failed point was replayed exactly and saved. Its exact Hessian matched
finite differences within $5.51\times10^{-11}$. The smallest eigenvalue was
$-1.20\times10^{-9}$ against a largest magnitude2.12; this did not meet the
registered negative-curvature threshold. Tested steps along that direction
did not improve the objective. Thus an obvious second-order escape was not
supported; this does not prove a strict local minimum or global impossibility.
[Curvature audit](../../MIXED_RADIX_CURVATURE_V1_AUDIT.json).

The next research decision is how to initialize and fit these structured
full-rank maps on native weights, with wiring and restart controls. The current
CPU construction and failed-start audit are implemented and executed. No native
structured-transform result or new behavioral circuit is claimed. The four
properties—OOD prediction, extraction, selective removal, and composition/reuse—
remain open for these candidates.
