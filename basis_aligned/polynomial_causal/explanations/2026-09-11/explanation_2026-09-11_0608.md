The mathematical follow-up identifies a shared restriction in these small

model yet.** No text was used in any of these discovery experiments.

## Why the input-space restriction matters

Write the full folded quadratic family as token-indexed symmetric matrices
$T_v$, and let

$$
S=\sum_v T_v^2.
$$

The23four-reader blocks collectively use at most92input directions. For their
joint input projector P, every fitted quadratic lies inside that subspace on
both input axes. Therefore its best possible captured coefficient energy obeys

$$
\sum_v\|PT_vP\|_F^2
\le\operatorname{tr}(PS)
\le\sum_{i=1}^{92}\lambda_i(S),
$$

where the eigenvalues are ordered largest first. Dividing by native coefficient
energy gives **18.7601% full** and **17.3403% centered**. This reuses an existing
saved spectrum; it did not require another GPU capture. Numerical eigen-residual
checks passed, but this is not an interval-arithmetic certificate.

The ceiling still permits improvement beyond5.94%; it does not explain away
the gap as an achieved optimum. It does show that this family cannot reconstruct
most weights. In the same unrestricted inside-subspace relaxation, at least352
input directions are necessary for50%full coefficient capture. These bounds
do not apply to behavioral usefulness, distribution-weighted errors, or every
compact arithmetic program. [Bound audit](../../FULL_QUADRATIC_INPUT_CEILING_V1_AUDIT.json).

## A different assumption: simple full-rank linear computations

A matrix can have full rank while being cheap to describe and apply. Instead
of learning every entry independently, learn a sequence of stages, each mixing
small groups of coordinates. The outputs of one stage are reused by the next.
Butterfly factorization provides an established learnable parameterization of
fast linear transforms; its success on structured transforms is not a theorem
that this model's weights have that structure.
[Dao et al., Learning Fast Algorithms for Linear Transforms](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/dao19a.html).

Our explicit mixed-radix construction uses the factorization
$1152=2^7\cdot3^2$: seven stages of two-coordinate mixing and two stages of
three-coordinate mixing. Each stage has independently learned small matrices
on a fixed wiring pattern. One full1152-by1152map then needs

$$
1152(7\cdot2+2\cdot3)=23040
$$

coefficients. Three such maps define a bilinear branch

$$
q_b(x)=B_b\big[(A_bx)\odot(C_bx)\big].
$$

Four branches retain4608products, matching the native product count, but use
276480coefficients for their linear maps instead of15925248independent dense
matrix entries. This counts a proposed bilinear component, not the whole
model; native unembedding, biases and background remain charged. Fixed wiring
and execution code are explicit. The construction allows full mode ranks,
so the92-reader ceiling does not apply.

The new assumption is strong and different: the relevant linear maps must
admit short compositions of small mixing operations. Coordinate ordering,
wiring flexibility, initialization and conditioning may all matter. Full rank
removes one restriction; it does not guarantee a good native approximation.

A24-dimensional planted construction used648coefficients instead of1728dense

## Weights-first methods, assumptions, and bottlenecks — 06:42 UTC

The discovery priority is weights first. FineWeb is the training-corpus validation source; Pile is a separately labelled OOD check. A million-token panel can test frozen candidates, but does not substitute for testing different structural assumptions. No new data-guided discovery is queued by this update.

The object is the entire family of token interaction matrices:

$$
T_v=\sum_{j=1}^{4608}(UD)_{vj}\,\operatorname{sym}(l_jr_j^\top),
\qquad q_v(x)=x^\top T_vx.
$$

Here $U$ is the unembedding, $D$ is the last bilinear layer's output map, and $l_j,r_j$ are its two input readers. Symmetrization means $(A+A^\top)/2$: only that part contributes when both inputs are the same $x$. We fit the matrices jointly to discover shared computations, rather than factor each token independently and hope their bases align. This quadratic object excludes the residual, bias and final normalization/nonlinearity; those must remain explicit in later model execution.

The [current method index](../../WEIGHT_ONLY_METHODS_INDEX.md) separates completed fits from open families. The main implemented methods are:

| Structural assumption | Actual fitting method | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Shared two-reader products | Joint L-BFGS, alternating least squares, and damped Gauss–Newton; component-energy regularization | Nonconvex, cancellation and scaling freedom; ALS and GN attempts remain unconverged |
| Shared signed squares | Joint optimization followed by scale-aware polishing | Closely related algebraically to products; not a wholly independent hypothesis |
| Sparse interactions in a shared input frame | Sparse edge selection and Riemannian conjugate gradient | Orthogonality and fixed support can exclude useful structures |
| Overlapping multi-output blocks | Exact conditional writer solves, manifold CG, exact-Hessian trust regions; conditional SVD updates | Small input spans/output ranks and slow joint convergence |
| Complete quadratic functions in small overlapping frames | Exact writer solves and Grassmann trust regions | Both starts converge, but the union of input spans limits total possible coverage |
| Sparse overlapping token-function dictionary | Sparse coding/dictionary updates and fixed-support debiasing | Simple token usage can conceal dense, complicated input functions |
| Shared invariant blocks or output modes | Congruence spectral analysis, output SVD, and rotations | Spectral solvability does not imply sparse, reusable arithmetic |
| Full-rank structured transforms around products | Mixed-radix small matrix stages; Adam initialization followed by exact-objective L-BFGS | Fixed wiring, depth, initialization and stage scaling remain restrictive |

These are established numerical approaches, not a claim that our implementation is state of the art. Tensor Toolbox documents [joint CP optimization](https://www.tensortoolbox.org/cp_opt_doc.html), while Pymanopt provides [Riemannian trust regions](https://pymanopt.org/docs/latest/optimizers.html). Our latest family is motivated by [butterfly factorization](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/dao19a.html); its success on structured transforms does not guarantee the trained model has that particular wiring.

Three assumptions deserve particular scrutiny. First, **low rank is not the same as a short computation**. The 23 four-reader frames are confined to at most 92 input directions; their certified coefficient-capture ceiling is 18.76%. A compact sequence of full-rank sparse maps avoids that restriction. Second, **a preferred basis is an assumption**: orthogonal readers, native neuron ordering, fixed multiplication graphs and fixed butterfly wiring may all hide the desired organization. Third, **the loss defines what matters**. Full coefficient Frobenius error uses no corpus, but is not neutral about scale or equivalent to natural-state prediction error. Centering output rows or adding penalties changes the question; numbers from those objectives must not be pooled into a leaderboard.

Antipodal symmetry alone does not guarantee a sparse factorization: every homogeneous quadratic function satisfies $q(-x)=q(x)$. Signed squares already respect that symmetry. Useful additional structure would have to concern shared directions, sparse interactions, blocks, hierarchy, or reusable composition.

The computational bottleneck is joint fitting, not needing more text. The nominal tensor has 50,304 token slices of size 1,152 by 1,152. We avoid materializing it: the existing 4,608-product representation permits exact contractions, and $U^\top U$ preserves the full unembedding metric without storing every vocabulary-sized intermediate. Nevertheless, pairwise product contractions and repeated line searches are expensive; the newest first chunk used 267 objective/gradient evaluations in 302.5 seconds. More steps are feasible, but time limits are not convergence.

The first native structured start captured **1.4968%**, stopped at its time limit, and has relative stationarity **0.261**, versus the required $10^{-4}$. Its last recorded capture increment was positive. The second start is live at this update. This is an optimization problem still open, not a failed structural hypothesis. See the [first-start receipt](../../STRUCTURED_BILINEAR_NATIVE_V2_SEED_0.json) and [progress audit](../../STRUCTURED_BILINEAR_NATIVE_V2_SEED0_PROGRESS_AUDIT.json).

Weight-only avenues still open include adaptive/heterogeneous blocks, richer overlapping hierarchies, learned or varied structured wiring, and tensor-train/hierarchical representations with justified index splits. Their usefulness depends on the precise assumed sharing; reshaping a tensor alone is not a discovery. We have not exhausted those avenues. Once candidates are stable, frozen FineWeb validation should test predictions and interventions; data-weighted discovery is a later, explicitly separate stage.
