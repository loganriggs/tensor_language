## The new combined structural assumption

For normalized MLP17 input $x$, approximate each centered token quadratic by

$$
Q_v\approx\sum_{j=1}^{512}A_{vj}H_j,
\qquad x^\top H_jx=(p_j^\top x)(q_j^\top x).
$$

The token coefficients $A_{vj}$ are signed and encouraged to be sparse. A token
can use several products; the same product can serve many tokens. Each product
has two explicit 1,152-dimensional readers. The common output function remains
separate. Normalization, tanh, residual background and bias are not absorbed into
this homogeneous quadratic approximation.

Unlike the previous 512-function dictionary, these functions are allowed outside
the native 1,152-dimensional output-function span. Requiring simple individual
products while also forcing them into that span could exclude useful decompositions:
products outside it can combine to yield a function inside it.

We minimize coefficient reconstruction error plus an absolute-value penalty:

$$
J=\frac{1}{2V}\sum_v\left\|Q_v-\sum_j A_{vj}H_j\right\|_F^2
+\frac{\lambda}{V}\sum_{v,j}|A_{vj}|,
\qquad \|H_j\|_F=1.
$$

The unit norm removes the arbitrary exchange of scale between a function and
its token coefficients. It does not require orthogonal functions or disjoint
input readers.

## Why this optimizer is different

[Low-rank-atom dictionary learning](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~fessler/papers/lists/files/proc/17/web/ravishankar-16-elo.pdf)
provides a close algorithmic template: alternate sparse coefficients and
structured matrix functions with exact conditional updates. Its original image
model and convergence assumptions are not identical to ours.

For a fixed function dictionary, the coefficient problem is convex; accelerated
soft-thresholding steps solve it. For one function with the codes and other
functions fixed, form the residual correlation

$$
R_j=\sum_v A_{vj}Q_v-\sum_{k\ne j}(A^\top A)_{jk}H_k.
$$

The exact best update maximizes $\langle R_j,H_j\rangle$. A symmetric real
product has at most one positive and one negative eigenvalue. We therefore
retain the strongest eigenvalue of each sign from $R_j$ and normalize their
joint coefficient norm. The result is the global optimum of this **one-function
conditional problem**, including a single signed square when only one sign is
present. It avoids a nonlinear reader solve for that update.

Sequential updates cannot increase the exact objective. They still do not imply
global convergence of the joint dictionary problem. We require two consecutive
checks that the codes are stationary and that the sum of all remaining exact
single-function improvements is at most $10^{-8}$ of the normalized target energy.
A time-limited chunk is explicitly labelled unfinished.

Dense CPU controls match the implicit objective and predicted conditional
improvement within $3.2\times10^{-16}$. Known indefinite and semidefinite
fixtures recover the exact spectral optimum.

## The completed output-interface audit

Let $U_c$ be the unembedding with its vocabulary mean removed. If a centered
token writer comes from a residual-space vector $w$, it has the form $U_cw$.
Arbitrary sparse token coefficients need not have that form.

For the previous debiased dictionary $\widehat F=AB$, where $B$ holds exact
quadratic-function coordinates, compute the orthogonal output projection

$$
P_c=U_c(U_c^\top U_c)^{-1}U_c^\top,
\qquad \widehat F=P_c\widehat F+(I-P_c)\widehat F.
$$

The second term accounts for **40.22% of the approximation's own centered
coefficient energy**. Projecting it away improves centered target capture from
20.72% to 29.05%, because the true centered target lies in this output space.
The coefficient error decomposes exactly:

$$
\|F-\widehat F\|_F^2
=\|F-P_c\widehat F\|_F^2+\|(I-P_c)\widehat F\|_F^2.
$$

This uses the complete function Gram matrix, including overlap between functions;
it is not a count or norm of sparse coefficients alone. Numerical orthogonality
and energy closure errors are below $1.4\times10^{-14}$.

The full, uncentered interface requires a separate check. Add back the exact
common function and project onto the column space of the original $U$. The
outside component is **29.63% of that full approximation's own energy**. Thus
centering alone does not explain the incompatibility.
[Audit receipt](../../SPARSE_WRITER_INTERFACE_V1_AUDIT.json).

Projection generally makes the token writers dense, and projecting the full
composition can also change the retained common function. Therefore it repairs
one algebraic interface at the expense of the literal sparse-token representation;
it is not an automatic adoption. A residual implementation additionally changes
the final normalization, which needs physical validation.

## What this changes

The product experiment tests whether sparse overlapping token groups can acquire simple
input computations. It does not yet enforce native-unembedding-compatible
writers. That restriction must be measured or built into a later version,
for example by parameterizing each writer as $Uw$ and encouraging concentrated
token effects rather than demanding many exact zeros.

This is a problem with one structural assumption, not evidence against shared
circuits. Recognizable token groups, simple products and legal model interfaces
must be established together before OOD prediction, extraction, selective removal
and composition can be credited. The next validation corpus remains FineWeb;
Pile remains a separately labelled shift check after discovery is frozen.

## Next executed step: distinguish the products from their writers

The managed writer audit started at 01:39:06. It freezes the returned products,
then refits coefficients with the exact same sparse links and with unrestricted
links. The first tests shrinkage; the second additionally tests support restriction.
For product Gram $G$ and target/function cross matrix $C$, unrestricted least
squares has the exact solution

$$
A_{\mathrm{LS}}=CG^{-1}.
$$

Here $C$ already factors through the native unembedding, so the unpenalized
optimum automatically respects its output space. This is a consequence of the
fixed-function linear problem, not a solution to the nonlinear reader search.

We also implemented a convex writer update that enforces the native interface
while encouraging concentrated centered token effects. Whiten the full unembedding
as $U=PR^\top$, where $P^\top P=I$, and write the executable token coefficients
as $UW=PZ$ with $Z=R^\top W$. Let $F=P-\mathbf1\overline P$ remove the vocabulary
mean. For fixed input functions, minimize

$$
\frac12\operatorname{tr}(ZGZ^\top)-\langle Z,C\rangle
+\lambda\|FZ\|_1.
$$

This fits the full uncentered quadratic target. The mean output participates in
reconstruction but is not penalized for token concentration. It is therefore a
different objective from the previous centered fit plus an exactly retained
common function; their capture numbers must keep their respective denominators.

The [alternating direction method of multipliers](https://stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/admm_distr_stats.pdf)
introduces an auxiliary matrix $A=FZ$. Updating $A$ uses soft thresholding;
updating $Z$ solves a linear matrix equation. Its left coefficient has a useful
rank-one form:

$$
F^\top F=I-Vm m^\top,\qquad m=\overline P^\top,
$$

$$
ZG+\rho(I-Vm m^\top)Z=C+\rho F^\top(A-Y),
$$

where $Y$ is the scaled constraint multiplier and $\rho$ controls the numerical
constraint penalty. This equation can be solved using the eigenvectors of $G$
and a rank-one correction. It is not a new nonlinear reader optimization.

The CPU implementation matches an independent constrained quadratic program
within $9.6\times10^{-12}$ in objective, and its linear-equation residual is
$2.8\times10^{-16}$. Native convergence requires both the writer optimality
conditions and the auxiliary constraint to hold; zeros in an unconverged
auxiliary matrix are not zeros in the actual executable writer.
