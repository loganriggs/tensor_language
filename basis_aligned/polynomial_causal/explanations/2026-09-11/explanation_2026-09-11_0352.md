# Can a few input features participate in most of the computation?

Allowing selected input features to interact with **all remaining directions**
finds substantially more structure than keeping only interactions inside their
subspace. At 128 readers, the extracted family captures38.56%of token-contrast
coefficient energy, versus6.41%for the inside-only part.

However, a very small global reader family cannot explain most of this tensor.
At11readers we obtain6.09%capture, and **any**11-reader family of this kind is
bounded above by6.80%. Better nonlinear optimization cannot close that gap to a
majority reconstruction. This is a restriction on one representation and metric,
not a claim that circuits or useful weight structure are absent.

## The representation

For token $v$, the quadratic contribution is $f_v(x)=x^\top Q_vx$, with symmetric
$Q_v$. Let the columns of $E\in\mathbb R^{1152\times r}$ be orthonormal shared
readers, and write

$$
P=EE^\top,\qquad C=I-P.
$$

The extracted family and its exact remainder are

$$
Q_v^*=Q_v-CQ_vC=PQ_v+Q_vP-PQ_vP,
\qquad R_v=CQ_vC.
$$

This keeps the inside interactions $PQ_vP$ **and** both mixed blocks $PQ_vC$
and $CQ_vP$. Only interactions entirely outside the selected space remain in
$R_v$. It has an exact local removal interpretation:

$$
x^\top Q_v^*x=f_v(x)-f_v(Cx).
$$

Here $x$ is already normalized. There is no renormalization of $Cx$, and this
identity is for the quadratic contribution, not the final normalized logits.

Using orthonormal columns does not restrict which reader spans we can represent.
Any independent, non-orthogonal reader family can be orthonormalized while
changing its partners. The restriction is the dimension of the shared span.
This family includes sums of products where at least one reader in every product
belongs to that span. It does not require both readers to belong to it.

## An achievable score and an optimizer-independent ceiling

Define the coefficient energy and source influence matrix

$$
T=\sum_v\|Q_v\|_F^2,\qquad S=\sum_v Q_v^2.
$$

The extraction is the orthogonal projection onto all symmetric forms with zero
outside/outside block. Consequently it is the best Frobenius approximation for
this fixed reader space, and

$$
F(P)=\sum_v\|Q_v^*\|_F^2
=2\operatorname{tr}(PS)-\sum_v\|E^\top Q_vE\|_F^2.
$$

Write $A=\sum_v\|E^\top Q_vE\|_F^2$ and
$B=\sum_v\|PQ_vC\|_F^2$. Then

$$
\operatorname{tr}(PS)=A+B,\qquad F(P)=A+2B.
$$

Thus $\operatorname{tr}(PS)\le F(P)\le2\operatorname{tr}(PS)$.
The maximum trace over rank-$r$ orthogonal projectors is the sum of the largest
$r$ eigenvalues of $S$, by the Ky Fan variational principle.
[Mathematical reference, Section 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8589322/).
Our tensor-specific consequence is

$$
F(P)\le U_r
=\min\left(T,\;2\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i(S)\right).
$$

Taking the leading eigenvectors provides an explicit candidate $P_{\rm spec}$
with $F(P_{\rm spec})\ge U_r/2$. This is a guaranteed factor-two approximation
for this coefficient objective. The eigendecomposition solves the trace
surrogate exactly; it does not generally maximize $F$ itself. The native result above measures the actual gap between this candidate and its ceiling.

## Cost and interpretation

An explicit implementation can sum $r$ shared-reader terms, each multiplying a
scalar read by a dense linear residual-space partner. That stores

$$
r(1152+1152^2)
$$

numbers before the retained unembedding/background. With these particular dense
partners, $r=11$ is the largest integer below the native quadratic MLP weight
count $3\cdot1152\cdot4608$. This is a price for one implementation. Structured
partners could be cheaper, so it is not a lower bound on every possible program.

We registered ranks 1, 4, 8, 11, 16, 32, 64 and 128 under both full-unembedding
and vocabulary-centered metrics. The resulting ceiling can rule out a specified
coefficient-capture target for **any** reader space of that size. It cannot rule
out many local groups, sparse graphs with many readers, different input metrics,
simple upstream producers, or meaningful behavior in a low-energy component.

The CPU controls match the projection, gradient, input-removal identity and
within-subspace basis changes to better than $5\times10^{-16}$. A planted family
containing only mixed interactions is recovered completely, while its inside-only
projection captures zero. This checks that the broader assumption was actually
implemented.

## Next executed consequence: compare the computations behind the readers

Raw read vectors can be orthogonal even when an upstream module writes the same
function into both directions. Conversely, nearby read vectors need not isolate
a distinct shared computation. The next tool compares the actual quadratic
functions after folding a reader through an earlier bilinear layer.

If its native product features are

$$
p_j(x)=(l_j^\top x)(r_j^\top x),
\qquad g(x)=D p(x),
$$

their exact coefficient Gram matrix is

$$
K_{ij}=\frac12\left[(l_i^\top l_j)(r_i^\top r_j)
+(l_i^\top r_j)(r_i^\top l_j)\right].
$$

For reader families$A$and$B$, their folded functions have cross-Gram matrix

$$
G_{AB}=A D K D^\top B^\top.
$$

Whitening each family's own Gram matrix on its supported span, then taking the
singular values of the whitened cross-Gram, compares the two **function spaces**.
A singular value of1means a shared normalized polynomial function exists in the
two spans;0means orthogonality in coefficient geometry. This comparison is
invariant to invertible changes of coordinates inside either reader family,
provided numerical rank is resolved. It is not a statistical independence test.

The implemented CPU control plants two orthogonal read vectors that read the
same producer function. Their raw overlap is0and their folded-function overlap
is1; an independent-function control remains0. Direct polynomial replay, dense
Gram and reader-coordinate controls pass, with maximum error
$7.6\times10^{-15}$. Zero-function spans are handled explicitly.
