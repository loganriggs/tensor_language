# A simpler baseline wins on coefficients; the learned dictionary wins on cached text

11 September 2026. This is a small frozen validation result, not fresh/OOD confirmation.

**The weights-first dictionary has a functional advantage on the checked FineWeb positions, even though it reconstructs the weight tensor less accurately than a simpler baseline.** That is a reason to continue investigating the representation. It does not yet make its individual features identified circuits.

The new control keeps 2,645 native bilinear products, chosen by their full-unembedding component energy, and optimizes their output weights exactly. It uses **9,141,120 matrix coefficients**, slightly fewer than the learned dictionary's **9,142,272**, and needs none of the dictionary's 1,179,648 sparse connection indices. The native baseline is therefore a conservative comparison at the same parameter budget.

The baseline captures **68.63%** of the folded tensor's coefficient energy, versus **65.00%** for each completed dictionary after its connection sweep. The dictionary has not demonstrated a coefficient-reconstruction advantage at this price. A random native selection captures59.39%, so the energy-based selection is doing useful work.

We then froze all three programs and checked two fixed positions in each of64 already cached FineWeb sequences: positions63 and127, for128 predictions total. No factors were fitted to these examples. The cached states let us replace the last bilinear layer and evaluate the actual residual addition, final RMS normalization, unembedding and tanh without running the model body again.

## Why the rankings can disagree

For a token's quadratic interaction matrix \(T_v\), the weight-only objective measures

$$
\sum_v\|T_v-\widehat T_v\|_F^2.
$$

Natural-input error instead depends on the inputs actually reaching the layer:

$$
\mathbb{E}_{x}\sum_v\left[x^\top(T_v-\widehat T_v)x\right]^2.
$$

These are different quantities. The latter depends on fourth-order input statistics; final RMS normalization and tanh further change how branch errors affect predictions. Our results show that the dictionary's remaining coefficient error matters much less on this panel than the native selection's error. They do not yet establish which particular shared features cause that advantage.

This follows the intended workflow: discover from weights, freeze the programs, then validate on data. The validation did not change the factors or incorporate text into their optimization.

## What remains uncertain

The numerical control passed: recomputing the native layer before the cached downstream computation gave relative logit discrepancy4.39e-7 and mean cross-entropy discrepancy2.04e-7. The prediction that native selection would also win behaviorally failed. The registered preservation prediction for **all three programs** failed because native selection missed its thresholds; both dictionaries individually meet the specified0.05-nat mean-change and0.05-KL bars.

These are128 positions from previously opened sequences. They are not a whole-corpus estimate, an untouched validation set or OOD evidence. Mean absolute per-position cross-entropy changes are still about0.133nats for both dictionaries, so their small average damage does not establish selective preservation at individual positions. Both parent fits remain unconverged, and their complete weight-space functions differ substantially. The ongoing joint penalized fit tests whether optimization and factor stability improve; no identical continuation has been automatically queued.
