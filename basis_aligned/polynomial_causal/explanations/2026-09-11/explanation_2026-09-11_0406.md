# Folding through MLP16 reveals a selection tradeoff

Selecting features jointly across layers improves their shared-computation score
substantially, but sacrifices how much attention routing they cover. We now have
a measured tradeoff to optimize, rather than assuming a decomposition found at
one interface is automatically useful at the next.

Since the [03:52 update](explanation_2026-09-11_0352.md), we completed a native
weight-only producer fold, red-teamed its weak result with an exact optimal
feature-selection calculation, and measured the new features' routing coverage.
The next joint objective is implemented and CPU-controlled; its native fit has
not run. **No text or activation fitting was used.**

The starting features were two different17-dimensional spaces per attention17
head: source features selected from the joint QK1×QK2 product, and value-source
features obtained by folding the compact unembedding/MLP17 component through
the head's output and current-value maps. We asked whether MLP16 produces shared
polynomial functions for those two sets of reads.

## What happened

The initial native calculation took2.00seconds on the managed GPU runner. Its
numerical checks passed, but both strong-sharing predictions failed:

| Comparison | Mean function-space overlap |
|---|---:|
| Original reader spaces, before the producer fold | 4.75% |
| Same readers folded through MLP16 | 5.81% |
| Rotated controls folded through MLP16 | 5.33% |
| Best of16rotated controls | 5.43% |
| Mismatched head pairings through MLP16 | 3.31% |

Overlap means the mean squared principal cosine between normalized polynomial
function spaces, defined below. It is not the percentage of behavior explained.
The observed orientation beats all16controls, but its excess over their mean is
only0.475percentage points, missing the registered5-percentage-point bar. No head
has a function cosine above0.95; the largest per-head values range0.394–0.696.
We preserve those failed predictions. There is a small descriptive enrichment,
not evidence of a nearly identical shared computation.

The controls apply the same signed permutation of residual coordinates to both
reader families. This preserves their raw geometric relationship while changing
their orientation relative to the producer. The producer's coefficient metric
also does not collapse into one dominant direction: its leading mode carries
0.65%of energy and848modes are needed for90%. Removing its radial trace component
barely changes the overlap, from5.808%to5.792%. These are weight-coefficient
statistics; the MLP16 dossier's natural-state and behavioral results use different
metrics and are not contradicted.

## Red-team: the original selection objective was restrictive

The frozen QK features were chosen for routing influence, not producer/value
sharing. To test that methodological explanation, we allowed **any** source
reader in each head's full joint key-weight row space, of dimension256. A singular
value decomposition gives the exact best alignment with the17fixed value-side
functions. It also supplies17key readers attaining that alignment; iterative
optimization and restart choices are unnecessary for this objective.

The CPU calculation took0.69seconds. Its mean overlap rose to **33.63%**, compared
with5.81%for the frozen routing-selected features: a5.79×increase. Thus the original
result substantially underestimated the sharing available to a jointly selected
feature family.

There is still a precise limit: even over the whole key space, the largest
possible function cosine for each head ranges0.672–0.843. No0.95match exists in
these particular key/value function-space pairs under the coefficient metric.
This does not rule out other unembedding components, nonlinear shared functions,
other upstream paths, or sharing that matters on natural model states.

We then measured the new features' joint QK numerator coverage at eight fixed
source positions, with query511. This0.21-second CPU audit replays the old
measurements to$3.4\times10^{-16}$:

| Feature selection | Producer-function overlap | Joint QK numerator touch, eight-position mean |
|---|---:|---:|
| Original routing-selected17features | 5.81% | 42.03% |
| Producer/value-aligned17features | 33.63% | 16.63% |

“Touch” counts the polynomial interactions involving at least one selected source
direction, including mixed interactions with unselected directions. It does not
measure the actual normalized attention effect. Better producer sharing therefore
comes with a large routing-coverage cost. The new features are not automatically
a better circuit decomposition.

## How the fold and comparison work

MLP16's quadratic producer is

$$
g(x)=D p(x),\qquad p_j(x)=(l_j^\top x)(r_j^\top x).
$$

The matrix of coefficient inner products between native products is

$$
K_{ij}=\frac12\left[(l_i^\top l_j)(r_i^\top r_j)
+(l_i^\top r_j)(r_i^\top l_j)\right],
\qquad G=DKD^\top.
$$

For reader matrices$A$and$B$, the folded functions have Gram matrices

$$
G_{AA}=AGA^\top,\quad G_{BB}=BGB^\top,\quad G_{AB}=AGB^\top.
$$

A Gram matrix contains all pairwise function inner products. Whitening rescales
and mixes each family's coordinates so its independent functions have unit norm
and are mutually orthogonal. We use eigenvalues above a relative$10^{-10}$rank
threshold; all registered native function spans retained rank17.

If$W_A^\top G_{AA}W_A=I$and$W_B^\top G_{BB}W_B=I$, the singular values of

$$
W_A^\top G_{AB}W_B
$$

are the principal cosines. They compare the best-aligned normalized function
combinations, then the next mutually orthogonal combinations. The mean of their
squares is the reported overlap. Invertible reparameterization inside either
reader family leaves its function space and these scores unchanged.

The new explicit-pair helper recovers those reader combinations, not just their
scores. For two unit functions$u,v$with cosine$c$, it can form

$$
f_+=\frac{u+v}{\sqrt{2(1+c)}},\qquad
f_-=\frac{u-v}{\sqrt{2(1-c)}}.
$$

Then each function has a shared average term and an opposite-sign difference
term. At$c<1$, calling the average term “shared” does not make the two original
functions identical; the remainder must be retained. The CPU exact-sharing,
partial-sharing, null-space and replay controls passed.

## Normalization and background were retained

Let$z$be the residual immediately before MLP16 and$x_0$the model's base state.
The local interface is

$$
x=\operatorname{RMS}(z),\qquad
h_{16}=z+g(x)+b_{16},
$$

$$
t=1.0234375\,h_{16}+8\,x_0,\qquad
s=\frac{t}{\sqrt{\|t\|^2/1152+\epsilon}}.
$$

The coefficients are the actual block17re-entry weights. For every tested reader,
the fold retains the$z$and$x_0$terms, MLP16bias and common source RMS divisor.
It replays the direct local source reads with relative error
$3.2\times10^{-15}$. The function-space metric isolates the quadratic producer;
it does not claim that this producer equals the complete source state. The
first-layer base-value stream is a separate path. Earlier model computation,
attention's two key normalizers, joint routing and later readout remain explicit
background dependencies.

## Next executed consequence: a joint objective

The next objective rewards producer sharing and routing influence together.
For fixed value readers$B$, define

$$
M=GB^\top(BGB^\top)^{-1}BG.
$$

For candidate key-reader columns$E$restricted to the joint key-weight row space,
their function-space sharing score is

$$
J(E)=\frac1{17}\operatorname{tr}
\left[(E^\top GE)^{-1}E^\top ME\right].
$$

Let$S$be the normalized joint-QK source-influence matrix and$\mu$its leading
17-eigenvalue sum. A combined objective can use

$$
\Phi(E)=\eta J(E)+(1-\eta)
\frac{\operatorname{tr}[(E^\top E)^{-1}E^\top SE]}{\mu}.
$$

Both terms depend on the subspace, not the choice of its coordinates. The weight
$\eta$sets the tradeoff and must be registered before a native fit. The routing
term is an influence surrogate, so actual numerator touch must still be measured
separately afterward. Native normalization and behavioral checks remain later
validation stages.

The analytic gradient, sharing-score identity, invertible coordinate changes and
finite-difference controls are implemented and passed; the largest finite-difference
error is$2.1\times10^{-11}$. Native joint optimization has not yet run. We should
reuse the existing constrained optimizer and require convergence rather than
declaring a fixed step budget sufficient.
