# Joint QK routing has stronger source structure than the first value-space test suggested

**A weight-only mathematical check found much stronger routing-related source
spaces than those inherited from the compact MLP component.** Seventeen source
directions selected directly from the joint QK1×QK2 polynomial participate in
59.6% of its coefficient energy on average, versus 7.1% for the component's
value-reader spaces. This is a useful correction to the initial weak result.

Most of that 59.6% involves interactions with outside directions. These are open
components with explicit dependencies, not closed 17-dimensional circuits.
No text data or task labels entered discovery, and no behavioral circuit is
claimed. The original weak-alignment predictions remain failed.

## What was tested first

The [previous update](explanation_2026-09-11_0226.md) found a stable MLP component
with one shared input reader and 16 partner readers. Folding those readers through
attention's output and value matrices gives 17 source readers per head.

The new question was whether **both QK branches together** use those same source
directions. For a distinct query state $q$ and source state $s$, a head's routing
numerator is

$$
N(q,s)=(q^\top A s)(q^\top B s).
$$

$A$ and $B$ fold in the respective query/key weights and the actual relative
position rotation. This is one biquadratic polynomial: degree two in query
inputs and degree two in source inputs. It is not a division of tasks between
QK1 and QK2. Query position is 8; source positions are 7 and 0. Self-position
would tie query and source variables and requires a different coefficient norm.

Let $P$ project onto a source-reader space, and split $s=s_{\rm in}+s_{\rm out}$.
Then the complete joint numerator splits exactly into

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\rm in}&=(q^\top As_{\rm in})(q^\top Bs_{\rm in}),\\
N_{\rm out}&=(q^\top As_{\rm out})(q^\top Bs_{\rm out}),\\
N_{\rm mixed}&=(q^\top As_{\rm in})(q^\top Bs_{\rm out})
+(q^\top As_{\rm out})(q^\top Bs_{\rm in}).
\end{aligned}
$$

These three coefficient tensors are orthogonal. I call the fraction in
$N_{\rm in}+N_{\rm mixed}$ the **touch fraction**: it measures interactions
involving the chosen space, not interactions computable entirely within it.

The normalized routing is $(N_{\rm in}+N_{\rm mixed}+N_{\rm out})/D$, with
the original four RMS factors and epsilon retained in $D$. This additive
decomposition is exact. Deleting input directions and recomputing $D$ is a
different intervention, so the normalizer dependencies cannot silently disappear.

## Initial result and executed red-team check

All nine heads and both source positions were compared against three random
17-dimensional downstream frames passed through the same native OV maps.
This controls generic OV/key alignment rather than comparing with arbitrary
source-space noise.

| Source space | Mean joint-numerator touch |
|---|---:|
| Frozen MLP component's value readers | 7.06% |
| Matched random downstream frames | 5.55% |
| Component readers after separating current/base value streams | 7.10% |
| Matched random frames after the same stream separation | 5.58% |

The registered target required a twofold advantage and at least two percentage
points of gain. Both clauses missed. The old head17.2/17.3 pair also missed the
registered 10% touch requirement. Dense controls and normalized routing replay
passed, with native error below $5.6\times10^{-15}$.

The stream check tests a specific alternative explanation: a joint current/base
reader may hide sharing that becomes visible when its two source streams are
separated. Enlarging the space that way barely changes the result. All heads
were inspected: head17.4 shows about a twofold advantage, but this was noticed
after the results and is exploratory. No behavior is assigned to it, and it
does not change the original aggregate failure.

## What the mathematical follow-up changed

The remaining alternative is that the MLP-selected value space is simply a poor
choice for routing. I derived a source influence matrix from the **entire joint
QK coefficient tensor**, without choosing tokens or activation samples.

Write the separately symmetric coefficient tensor as $T_{ijab}$, with query
indices $i,j$ and source indices $a,b$. Define

$$
S_{ab}=\sum_{i,j,c}T_{ijac}T_{ijbc},
\qquad E=\|T\|_F^2=\operatorname{tr}S.
$$

$S$ is positive semidefinite. Its eigenvectors identify source directions that
participate strongly in the coefficient tensor. For any rank-$r$ projector $P$,

$$
E_{\rm touch}(P)=2\operatorname{tr}(PS)-E_{\rm in}(P)
\leq \min\!\left(E,\,2\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_i(S)\right),
$$

where eigenvalues are ordered largest first. Thus the spectrum supplies an
upper bound applying to **any** source space of that size. Taking its leading
eigenvectors also supplies a concrete candidate, whose exact touch is measured
separately. That candidate need not globally maximize touch because the
subtracted inside energy also depends on $P$.

The large tensor is never formed. The source dependence lies in the combined
key-weight span, of dimension at most 256. Differentiating the exact coefficient
norm with respect to a source metric gives $2S$ in that small span. Dense tensor,
trace, positivity and projection-identity controls passed to $5.2\times10^{-16}$.
This is an exact spectral calculation, with no iterative convergence issue.

**The native calculation found 45.7–70.2% touch across the 18 head/position cases,
averaging 59.6%.** That is 8.40 times the stream-separated MLP value-space result.
The rank-17 upper bounds range from 53.1% to 92.4%; these are loose bounds, not
achieved captures. The constructive results themselves rule out a universal
25% ceiling in these cases. The choice of source space matters substantially.
[Influence controls](../../JOINT_QK_SOURCE_INFLUENCE_V1_CONTROL.json),
[native constructive result and bounds](../../JOINT_QK_SOURCE_BOUND_V1_RESULT.json).

The average energy partition for the spectral spaces is **14.5% inside,
45.1% mixed, and 40.4% outside**. The mixed term requires both selected and
outside readers. Neither a 59.6% behavioral explanation nor a 17-variable
closed implementation follows from this result.

## Are the spaces shared or stable?

A further CPU comparison used the saved source spaces in common raw input
coordinates. Within the same head, the two source distances have mean squared
principal cosines of 0.746–0.896. This overlap measure is one for identical spaces
and zero for orthogonal spaces; it ignores their arbitrary internal bases.
Some individual directions change substantially, so the whole spaces are not
identical. Across different heads, the largest such overlap is 0.075.

These are descriptive comparisons at two fixed distances, not an OOD text test.
They suggest more agreement within a head across distances than between heads,
while leaving smaller shared directions and task-specific reuse open.
[Executed space comparison](../../JOINT_QK_SOURCE_SPACE_COMPARISON_V1_AUDIT.json).

The next useful object is a coupled routing/value component with its mixed
inputs and normalizers explicit. The calculations here give source readers and
interfaces for that work. They do not justify another corpus-fitting campaign:
FineWeb remains validation of frozen candidates, and Pile remains separately OOD.
