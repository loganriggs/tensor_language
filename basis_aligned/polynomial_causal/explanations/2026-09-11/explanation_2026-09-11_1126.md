# Direct fitting improves the polynomial; individual features are still mixed

**11 September,11:26UTC.** Since the10:21update, the important change is that
we now optimize the composed bilinear-layer/unembedding computation directly.
The first live fit captures about**64.68% of its coefficient energy**, versus
**53.95%** at initialization with the output weights already optimized. It has
not converged, and the second start has not yet supplied a result. These are
weight-function measurements, not accuracy or identified circuits.

Three further developments clarify what to do next:

- Improving the older individual-reader objective added less than0.1percentage
  point and did not converge. The direct objective gave a much larger gain.
- Changing sparse connections helps beyond simply refitting their values.
  A32reader test improves the complete polynomial, but only by**0.001025
  percentage point**. A full sweep with an identical-capacity refitting control
  is queued after the current fit.
- Individual shared features are still mixtures of many output computations.
  Their removal functions are not yet simple circuit units. An exact
  counterexample shows that this can reflect the chosen coordinates even when
  the underlying polynomial consists of independent simple computations.

No new million-token discovery run was added. Weights remain the discovery
source; frozen candidates will be validated on FineWeb, with Pile separately
labelled as distribution shift. No new claim of the four behavioral properties.

**What the current factorization learns.** A shared input dictionary is a
matrix whose rows define linear features. If the normalized MLP input is x,

$$
 z=Bx,\qquad
 \widehat F(x)=UD\big[(C_Lz)\odot(C_Rz)\big].
$$

B has2304rows. Each of the4608Left and4608Right readers uses128of these
features, specified by sparse coefficient matrices C_L and C_R. D maps products
to the residual stream, and U is the whole unembedding. The product pairing
and128connection positions are currently fixed. Feature directions, sparse
coefficient values and D can change. Bias/background are retained and charged;
final RMS normalization and logit soft-capping are outside this quadratic fold.

The fit minimizes relative squared error of the symmetric coefficient tensor:

$$
 \min_{B,C_L,C_R}\min_D
 \frac{\|T-\widehat T(B,C_L,C_R,D)\|_F^2}{\|T\|_F^2}.
$$

For every nonlinear parameter evaluation, the inner output-weight least-squares
problem is solved exactly. This is **variable projection**: eliminate the
linear unknowns before optimizing the nonlinear ones. The remaining joint
optimizer is L-BFGS-B, which builds a limited-memory curvature approximation.
Full tensor contractions avoid constructing every vocabulary-by-input-by-input
entry. The method and its restrictions are in the
[registered protocol](../../PROJECTED_SPARSE_DICTIONARY_FIT_V1_PREREGISTRATION.md).

“Capture” means one minus the displayed error.64.68%capture still leaves
35.32%relative squared coefficient error. It says neither that64.68%of tokens
are correct nor that64.68%of the model has been explained. The first fit's loss
has nearly plateaued while gradients remain above the registered stopping
threshold; component cancellation and conditioning are growing. More steps
have not yet established convergence. The second start and final stability
comparison remain pending.

**Why changing connections is a different experiment.** For one reader a,
freeze its partner b, output writer w, and all other products. Its full folded
squared error is an exact quadratic:

$$
 a^\top H a-2a^\top r+\mathrm{constant},\qquad
 H=\frac{\|w\|^2}{2}\big(\|b\|^2I+bb^\top\big).
$$

Here w includes the unembedding metric, and r includes residual interactions
with all other products. This is not independent-reader approximation. We
first optimize coefficients on the current128connections, then find the best
single connection to remove and replace. Schur complements compute the exact
conditional least-squares gains without fitting every candidate from scratch.

The [native32reader probe](../../NATIVE_SUPPORT_EXCHANGE_V1_AUDIT.json) found
an improving swap for every sampled reader. Median swap gain was2.52e-7 of
native coefficient energy, compared with6.25e-12 from the same-support refit.
The large ratio should not obscure the small absolute gain. Applying these
changes sequentially, recomputing the residual each time, improved capture by
1.025e-5. An independent full-polynomial contraction verified the combined gain.
One initial execution failed on a duplicated logging key; the
[separate repair](../../SEQUENTIAL_SUPPORT_EXCHANGE_V2_AUDIT.json) preserved
the experiment and passed its checks. The
[queued full sweep](../../FULL_SUPPORT_EXCHANGE_V1_PREREGISTRATION.md) compares
all9216reader exchanges against a separate same-support-refitting sweep for
each final parent. It does not assume the small independent gains add up.

**Are shared features already simple computations?** Removing a feature z_i
from both input branches has an exact effect of the form

$$
 \Delta_iF(x)=(b_i^\top x)M_ix.
$$

M_i describes everything written when that feature is used, including the
correction for products using it on both sides. If M_i has one effective
input/output direction, this is a simple shared-reader product. If it needs
many directions, removal mixes many computations. For unit b_i, the correct
coefficient metric is

$$
 H_i=\tfrac12(I+b_ib_i^\top),\qquad
 \|\Delta_iT\|_F^2=\|M_iH_i^{1/2}\|_F^2.
$$

The singular values of M_iH_i^{1/2} give the best rank-constrained partner
approximation for this fixed feature. This reuses earlier shared-reader
spectral work; it is not a new factorization theorem.

For16evenlyspaced features in a frozen intermediate checkpoint, rank1captures
**14.8–31.1%** of their removal functions. Rank16captures**40.4–55.7%**, and
90%capture requires**136–167directions**. Both simplicity predictions failed.
All16removals and120pair interactions match the existing sparse executor to
about1e-13relative error. Thus the broad spectra are real for this candidate's
chosen internal interface. [Primary receipt](../../FROZEN_FEATURE_REMOVAL_V1_AUDIT.json).

The strongest alternative explanation was tested immediately. Consider the
exactly separable polynomial

$$
 F(x)=(x_1^2,\ldots,x_{128}^2).
$$

In its natural coordinates each feature removal has rank1. Represent exactly
the same function in a dense orthogonal feature basis: rank16then captures only
12.55%of a removal function, and90%requires115directions. The executor confirms
the overall function is unchanged. Therefore broad removal spectra do not
prove absent underlying structure. The dense rotated representation also uses
more meaningful connections; this counterexample does not demonstrate a native
repair at our current sparsity or price.
[Counterexample receipt](../../FEATURE_REMOVAL_COORDINATE_V1_CONTROL.json).
