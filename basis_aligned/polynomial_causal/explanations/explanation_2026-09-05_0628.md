# Circuit-only goal, runner correction, and a source-level numbered-list result — 2026-09-05 06:28 UTC

## Current goal

The immediate goal is to make the repository reliably produce **hundreds of high-quality circuit records**. A useful
record should say what information a circuit reads, what computation it performs, what it writes, and what later
computation uses that result. It should eventually predict held-out examples and support a targeted removal, swap, or
edit without changing unrelated behaviors.

The operating target is one cheap causal screen—or an honest null—per roughly ten serial minutes. A screen is a first
causal measurement, not a fully established circuit. Expensive held-out, out-of-distribution, composition, and removal
tests are promotion work for candidates that survive the cheap screen.

The hourly checkpoint now explicitly rejects three recurring failures:

1. drifting into rank reduction, activation reconstruction, variance preservation, quantization, or compression;
2. attaching so many backup checks to a basic screen that an hour is spent before one causal result exists; and
3. repeating old work because dossiers and prior nulls were not searched first. In particular, reconstructing activation
   energy is not the target. Cross-entropy loss and task logits are better functional measurements, but even they do not
   replace causal transfer and selective controls.

## Why the first managed attempt produced no result

The 06:20 managed process printed the dry-run plan and exited without loading the model. Two old experiment modules set
the environment variable `BQLIB_NO_MODEL=1` merely by being imported. The new runner imported those modules and then
mistook their internal test setting for an instruction from the queue.

This was an invalid execution, not a scientific null: there were no model forwards and no result was scored. The log
hash and diagnosis are preserved in a compact invalid-execution receipt. The reusable fix is that both the candidate
builder and runner save the caller's execution-mode setting, import the old helpers, and then restore the caller's
setting. Seven focused tests and a clean-process import test pass. The corrected runner was committed, pushed, and run
through the content-bound managed queue.

## What the numbered-list experiment computed

Earlier exact work identified a cached-token contribution at attention layer 8. Let

$$
T_h = a_h(q,s)\,W^O_h v_h(s),
$$

where $h$ is head 3 or head 7, $a_h(q,s)$ is that head's attention weight from the final query position $q$ to the
registered source position $s$, $v_h(s)$ is the cached value vector at that source, and $W^O_h$ maps the head vector
back into the 1,152-dimensional residual stream. The previously studied complete contribution was

$$
T = T_3 + T_7.
$$

The new factorial ran the unmodified model, an exact replay, removal of $T_3$, removal of $T_7$, and removal of both.
It used 128 held-out SELECT endpoints from two successor-list families, repeated-index lists, and word-copy controls.
For each intervention the main behavioral quantity was answer-margin damage:

$$
D = (\ell_{\text{answer}}-\ell_{\text{foil}})_{\text{native}}
  -(\ell_{\text{answer}}-\ell_{\text{foil}})_{\text{intervened}}.
$$

Positive $D$ means removing the term made the intended answer less preferred. The interaction was

$$
I = D_{3+7}-D_3-D_7.
$$

If the two terms acted independently downstream, $I$ would be near zero.

## Result

The instrument was exact: both the native replay error and the reconstructed joint-term error were zero at the stored
precision. Average answer-margin damage across the two successor-list families was:

| Removed term | Successor lists | Repeated-index lists | Word copy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $T_3$ | +0.874 | -1.091 | -0.151 |
| $T_7$ | +0.309 | -0.295 | -0.077 |
| $T_3+T_7$ | +1.946 | -1.397 | -0.214 |

Both individual terms help the successor behavior, with $T_3$ contributing more. But neither is a selectively necessary
semantic unit under the registered controls: removing it substantially *improves* the correct margin on repeated-index
lists. The sign difference is informative. It says the same exact source contribution participates differently depending
on list state; a native head is not the final circuit basis.

The two terms also interact downstream. Their joint removal causes 0.763 more margin damage than the sum of separate
removals, with a grouped 95% bootstrap interval of 0.715 to 0.811. Thus the result supports a cooperative, context-dependent
service, not two independent or redundant source heads.

This is recorded as an honest null for the preregistered claim “one individual cached-head source is selective.” It does
not erase the positive result that both terms matter for successor lists.

## What is running next

The next circuit-level split is already underway on CPU: a fresh layer-13/head-8 pending-bracket factorial separates the
value contribution by source region—prefix, the semantic opening bracket, and tokens after that opener—and measures all
eight present/absent combinations. It includes exact native replay and the complete-head ceiling. This asks whether the
head's payload has a semantic source-region decomposition, instead of treating the whole head as the true unit. It does
not scan ranks or optimize activation reconstruction.
