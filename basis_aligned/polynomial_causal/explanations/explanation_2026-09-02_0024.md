# The vocabulary family exposed a bad composition normalizer — 2026-09-02 00:24 UTC

## Goal and current position

Our goal is to turn bilin18 into a smaller, understandable tensor program that does more than reproduce ordinary
activations. A useful decomposition should let us predict behavior on new data, extract a circuit, remove that circuit
without damaging unrelated computations, and predict how extracted parts interact or get reused.

The current experiment is the concrete implementation of the “learn what simplicity is from solved circuits” idea in
the 20:54 explanation. We are building candidate replacement programs in three teaching families, measuring their
removal and composition consequences on held-out documents, and only then fitting a small rule that predicts which
structural properties lead to useful circuits. MLP0 is valid teaching family 1. The later-MLP PCA programs are valid
family 2 after their independent replication. The 23 vocabulary programs have now run, but they do **not** count as
family 3 under the registered test.

That is not because the vocabulary programs lacked a stable signal. The run found a specific mathematical problem in
how we normalized the composition interaction. The failed registration remains failed; the proposed replacement score
will be frozen and tested on independent documents rather than used to rewrite the result.

## Terms in ordinary language

- A **candidate program**, written `P`, is a complete proposed replacement for one part of the model. Here it keeps the
  input token embedding exactly native and replaces only the final map from the 1,152-number hidden state to the
  50,304 vocabulary logits.
- A **program rank** is the number of learned shared directions retained in that output map. Rank 512 retains more of
  the fitted output structure than rank 256, 128, or 0. This is low-rank matrix structure, not floating-point
  quantization and not a claim that the whole transformer has that tensor rank.
- A **token loss** or **cross entropy**, written `L`, is `-log(probability of the correct next token)`. Smaller is
  better. Every condition produces one loss number for each evaluated token.
- The **removal intervention** replaces the output of native attention layer 16 by its calibrated mean. It asks whether
  the candidate preserves the causal effect of removing that distant computation.
- The **composition partner**, written `Q`, is the already physical rank-2 quadratic replacement for MLP16. It asks
  whether `P` and a separately changed downstream computation combine additively or interact.
- A **wave** is one pre-fixed half of the documents. Agreement between waves checks that a conclusion is not caused by
  a particular group of documents.
- A **registered direction** is a prediction made before seeing these outcomes, such as “rank 512 should have less
  consequence error than rank 256.” There were 14 adjacent-rank predictions.
- A **strong null** is a pre-registered stop condition. It means this family cannot be accepted for the stated use; it
  does not mean every measured quantity is zero or random.
- **Pearson correlation** measures whether continuous values rise and fall together across two document waves. A value
  near 1 means highly reproducible magnitudes. It does not by itself say that higher rank moved in the desired
  direction.

## Exact computation

The run used 96 held-out documents, each producing 256 next-token predictions, for 24,576 token losses per condition.
It first captured the exact 1,152-number final hidden state under three transformer conditions: native, attention16
removed, and the MLP16 partner. Because every vocabulary candidate changes only the final vocabulary map, all 23
candidates can then be evaluated exactly against those saved hidden states. This is algebraically the same output as
running the whole upstream transformer separately for every candidate; it avoids repeating identical work.

For each token, let:

- `L_N` be native loss;
- `L_NR` be native loss after the attention16 removal `R`;
- `L_P` and `L_PR` be candidate loss without and with that removal;
- `L_Q` be loss with the MLP16 partner;
- `L_PQ` be loss with both the candidate and partner.

The native removal effect and candidate removal effect are

`R_N = L_NR - L_N`, and `R_P = L_PR - L_P`.

The removal error is

`E_remove(P) = ||R_P - R_N||_2 / ||R_N||_2`.

Here `||x||_2` means: square every token-level value, add the squares, and take the square root. The denominator is the
same native removal size for every candidate, so candidates are compared against one fixed ruler.

The non-additive composition interaction is the rectangular finite difference

`I(P,Q) = L_PQ - L_P - L_Q + L_N`.

If the two changes combine exactly additively, `I(P,Q)` is zero. The registered composition score was

`E_comp_old(P) = ||I(P,Q)||_2 / ||(L_P-L_N) + (L_Q-L_N)||_2`.

The numerator is the interaction we care about. The denominator is the size of the additive prediction—but unlike the
removal denominator, it changes with `P`.

## Result

![Vocabulary consequence result and denominator diagnostic](assets/rung454_vocabulary_consequences.png)

The instrument was exact: native hidden-state and loss replays had zero maximum difference, all 32,768 calibrated
attention positions were present, the removal and partner interventions each fired 24 times, and the sealed attention0
family remained closed.

The removal score behaved extremely cleanly. All 14/14 rank predictions were correct on the full 96 documents and on
each 48-document wave: 100% against registered bars of 85% for the full set and 70% per wave.

The registered composition score failed: only 5/14 directions, or 35.7%, were correct on the full set. The two waves
gave 3/14 (21.4%) and 6/14 (42.9%). This is below the pre-registered 50% strong-null boundary. The values themselves
were reproducible between waves—Pearson `.9833`, while removal was `.9949`—but they reproducibly moved in the wrong
rank direction. Stable wrong-way behavior is still a failure.

The labels varied substantially enough to measure: the full range was `.8286` for removal and `.0888` for registered
composition. Thus this was not a “nothing happened” result.

## Why the composition score reversed the conclusion

The right panel isolates one rank ladder. From rank 0 to rank 512, the interaction size actually falls from `82.15` to
`58.10`: the higher-rank program has a smaller non-additive interaction. But the candidate-dependent denominator falls
even faster, from `934.15` to `468.80`. Dividing the two makes the registered ratio rise from `.08794` to `.12393`, so
the score reports the improving candidate as worse.

This is a normalizer artifact, not evidence that division is always wrong. A denominator is useful when it supplies a
common scale. Here each candidate brought its own ruler, and the ruler shrank faster than the quantity being measured.
Across all 14 registered vocabulary rank edges, the raw interaction size improves 14/14 while the registered ratio
improves only 5/14.

The natural fixed-ruler companion is

`E_comp_fixed(P) = ||I(P,Q)||_2 / ||L_Q-L_N||_2`.

The denominator is now the same physical partner effect for every candidate, exactly paralleling the fixed native
effect used by the removal score. On already-open teaching results this fixed score orders all MLP0 ranks, the main
MLP-PCA rank ladder, and all 14 vocabulary edges correctly. That is a useful diagnosis, but it is post-hoc evidence,
not a prospective pass.

## Tensor-network interpretation

Both measurements are mixed finite differences. Removal applies the candidate change and the removal change and asks
for their cross-effect; composition applies the candidate and partner changes and asks for their cross-effect. In a
tensor network, a local tensor is not important merely because its raw weights are large. Its importance is determined
by the rest of the contracted network—its downstream **environment**. The fixed partner-effect ruler is a simple
empirical environment scale. This is why the current mathematical direction is to compare candidate tensors under
their contracted downstream response, rather than under raw weight distance or stored parameter count alone. The
full three-hour review is in
[`THREE_HOURLY_MATHEMATICAL_REVIEW_2026-09-02_0023.md`](../THREE_HOURLY_MATHEMATICAL_REVIEW_2026-09-02_0023.md).

## What happened to the SAE and continuous-function directions

They were not silently abandoned. The exact raw-factor Archetypal Q/K dictionary and the 32-atom response-space
convex dictionary were run and failed their registered identification tests; their dependent extraction experiment is
therefore scientifically closed unless the mathematical object changes. The continuous response-space route using the
supported rung-424/425 quotient remains queued after the current consequence-label protocol. It will test selective
removal and predictable interactions of continuous coordinates rather than merely naming visually pleasing SAE atoms.

Likewise, optimizing a new decomposition under a learned simplicity rule is deliberately later, not dropped. Running
it before the rule survives a sealed circuit family would let the optimizer exploit our current definitions. The
current label work is the prerequisite that turns that idea into a falsifiable experiment.

No floating-point quantization is being proposed as interpretability. The candidate structure here is low-rank/shared
computation. Half-precision copies of token-loss traces exist only as compact audit bundles; the registered scientific
metrics were computed before serialization in full working precision. Reducing the number of bits in a stored number
would not by itself advance OOD prediction, circuit extraction, selective removal, or predictable composition.
