# MLP10 family test: exact pieces, wrong grouping

## Goal

The project goal is a smaller transparent tensor program that tells us what information each circuit reads, what
operation it performs, what it writes, and how later computations use it. The pieces must predict unseen and shifted
data, work when combined, and support selective removals or edits without damaging unrelated behavior. Fewer numbers
or lower rank is not enough by itself.

## What we tested

MLP10 is bilinear. If its normalized input is `z`, its hidden product is

`h = Left(z) * Right(z)`,

where `*` means coordinate-by-coordinate multiplication, and its residual-stream output is

`y = Down(h) + bias`.

We already have an exact split of `z` into 22 earlier writes: the embedding, 11 attention outputs, and 10 earlier
MLP outputs. Expanding the multiplication gives 253 unordered source-pair terms. Rung508 grouped those exact terms
using six fixed, human-chosen families: embedding; early attention; the attention5--8 equality interval; later
attention; early MLPs; and later MLPs. This makes 21 unordered family pairs.

For each family pair, we measured a real finite intervention. Under an equality-score replacement we computed the
family term's change in MLP10's output, subtracted that change from the deployed BF16 output, recomputed layers11--17,
and measured the resulting change in cross-entropy loss. Positive loss change means the removed term was helping.
This is not a gradient approximation and is not a low-rank reconstruction.

## Result

The computation and interventions were valid, but the proposed grouping failed: **0 of 21 family terms** had a
stable causal effect across both document halves and all four implementations of the equality score.

- The run used exactly 5,828 forward passes and no backward passes.
- All 5,456 requested changes were applied and changed the MLP10 write.
- Replaying the intact model was exact; the maximum factor error was `4.69e-14`.
- The 21 groups reconstructed the 253 exact terms to relative squared error `1.06e-14`.
- Alternative equality-score implementations recovered 89.6%--113.8% of the native copy effect, so the parent
  experiment remained calibrated.
- The required within-source repeat cosine was 50%. The best group's worst-case value was only 28.8%.

Some groups looked strong in one condition and reversed in another. For example, equality-attention with itself had
97.5% repeat cosine for the native score, but its worst score-source repeat was -47.4%. That is exactly why we require
the same proposed circuit to predict more than one background: a favorable single condition would have given a
misleading circuit label.

## Interpretation

The null does **not** say MLP10 lacks structure. It says the six broad architecture categories are not the stable
units used by the later network. Together with rung507, it rules out two tempting bases:

1. choosing exact terms by their gradient; and
2. grouping exact terms by broad source location.

Both are valid ways to write the algebra, but neither predicts finite causal effects reliably. This is directly
relevant to the circuit goal: the model's useful variables appear to cut across those hand-chosen boundaries.

## Next computation

The next experiment will treat each bilinear component as a coupled object with three parts: what its Left branch
reads, what its Right branch reads, and what its Down vector writes. Candidate groups will be learned from those
three parts together with their downstream causal effects. A candidate only counts if it predicts finite removal
effects on held-out documents and under multiple equality-score implementations. This is intended to find, for
example, different inputs that later computation treats as the same output variable, or a shared input variable that
branches into different outputs.

The selection objective will not be low rank, storage reduction, or reconstruction error. Those can later price an
already identified circuit, but they cannot identify one here.
