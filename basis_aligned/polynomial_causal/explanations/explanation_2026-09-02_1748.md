## 14. Splitting attention scores into query and key sides also failed

Rung496 asked whether two attention1 heads share only one side of one attention score. Each head was represented by
five functional inputs: the query and key of its first score (`Q1`, `K1`), the query and key of its second score
(`Q2`, `K2`), and its value/output path (`V`). For each MLP0 branch removal, all32 normal-versus-removed combinations
of these five inputs were evaluated. Their exact finite interaction terms were then divided equally among the inputs
that participate in each interaction. We also measured changing each input first and changing it last, so a result
could not depend only on that bookkeeping convention.

The computation was numerically sound. The32 combinations reproduced the complete attention1 change, the allocated
pieces summed back to it, the downstream derivatives summed correctly, and all registered call counts matched.

The scientific result was a strong negative. Among288 allowed cross-head query/query and key/key pairs,131 were
large enough on the first250 documents to be tested. The best selected pair was the second-score query side of
head0 and head2. Its downstream circuit effects had55.3% cosine agreement on the documents used to choose it, but
agreement became −3.9% on the next250 documents. After allowing one scale factor, the unexplained effect increased
from83.3% to99.9%. Direct “change this input first” and “change it last” measurements also changed sign. The later500
documents and30 further circuits remained unopened.

![The best selected query-side pair disappears on confirmation](assets/attention1_query_key_rung496.svg)

The important interpretation is not that attention heads are the right units. It is narrower: under these62
downstream circuit measurements, neither whole score/value pieces nor separate query/key sides expose a stable
variable shared across attention1 heads. The large first-half value was a selection effect from choosing among many
pairs, which is exactly why the second half was reserved.

## 15. Why we did an archive audit before inventing another decomposition

After the negative result, the proposed alternative was to group model parts by their behavior under actual finite
actions: remove a part, restore it, replace it with another part, and combine that change with a second intervention.
Two parts belong together only if later computations continue to treat them equivalently across this action table.

Rung497 checked whether old results already contained enough compatible measurements to do this without another GPU
run. It audited15 evidence records across five families: equality-attention terms, later equality corrections, MLP0
branches, MLP1 write changes, and attention1 score/value pieces. Every recorded file and hash was valid, but no family
contained all of the following in one compatible dataset:

- effects for individual examples;
- at least two document splits;
- the same definitions of removal, restoration, replacement, and composition;
- the real remainder of the model after the edit;
- a dedicated task measurement; and
- a held-out set of downstream circuits.

This means joining the old tables would silently compare different experiments. It does not mean those results are
bad; it means they were not designed as one transition table. Local derivatives in the attention1 work also do not
count as finite replacements.

## 16. The next experiment calibrates the method on a computation we already know

Rung498 is now preregistered. It uses the known equality matcher rather than searching for a new grouping immediately.
The positive relation is layer5 head5's matching score replacing layer8 head4's score while layer8 keeps its own
values and output. The fixed controls are layer7 head3's score and layer5 head5's value/output contribution.

For every donor, the experiment will measure layer8 head4 in five states: normal, equality term removed, donor score
with layer8's output, layer8's score with the donor output, and donor score with donor output. It repeats all five
states with the earlier donor contribution present and removed. Thus removal, restoration, replacement, composition,
and stability under a second action all use the same examples and the same physical continuation through the model.

The first500 documents and32 circuits are divided into two fixed halves. The remaining500 documents and30 circuits
open only if the method recovers the known positive in both halves and both backgrounds, rejects both controls, and
preserves its circuit-effect pattern when the earlier donor service is removed. A pass would calibrate the grouping
method for a later search; it would not itself discover or compress another circuit. A failure would tell us whether
the problem is the observation set, the control discrimination, or dependence on the earlier-head background.

This is the most direct response to the recent nulls: stop asking whether components merely look similar, and test
whether they remain substitutable under the actions that an extracted circuit must support.
