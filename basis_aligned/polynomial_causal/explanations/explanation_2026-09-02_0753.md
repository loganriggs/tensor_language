# The gauge-aware chapter: three MLPs, one task, no shared coordinates (rungs 477–479)
*2026-09-02 07:53 UTC — written at the chapter verdict*

## The question this chapter asked
The equality circuit's context correction lives in three MLPs (8, 9, 12) that
provably cooperate on the task. Do they share an internal representation — do
pieces of different MLPs implement the same downstream computation in some
common coordinate system?

## Three ways of asking, three clean nulls
1. **Native coordinates (477/477b).** Of 13,824 individual product terms,
   only ~11 have reproducible downstream response profiles at all; every
   cross-MLP mutual-nearest graph is empty. (A real data defect — one batch
   straddling the document-half boundary — was found, root-caused, and
   repaired without touching the verdict; the null stands on certified-clean
   data.)
2. **Sparse learned mixtures (478).** Six-term weighted combinations fit the
   shared response target beautifully in the view they were fit on
   (cosines .98+) — and transfer at .06, *below* what deliberately
   misaligned controls achieve (.34). Perfect in-sample fit plus
   worse-than-control transfer is the signature of interpolating noise
   geometry, not of finding a mechanism.
3. **Coordinate-free reader algebra (479, today's verdict).** The deepest
   version: each MLP's action on a downstream direction g is exactly a
   quadratic form Q(g) = sym(Lᵀ diag(Dᵀg) R). If two MLPs shared block
   structure, some non-scalar matrix would commute with all their Q's —
   a property INVARIANT to how you rotate coordinates inside blocks, so the
   native-coordinate gauge that defeated attempts 1 and 2 cannot defeat
   this one. Result: the commutator statistic λ₂ of the real families
   (.036) is indistinguishable from Haar-conjugated controls whose
   cross-MLP alignment was destroyed (ratios .95–.97 vs the ≥4× bar).
   There is no shared block structure to find, at the registered
   32-dimensional task resolution.

## Why we trust the null
An independent CPU calibration (no model, run *before* outcomes opened,
hashes frozen into the registration) measured what pure noise does to every
statistic in the screen: zero false positives in 32 null families, and a
measured "interpolation floor" — even structureless families admit
best-gap splits with off-block mass ~.12–.24. The real data came in at
.34–.40 off-block: not merely failing the bar, but *noisier than noise's
best disguise*. And the λ₂ ≈ control finding shows the screen's primary
statistic was measuring each MLP's internal smoothness — the registered
conjugated controls existed precisely to catch that, and did.

## What this means
The three correction MLPs are **functionally composable but
representationally individual**. They obey a common behavioral law
(far+/near−, alignment .992), compose positively on the task, and can be
swap-tested as a unit — yet at every grain tried (heads earlier, product
terms, sparse mixtures, gauge-invariant blocks) each module keeps its own
private coordinate system. "Same computation, different implementation" is
not a failure to find the circuit; it now looks like a *property of the
model*: bilin18 reuses task behavior without reusing representation across
modules — compression lives in task geometry, never in shared coordinates
(the program's oldest structural result, §2560, now confirmed from a
fourth independent direction).
