# Circuit research update — 2026-09-04 07:45 UTC

## Goal and current decision

The goal is to replace the 546-million-parameter bilin18 transformer with a smaller explicit tensor program whose
parts correspond to computations. A successful circuit should say what information is read, what operation is
performed, what is written, and how later components use it. It should predict new and shifted examples, compose with
other extracted circuits, and permit a targeted swap, edit, or removal without unnecessarily changing unrelated
behavior.

Since the 07:18 update, the strict track has moved from the failed verbatim-copy capability test to a repaired
subject–verb-agreement dataset. The first version of that dataset was rejected before any model call, repaired, and
then approved by a fresh independent review. An outcome-blind FIT-only compiler is now being built. No task-14 model
or GPU run has happened yet.

## 1. Why the earlier width was 768

There was no natural cutoff at 768. For normalized late-MLP inputs $x_l$, we averaged the centered covariance
matrices,

$$
C=\frac{1}{|L|}\sum_{l\in L}\mathbb E\left[(x_l-\mu_l)(x_l-\mu_l)^\top\right],
$$

sorted its eigenvectors by the input variance they described, and tested the preset widths

$$
k\in\{128,256,384,512,768,896,1024,1088\}.
$$

The loss curve and the eigenvalue spectrum changed smoothly. There was no gap at eigenvector 768. In one intervention
over seven MLPs, keeping 256, 512, and 768 directions added approximately 0.397, 0.192, and 0.084 nat of
cross-entropy loss. We used 768 because it kept two thirds of the 1152-dimensional residual stream, left a sizeable
384-dimensional complement to test, and caused a measurable but tolerable change. It was an experimental operating
point, not evidence that the model contains exactly 768 semantic variables. This route is closed as circuit discovery:
it measured variance and compression, but did not identify, split, group, or selectively manipulate computations.

## 2. The new subject–verb-agreement task

The task asks whether the continuation should be the single token ` is` or ` are`. For an ordinary subject, the
answer follows the grammatical number of the subject head $n_H$, not the number of a nearer distractor noun $n_A$:

$$
y(n_H)=
\begin{cases}
\texttt{ is}, & n_H=\text{singular},\\
\texttt{ are}, & n_H=\text{plural}.
\end{cases}
$$

For each prompt, the measured margin is

$$
m(x)=\ell_{y(x)}(x)-\ell_{\bar y(x)}(x),
$$

where $\bar y$ is the other copula. A positive margin means the model assigns the grammatical answer a higher logit.

Each linked panel has four one-token edits:

| family | edit | expected answer | purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | change only the subject-head number in a prepositional phrase | changes | Tests whether head number controls agreement despite a nearer noun. |
| A2 | make the same head-number change in a relative clause | changes | Tests the same variable on a different syntactic surface. |
| P | replace the nearest distractor with a different noun of the same number | unchanged | Controls for noun identity and vocabulary. |
| C | change the distractor's number while the subject is two singular nouns joined by “and” | remains ` are` | Tests that nearby number does not control agreement and defeats “first singular noun means `is`.” |

There are 32 panels in each of FIT, SELECT, TEST, and out-of-distribution (OOD), or 512 logical rows in total. The four
phases use disjoint noun vocabularies and literal templates. OOD additionally moves the subject after two distractors
or adds a second distractor, testing rules tied to a fixed token position or short prompt.

The older agreement work is being used only as prior information. It showed 80/80 forced-choice successes on a much
simpler dataset and suggested an early number-sensitive residual feature with a later reader near layer 11. It did
not establish a complete circuit. In particular, removing the old proposed pair `{L11H3, L15H5}` reduced natural-text
agreement accuracy by only 0.0052, far below its registered 0.10 requirement. The new task therefore does not begin by
assuming those heads are the answer.

## 3. Two defects caught before freezing the data

The first task-14 candidate, commit `31b812b`, passed its own 14 tests but failed independent review for two reasons.

First, FIT P and TEST A1 used the same literal template,

```text
The {head} behind the {attractor}
```

under two different names. The noun vocabularies differed, but the syntactic surface did not, so calling the templates
phase-disjoint was false.

Second, the coordinated-subject family had 32 row records in FIT, SELECT, and TEST but only 16 distinct unordered
edits. The other 16 repeated the same two prompts with base and donor reversed. Consequently, the proposed 256 FIT
row-side evaluations contained only 224 unique prompts.

These were dataset-validity failures, not negative results about the model. No model was loaded and no GPU job was
run. The exact rejected commit and review remain preserved so the correction is auditable.

## 4. The repaired and approved authority

The replacement at commit `e9686bc` makes two outcome-blind changes:

1. TEST A1 now uses the unique surface `The {head} in front of the {attractor}`.
2. In panels 16–31, the second coordinated head uses a shifted but still balanced noun assignment. Each noun still
   appears exactly twice in that role, while the prompt pairs are no longer repetitions.

The validator now checks literal template strings, not merely their names. It also requires, separately in every
phase:

- 32 unique C base prompts;
- 32 unique C donor prompts;
- no prompt shared between those two sets; and
- 32 distinct unordered C edits.

The replacement has 256 unique FIT prompt evaluations. It passed 16/16 focused tests, 107/107 broader tests in the
independent review, and 12/12 additional attempts to reconstruct the old defects or coherently alter rows. The reviewer
approved the exact replacement bytes for one next step only: building a CPU-only capability compiler.

## 5. Exact planned capability computation

The smallest FIT test groups the 32 prompts for each family into one batch. It makes four base calls followed by four
donor calls:

$$
4\text{ families}\times2\text{ sides}=8\text{ forward calls}.
$$

For every prompt it retains only the answer logit and the other copula's logit, both as 32-bit floats:

$$
8\times32=256\text{ prompt evaluations},
$$

$$
256\times2\times4=2{,}048\text{ raw numeric bytes}.
$$

It retains no full vocabulary logits, activations, gradients, component rankings, attention-head labels, MLP labels,
or fitted subspace. Base and donor pooled accuracy must each be at least 85%. Every A1/A2/P side and every
number-conflict subset must also reach 85% with a positive mean margin. Each coordinated C side must reach 75% with a
positive margin. A failure stops the task with every localization field empty; the thresholds and examples will not
be repaired after seeing model values.

Passing would establish only that the model performs the registered behavior strongly enough to study. It would not
identify a circuit. The next scientific step would then use answer-changing interchanges plus P/C invariance controls
across held-out nouns and syntax to find where grammatical subject number is represented and consumed. Only after
that would we translate a causally identified subspace into the model's bilinear weights.

## 6. The mathematical weight-only route was tested more fairly

For a gate-free bilinear MLP and an output direction $u$, the exact symmetric input-space bilinear form is

$$
M_u=\operatorname{sym}\!\left(L^\top\operatorname{diag}(D^\top u)R\right).
$$

Here $L$ and $R$ are the two input branches and $D$ maps their elementwise product back to the residual stream. A
recent method proposes ranking $M_u$'s eigenvectors by eigenvalue magnitude. We tested progressively stronger versions
against directly measured causal importance.

![Correlation of the bilinear eigenvector ranking with causal importance](assets/bilinear_eigen_causal_ranking_2026-09-04.svg)

- Raw eigenvalue magnitude had median rank correlation $-0.446$: larger eigenvalues tended to rank less causally
  important directions higher.
- Multiplying by how strongly the actual inputs use each eigenvector is the algebraically appropriate correction, but
  the correlation remained $-0.191$.
- Contracting the weights with an output direction already known from causal experiments improved the correlation to
  $+0.383$, but missed the preregistered $+0.60$ predictive threshold.

The exact algebra is not the failure. The missing ingredient is choosing the output direction that represents the
behavior; weights alone did not supply it. This supports the current ordering: identify a task variable with valid
counterfactuals and interventions first, then use the tensor structure to express that identified computation in
weights. It does not support another arbitrary eigenvector cutoff.

## 7. Separate battery repair

A separate diagnostic lane repaired the old behavior battery's weak copy control. Repeating the copied label three
times raised usable controls from 3 of 8 to all 7 currently live behaviors; for example, the month control margin moved
from $-0.72$ to $+1.89$. One shared design worked across all seven, which is stronger than choosing a different fix per
task. The selectivity experiment has deliberately not been rerun, so this repair does not retroactively change its
old conclusion. It only makes a future rerun valid.

This copy-control repair is separate from task 14's coordinated-subject control. Neither result is being used as a
task-14 model outcome.

## 8. Current boundary

The exact task-14 replacement authority is approved. The active work is now the FIT-only authority artifact,
preregistration, compiler, and adversarial tests. A model-facing producer, managed adapter, and GPU execution remain
separate later gates. This keeps the computation small while preventing a clean-looking but invalid dataset from
entering the circuit ledger.
