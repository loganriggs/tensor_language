# High-quality circuit work: what is solid and what is not yet — 2026-09-03 22:05 project log

## Short answer

The circuit work is becoming substantially more reliable, but we do not yet have a complete circuit that satisfies all
of our goals. We have:

- an independently audited behavioral target for induction-like copying;
- an exact candidate split between **which source is selected** and **what value is copied**;
- a model intervention that tests those two parts separately and jointly; and
- a two-agent builder–critic process that has already prevented two unsafe GPU runs.

The current R585 experiment has not run on the model. Its first implementation review found six defects. After those
were repaired, a fresh review found three more failures in the evidence and crash-recovery boundary. A second repair
passed the parent test suite and static checks, but another exact-byte review found four deeper ways for saved evidence
to claim the wrong computation. A third prospective repair is active. No threshold was loosened and no negative or
positive model result has been seen.

This is slower than immediately running a patching experiment, but the resulting code and tests will be reused for the
next circuits. That is the intended bootstrap effect.

## What “high-quality circuit” means here

We are not treating “a low-rank approximation has low cross-entropy cost” as a circuit result. A useful circuit should
eventually do all of the following:

1. state the computation in ordinary terms—what it reads, computes, and writes;
2. group parts of different heads or MLPs when downstream computation treats them as the same variable, and split a
   head or MLP when it performs several jobs;
3. predict held-out and genuinely shifted examples;
4. run as an extracted computation, or reproduce the intended causal effect through a precise interface;
5. allow the proposed computation to be removed or changed without damaging unrelated behavior;
6. predict how reused parts compose with task-specific parts; and
7. remain identifiable across data samples and harmless internal changes of basis.

Storage, number of parameters, compute, edges, and states matter as prices of an explanation that already meets these
behavioral tests. Rank reduction by itself meets none of them.

## The strongest current behavioral target

R586 tests whether the model actually performs the selector-by-payload behavior that R585 is supposed to explain. A
prompt contains two possible matching sources and two ways of assigning their payloads. We vary:

$$
(s,p)\in\{(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\},
$$

where $s$ says which source matches the final query and $p$ says which payload is attached to that source. Across the
four conditions, FIT correct-answer rates range from 77.8% to 94.4%; SELECT rates range from 80.6% to 91.7%. Breaking
the selected match changes the expected answer margin in the predicted direction on 97.9% of FIT rows and 98.6% of
SELECT rows.

R587 independently reconstructed the saved rows, factorial comparisons, bootstrap samples, model-call count, split
boundary, and result hashes without calling the model. That audit held. This means the behavior is a sound target; it
does not yet tell us which internal computation implements it.

## The exact internal computation being tested

At one attention site $h$ and semantic source role $r$, define:

$$
e_h^x(r)=p_h^x(q,k(r))\,
\mathbf 1[\text{token before source }r=\text{query token}],
$$

$$
u_h^x(r)=W_h^O v_h^x(k(r)).
$$

$e$ is the continuous attention score, restricted to the registered equality-successor edge. $u$ is the source's
value vector after the head's output matrix, so it is already a vector in the 1,152-dimensional residual stream. Their
exact contribution is

$$
T_h^x=\sum_{r\in\{A,C\}} e_h^x(r)u_h^x(r).
$$

For a recipient prompt $x$ and donor prompt $y$, R585 inserts four combinations at L5H5, L7H3, L8H3, and L8H4:

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_h^{xx}&=\sum_r e_h^x(r)u_h^x(r) &&\text{replay},\\
t_h^{yx}&=\sum_r e_h^y(r)u_h^x(r) &&\text{score-only swap},\\
t_h^{xy}&=\sum_r e_h^x(r)u_h^y(r) &&\text{value-only swap},\\
t_h^{yy}&=\sum_r e_h^y(r)u_h^y(r) &&\text{joint swap}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Before intervention, all recipient and donor factors are cached from their native runs. During intervention, the code
removes the current live equality term and inserts one frozen combination. L8H3 and L8H4 are computed from the same
pre-edit layer-8 state so one head's edit cannot silently alter the other head's supposedly fixed factor.

The key evidence is an opposing prediction:

- changing only the selected source should be transferred by the score-only arm while the value-only arm is a no-op;
- changing only the source's payload should be transferred by the value-only arm while the score-only arm remains
  small; and
- changing both should require the joint combination.

This is more informative than ablating a whole head. It can distinguish source selection from copied content even when
both are distributed across several heads.

## What the two-agent review actually caught

The first critic found six problems before a model run:

1. the “non-equality remainder” was defined by subtracting the tested equality term, making its reconstruction check
   circular;
2. the literal 20,736 endpoint-by-site-by-role operations were not frozen and hashed;
3. the bootstrap scorer did not prove that its realized 124 statistics per split equaled the preregistered list;
4. evidence files, checkpoint identity, and the receipt were not validated deeply enough;
5. NaN or infinity values could evade comparisons before publication; and
6. a crash could leave a partial result package that blocked a safe retry.

The builder repaired those. Parent tests passed, but a new critic then found:

1. a completed 459-forward scientific null could still contain no evidence files;
2. evidence IDs could be internally self-consistent yet invented rather than equal to the preregistered rows; and
3. the managed launcher checked that output paths were unused before calling the crash-recovery function, making the
   recovery code unreachable after a hard interruption.

The second repair now requires different exact evidence contracts for the two possible phases:

| Completed phase | Endpoint rows | Directed prompt pairs | Intervention-arm rows | Factor checks |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FIT only | 1,728 | 3,744 | 11,232 | 6,912 |
| FIT + SELECT | 2,592 | 5,616 | 16,848 | 10,368 |

Every ID must equal the independently generated authority list. A scientific null must be as auditable as a held
result. Crash recovery is now reached through the real managed launcher and may quarantine only marker-bound,
recognizable R585 files; it refuses a complete result or arbitrary occupied path without moving it.

Parent validation of the second repair gave 103 acceptance passes with six strict expected failures bound to the two
obsolete blocked commits, plus all 16 live versions of the first blocker tests. Both experiment gates and both advisory
preflights passed. The candidate was committed as `a19c029fd` and was then independently blocked for four additional
reasons:

1. an authorized endpoint ID could contain altered tokens or semantic coordinates;
2. an authorized directed-intervention ID could name invented recipient and donor endpoint IDs;
3. saved removal and insertion-difference vectors could disagree with the exact arm reconstructed from saved $e$ and
   $u$ factors; and
4. a saved answer margin could contradict the saved answer and comparison logits.

These files were the right size, used authorized top-level IDs, and had correct hashes. What was missing was a
recomputation of their meaning. The third repair therefore joins tokens and positions to the frozen endpoint rows,
joins directions to their exact recipient and donor, reconstructs every intervention vector from saved factors, and
recomputes margin, cross-entropy, and vocabulary-change identities from primitive logits. This new failure class is now
also part of the shared agent prompt.

## What the downstream MLP result contributes

R584 exactly split how later bilinear MLPs respond to the broad numbered-list signal. For a background input $x$ and
added signal $\delta$,

$$
M(x+\delta)-M(x)
=D[(L\delta)\odot(Rx)+(Lx)\odot(R\delta)]
+D[(L\delta)\odot(R\delta)].
$$

The first term is the signal interacting with the rest of the MLP input; the second is the signal interacting with
itself. All twelve fixed terms across MLPs 8, 10, 12, and 14 were causally active, but none passed every selectivity and
cross-representation gate. R588 independently audited that FIT-only null.

This says the coarse algebraic split is not yet the right semantic basis. It does not say the numbered-list circuit is
absent. The next version should split the broad signal by downstream use—visible-label identity, successor action, and
copy or arithmetic-conflict action—rather than by MLP or rank.

## A mathematical route beyond native heads

The three-hour mathematical review identified a possible cross-head basis. Let donor example $i$ supply scores,
recipient example $j$ supply values, and fixed downstream readout $w_k$ measure the output. Define

$$
A_{ia}=e_a^i,\qquad B^{(a)}_{jk}=\langle w_k,u_a^j\rangle,
$$

where $a$ indexes a site and semantic role. Then the immediate crossed output is

$$
\mathcal T_{ijk}=\sum_a A_{ia}B^{(a)}_{jk}.
$$

This is a block-term tensor decomposition. Keeping donor, recipient, and output as separate axes can remove ambiguity
that remains if the data are flattened into a matrix. Published uniqueness results give exact conditions under which
the blocks are recoverable up to within-block coordinates. Our current R585 observations are sparse and structured, so
those theorems do not yet apply directly. After valid factor evidence exists, we can construct the complete crossed
tensor from cached $e$ and $u$, test the uniqueness conditions, recover candidate blocks, and then apply the same
held-out transfer and selective-removal tests. Algebraic uniqueness alone will not count as circuit meaning.
