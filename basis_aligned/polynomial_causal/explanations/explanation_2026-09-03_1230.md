# Where bilin18 can be compressed, and where it can't: the exact-rank map (2026-09-03 12:30 UTC)

Plain-language chapter (Claude lane, ledger §2673/§2675/§2676/§2679), expanding project shorthand. Sign
convention: none needed here — these are exact structural facts computed from the weights, not loss numbers.

## The question

The program's goal is a smaller, editable tensor program that still predicts like bilin18. A natural hope was to
find the model's feed-forward blocks (the "MLPs") implemented by a few reusable low-dimensional operations — a
small vocabulary you could store cheaply and edit. Months of effort (the "equality-score" arc) tried to find
such reusable pieces by causal grouping and kept getting nulls. This chapter asks the question exactly, from the
weights, with no noise and no forward passes: is there low-dimensional structure to compress, and if so, where?

## The key object

Each MLP block is bilinear: MLP(z) = Down[(Left z) * (Right z)]. Fix a token and let the context vary; the piece
of the MLP's output that mixes token and context is an exact linear operator K on the context — and crucially,
K is LINEAR in the token's embedding. So the 50,257 token operators are not independent: they all live in a
subspace of dimension at most the model width (1152). The real compression question is therefore not "are two
tokens' operators equal" (they're not — that grouping failed) but "how many dimensions does the whole family of
operators actually span" — its effective rank. This is an exact eigenvalue computation.

## What we found (exact, noise-free)

1. **The MLP token-context operators are HIGH-RANK, in every block (§2673, §2675).** MLP0's family has effective
   rank ~438 of 1152; across all 18 blocks the effective rank runs 438-749, and 90% of the family's energy needs
   611-754 dimensions. Zero of 18 blocks are low-rank. There is no small shared operator vocabulary.

2. **The MLP context-only branch is HIGH-RANK too (§2676).** MLP0's context-times-context quadratic map has
   effective rank ~929 of 1152 — even higher. So MLP0 has no low-dimensional handle in ANY of its exact branches.

3. **Attention is DIFFERENT — it is compressible, because of an architectural bottleneck (§2679).** The attention
   score is a squared-bilinear form built from per-head query/key projections, and each of the 9 heads is a
   128-dimensional bottleneck (9 x 128 = 1152). Measured with the same effective-rank tool, the per-head pattern
   sits at ~69 of the 128 head bound. That head-dim cap is exactly why the earlier "frontier" work could compress
   attention (rank truncation + 16-bit storage gave ~50% byte savings), while it could never compress the MLPs.

## The map, in one sentence

bilin18's compressibility lives in its ATTENTION (head-dim-bottlenecked, genuinely low-dimensional), NOT in its
MLPs (full-width, genuinely high-rank token-context operators). This is the exact structural reason the frontier
succeeded in attention and every attempt to find a small reusable MLP circuit — by grouping (rung525/526/527),
by distributed state (rung528), by shared consensus (rung529, a real-but-uncertified near-miss), or by low rank
(this chapter) — did not.

## Why it matters (the honest bottom line)

A "smaller predictive program" for bilin18 cannot come from compiling the MLPs into a few reusable low-rank
pieces — the exact rank forbids it. The realistic smaller program is: the already-demonstrated attention
compression (the frontier) PLUS a faithful, but genuinely high-rank, MLP surrogate. The MLPs are not hiding a
simple circuit; they are doing high-dimensional token-conditioned context arithmetic, and that is a measured
property, not a failure of search. One important distinction the chapter also draws: being weight-compressible
(few bytes to store, as attention is) is NOT the same as having a small reusable causal circuit — even
compressible attention directions did not yield reusable source-conditioned circuits (rung530). Compression and
circuit-reusability are different axes, and bilin18 offers the first (in attention) but not the second.

Caveat: the operator ranks use the token-embedding input distribution (exact for the first block, a lower-bound
proxy for deeper ones — a richer input can only raise the excited rank, so "high-rank everywhere" is robust).
Nothing here changes the strict explained fraction; it is an exact structural bound that redirects the search.
