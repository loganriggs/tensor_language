# Where the price of shrinking bilin18's writes actually lives (2026-09-03 16:42 UTC)

Plain-language chapter (Claude lane, ledger §2694/§2696/§2697), expanding project shorthand. Sign convention (§2135):
every loss number below is CE ADDED ABOVE THE REAL MODEL on held-out text — LOWER IS BETTER; 0 means "as good as the
original". Nothing here changes the strict explained fraction (5.348% / 10.923% / 4.727 nat / 0 of 68).

## The question

Each of bilin18's 36 sub-blocks (18 attention layers, 18 MLPs) writes a 1152-dimensional vector into the residual
stream at every token. The compressibility chapter (explanation_1230) showed the MLPs' internal operators are
exact-high-rank, so they cannot be compiled into a few reusable low-rank pieces. But that is a statement about what a
block CAN compute. A different question is what the REST OF THE NETWORK actually READS from each block's output. If a
block writes a rich vector but downstream only uses a thin slice, that slice — not the block — is the object to
identify, edit, and eventually replace. §2696 measured this for every write, one at a time.

## The instrument

Fit a principal-component basis for each block's write on documents 0–95; on held-out documents 96–159, replace that
one write by its projection onto its top 32 components (plus its mean), leave everything else exactly native, and
measure the CE added. Repeat for all 36 writes. It is a MAP, not a circuit: one number per site, no joint edits.

## What the map says

1. **Variance does not predict use.** A write's own spectrum (how many directions its variance occupies) is nearly
   uncorrelated with how much the network loses when the write is truncated (rank correlation .23 across 36 sites,
   and NEGATIVE, −.43, inside the MLP family). The registered hypothesis "high-rank writes are expensive" was wrong —
   the eight highest-rank writes (MLPs 7–14, spectra needing 559–679 directions) each cost under .045 nat at rank 32.
   The registered null ("all of them under .10") held.

2. **The price is in the EARLY MLPs.** mlp1 alone costs .883 nat at rank 32; mlp2 .220, mlp0 .165, mlp3 .130. Those four
   blocks are 59% of the summed single-site price (2.371 nat over all 36 sites). Depth is the real predictor (rank
   correlation with layer index −.81 for MLPs, −.88 for attention: the earlier, the pricier).

3. **Attention writes are all cheap** at rank 32 (18 sites sum to .429; the largest, attn0, is .066). attn5 — the
   layer where the compressed "frontier" program pays its largest price — costs only .047 on its own. So that price
   cliff is not a single-site property of attn5's write; it comes from installing many replacements at once together
   with the early MLPs.

4. **The final two MLPs (16, 17) are functionally thin but with a fat tail** (§2694): rank 8 already recovers most of
   MLP16 (.036 added) but MLP17 needs more (.083 at rank 8, .043 at rank 64), and truncating both together costs more
   than the sum of the parts (.172 vs .036 + .083) — the two blocks' outputs are read jointly.

## Why this matters for the program

- The finer-grain work Logan asked for ("specific subspaces, specific interaction terms, smaller than an MLP block") has
  a natural home: blocks 7–17, whose causally-read output is genuinely low-dimensional even though the blocks' internal
  computation is not. Two probes now target exactly that: the write of MLP16/17 decomposed into eight exact quadratic
  forms in the block input (the "interaction terms" — running), and a second-order "Fisher certificate" that predicts
  the price of editing any direction without a forward pass per candidate (registered 16:31).
- The early MLPs 0–3 are the opposite: their writes are causally dense far beyond what truncation at rank 32 can keep.
  They are not approachable by this tool; they need either an exact treatment (the token-context operator arc, §2673/
  §2675) or must be kept whole in any smaller program.
