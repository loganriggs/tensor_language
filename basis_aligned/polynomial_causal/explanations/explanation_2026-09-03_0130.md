# The equality-score gauge: what we learned reverse-engineering bilin18's copy matcher (2026-09-03 01:30 UTC)

## The goal, restated

We are compiling bilin18 (546M params) into a smaller, executable, editable
tensor program whose parts are actual computations — each specifying what it
reads, what it computes, what it writes, and which later parts use that write.
A part earns "circuit" status only by prediction on held-out/OOD text,
extraction, selective removal, predictable composition, and stable
identification — never by low rank, reconstruction error, or average loss
alone.

## The story of one circuit: the equality (copy) matcher

Earlier work found that layer-5-head-5's equality-matching SCORE can drive
layer-8-head-4's own output on held-out text — "same read operation, different
write." That opened a question: how many distinct score computations does the
model really have, and can we group them?

**Result 1 — a validated sign gauge (the strongest positive).** Four attention
heads compute an equality score into the layer-8 recipient. We proved,
prospectively on held-out documents, that they are TWO abstract copy-score
computations up to a Z2 SIGN FLIP: two of them supply the recipient with a
positive scale, the other two require exactly a sign flip, and the copy effect
is otherwise identical. The sign is a real downstream-causal variable (removing
the wrong orientation fails). So the score dictionary is two entries + a sign
bit, not four — a genuine, causally-backed reduction.

**Result 2 — an exhaustive negative below the "action" grain.** We then tried
to group the four implementations at every FINER level inside the recipient's
downstream MLP: whole writes, exact source-pair terms, source families, fitted
latent atoms, all pairs of terms, three exact identity branches, and every one
of 113,568 sparse signed combinations. NONE is a shared, portable variable
across the four implementations. The four score implementations produce the
same downstream EFFECT through internally DIFFERENT computations — the identity
holds at the "action" level but not at any decomposition of it.

**Result 3 — the constructive residue.** The negative is not chaos. The
DIFFERENCE between any two implementations lives in one FIXED low-dimensional
factor direction (query-side terms at attention11, left/right at MLP11) —
identical in shape across all 18 branch/implementation combinations — with a
magnitude that is mostly a per-branch scale (approximately, within ~4%, one
scalar field). And the aggregate copy-TASK effect the program must reproduce is
near-rank-1 across the four implementations: effectively ONE score template
covers ~97% of the copy task, with a stable implementation loading. So the
implementations differ only in a small, fixed-direction, mostly-scalar way that
the downstream reader integrates consistently — which is precisely WHY the
action-level gauge holds.

## What this means for the compiled program

The equality-score dictionary can carry two templates + a sign bit instead of
four (Result 1), and the copy task itself needs effectively one template
(Result 3) — a concrete dictionary saving with causal and task-space backing.
But the four implementations are NOT interchangeable as internal circuits
(Result 2): the compiled program can share the score at the action interface,
not below it.

## Honest boundaries

Every claim here is at the equality-matcher's copy task on the tested corpora;
the payload/output side stays distinct, and none of this touches the model's
larger price gaps (the compiled-program frontier's m16 remainder and
attention-5 write cliff), which are a separate sub-program. The method that
produced these results — freeze a falsifiable prediction with measured bars
before running, gate every instrument with planted-truth recovery, and preserve
every null — is the reason the positive results are trustworthy and the six
negatives are informative rather than wasted.
