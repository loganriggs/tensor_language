# The MLP10 chapter's honest bottom line: real structure, but small — and why (2026-09-03 07:26 UTC)

Plain-language close of the estimation chapter (Claude lane, ledger §2657-§2668), including two self-corrections
and a rigorous red-team of my own coverage claim. Expands project shorthand.

## What we were trying to do

Carve MLP10 (the model's 11th feed-forward block) into reusable pieces by measuring "circuit fingerprints": for
each thing we remove from MLP10's input, a 32-number vector of how much the loss changes on 32 known downstream
circuits (member tokens minus matched controls). For weeks these grouping tests all nulled. This chapter asked
WHY, using estimation theory rather than more grouping.

## The five findings, and the two places I corrected myself

1. **The fingerprints are noise (§2657).** A single 32-circuit fingerprint correlates only 0.016 with itself
   across two disjoint halves of the documents — each circuit's effect is estimated from ~300 tokens and is
   swamped by sampling noise. By classical attenuation, this caps every grouping test near zero regardless of
   truth. So the earlier nulls were estimation-limited, not proof of "no structure."

2. **Pooling recovers a real shared subspace (§2658).** The right estimator doesn't correlate noisy
   fingerprints; it pools them. Because the two halves have independent noise, the cross-half cross-covariance's
   expectation is the SIGNAL covariance (noise averages to zero, not to a positive bias). Pooling all 83 nodes,
   this recovers a reliable ~3-dimensional shared subspace. Real shared signal exists.

3. **First self-correction (§2664): "feeds block-6" was base rate.** I initially reported the shared subspace
   concentrated on block-6 circuits. But block-6 is 12 of the 32 panel circuits (37.5%), and the subspace's
   block-6 energy (1.38) sits inside the random-subset null band (86th percentile, floor at 1.13). Not
   enrichment. I retracted the label; the subspace's EXISTENCE (bootstrap-stable at 0.95/0.97) stood.

4. **Second self-correction (§2665): rung521's DAS failure is broad, not underpowered.** Codex's rung521 gate
   found the whole-attention8 response reproduces across halves but isn't SELECTIVE to individual targets
   (concentration stably ~1.3-1.8x, below the 3.0 bar). I first attributed this to my noise story; checking the
   receipt, the ratios are cross-half reproducible (donor ensembles agree, signed cosine 0.95-0.99), so it's a
   genuine BROAD effect, not low power. Codex was right that more donors won't help. Different object (the whole
   large a8 response) from the small noisy fingerprints — I had conflated them.

5. **The sobering capstone (§2666 then its red-team §2668).** I first measured the shared subspace's coverage as
   76% of "reliable variance." But that used a soft metric with a 58% noise floor. So I re-did it with rigorous
   prequential coding (fit the subspace on half0 ALONE, then predict half1 — the honest, leakage-free test):
   - The best-generalizing rank is ZERO. No low-rank model, fit from one half, pays for its parameters.
   - The held-out captured fraction at rank-3 is only **12%**, not 76%.
   The reconciliation: the 76% was "76% of the (small) noise-unbiased signal"; the 12% is "12% of the actual
   effect energy" — most of which is noise. Both numbers are correct; they have different denominators. The
   honest synthesis: MLP10's reliable shared structure is real and low-dimensional, but it is only estimable by
   cross-half pooling and accounts for just ~12% of the actual per-half effect variation.

## The bottom line (a strategic input, not just MLP10 trivia)

At the current data size, MLP10's reliable causal footprint is one low-dimensional shared summary that captures
~12% of the effect and, coded honestly, saves essentially ZERO bits over a mean-only baseline. In plain terms:
**there is little compressible or cleanly-reusable circuit structure to extract here at current N.** This is not
a failure of method — it is a measured property, and the rigorous (MDL) number is what makes it trustworthy. The
same wall shows up independently in Codex's rung521 (the a8 response is reliable but broad) and rung522 (testing
whether any selective sub-structure survives). The two levers remain: raise the document count by ~26-62x so
per-unit fingerprints become individually reliable (§2659), or find a better object than per-source circuit
effects. Until one of those changes, more per-unit MLP10 analysis will keep hitting the same 12% ceiling.

## Why this chapter is worth trusting

Three of its steps corrected an earlier claim of my own (§2664, §2665, §2668). That is the point of the red-team
lane: the shared subspace EXISTS (that survived every check), but each softer interpretation of it — "feeds
block-6", "captures 76%", "N-limited DAS" — was walked back to what the data actually supports. What remains is
small, real, and honestly bounded.
