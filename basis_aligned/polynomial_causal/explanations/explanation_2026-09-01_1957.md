# Full update since 18:07 — 2026-09-01 19:57 UTC

## Goal and short answer

Our overall goal is to replace bilin18 with a smaller tensor program whose computations we can explain, whose
predictions remain accurate on fresh and shifted text, and whose responses to causal edits remain faithful.

Since the 18:07 explanation, the attention0 work has moved through eight numbered experiments, two named sub-rung
replications/repairs, and the preserved mechanical receipts needed to make rung 424 conform to its frozen
instrument. The central conclusion is now much sharper:

- architectural attention heads are not the best explanatory basis;
- there is real input geometry shared across heads, but it is broad and continuous rather than a small dictionary
  or one low-rank common subspace;
- the task-relevant output payload occupies that same broad token geometry;
- a jointly fitted continuous decomposition of the complete
  `first QK score × second QK score × output payload` product preserves almost all measured downstream computation;
- the result is an identification, not yet a compression, because it still computes the native Q/K/V/O generators.

No adopted whole-model frontier changed during this interval. The current 43-behavior physical byte tier remains
511,758,646 stored scalar values and 1,023,517,292 stored bytes. The new work instead identifies a promising new
attention0 representation that may eventually reduce that price.

## 1. Where the 18:07 update stopped

The 18:07 file had established three relevant facts.

First, separating attention0 contributions by architectural head was not enough. Head 3 carried most of one MLP0
interaction path, but the immediate downstream responses did not reveal a clean set of duplicate head services.

Second, “the same feature” needed to mean “the same under downstream computation,” not “similar vectors in the
residual stream.” If a downstream reader cannot distinguish two directions, they should be eligible for the same
equivalence class even if their native coordinates differ.

Third, section 14 recovered the old sparse QK–OV work. That work learned per-head
`(key branch 1, key branch 2, value)` sparse triplets and applied a CP decomposition to the resulting sparse
third-moment core. It did not learn one cross-head Tucker model. The remaining question was therefore whether the
complete double-QK-and-output calculation admitted a shared cross-head decomposition.

The new experiments answer successive parts of that question.

## 2. Rung 418: no cross-head QK atom vocabulary

Attention0 has nine heads and two multiplicative QK branches. Each branch has query and key token functions. After
folding every one of the 50,257 real token embeddings through the exact layer-0 maps, the experiment compared these
token functions without depending on each head's private coordinate basis.

A token-function subspace is simply a matrix with tokens as rows and scalar functions as columns. Two such spaces
are compared using their projectors or principal angles. This is invariant to rotating or rescaling the private
coordinates inside a head.

The registered atomic-sharing hypothesis failed:

- zero of 306 eligible cross-head branch comparisons reached the required shared-atom threshold;
- the best complete branch-score prediction explained 43.6% of held-out variation, below the 60% bar;
- the best single-factor prediction reached 49.9%, just below its 50% bar.

But the null was not “nothing is shared.” Cross-head token-function prediction generally explained roughly 42–50%,
and the mean projector overlap was 19.11% with a standard deviation near 1.0 percentage point. Token permutation
gave only about 0.3% overlap. Thus sharing is real, uniform, and diffuse; it does not appear as a small set of exact
atoms.

## 3. Rungs 419, 419b, and 421: the output payload transports, but it is not a discrete vocabulary

The next test folded each head's exact value and output maps and kept the 16-dimensional output interface that is
most useful to MLP0 and attention1. For head `h` and source token `t`, this payload is

`p_h(t) = U16^T O_h V_h RMSNorm(embedding(t))`.

`V_h` is the head's value map, `O_h` is its block inside the attention output map, and `U16` is the fixed
1,152-by-16 task interface. This gives 16 numbers per head and token.

The experiment compared two ways to spend 256 codebook centers:

- one global codebook shared by all heads;
- an optimally allocated collection of private per-head codebooks with the same total number of centers.

The proposed discrete vocabulary failed. The global codebook reduced held-out distortion by only 1.971% relative
to the private allocation, versus a registered 15% target. Only 6.64% of centers had substantial use from at least
three heads. Independently permuting token identities inside each head reproduced almost the entire global advantage
(1.963%), and ordinary Euclidean geometry gave a slightly larger 2.290% advantage. The apparent sharing was pooled
allocation geometry, not a common semantic atom dictionary.

Head 3 used 157 of the private control's 256 centers. This was another sign that head 3's output behavior is unusually
private even when its inputs later prove highly shared.

One different result survived: the global approximation transported through the native double-QK routing and
downstream readers. On the original SELECT rows it explained 70.05% of the routed U16 write and 75.93% of the mean
six-reader response, about 5.5 percentage points above the private arm. Its language-model cross-entropy damage was
+0.00630 nat versus +0.00776 nat for the private arm; lower is better.

The original rung-419 receipt also failed an absolute floating-point replay check even though its relative squared
error was 4.4e-13. Rung 419b explicitly added the measured numerical remainder and obtained exact replay. This
repaired only the instrument; the discrete-vocabulary null remained unchanged.

Because the surviving transport result had passed close to one threshold, rung 421 rebuilt the same deterministic
instrument and evaluated 96 documents with zero row overlap with all rung-419 roles. The result replicated:

| fresh-row measurement | global | private | global advantage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| routed U16 R² | 69.064% | 63.340% | 5.724 points |
| mean downstream R² | 74.610% | 68.798% | 5.812 points |

The global codebook also had lower CE damage in both independent 48-document waves. We can therefore use the U16
interface as a real downstream measurement interface. We still cannot call its discrete centers a vocabulary.

## 4. Rungs 420 and 422: there is no privileged rank-24 common carrier

The 19% pairwise QK overlap suggested a simple model: perhaps every 128-dimensional head branch contained one
common subspace of dimension approximately `0.19 × 128 ≈ 24`, plus an independent private remainder.

Rung 420 formed the leading rank-24 eigenspace of the average token-function projector. The estimator was stable,
and the query-side and key-side carriers had 61.2% overlap with one another. Nevertheless, the simple carrier model
failed:

| held-out measurement | query side | key side | registered target |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| branch variation captured | 57.4% | 54.7% | at least 65% |
| carrier-only pair overlap | 31.6% | 28.0% | at least 70% |
| overlap remaining after removal | 14.285% | 14.308% | at most 3% |

The remaining overlap is far above the roughly 0.3% token-permutation floor. Removing the carrier helps more than
removing an arbitrary subspace, but it does not isolate all sharing.

Rung 422 then swept carrier ranks 24, 40, 56, 72, 88, and 104 without changing the object. The residual overlap fell
almost linearly, by about 0.0014 per added rank, and remained approximately 3.13% even at rank 104 of 128. Query and
key curves agreed within 0.001. There was no cliff or privileged dimension. Sharing is spread over most of the token
function space.

The positive MLP0 connection also survived. The rank-24 QK carriers overlapped MLP0's complete degree-one token
action by 76.4% and 73.0%, while overlap with its nonlinear token-only remainder was only 0.044% and 0.022%.
However, they aligned even more strongly with MLP0's normalized input itself, at 83.5–84.6%. The careful conclusion
is that QK and the linear part of MLP0 inherit a broad shared token-input geometry—not that a special MLP0 feature
was found.

## 5. Rungs 423 and 423b: QK and OV use the same broad token geometry

Rung 423 directly compared the per-head U16 payload token functions from the OV side with the QK carrier from the
input side. This was the missing link between the two arcs.

Across query/key sides and FIT/SELECT token splits, mean payload-to-carrier overlap was 20.5–21.7%. Matched random
and token-permuted controls were approximately 0.06–0.24%. The real alignment is therefore about 90–370 times the
control, depending on the split. Per-head ordering was stable across token splits.

One randomized carrier solve was slightly unstable, so the original A instrument predicate failed. Rung 423b
replaced only that randomized solve with an exact deterministic eigendecomposition. All instrument checks then
passed, and every substantive overlap reproduced within 0.002. The cross-arc connection is not solver noise.

The registered privacy prediction was wrong in an informative way. Head 3 was predicted to have the least carrier
overlap because its downstream output was private. Instead:

- heads 3 and 7 were the strongest carrier users, near 40% overlap;
- head 6 was consistently the carrier outsider, near 7–9%;
- MLP0 degree-one alignment showed the same head ordering.

Input sharing and output substitutability are therefore different questions. Head 3 can read a strongly shared
input geometry and still produce an output service that other heads cannot replace.

## 6. Rung 424: a near-lossless continuous decomposition of the complete product

The decisive test stopped treating a head as the feature unit. For natural edge `n`, head `h`, and task-output
coordinate `c`, it used the exact product

`e[n,h,c] = s1[n,h] × s2[n,h] × p[n,h,c]`.

Here `s1` and `s2` are the two rotary QK scores and `p` is the exact U16 payload. Every causal source was included at
query positions 16, 32, ..., 240 in 96 FIT and 96 disjoint SELECT documents.

Three equal-size arms used six modes across the nine `s1` head scores, six across the nine `s2` scores, and 32 modes
across the 144 head-by-U16 payload coordinates:

1. marginal PCA fitted the three projections separately;
2. joint fitting optimized the same projections for the complete product and its head-summed downstream response;
3. a wrong-pairing control retained all fitted modes but paired branch-2 head `h+4` with branch-1 head `h`.

The final held-out result was:

| measurement | marginal PCA | joint product | wrong pairing |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| summed-edge relative MSE | 31.675% | **0.972%** | 162.977% |
| routed U16 R² | 70.787% | **98.525%** | -71.812% |
| mean six-reader R² | 75.874% | **99.285%** | -48.856% |
| CE damage | +0.004356 nat | **+0.000200 nat** | +0.550677 nat |

Every individual reader—MLP0, both query and both key branches of attention1, and attention1's fresh-value
branch—had joint R² between 99.187% and 99.445%. Independent FIT-half subspace overlaps were 94.15%, 98.86%, and
90.22% for the two score projections and payload projection.

The wrong-pairing control is crucial. It keeps the same values, ranks, spectra, and parameter count, but destroys
the correct three-way contraction and performs worse than writing zero. Therefore the result is not merely “keep
the highest-variance directions.” It identifies the pairing between the two score feature sets and output features.

Three pre-scientific receipts were preserved. The first compared two different hash conventions. The second exposed
a nominal float32 eigenbasis whose maximum orthogonality error was 3.91e-5, above the frozen 2e-5 limit. The final
repair QR-orthogonalized the same eigenspace, reducing error to 3.58e-7 without changing data, ranks, seeds,
objectives, controls, prices, or scientific bars. In the conforming receipt A/B/C/D all hold and the strong null is
false.

This is still not a compressed model. The 23,310-number screen retains every native Q/K/V/O generator and therefore
saves zero model values. It identifies what should be generated directly next.

## 7. Rung 425: the complete block replicates on entirely unused documents

Before building a physical factorization on rung 424, a fresh-row guard refitted the unchanged pipeline on FIT,
reproduced the original SELECT result exactly, and evaluated 96 documents from a different row receipt. These rows
had zero overlap with rung 424's FIT/SELECT roles and also did not overlap rung 421's earlier fresh slice.

The off-row result was:

| fresh measurement | marginal PCA | joint product | wrong pairing |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| summed-edge relative MSE | 34.362% | **1.033%** | 165.524% |
| routed U16 R² | 68.819% | **98.396%** | -73.905% |
| mean six-reader R² | 73.350% | **99.208%** | -50.736% |
| CE damage | +0.006430 nat | **+0.000172 nat** | +0.430913 nat |

Every individual joint-reader R² remained between 99.091% and 99.383%. All registered instrument, bridge,
fresh-row, and control-ordering predicates hold; the row-specificity null is false. This removes the concern that
rung 424 found an unusually favorable 96-document SELECT sample. It still does not license compression because the
same native generators remain in the screen.

## 8. Combined interpretation

The seven experiments rule out three simple stories:

1. heads are reusable modules that should be clustered as wholes;
2. cross-head sharing is a finite atom vocabulary;
3. one small global carrier plus independent private remainders explains QK.

The surviving representation is a continuous block model. One first-score feature may combine with several
second-score features and several outputs; conversely, different input pairs may lead to the same downstream output
mode. Schematically,

`edge ≈ sum_{r,s,u} core[r,s,u] × score_feature1[r] × score_feature2[s] × output_feature[u]`.

The learned subspaces are safe under private head-coordinate changes because the comparison is made on complete
token functions, realized edge products, and downstream responses. The head index remains a contraction index, not
the proposed semantic basis.

## 9. What the 62 circuit checks actually are

The project often calls them “62 circuit certificates,” but **62 circuit behavior checks** is more accurate. They
are 62 registered behavior tags with sets of member token positions and a reference effect obtained from a native
mean-ablation experiment. They are not 62 mathematical proofs, and not every tag has a complete human-language
semantic interpretation.

Let `M_c` be the member positions for behavior tag `c`. Let

`d_i = CE_candidate(i) - CE_native(i)`

be the candidate's cross-entropy change at token position `i`, and let `r_c` be the behavior's stored native
mean-ablation reference magnitude. The current check is

`v_c = mean_{i in M_c} |d_i| / (0.5 × r_c)`.

Behavior `c` passes when `v_c < 1`. Thus “43/62” means 43 tags have candidate damage below half of their native
ablation reference. Lower CE damage is better. The check concerns an unablated candidate's prediction fidelity on
behavior-relevant positions; it does not by itself prove that the candidate reproduces a signed causal edit. That is
why separate original-native intervention gates are still required.

The full 62-dimensional vector `v`, rather than only its thresholded count, is the useful object for decomposition.
It records which downstream behaviors distinguish a candidate's error.

## 10. Using the 62 behaviors to find the right decomposition

Yes—this is a promising extension of the user's downstream-discrimination idea. It should be used as a quotient or
metric, not simply as a leaderboard count.

For a local attention feature or error direction `u`, define its downstream response vector

`Phi(u) = [response on behavior 1, ..., response on behavior 62]`.

Two local features are equivalent for this battery when `Phi(u-v)` is small. This can merge native directions that
look different geometrically but have the same downstream consequences, and it can separate directions that look
similar locally but affect different behaviors.

There are two practical versions.

### 10.1 Finite-response version

Insert or remove one candidate block at a time and record its signed per-position loss change. Aggregate those
changes into 62 normalized response coordinates. Then decompose the complete QK1×QK2×OV tensor using similarity in
this response space. This is the most faithful version, but it requires many full forwards.

### 10.2 Circuit-weighted local metric

At each registered member position, compute the local derivative `J_i` mapping an attention0 edge-output error to
the final loss or logits. A tag-balanced quadratic metric is

`G_62 = sum_c [1 / (|M_c| × tau_c²)] sum_{i in M_c} J_i^T J_i`,

where `tau_c = 0.5 × r_c`. If positions occur in several tags, their weights must be divided by their tag overlap so
they are not counted repeatedly. A candidate edge error `delta e` then has approximate circuit cost

`(delta e)^T G_62 (delta e)`.

This has the same mathematical form as the six-reader response metric already used in rung 424, but its readers are
the model's final behavior-sensitive losses rather than only MLP0 and attention1. We can therefore fit the same
rank-6/rank-6/rank-32 block model under `G_62`, then test exact forward behavior.

## 11. Why the raw 62-count cannot choose the decomposition by itself

Across the existing pure-QK rank ladder, the 62-dimensional normalized damage vectors lie almost on one ray:
the measured R² is 0.99945, and leaving out one rank still predicts its thresholded behavior count exactly. The
same ray predicts several QK-plus-MLP compositions within roughly zero to two checks.

That means a decomposition can gain checks merely by reducing overall error magnitude while leaving the pattern of
behavior damage unchanged. Optimizing “number below threshold” would then rediscover generic fidelity, not a better
mechanistic basis. The count is also discontinuous and easy to overfit near thresholds.

A circuit-guided decomposition should count as specifically better only if, at matched storage and matched overall
CE damage, it improves the held-out 62-vector beyond this one-dimensional prediction. Evidence would include:

- extra held-out behavior checks beyond the count predicted from aggregate CE intensity;
- a smaller normalized damage vector on behaviors that the ordinary PCA decomposition misses;
- an advantage over a control that randomly permutes behavior tags while preserving member counts and thresholds;
- preservation on new member positions or an independently regenerated behavior census;
- no regression in the separate signed intervention gate.

If the circuit-weighted model stays on the same universal ray, or a tag-permuted metric performs equally well, then
the 62 battery is useful for final validation but not for discovering this decomposition.

## 12. A leakage-safe experiment

The existing 62 battery has already influenced many model choices, so evaluating a newly circuit-optimized model on
the same positions would be post-selection. A clean test should proceed as follows:

1. Freeze the 62 tag definitions, native ablations, normalization rule, and price before fitting.
2. Split member documents—not individual overlapping positions—into FIT and SELECT roles with zero document and row
   overlap. Recompute local response weights only on FIT.
3. Compare equal-rank and equal-price decompositions:
   ordinary marginal PCA, rung-424's six-reader joint metric, the new 62-behavior metric, and a tag-permuted control.
4. Select no ranks or thresholds from SELECT. Evaluate complete edge error, routed response, exact CE, the continuous
   62-vector, and thresholded count on SELECT.
5. Re-instantiate the same behavior definitions on fresh FineWeb or shifted WikiText positions before any adoption
   claim.
6. If one physical generator is selected, run the existing signed intervention and composition gates unchanged.

This gives the 62 behaviors the right role: they define which distinctions matter globally, while held-out members
and controls determine whether that information found a genuinely better basis.

## 13. Current next steps

The immediate physical problem has two parts.

The output side is algebraically straightforward but not sufficient by itself. Once the 32 payload modes are
frozen, their head/output loadings can be folded through the exact attention0 V and O maps to produce a direct
1,152-to-32 generator. However, attention0's value map also creates the full 1,152-dimensional first-value broadcast
consumed by later layers. Earlier work showed that this broadcast is exactly token-determined, but storing it as a
50,257-by-1,152 lookup table is larger than the native map. Rung 424 measures only the output-projected U16 path; it
does not replace or compress the full broadcast. A physical payload candidate must therefore retain that native
broadcast, factor it separately, or prove that a smaller shared generator preserves both roles. The cheap U16 fold
alone cannot be billed as a complete attention0 value-map saving.

The score side is harder. Each head performs its own RMS normalization before each bilinear QK dot product, so a
linear combination of head scores cannot simply be rewritten as one ordinary query and key pair. The next screen
must compare a direct composite-bilinear factorization with two exact older baselines. The shared-QK program already
uses one rank-384 residual-state encoder plus four Q/K/Q2/K2 decoders per site at a `5 × 1,152 × 384` factor price;
it retained about 99.4% of the attention subsystem on two held-out roles. A matched independent program retained
about 99.84% at the larger `8 × 1,152 × 384` factor price. The newer adopted context-QK family instead factors
individual head maps at width 64. Any rung-424-derived generator must beat these constructions at an equal literal
price, not rediscover a shared input encoder under a new name. The circuit-weighted metric should be one arm of that
comparison, not a replacement for literal price, fresh-text CE, or causal validation.

The highest-information decision is therefore:

- can directly executable composite score and payload generators reproduce the rung-424 block without native
  per-head generators;
- and, at equal price and aggregate CE, does the 62-behavior response metric choose a decomposition whose held-out
  behavior vector is better than the universal damage-ray prediction?

A positive result would connect the user's desired interaction-determined basis to an actual compression candidate.
A negative result would still be useful: it would show that rung 424 is a compact downstream readout of an expensive
native computation, not a compact generator of that computation.
