# Circuit-only update — 2026-09-05 07:13 UTC

## Current goal

The active agenda is to build a reusable circuit-analysis codebase and use it to produce hundreds of high-quality,
nonduplicated causal circuits. A useful circuit should explain what information is read, what computation is performed,
what is written, and how later parts of the model use it. It should predict held-out or out-of-distribution examples and
support a targeted interchange or removal without substantially changing unrelated behavior.

Rank reduction, quantization, activation variance, and activation reconstruction are not current goals. In particular,
accurately reconstructing an activation does not show that a proposed component explains task behavior. The primary
measurements are changes in cross-entropy loss, answer-logit margins, counterfactual transfer, and selective removal.

The serial target remains one honest basic circuit screen or informative null in about ten minutes. A cheap screen gets
only the checks required to establish that the model performs the behavior, the intervention is live, and the simplest
shortcut is tested. Larger confirmation suites are reserved for candidates that first show a signal.

## A reusable control warning

The audit of the common fast-screen framework found that the old absolute control threshold did not discriminate among
the 14 available screens. Controls that changed the endpoint answer moved by about the full target effect, while
same-answer controls never exceeded 0.122 against a threshold of 0.35. Therefore, passing that threshold did not by
itself establish that unrelated behavior was spared.

The publisher now emits a warning whenever a control holds the endpoint answer fixed. Such a result is described as
answer-preserving invariance, not as cross-behavior selectivity. A stronger claim requires an active unrelated behavior
whose measured output could actually change. This guard costs no model forwards.

## Exact shared/type decomposition of the bracket-opener write

Previous removal showed that the semantic-opener contribution of attention head L13H8 was necessary but broad: removing
it damaged both the target closer choice and matched same-state controls. We tested whether this write consists of a
shared “a closer is pending” part plus a delimiter-specific part.

For the same lexical template written with parentheses, square brackets, or quotes, let

$$
t_{g,c}=p_{g,c}(q,o)W_Ov_{g,c}(o)
$$

be the exact contribution from opener position $o$ to final query position $q$. Here $p(q,o)$ is the attention
probability assigned to the opener, $v(o)$ is that opener's value vector, and $W_O$ maps the head value into the
1,152-dimensional residual stream. For each matched three-delimiter group $g$, define

$$
\mu_g=\frac{t_{g,\mathrm{paren}}+t_{g,\mathrm{square}}+t_{g,\mathrm{quote}}}{3},
\qquad
\delta_{g,c}=t_{g,c}-\mu_g.
$$

$\mu_g$ is the arithmetic part shared by the three delimiter variants; $\delta_{g,c}$ is the type-specific difference.
Their sum exactly reconstructs the natural write, and the three differences sum to zero. This fixes the decomposition
by symmetry rather than fitting a low-rank subspace.

The causal arms were:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{natural type swap} &: h-t_c+t_{c'},\\
\text{remove type difference} &: h-t_c+\mu_g,\\
\text{remove shared part} &: h-\mu_g.
\end{aligned}
$$

The experiment used 24 fresh prompts forming eight complete aligned delimiter triplets. All five conditions were batched
into five model forwards. Exact replay error was zero.

## Result: the centered parts are causal, but the clean output split is false

The intervention was live. Natural type swaps moved the donor closer in the predicted direction on every row. Removing
the type difference damaged the native closer direction on every row. The normalized type-removal effect was also stable
under the matched wording changes: the median absolute difference was 0.030, far below the preregistered maximum of 0.25.

The stronger claim required the type-specific intervention to move the centered closer-type score at least twice as
much as the mean of all three closer logits. It passed for direct prompts at 2.188 but missed for completed-and-reopened
prompts at 1.864. Removing the proposed shared part was also not cleanly common-mode: its common-to-type effect ratio was
1.527 rather than the required 2.0.

This is an informative null, not a threshold to relax. The arithmetic shared and type-specific vectors have repeatable
causal effects, but later computation does not map them into independent “common support” and “delimiter identity” output
axes. The result is stored in
`circuits/fast_screens/bracket_l13h8_semantic_open_shared_contrast_v1_result.json` with SHA-256
`37014b88e9341d63626d5152fca87e2df7de90c7dc76e2b309935bc330806c3a`.

## Immediate next computation

The next screen uses fresh matched triplets and adds only the condition where both $\mu_g$ and $\delta_{g,c}$ are
removed. This gives the exact two-factor interaction after the head write. For an output score $F$, the interaction is

$$
I = F(\mu+\delta)-F(\mu)-F(\delta)+F(0).
$$

If $I$ is small, the two components are causally additive but our chosen output coordinates are oblique. If $I$ is
large, downstream nonlinear computation genuinely composes the shared and type-specific parts. This directly studies
circuit composition and costs one additional forward; TEST and OOD data remain closed.

## 07:21 result: the downstream interaction is small

The fresh SELECT run passed every instrument check, and natural delimiter swaps again moved the donor closer in the
predicted direction on every target row. The normalized interaction magnitude was:

| prompt family | median interaction as a fraction of total opener effect |
|---|---:|
| direct pending opener | 7.34% |
| completed delimiter, then reopened | 6.21% |
| matched wording rewrite | 6.91% |
| matched punctuation rewrite | 6.15% |

All four are far below the preregistered 25% boundary. Therefore, the exact shared vector $\mu$ and type-specific
vector $\delta$ act approximately additively through the downstream model. The earlier clean-axis null is not caused
by a large nonlinear interaction between them. Instead, the simple “correct closer versus the mean closer logit” and
“mean closer logit” coordinates are oblique to the way later layers read these vectors.

This narrows the next circuit problem: find the later computations that read $\mu$ and $\delta$, and define their
meaning by those downstream uses. That is the interaction-determined basis discussed previously. It may group several
native heads or split one head further, but it will be judged by task prediction and causal interchange/removal rather
than by activation rank. The SELECT result is
