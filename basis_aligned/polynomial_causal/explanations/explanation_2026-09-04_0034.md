## The overall goal

We are trying to replace the model with a smaller, understandable tensor program whose parts are actual computations,
not merely low-rank directions. A satisfactory circuit should eventually:

1. say what information is read, what operation is performed, and what is written;
2. merge pieces of different heads or MLPs when downstream computation treats them as the same variable, and split a
   native module when its pieces do different jobs;
3. predict activations and behavioral effects on held-out and out-of-distribution inputs;
4. reproduce the target computation when isolated with a clearly specified background;
5. support selective removals, swaps, or edits without damaging unrelated computations;
6. explain how recovered pieces compose and are reused; and
7. remain stable across data splits, restarts, and equivalent internal coordinate choices.

Storage, runtime, rank, cross-entropy, and reconstruction error are prices and controls. They do not by themselves
identify a circuit.

## What R591 actually computed

R591 made 234 forward passes on the FIT split only. It did no training, opened no SELECT or OOD data, and was forbidden
from producing a scientific R585 result. It repeatedly compared final vocabulary logits under carefully separated
conditions:

- **Native:** run the untouched model.
- **Read-only observation:** record the proposed attention factors but return the untouched attention output.
- **Replay:** reconstruct the recipient's proposed equality-copy term and insert it back.
- **Fixed-shape membership comparison:** change which examples share a batch while keeping the tensor dimensions fixed.
- **Padding comparison:** run the same examples with their natural length versus padding every sequence to length 30.

The numerical boundary was a maximum absolute final-logit difference of $10^{-5}$. This is not a cross-entropy
threshold. If any vocabulary logit differs by more than this in a condition that should be a no-op, later causal
effects cannot be attributed cleanly to the intended intervention.

### Why algebraically equal replay was not numerically equal

At one head, the equality-copy contribution can be evaluated in either order:

$$
W_O\left(\sum_r e_r v_r\right)
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\sum_r e_r(W_Ov_r).
$$

Here $e_r$ is a scalar attention coefficient, $v_r\in\mathbb R^{128}$ is the head's value vector at source role $r$,
and $W_O$ maps that value to the 1,152-dimensional residual stream. The two expressions are equal over real numbers.
On the GPU they perform multiplications and additions in a different order, so rounding differs. The largest local
difference was $9.16\times10^{-5}$ at L8H3, and later layers propagated part of it to the logits.

This is not useful floating-point compression or an interpretable feature. It is measurement error caused by changing
the implementation of a supposed no-op.

### Why padding matters even though padding tokens are not attended to

Changing the padded tensor width can make the GPU select different matrix-multiplication kernels or reduction layouts.
That changes rounding even when the added positions are causally masked. R591 showed that changing the *other examples*
in a fixed $[32,30]$ batch had exactly zero effect, so the problem is physical tensor geometry, not cross-example
interaction.

## The repaired R592 computation

For endpoint $x$, head/site $h$, and each of the two registered matching-source roles $r\in\{A,C\}$, define

$$
e^x_{h,r}=\text{attention coefficient on source role }r,
$$

$$
u^x_{h,r}=W_h^Ov_h^x(r)\in\mathbb R^{1152}.
$$

$e$ says how strongly the head selects a source; $u$ is the content that source would write after the head's output
matrix. The registered equality contribution is

$$
B_h(E_x,U_x)=\sum_{r\in\{A,C\}}e^x_{h,r}u^x_{h,r}.
$$

For recipient prompt $x$ and donor prompt $y$, R592 changes the native output by one centered difference:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\text{selection}} &= B(E_y,U_x)-B(E_x,U_x),\\
\Delta_{\text{content}} &= B(E_x,U_y)-B(E_x,U_x),\\
\Delta_{\text{both}} &= B(E_y,U_y)-B(E_x,U_x).
\end{aligned}
$$

Replay is created as a literal zero tensor rather than evaluating $B(E_x,U_x)-B(E_x,U_x)$ twice. Every directed
batch is run five times—native, zero replay, selection, content, and both—with byte-identical tokens, ordering, batch
size, padding, and query positions. All sequences have physical width 30. SELECT's final batch remains 16 real rows;
it is not filled with duplicates.

The price is exactly

$$
54+5(117)=639\quad\text{FIT forwards},
$$

$$
27+5(59)=322\quad\text{SELECT forwards},
$$

or 961 forwards maximum, with no backward pass or weight update.

### What result would support the proposed decomposition?

- When only the selector changes, the selection intervention should transfer the answer and the content intervention
  should be a no-op.
- When copied content changes but the match structure is preserved, the content intervention should transfer the answer
  and the selection intervention should stay small.
- Changing both should equal the two individual changes plus the bilinear interaction

$$
\Delta_{EU}=\sum_r(e^y_r-e^x_r)(u^y_r-u^x_r).
$$

- These effects must repeat by direction and prompt condition, survive the frozen controls and grouped bootstrap, avoid
  broad vocabulary damage, and repeat on SELECT without refitting.

The last equation is exact finite-difference accounting for two interacting mediators. It prevents the effect that
requires both selection and content from being silently credited to either one alone.

### What R592 still cannot prove

R592 changes a partial contribution to the output of four heads. It does not guarantee that any real query/key state
would produce exactly those changed coefficients while keeping every other attention coefficient fixed. It also does
not remove the full native attention computation, isolate a sufficient circuit, or show that these four head labels are
the best semantic basis.

The new mathematical review makes one of those limitations testable. If $F(z)$ is the complete attention-coefficient
vector produced by a candidate query/key state $z$, and $d$ is R592's desired coefficient change, compute the Jacobian
$J=\partial F/\partial z$ and solve

$$
\delta z=J^+d,
\qquad
\rho=\frac{\|J\delta z-d\|_2}{\|d\|_2}.
$$

$J^+$ is the pseudoinverse, which returns the smallest first-order state change that best matches $d$. A large residual
$\rho$ would show that the surgical coefficient change is not even locally realizable by that state. A small residual
would justify a stricter nonlinear state-swap test; it would not alone prove success.
