The 20:54 explanation contained good proposals, but it blurred three statuses: completed experiments, conditional next
steps, and research designs that had not yet been run. Two important items were indeed left as prose:

- the Archetypal-SAE comparison intended to address unstable sparse Q/K atoms;

## What rung439 computes

For every rung430 query or key decoder atom `d`, rung439 solves an approximate convex projection using Frank–Wolfe:

`min_c ||c-d||^2`, with `c` in the convex hull of all FIT token rows.

Every Frank–Wolfe step scans the complete FIT set, chooses one real token row, and updates nonnegative mixture weights
that sum to one. There is no hidden nearest-neighbor pool. Because sparse coefficients can absorb positive scaling,
the deployed decoder stores the normalized direction `c/||c||`, while the unnormalized `c` and its mixture weights
are retained as the hull certificate.

The registered arms are an unsigned hull, a signed hull, a fixed relaxed signed hull, and an entry-permuted-token
hull. The last control preserves each head/branch marginal distribution but destroys the fact that all entries came
from one token. A real geometric result therefore requires lower projection error than that matched control for both
query and key sides.

Atom identification is not scored with raw Q/K decoder cosine. Each atom is converted into its exact score vector
against frozen real token anchors and rotary offsets. Independent restarts are matched in this observable profile
space. The run also applies legal phase rotations that commute with rotary position encoding; score profiles must be
unchanged. This prevents a private-coordinate convention from masquerading as identifiability.

Finally, the hull decoders retain rung430's exact sparse supports, coefficients, biases, and 15,583,320-byte deployed
price. Their full score pattern, attention write, six downstream responses, and suffix cross-entropy are measured on
the 96-row FINAL role that rungs426 and 430 did not open. Stability alone cannot pass the experiment.

## The learned-simplicity experiment that still needs implementation

The protocol in `SIMPLICITY_CONSEQUENCE_VALIDATION_V1.md` is an experiment design, not a result. Its first executable
version will create one row per *candidate program*, not one row per document. Each row has:

- pre-outcome structural measurements: complete producer/decoder/index price, operations, interface dimension,
  graph edges, gauge-safe dimension, locality, reuse, conditioning, and validation causal distortion;
- separately stored consequences: OOD transport, extraction accuracy, signed removal fidelity, non-target
  collateral, and composition prediction error;
- circuit family, module, grammar, seed, chronological availability time, and whether the experiment was selected
  adaptively.

The first backtest will freeze a historical cutoff, fit only on candidate results available before that time, and
predict later whole-circuit outcomes while holding out entire module/grammar families. It must beat bytes alone,
rank alone, sparse-edge count, and shuffled labels at matched validation causal distortion. Because historical
experiments were adaptively chosen, a positive backtest is only a filter. It may freeze a rule for one prospective
circuit-family prediction; it cannot establish the rule by itself.

The anti-Goodhart separation is physical: structural features and outcome labels will be different files, and the
sealed-family label file will not be loaded by the training/selection program. A rule revised after seeing a sealed
failure becomes a new generation and needs a new sealed family.

## Rung439b result

The first receipt failed only its gauge instrument because one side used BF16 rotary tables. It remains preserved as
invalid. A separately registered repair recomputed both sides of the mathematical gauge identity with exact float64
rotary tables, reducing the discrepancy to `2.22e-16` without changing any scientific value.

The real-token signed hull is genuinely different from the destroyed-token control. Mean squared projection residual
is `.7055` versus `.8938` on queries and `.7062` versus `.8923` on keys—about a 21% improvement on both sides.
This confirms that whole-token convex geometry exists.

It is not a useful causal basis. On the unopened FINAL documents:

| arm | score-product error | attention-write error | mean six-reader error | CE damage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| unchanged score-trained `U54` | `.1892` | `.3407` | `.2305` | `+.02534` nat |
| strict signed hull `SH54` | `2.4681` | `1.4582` | `.5294` | `+.05539` nat |
| 25%-relaxed hull `RSH54` | `1.2980` | `.9432` | `.4188` | `+.04119` nat |

Observable restart cosine improves from `.288/.313` to `.470/.512` for query/key atoms, but misses the registered
`.60` floor. Projections from disjoint FIT halves reach `.751/.769`, below `.80`. Thus the hull makes atoms somewhat
more conventional and data-grounded, but not stable enough to identify and far less faithful to the computation.
A/B pass, C/D fail, and the strong null fires. The conditional extraction/removal gate is not run.

This closes convex anchoring of the raw folded factor rows at this 512-atom/k27+k27 object. It does not close a
dictionary whose atoms live directly in a downstream causal-response quotient; that is a different representation.
