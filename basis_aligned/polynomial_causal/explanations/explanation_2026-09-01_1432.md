# Plain-English MLP0 token-group update — 2026-09-01 14:32 UTC

## Question

The exact token-only split showed that MLP0 writes a shared operating point `M`, a compact token-identifying linear
modulation `L`, and an additional quadratic correction `Q`. The next question was whether multiple tokens have the
same downstream effect even when their `L` or `Q` vectors are stored in substantially different directions.

## A deliberately hard interchange test

We kept fitting tokens and held-out tokens completely separate. For each held-out receiver token, a candidate donor
had to satisfy component cosine `<=0.50`; this rules out simply choosing another token stored in nearly the same
direction. We selected donors whose conditional effect was nearest in attention1 or MLP1, then physically swapped
only that donor component into the receiver's otherwise native `M+L+Q` computation.

Selection in attention1 was validated in MLP1, and selection in MLP1 was validated in attention1. Raw-embedding
nearest neighbors, component-vector nearest neighbors, and fixed random donors were controls. Every held-out token
had eligible far-direction donors, the maximum selected component cosine was 0.4999999, and the live-action error was
about `3e-8`.

## Result

The exactness and widespread-niche predicates held, but the registered global `L` and `Q` equivalence predicates
failed. The strong null did not fire because there is real signal; it is not strong enough to support a general token
partition.

### Linear modulation `L`

| donor rule | physical attention1 cosine | physical MLP1 cosine | both-consumer cosine ≥0.8 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| attention-effect nearest, component cosine ≤0.5 | **0.837** | 0.651 | 5.30% |
| MLP-effect nearest, component cosine ≤0.5 | 0.683 | **0.719** | 9.18% |
| component-vector nearest | **0.880** | **0.835** | **59.43%** |
| raw-embedding nearest | 0.656 | 0.691 | 18.84% |
| random donor | 0.193 | 0.166 | 0.21% |

Far-direction effect matching is much better than random and reveals real local pairs, but it does not beat the
simple component-vector-nearest control. It also missed the frozen cross-consumer floors and raw-neighbor margins.
The broad conclusion is that `L` has some many-to-one effects, but its useful equivalence is mostly already reflected
in where the component is stored.

### Quadratic correction `Q`

Attention-effect-selected far donors preserve attention direction at cosine 0.777 but transfer to MLP1 at only
0.338, with negative global R². MLP-effect-selected donors transfer back to attention at 0.697, but preserve MLP1 at
only 0.492. Only 0.43%–1.44% of these swaps preserve both consumers above 0.8. The global `Q` equivalence predicate
therefore fails: its causally correct effect is predominantly private to the particular token.

## The interesting local niches

The negative global result should not erase the strongest individual pairs. The far-direction constraint still
found physically successful examples such as:

- ` primarily` ↔ ` largely`;
- ` Although` ↔ `While`;
- ` 1950` ↔ ` 1938` or ` 1959`;
- ` 1926` ↔ ` 1979`;
- ` 1934` ↔ ` 1925`.

These are exactly the kind of semantic equivalences we hoped could exist outside simple storage proximity. But they
are a minority and were selected as best examples after the aggregate test. They are evidence for local hypotheses,
not permission to compress the whole vocabulary into categories such as “years.” A future semantic validation would
need prospectively frozen category membership and unseen-token interchange.

## Current token-only explanation

The best-supported account is now:

- `M`: one shared operating point;
- `L`: a compact token-label/control signal with mild shareable structure, largely tracked by component geometry;
- `Q`: an additional, mostly token-private quadratic correction;
- exact token identity remains necessary globally.

This answers the proposed “numbers” compression honestly: number-like equivalence appears in examples, but the
registered whole-vocabulary grouping test does not justify replacing exact identities with a sparse class code.

## Next direction

The next independent route is consumer-aware quadratic spectral analysis. Instead of asking which tokens can share
one `Q`, it asks which directions of the token-private quadratic correction each consumer actually reads. A small,
stable reader-weighted spectrum could still simplify the bilinear computation without pretending that different
tokens are interchangeable. This remains token-only work; token-by-context and context-only claims still require
separate live-context experiments.
