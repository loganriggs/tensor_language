# Plain-English correction and update — 2026-09-01 14:15 UTC

## The correction

The preceding update reported that a raw-token-predictable MLP0 component reproduced 97.2% of the immediate joined
attention1/MLP1 causal response. That measurement is numerically correct, but the interpretation was too strong:
the fitted component included a learned constant mean write as well as token-dependent variation.

The stronger follow-up kept the same mean but shuffled which token-specific output was paired with each token. That
control still achieved **0.925 joined response R²**. Therefore most of the 0.972 figure cannot be attributed to token
identity. I withdraw the wording that token identity itself was 97%-causally sufficient.

## Corrected token-only decomposition

The exact normalized input to MLP0 is called `z`. MLP0's bias-free computation is exactly quadratic in `z`:

`F(z) = Down((Left z) × (Right z))`.

Across the finite vocabulary, we decomposed this quadratic function into a common mean plus its best degree-one
token-dependent component at ranks 16 through 1,152. The exact folded `z` coordinate and the earlier raw-token
coordinate give almost identical write-reconstruction curves; even full rank explains only about 39.7% of MLP0
write variation. Thus the remaining quadratic variation is large.

However, the compact degree-one component carries token identity extremely well:

| degree-one rank | stored values | exact held-out token top-1 | joined causal response R² |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| mean/shuffled control | — | 0% | 0.9253 |
| 16 | 39,168 | not scored | 0.9535 |
| 64 | 149,760 | **97.6%** | **0.9640** |
| 128 | 297,216 | **99.93%** | 0.9679 |
| 256 | 592,128 | **100%** | 0.9704 |

The control matters. Rank64 does not explain 96.4% from scratch; the common mean already explains 92.5%. Relative
to the error left after the mean, rank64 removes about **52%** of the remaining joined-response error. For attention1,
the mean control reaches 0.720 R² and rank64 reaches 0.886, removing about **59%** of the remaining error. That is a
real token-specific contribution, especially for attention1, but it is not the whole 96.4%.

The current token-only picture is therefore:

`large common write + compact token-identity modulation + broad quadratic residual`.

The compact modulation nearly uniquely labels every token. MLP1's immediate response is dominated by the common
write, while attention1 depends more strongly on the token-specific modulation. The broad residual remains
downstream-relevant enough that the registered strong null fired: a shuffled map came within 0.05 joined R² of the
real rank256 map. No live token-by-context promotion is licensed.

## Why this still answers the architectural question

Every block has the raw token vector, but raw identity and a prepared downstream control signal are different
objects. MLP0 appears to provide:

- a common operating-point shift used heavily by MLP1;
- a compact token-specific modulation that makes identity accessible to attention1 and refines MLP1;
- additional high-rank quadratic variation whose role is not yet isolated.

So the model is not redundantly copying the embedding. It is setting a shared operating point and translating token
identity into a compact modulation. We can call the modulation token-identifying because held-out nearest-vocabulary
retrieval is 97.6% at rank64; we cannot yet call it a complete causal replacement.

## Next step after the strong null

We will not tune the rank curve or promote it into live context. The next analysis must explicitly condition every
causal comparison on the common mean and use incremental error reduction rather than raw response R². It will ask
whether the remaining token-specific modulation and the orthogonal quadratic residual have distinct effects on
attention1 and MLP1. Only a prospectively controlled separation can license transport into the token×context branch.
