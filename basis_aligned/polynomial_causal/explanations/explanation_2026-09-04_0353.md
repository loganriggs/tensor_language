# Circuit work update — 2026-09-04 03:53 UTC

## The high-level goal

We are trying to replace the 546-million-parameter bilin18 transformer with a smaller, explicit program whose parts
have computational meanings. A successful circuit should say:

1. what information it reads;
2. what operation it performs;
3. what it writes and which later computations use that write;
4. how it behaves on held-out and out-of-distribution examples;
5. whether it can be swapped, removed, or edited without unwanted effects on unrelated behavior; and
6. whether the same internal operation is reused by several tasks.

Parameter count and cross-entropy preservation matter for the eventual compiled model, but neither identifies a
circuit. This is why the late-layer 768-dimensional analysis has stopped: 768 was one point on a smooth curve, not a
natural boundary in the model.

The work since [the 03:14 explanation](explanation_2026-09-04_0314.md) has returned to behavioral circuits and has
also changed how we plan to scale the work.

## 1. The 768-dimensional split is closed

The 1152-dimensional residual stream was projected onto the top $k$ eigenvectors of a covariance matrix fitted from
late-MLP inputs. We tested preset values including

$$
k\in\{128,256,384,512,768,896,1024,1088\}.
$$

There was no eigenvalue gap at 768. For one seven-MLP intervention, the added cross-entropy at $k=256,512,768$ was
approximately $0.397,0.192,0.084$ nat: a smooth improvement as more directions were retained. The later gate experiments
also found that error closely followed the amount of discarded covariance energy. Therefore “768-dimensional channel”
was too strong. The valid description is only conditional: *if* we choose this projection, we can measure how the model
uses its two parts. It does not identify a semantic component, so no more experiments are allocated to that line.

## 2. Correcting the attention-to-reader experiment

The previous proposal crossed an attention-block-5 intervention with deletion of a later module. That four-condition
experiment computes an interaction,

$$
I=Y(\text{swap},\text{reader})-Y(\text{native},\text{reader})
 -Y(\text{swap},\text{reader removed})+Y(\text{native},\text{reader removed}),
$$

where $Y$ is the donor-directed answer-logit margin. This is a valid measurement of whether the swap's effect depends
on the reader being present. It is not, by itself, proof that the reader mediates the swap: deleting a whole module can
disturb parallel computations and change the operating point of everything after it.

The authority audit also found that the proposed reader sites were partly misdescribed. Earlier work moved an equality
*score* from attention head L5H5 into L8H4 while retaining L8H4's value/output computation. It did not show that L8H4
normally reads L5H5's residual write. L13H8 is a real causal site for the pending-opener task, but we have not shown that
attention block 5 supplies the relevant state to it.

The corrected experiment therefore has three stages:

1. Swap a defined slice of attention block 5 at the final query, then measure which later normalized module inputs
   actually change. A gradient times the observed state change is used only to screen possible readers on FIT data.
2. Under the upstream swap, give a selected reader its original recipient input. This is a **clamp**: the reader still
   performs its normal computation, but the upstream-induced change cannot enter through that interface. If the answer
   movement disappears, the interface is necessary for that effect.
3. In the native recipient run, give the reader only the state change caused by the upstream swap. This is an
   **injection**. If it reproduces the signed answer movement, the state change is sufficient at that interface.

For a bilinear MLP reader,

$$
M(z)=W_D[(W_Lz)\odot(W_Rz)]+b,
$$

the observed finite change $\delta=z_1-z_0$ has the exact weight-level expansion

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(z_1)-M(z_0)=W_D\big[& (W_L\delta)\odot(W_Rz_0)
 +(W_Lz_0)\odot(W_R\delta)\\
 &+(W_L\delta)\odot(W_R\delta)\big].
\end{aligned}
$$

These are the left-input contribution, right-input contribution, and their finite interaction. The full sum must
reconstruct the observed MLP-write change before we interpret any term. This translates a causally validated state
interchange into the actual quadratic weights without assuming that native heads or hidden coordinates are the final
semantic basis.

The corrected design is now approved for a separately reviewed preregistration. Its maximum cost is known exactly:
11,842 forwards and 558 backwards through the FIT stopping point; 17,102/558 through SELECT; and 27,622/558 if the
unchanged claim earns FINAL_TEST and OOD. We are not executing the earlier deletion-based design.

## 3. What the numbered-list downstream-reader experiment found

The numbered-list circuit predicts the next label in prompts such as

```text
21. red
22. blue
23
```

Earlier work isolated an exact attention-block-8 contribution $T$ written at the final query from the final visible
label. Removing all of $T$ damaged the correct numeric margin by about 2.1 logits on held-out SELECT examples, but the
same removal also helped an active repeated-label copy control. Thus $T$ was causal but not selectively removable.

The new experiment carried $T$ separately through the residual stream and prevented different later computations from
reading it:

- `DIRECT` prevented only the final normalization and unembedding from reading $T$;
- `READS` prevented MLP8 and all attention/MLP components in blocks 9–17 from reading it;
- `FULL` prevented both and exactly reproduced deleting $T$ from the residual path;
- individual and joint arms tested specific downstream readers.

An independent reanalysis resampled the 16 generated SELECT groups rather than treating correlated row variants as
independent observations:

| Intervention | Mean correct-margin damage | Group-bootstrap 95% interval | Relative effect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| all paths from $T$ | 2.121 | [2.024, 2.215] | 100% |
| all downstream component reads | 1.914 | [1.788, 2.041] | 90.3% of all paths |
| direct final read only | 0.218 | [0.211, 0.223] | 10.3% of all paths |
| MLP8 and MLP10 jointly | 0.701 | [0.671, 0.732] | 36.6% of downstream reads |

The useful conclusion is that the numbered-list effect of this particular attention contribution is mostly computed
through downstream component reads, rather than being a complete successor answer written directly into the logits.
MLP8 is the largest single reader. The downstream computation is nevertheless distributed: the physical MLP8+MLP10
joint intervention explains only 36.6%, failing the registered 50% concentration test.

There were two important corrections to the stronger initial interpretation:

- We cannot yet say that $T$ is a context-independent copy of the label. Its attention coefficients depend on context,
  and its direct unembedding projection was almost neutral between the visible label and its successor.
- Small singleton effects do not prove that the MLP readers are redundant. Their joint effect contains interactions.
  The physical MLP8+MLP10 effect was 0.120 logit larger than the sum of their singleton removal effects.

The selected two MLPs also account for much of the unwanted effect on the repeated-label copy control: preventing them
from reading $T$ improves that control's cross-entropy by 0.267 nat, versus 0.352 nat for deleting every path from $T$.
That is evidence of shared collateral, not selectivity. Therefore this run refines the existing numbered-list circuit;
it does not count as a new completed circuit and does not yet license FINAL_TEST/OOD.

The exact computational price was 457 batched model calls, corresponding to 20,212 example evaluations and zero
backward passes. The original estimate of roughly 19,600 omitted repeated native/reference evaluations. The reusable
tool will record both units.

## 4. The 20/80 change: one circuit battery, many behaviors

The main bottleneck has not been GPU time. The numbered-list experiment used about 20 seconds of GPU, while its bespoke
dataset, intervention code, evidence checks, review, and write-up took much longer. Repeating that process twenty times
would be the wrong scaling strategy.

We are now building one reusable circuit battery. A task supplies only a declarative prompt generator, its answer rule,
and its causal transformations. The shared machinery handles:

1. native capability and tokenizer checks;
2. FIT-only writer localization across attention heads and MLPs;
3. frozen SELECT interchange, removal, and active controls;
4. downstream path splitting for successful writers;
5. TEST/OOD opening only after an unchanged claim passes;
6. grouped uncertainty estimates, exact execution price, typed evidence, and atomic publication; and
7. a cross-task component-by-behavior response table for detecting reuse.

The common compiler for authority tables, call schedules, split closure, evidence validation, and publication has now
passed four adversarial review rounds plus 86 CPU tests. We have stopped hardening it in the abstract and are integrating
the battery with it. This avoids creating another parallel framework.

The task protocol uses a common schema, but not an identical text edit for every behavior. Each task must declare:

- two independently generated answer-changing transformations;
- at least one answer-preserving transformation;
- an active control with its expected effect;
- frozen FIT/SELECT/TEST/OOD seeds and generator validity checks; and
- a group identifier so related prompts are resampled together.

The active bank contains twenty behaviors. The existing six cover induction, pending delimiters, numbered lists,
numeric sequences, explicit pointer lookup, and equality-based source selection. Fourteen additions cover month,
weekday, alphabet, and ordinal succession; quote, HTML-tag, and code-fence closing; subject–verb agreement; article
selection; numerical comparison; positional-list and named-field retrieval; list-marker continuation; and
sentence-boundary capitalization. The full transformation definitions are in
`CIRCUIT_BATTERY_BEHAVIOR_BANK_2026-09-04.md` one directory above.

That document is a candidate bank, not frozen execution authority. Its first audit immediately found useful design
errors. An `a/an` task cannot ask the model to choose an article before the controlling word is visible; HTML tags and
code fences often require several tokens and may share the same first token; an equal-number comparison gives the same
answer and foil; and named-field retrieval is probably a surface variant of the existing pointer/equality task rather
than a distinct behavior. These are being repaired or merged before code runs. This is the intended bootstrap: fix one
shared generator once rather than discover the same counterfactual flaw in twenty separate experiments.

The first live task-generator draft was also blocked before freezing because it used Python's process-randomized
`hash()` to create seeds, omitted the shared group identifier, encoded prompts and answers separately instead of
checking their joint tokenization, and called weekday/month splits value-disjoint even though every split reused the
same weekday/month values. The generic integration tests now make these failures explicit requirements.

Twenty tested behaviors do not imply twenty distinct circuits. If month, weekday, alphabet, and ordinal continuation
share one downstream successor operation with different input readers, the correct result is fewer reusable circuit
variables. Conversely, if one native MLP divides into distinct effects for several tasks, the battery should split it.
The cross-task held-out intervention profiles—not module names or output-vector cosine—will decide.

## 5. The one remaining bespoke rung

R593 is the repaired induction selector/content experiment. It tests a below-head factorization that the generic first
battery cannot express: whether a fixed equality-based selector coefficient combines with the copied content, and
whether their interaction predicts the answer under controlled swaps. Its scientific predictions and thresholds have
not changed from the invalid R592 run.

The repair now derives every support mask from the frozen authority, computes equality, complement, and full attention
contractions independently in float64, and retains the absolute $10^{-5}$ reconstruction test. It also fixes invalid-run
publication: a failed early call can publish only the exact completed array prefix named by the fsynced ledger, never
unwritten preallocated tails. The model-free suites currently pass 31 R593 tests and 25 inherited streaming/storage
tests. A fresh independent exact-byte review is in progress; no GPU execution is authorized before it approves.

We will finish this already-frozen experiment because it asks a deeper circuit question than the generic head/MLP
screen. After it, new circuit work uses the shared battery unless a result specifically requires a below-module
follow-up that the generic schema cannot represent.
