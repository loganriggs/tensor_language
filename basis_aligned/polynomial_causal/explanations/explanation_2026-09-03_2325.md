# bilin18 as a width program: one bus for the back half, sixteen rotating frames for the front (2026-09-03 23:25 UTC)

Plain-language chapter (Claude lane, ledger §2742–§2757), expanding project shorthand. Sign convention throughout: every
number is CE ADDED ABOVE THE REAL MODEL on held-out documents — LOWER IS BETTER; a "+" is extra damage. The frontier
(§312/§2125, norm-2304 at 2.6735) is untouched by everything here. The strict explained fraction (5.348% / 10.923% /
4.727 nat / 0 of 68 behavior-level circuits) is unchanged: what follows is a description of WHERE each sublayer reads and
writes, not a behavioral circuit with selective removal.

## The question

bilin18's residual stream is 1152-dimensional. Every one of its 36 sublayers (18 attention, 18 MLP) reads the whole stream
and writes back into it. A "width program" asks: through how narrow a window does each sublayer actually read, and can many
sublayers share one window? If they can, the model is a much smaller object than its parameter count suggests — a few
frames plus what happens inside them.

## The tool

For each sublayer we fit, on 96 documents, the covariance of its rms-normalised input and keep the top-k eigenvectors (its
"own frame" at width k). At run time the sublayer sees only the projection of its input onto that frame; the rest is replaced
by the fit-set mean. Attention re-computes its scores from the projected input. Everything is then scored by cross-entropy on
64 fresh documents. A "shared frame" is the same construction on the averaged covariance of several sublayers.

## What we found

1. **The back half of the model reads through ONE frame (§2742–§2745, §2754).** The 14 late sublayers (blocks 11–17) can all
   read through a single 768-frame for .109 nat; at width 1024 the whole program costs .034. Pushing the shared region forward,
   the split point is block 8, and it is causal: 16 own frames for blocks 0–7 plus ONE 1024-frame for the twenty sublayers of
   blocks 8–17 costs .0374; moving the split to block 6 costs .0065 more, moving it to block 10 buys only .0023 back.

2. **The back half also WRITES through that frame — a bus (§2747–§2748, §2756).** There is no shared write frame in the sense
   of the writes' own covariances, but the writes' part that lies outside the shared READ frame is not needed by any later
   sublayer: it can be summed into a side-channel that joins the stream only at the unembed. Doing this for all twenty
   sublayers of blocks 8–17 costs nothing (.0362 vs .0374); deleting the remainder instead costs +.022, and 59% of that is the
   readout's share. The out-of-frame remainder is ≈ 7% of each late write's energy — least for the last two MLPs (1% — they are
   already low-rank, §2747) and most for block 14 (9%). The side-channel itself is wide (effective rank 218 of the 384-dim
   complement, §2752); truncating it is not free.

3. **The front half does NOT share frames: sixteen genuinely separate objects (§2749–§2750, §2753, §2755, §2757).** Blocks
   0–7 each read through their own frame. Every attempt to compress those frames failed as registered: one shared early frame
   +.123, grouped frames +.036…+.114, a 3-block sliding window +.027, leave-one-out +.070, neighbours only +.081, and "the
   predecessor's frame with 32/64/128 directions swapped" +.064/.044/.040 — the last three with their nulls MET. The
   construction-free measurement (principal angles) says why: consecutive early frames differ by a median 135 of the at most
   384 angles that two 768-frames can disagree on, and the rotation is broad and monotone toward the bus frame (Spearman
   −.985 with depth).

4. **Where the rotation comes from (§2757, observed, not yet registered).** Each block begins by blending the stream with the
   token embedding (x ← λ₀x + λ₁x₀). The frame turns 1.4–1.9× more across that block boundary than inside a block (across the
   attention write). The "rotating early frame" is largely the embedding blend re-mixing the read frame block by block; once the
   blend stops mattering, the frame settles — and the last block turns away from the bus again, presumably toward the readout.

## The program, stated

At width 1024: blocks 0–7 read the stream through 16 site-specific 1024-frames; blocks 8–17 (twenty sublayers of both kinds)
read AND write through one shared 1024-frame; the ≈ 7% of each late write that falls outside it is accumulated in a
side-channel added just before the unembed. Price of that description: 0.036 nat added above the real model. Frame parameters:
17 × 1152 × 1024 ≈ 20M numbers; the back half's internal computation lives in 1024 of 1152 dimensions.

## What it does not say (the honest bottom line)

A frame is a window, not a mechanism. Nothing here names a behavior, removes one selectively, or shows two blocks re-using a
component. The angle count does not predict CE price (blocks 8–10 differ from the bus by 150–230 large angles yet join it for
.002–.004 — the rotated directions carry little energy), so "same frame" is an energy-weighted statement, not a geometric one.
The metric-constructed-basis closure (§2118) still stands: these frames are data covariances scored by CE; no frontier
compression is claimed. Open next: is the bus frame simply the late frame (blocks 11–17's, or 14–17's), and does the unembed read
through it (registered 23:22Z, bus_frame_identity_and_readout_probe).
