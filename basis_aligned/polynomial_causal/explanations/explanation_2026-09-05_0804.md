# Circuit-program update — 2026-09-05 08:04 UTC

## Current goal

The controlling goal is now:

> Build a solid reusable codebase that can produce hundreds of high-quality, nonduplicated causal circuits efficiently, then use that evidence to find the model's natural computational decomposition.

The working target is one honest basic circuit screen or informative null per roughly ten serial minutes. A useful circuit should eventually support meaningful counterfactuals, prediction on held-out or out-of-distribution examples, extraction of the actual computation, and selective removal or interchange without damaging unrelated behavior. Reuse across tasks and composition with other circuits are additional evidence that the units are natural.

Rank reduction, quantization, activation variance, and activation reconstruction are not current goals. They do not by themselves tell us which computation causes behavior, generalizes, or can be selectively changed.

## What was just tested

The current bracket circuit contains an exact contribution from attention head 8 in layer 13. At the final prediction position, the head reads the earlier unmatched opener and writes a 1152-dimensional vector into the residual stream. For each context with three possible closer-token constructions, that opener write can be split exactly into

$$
t_{g,c}=\mu_g+\delta_{g,c},
$$

where $\mu_g$ is the average write across the three constructions and $\delta_{g,c}$ is the construction-specific difference. By construction,

$$
\sum_c \delta_{g,c}=0.
$$

Both parts causally affect the answer. Earlier this hour, restoring MLP15, three response-localized attention heads, all three heads jointly, or any one complete downstream module failed to recover a meaningful fraction of either removal effect. This showed that a downstream activation can visibly respond to a circuit without mediating its behavioral effect.

The new experiment tested the combined downstream modules rather than another single module. It crossed two choices:

1. Is the layer-13 opener factor native or removed?
2. Are all later module writes fixed to the values produced by the native run or the removed-factor run?

This gives four exact model executions. If $Y_{NN}$ is the native/native three-closer logit vector, $Y_{RN}$ removes the opener factor while holding later writes native, $Y_{NR}$ keeps the opener but installs the later writes produced after removal, and $Y_{RR}$ is the natural removal run, then

$$
D_{\mathrm{total}}=Y_{NN}-Y_{RR},
$$

$$
D_{\mathrm{residual}}=Y_{NN}-Y_{RN},
\qquad
D_{\mathrm{writes}}=Y_{NN}-Y_{NR},
$$

$$
I=Y_{NN}-Y_{RN}-Y_{NR}+Y_{RR}.
$$

The exact identity is

$$
D_{\mathrm{total}}=D_{\mathrm{residual}}+D_{\mathrm{writes}}-I.
$$

Here “residual route” means that the original layer-13 vector remains in the residual stream while every later module contributes its native write. “Later-write route” means the change in the outputs written by all later attention and MLP modules. $I$ is the part that only exists because the opener change and the later-write change occur together.

## Result

All capability, exact-replay, live-removal, and algebraic-identity checks passed. The model ran eight forwards over the frozen 24 examples.

For the centered logits of the three possible closer tokens:

- The residual-route projection was 97.4% to 122.4% of the total median effect.
- The combined later-write projection was -22.1% to +3.7%.
- The absolute interaction projection stayed below 1.9%.

The values above 100% are not a probability or double counting. They mean the residual route pushes the closer logits farther in the final direction than the observed total change, while another effect partially pushes back.

CE shows what pushes back. Removing an opener factor while forcing later module writes to stay native caused 0.73-1.35 nat median CE damage. In the natural removal run, the later modules changed their writes and repaired roughly 0.35-0.55 nat of that damage. Therefore:

> The semantic closer-choice signal travels mainly as the original residual write, while later modules provide broader compensation rather than copying the same closer-logit signal forward.

This distinction matters because CE depends on the full vocabulary distribution and the nonlinear softmax. A path can have little projection onto the three chosen closer logits and still substantially improve CE by changing other logits. This is now recorded as canonical Lesson 118 so it is not lost or rediscovered.

## Next experiment already in progress

The next claimed screen asks whether the surviving residual route can be written as an explicit weight-level circuit. It will use three forwards and no fitted subspace.

The model's final readout is

$$
\operatorname{logits}(x)=30\tanh\!\left(\frac{W_U\,\operatorname{RMSNorm}(x)}{30}\right),
$$

where $W_U$ is the fixed unembedding matrix mapping the 1152-dimensional residual stream to vocabulary logits. Because the later writes are held native, the native and removed final residuals differ by the exact $\mu$ or $\delta$ vector, up to the known residual scaling. The new computation will separate:

1. the direct fixed-weight contribution $W_U(\mu/r)$ or $W_U(\delta/r)$;
2. the correction caused by the change in the final residual's RMS scale;
3. the correction from the output $\tanh$ softcap.

Their sum must reproduce the actual causal closer-logit change. If the first term dominates, we have a compact explicit circuit statement: the L13H8 bilinear attention score selects an opener value, $W_O$ writes it into the residual stream, and the fixed unembedding reads that vector as closer-token evidence. If normalization or softcap is material, that interaction becomes part of the extracted circuit rather than being hidden inside a “logit lens” approximation.

The prior-art search and active claim are recorded, and Claude was warned not to duplicate this circuit family.
