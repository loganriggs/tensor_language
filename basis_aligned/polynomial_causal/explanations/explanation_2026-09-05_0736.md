# Circuit-only goal and first downstream mediation result — 2026-09-05 07:36 UTC

## Current goal

The controlling goal is now:

> Build a reliable, reusable codebase that can produce hundreds of high-quality causal circuits without repeating old work, then use those circuits to discover the model's shared and task-specific computations.

A useful circuit should eventually support meaningful counterfactual predictions, held-out or OOD generalization, extraction of the actual computation, and selective removal or interchange without damaging unrelated behavior. A whole attention head or MLP is allowed as an intermediate localization unit, but is not automatically the final explanatory basis.

Rank reduction, quantization, activation variance, and activation-space reconstruction are excluded as primary directions. They can make an array smaller without identifying which computation causes behavior. The main outcome measures are task behavior, correct-answer cross-entropy (CE), logit changes, and causal interchange or removal effects.

- Four downstream outputs passed its initial response criteria: layer-14 head 1, layer-15 head 3, MLP15, and layer-16 head 1.

## Exact computation tested

For a prompt group $$g$$ and delimiter type $$c$$, the exact contribution from the semantic opener through layer-13 head 8 is

$$
t_{g,c}=p(q,o)W_Ov(o),
$$

where $$p(q,o)$$ is the head's attention score from the final query position $$q$$ to the opener position $$o$$, $$v(o)$$ is that opener's projected value, and $$W_O$$ maps the head output into the residual stream.

Across the three delimiter types, we use the exact arithmetic split

$$
\mu_g=\frac{1}{3}\sum_c t_{g,c}, \qquad
\delta_{g,c}=t_{g,c}-\mu_g.
$$

Here $$\mu$$ is the part shared across the delimiter triplet and $$\delta$$ is the delimiter-dependent difference. This fixes the ambiguity by requiring $$\sum_c\delta_{g,c}=0$$; no PCA, SAE, rank choice, or fitted basis is involved.

The new six-forward experiment used 24 fresh prompts and evaluated:

- native model;
- exact native replay;
- remove $$\mu$$;
- remove $$\delta$$;
- remove $$\mu$$, then restore only the native final-position MLP15 output;
- remove $$\delta$$, then restore only the native final-position MLP15 output.

After the MLP15 restoration, layers 16 and 17 were recomputed normally. Thus the question was: *does restoring MLP15 undo a meaningful part of the behavioral effect caused by removing the upstream factor?*

For the three possible closing tokens, let $$C(z)$$ be their three logits after subtracting their mean. For each removal,

$$
d=C(z_{\mathrm{native}})-C(z_{\mathrm{removed}}), \qquad
r=C(z_{\mathrm{restored}})-C(z_{\mathrm{removed}}).
$$

The recovered fraction along the original effect was

$$
\operatorname{recovery}=\frac{r^\top d}{d^\top d+10^{-8}}.
$$

We also measured correct-answer CE directly. Positive removal damage means removing the factor made the prediction worse; positive CE rescue would mean restoring MLP15 repaired it.

## Result: MLP15 is responsive but is not a substantial mediator

The instrument was valid: native replay was exact, native closer accuracy was 100% in every prompt family, and both factor removals produced clear output changes.

| Prompt family | Factor removed | Median output-effect norm | Median fraction recovered by MLP15 | Median CE damage from removal | Median CE rescue from MLP15 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Direct | $$\delta$$ | 1.809 | 1.98% | +0.536 | -0.036 |
| Direct | $$\mu$$ | 0.533 | 1.32% | +0.306 | -0.072 |
| Completed then reopened | $$\delta$$ | 1.327 | 1.88% | +0.320 | -0.027 |
| Completed then reopened | $$\mu$$ | 0.428 | 2.20% | +0.295 | -0.038 |

The matched surface and punctuation rewrites gave the same qualitative result: only about 1.4–2.0% recovery, with negative median CE rescue.

The rescue vector usually pointed in roughly the correct direction, which explains the high cosine values, but it was tiny. Direction alone is not enough: a vector can be parallel to the causal effect while recovering almost none of its magnitude. Restoring MLP15 also made CE slightly worse in every family-factor cell.

So MLP15's response to the pending-opener change is real but mostly observational: it tracks the changed state without carrying a substantial fraction of the downstream behavioral effect. This is an informative causal null and prevents us from treating structured activation similarity as downstream use.
