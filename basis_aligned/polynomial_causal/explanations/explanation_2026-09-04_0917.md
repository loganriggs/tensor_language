# Circuit research update — 2026-09-04 09:17 UTC

## Goal and the main change since the 09:07 update

The goal is to replace the 546-million-parameter bilin18 transformer with a smaller explicit tensor program. Its parts
should say what information is read, what computation is performed, what is written, and how later computations use
the result. A proposed circuit must predict held-out and shifted examples, survive valid counterfactual swaps, support
targeted removal or editing, and compose with other recovered circuits. Fewer dimensions or parameters are useful only
when they preserve those properties; compression by itself is not a circuit explanation.

The main strict-track result is that the repaired subject–verb-agreement task passed its frozen native-capability test
and then passed an independent audit. This opens causal localization on the FIT split. It does **not** yet tell us where
the agreement computation is or which attention/MLP terms implement it.

## 1. What the model just passed

Every prompt asks the model to choose between the one-token continuations ` is` and ` are`. The measured answer margin
is

$$
m_{\mathrm{correct}}(x)
=\ell_{\mathrm{correct}}(x)-\ell_{\mathrm{other}}(x),
$$

where $\ell$ denotes a model logit. A positive margin means the registered grammatical answer has the higher logit.

The four prompt families separate subject number from nearby noun identity and number:

| family | change between the linked prompts | what it tests |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | change the subject-head number in a prepositional-phrase sentence | The correct `is`/`are` answer must change despite a nearer noun. |
| A2 | make the same subject-number change in a relative-clause sentence | The same rule must work in a different syntax. |
| P | replace a nearby noun while preserving its number and the subject number | Noun identity may change, but the grammatical answer does not. |
| C | change the nearby noun's number under a coordinated plural subject | The nearer noun changes, but the complete subject remains plural and the answer remains `are`. |

The run made exactly eight batched forward calls: four families on the base side and the same four on the donor side.
There were 32 prompts per call, hence

$$
8\times32=256\text{ prompt evaluations}.
$$

It retained only two 32-bit logits per prompt, so the raw numeric evidence was

$$
256\times2\times4=2{,}048\text{ bytes}.
$$

No activations, gradients, attention-head rankings, MLP rankings, or learned subspaces were produced by this run.

## 2. Why the seven C errors stay in the next experiment

It would be easy to keep only prompts the model already answers correctly. That would bias the circuit search toward
examples selected using the outcome. We therefore keep every C prompt, including the `key` and `dog` cases associated
with several misses.

C is not required to have identical raw logits after its nearby-noun edit. Context really changed, so model confidence
may legitimately change. Its role in localization is narrower: a candidate **grammatical subject-number coordinate**
should not treat an attractor-number change as a change in the complete subject's number. We will measure whether the
candidate coordinate or its causal effect changes, while preserving all other possible context effects.

## 3. The next question is causal state, not another rank sweep

Define the high-level grammatical state as

$$
s(x)=
\begin{cases}
-1,&\text{singular subject},\\
+1,&\text{plural subject}.
\end{cases}
$$

The primary hypothesis is that this binary variable can be represented by one direction in the 1152-dimensional
residual stream. Calling that direction rank one is not a claim that the model is generally low rank. It is a concrete
hypothesis about the representation of one two-valued variable. Rank two and rank four will be matched controls: if
they rescue transfer failures, the one-coordinate account is rejected rather than celebrated as compression.

The FIT prompts are being split before localization into equal discovery and validation halves. Groups that reverse
the same lexical prompt are kept together, preventing prompt leakage. Each half has all four combinations of subject
number and attractor number. Discovery may choose sites and fit directions; validation may be evaluated only after the
choice and may not be used to select another seed, site, threshold, or rank.

The design uses several natural donors for each target:

- the linked one-token subject-number edit;
- two opposite-number donors with different nouns but the same syntax;
- an opposite-number donor using the other syntax; and
- opposite-number P donors that change noun identities while preserving the grammatical operation.

A direction that works only for the linked one-token edit is therefore diagnosed as a lexical edit direction, not a
general grammatical-number state.

## 4. The exact intervention

Let $r_{b,p}(x)\in\mathbb R^{1152}$ be the residual vector for prompt $x$, at block boundary $b$ and semantic token
position $p$. The two position types are the subject head $H$ and the final prediction position $Q$. For a unit vector
$u$, the one-dimensional projector is

$$
P=uu^{\mathsf T}.
$$

To transplant only that coordinate from a natural donor $d$ into target $x$, compute

$$
r'_{b,p}(x\leftarrow d)
=r_{b,p}(x)+P\bigl(r_{b,p}(d)-r_{b,p}(x)\bigr).
$$

Everything outside that coordinate and token position remains from the target prompt. This is Distributed Alignment
Search (DAS): optimize $u$ while the model weights remain frozen so that the low-level intervention behaves like the
declared high-level subject-number swap.

For every answer-changing pair, use the fixed logit contrast

$$
m(x)=\ell_{\texttt{ are}}(x)-\ell_{\texttt{ is}}(x)
$$

and score the signed effect

$$
E(x,d)=\frac{s(d)-s(x)}{2}
\left[m(x\leftarrow d)-m(x)\right].
$$

Positive $E$ means the intervention moves the answer toward the donor for both singular-to-plural and
plural-to-singular swaps. Two main summaries are then

$$
\operatorname{direction}
=\frac{\#\{E>0\}}{\#\{\text{pairs}\}},
$$

and

$$
\operatorname{recovery}
=\frac{\text{mean effect of the one-coordinate swap}}
{\text{mean effect of swapping the complete 1152-dimensional residual vector}}.
$$

The complete-vector swap is a causal ceiling, not the desired explanation. If even it cannot move the answer in the
registered direction, the site is not interpretable for this experiment.

## 5. What would count as identifying the state

The planned evidence has several independent parts:

1. **Sufficiency:** transplanting the coordinate moves A1, A2, and opposite-number P prompts toward the donor answer,
   across different nouns, syntaxes, and donor choices.
2. **Invariance:** P same-state edits and C attractor-number edits produce little movement through the proposed
   coordinate. C is a required control at the final prediction position; its two conjunct positions are reported but
   are not forced into an invalid single-head-token definition.
3. **Necessity:** replacing the coordinate by its midpoint between singular and plural reduces native support for the
   correct agreement answer in both directions.
4. **Cross-fitting:** a direction fitted only on A1 must work on A2, and vice versa.
5. **Stability:** at least four of five optimizer seeds must satisfy the directional tests; the validation split cannot
   choose a lucky seed.
6. **Binary-state falsification:** rank two or four must not repair a failed syntax/donor/control test or improve
   validation recovery by more than 0.10.

Gradients are permitted only to shortlist residual sites. A large gradient says the output is locally sensitive to a
site; it does not identify a grammatical variable. All scientific claims come from finite swaps, removals, held-out
transfer, and controls.

## 6. State formation, readers, and interactions are different claims

A sudden increase in successful $Q$-position swaps across one block tells us where a useful prediction-position state
is formed or transported. It does not by itself identify a reader.

To test a handoff, first swap the upstream subject coordinate and then reset the proposed downstream coordinate to its
original target value. If the upstream effect disappears, the downstream state mediates it. Conversely, neutralize
the upstream coordinate and insert the donor's downstream coordinate; if the answer effect returns, the downstream
state is sufficient to rescue the handoff. Only an ordered subject-site-to-prediction-site reset/rescue result earns
the word “reader.” If no sharp or ordered handoff exists, the result remains “state supported, reader unresolved.”

Single-site removal can miss redundant routes or mix in interactions with other sites. For two sites $i$ and $j$, let
$D_i$, $D_j$, and $D_{ij}$ be the signed damage from neutralizing each alone and both together. The finite interaction is

$$
I_{ij}=D_{ij}-D_i-D_j.
$$

Weak single-site damage but strong joint damage with positive $I_{ij}$ is evidence for two jointly required or
mutually covering routes. Two individually strong sites whose joint damage is no larger than either alone are more
consistent with two points on one serial path. These classifications must repeat on validation; ordinary one-at-a-time
activation patching cannot settle them.

## 7. How a successful residual state becomes a weight-level explanation

For a bilinear MLP, let its normalized input be $z\in\mathbb R^{1152}$ and write

$$
F(z)=W_D\left[(W_Lz)\odot(W_Rz)\right],
$$

where $W_L,W_R\in\mathbb R^{4608\times1152}$, $W_D\in\mathbb R^{1152\times4608}$, and $\odot$ means elementwise
multiplication. After a causal input direction $q$ and causal downstream read direction $v$ are validated, the scalar
effect along $v$ is the exact quadratic form

$$
v^{\mathsf T}F(z)=z^{\mathsf T}Q_vz,
$$

$$
Q_v=\frac12\left[
W_L^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{diag}(W_D^{\mathsf T}v)W_R+
W_R^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{diag}(W_D^{\mathsf T}v)W_L
\right].
$$

Writing $z=\alpha q+c$ with $q^{\mathsf T}c=0$ gives

$$
v^{\mathsf T}F(z)
=\alpha^2q^{\mathsf T}Q_vq
+2\alpha q^{\mathsf T}Q_vc
+c^{\mathsf T}Q_vc.
$$

These are exactly the subject-state self-interaction, subject-state-times-context interaction, and context-only term
for that downstream effect. They are obtained by contracting known weights, with no PCA, SAE, quantization, or
approximate rank cutoff. Their interpretation still depends on the earlier causal tests: weight algebra tells us how a
validated state is computed, but cannot choose the state from weights alone.

## 8. Separate frontier-price result

The parallel frontier lane found that replacing the fitted attention dictionaries for layers 2–9 by eight fixed
vectors changes frontier cross-entropy by 0.0000 nats at the displayed precision while removing 42.55 million
parameters. The comparison without attention 5 also changed by only +0.0001 nat. This is useful progress on literal
program price, and the upcoming repeated-baseline run is measuring whether those tiny differences are below the
instrument's own resolution.

It is not circuit identification: the result does not say what those eight vectors compute, predict a task-specific
counterfactual, or enable a selective edit. It belongs on the price ledger, not as a substitute for the agreement-state
work above.
