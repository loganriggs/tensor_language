# Circuit research update — 2026-09-04 10:08 UTC

## Where we are

The goal is to replace the 546-million-parameter bilin18 transformer with a smaller explicit tensor program. A useful
part of that program should say what information is read, what operation is performed, what is written, and which
later computations use it. It should predict unseen examples, support targeted swaps or removal, compose with other
recovered parts, and remain identifiable across data splits and changes of basis. Lower rank or fewer parameters are
not enough by themselves.

The main advance since the 09:17 update is an independently approved causal design for locating subject–verb
agreement. **The localization itself has not run yet.** We first found that the original design could produce a very
convincing but wrong answer, repaired that problem prospectively, and had a different agent reconstruct and approve
the repaired authority. A CPU-only physical-call compiler is now being built. Model and GPU access remain closed
until that compiler receives another independent review.

## Why 768 eigenvectors were used earlier

There was no eigenvalue cutoff at 768. For normalized late-MLP inputs $x_l$, the earlier experiment formed an average
centered covariance matrix

$$
C=\frac{1}{|L|}\sum_{l\in L}\mathbb E[(x_l-\mu_l)(x_l-\mu_l)^{\mathsf T}],
$$

ordered its eigenvectors by captured variance, and tested the preselected widths

$$
k\in\{128,256,384,512,768,896,1024,1088\}.
$$

The error changed smoothly. In one seven-MLP intervention, keeping 256, 512, and 768 directions added about 0.397,
0.192, and 0.084 nat of cross-entropy. We continued with 768 because it keeps two thirds of the 1,152-dimensional
residual stream, leaves a substantial 384-dimensional complement, and caused measurable but usable damage. It was a
stress-test resolution, not evidence for 768 semantic variables. That variance-compression route is closed as a
circuit-discovery method.

This earlier late-layer result was **corpus-wide, not circuit-specific**. The shared covariance frame was fit on 96
ordinary natural-text documents (documents 96--191, 24,576 token positions) and the causal CE measurements used a
disjoint 64-document set (documents 0--63, 16,384 predicted tokens). It therefore generalised across held-out samples
from that fixed corpus; it was not restricted to induction, lists, agreement, or another named circuit. It has not been
tested here on strongly shifted data such as code, mathematics, synthetic circuit prompts, or another language, so it
should not be described as universal across all data. Once the projector is fixed, the algebraic split of a bilinear
MLP into core--core, core--complement, and complement--complement terms is exact for every input vector; the claims
about loss and functional importance remain empirical averages over the stated natural-text evaluation set.

## The behavior we are localizing

The task asks the model to choose between the one-token continuations ` is` and ` are`. Its fixed output score is

$$
m(x)=\ell_{\texttt{ are}}(x)-\ell_{\texttt{ is}}(x).
$$

The repaired native-capability test was correct on 249 of 256 prompts, or 97.27%. Ordinary prepositional-phrase,
relative-clause, and noun-replacement cases were all correct. Coordinated-subject cases were 28/32 and 29/32 on the
two natural sides. All seven errors remain in localization; filtering them out would select examples using the answer
we later want to explain.

The first localization design had 704 natural target–donor relationships. An independent review found that every
answer-changing example could be explained by a direction encoding whether one noun token was morphologically
singular or plural. Coordinated subjects such as “the bird and the cat” contain two singular noun tokens but are
grammatically plural. Because the first design used those prompts only as no-change controls, a direction could label
all of them singular and still pass every stated gate. The design was therefore invalid before any implementation or
GPU run. That failure is now recorded in the canonical circuit dossier rather than left as prose someone could miss.

## What v2 changes

V2 materializes an exact 16-group discovery split, 16-group validation split, 256 endpoints, and 1,088 donor
relationships. It preserves the original 704 relations and adds 384 at the final prediction position:

| target and donor | type of test | count in each split |
|---|---|---:|
| coordinated plural → ordinary singular | answer must move toward `is` | 64 |
| ordinary singular → coordinated plural | answer must move toward `are` | 32 |
| coordinated plural → ordinary plural | answer should remain plural | 64 |
| ordinary plural → coordinated plural | answer should remain plural | 32 |

Each direction is evaluated separately for the two ordinary syntactic families. Attractor number is matched, noun
pairs and groups differ, and donors never cross the discovery/validation boundary. Every coordinated-subject endpoint
receives both singular answer-changing donors and plural no-change donors.

The design now distinguishes two possible variables:

- At the subject-head position $H$, we test only a **local noun-number carrier**. This may legitimately encode the
  morphology of one noun. Coordinated subjects do not enter an $H$ success claim.
- At the prediction position $Q$, we test the **number of the complete subject**. Ordinary plural and coordinated
  plural subjects must have the same causal state even though their surface noun structure differs.

For a candidate direction $u\in\mathbb R^{1152}$, the one-dimensional projector is $P=uu^{\mathsf T}$. Distributed
Alignment Search (DAS) learns $u$ while leaving the model weights fixed. A donor interchange is

$$
r'(x\leftarrow d)=r(x)+P\bigl(r(d)-r(x)\bigr).
$$

Only one residual vector at one token position is changed. For opposite grammatical states, the signed effect is

$$
E_P(x,d)=\frac{s(d)-s(x)}{2}\left[m(x\leftarrow_Pd)-m(x)\right],
$$

where $s=-1$ for singular and $s=+1$ for plural. Positive $E_P$ means movement toward the donor answer in both causal
directions. Recovery compares the mean one-direction effect with the effect of transplanting the complete
1,152-dimensional residual vector at the same site. A site is not eligible unless this full-state intervention is
causally active in every required donor family.

V2 also requires **absolute coordinated-plural alignment**. Discovery-only ordinary singular and plural means define
a midpoint $c$ and separation $\delta$. Each coordinated-subject coordinate is normalized as

$$
z_C(x)=\frac{u^{\mathsf T}r_Q(x)-c}{\delta/2}.
$$

An ideal singular state is $-1$ and an ideal plural state is $+1$. On untouched validation prompts, coordinated
subjects must lie on the plural side under separate median, sign-fraction, and lower-quartile tests. This blocks the
specific false solution found in v1; the bidirectional causal swaps additionally require that the coordinate actually
affect the model's answer.

## Necessity, interactions, and the reader test

Sufficiency asks whether inserting the donor coordinate produces the intended effect. Necessity instead replaces the
candidate coordinate by the discovery midpoint and measures how much native agreement evidence disappears. It is
tested separately in all four syntax-by-subject-state cells.

One-site removal can miss redundancy. If the selected $Q$ site is not individually necessary, v2 takes the two
prediction-position sites chosen on discovery and measures

$$
I_{ij}=D_{ij}-D_i-D_j,
$$

where $D_i$ and $D_j$ are the signed effects of neutralizing either site and $D_{ij}$ is the effect of neutralizing
both in chronological order. The registered redundant-state case requires both single effects to be weak, the joint
effect to be strong, and $I_{ij}$ to be positive on every validation cell. This directly addresses hidden interaction
effects rather than assuming that attention heads or MLPs act independently.

To test an ordered handoff from $H$ to $Q$, the experiment performs two finite interventions:

1. insert a donor coordinate at $H$, then restore the native target coordinate at $Q$; and
2. neutralize $H$, then insert the natural donor coordinate at $Q$.

The first should remove at least 70% of the upstream effect; the second should recover at least 70% of the ordinary
$Q$-only effect. Sign reversal and overshoot fail. A rise in a probe score or gradient cannot substitute for these
causal tests.

Rank one is primary because the proposed state is binary, not because compression is the goal. Rank two and rank four
receive the same fitting opportunity only as falsifiers. If either rescues a failed family or improves a required
recovery by more than 0.10, the one-coordinate hypothesis is rejected; the higher rank is not relabeled as the
discovered circuit.

## A separate correction from the model-compression lane

Several apparent zero-cost attention-dictionary simplifications were withdrawn after checking the evaluated program:
the changed dictionaries were not installed in it. A disconnected intervention had looked like a free component.
The standing rule is now to prove physically that every changed object participates in the evaluated program and that
a positive control moves the output.

The same lane found that removing two fitted MLP parts together costs 3.2104 nats although their individual costs sum
to 1.4350. The parts compensate for one another, so single-component ablations substantially understate their joint
importance. This supports the interaction-aware task-14 design, but these frontier measurements still concern global
approximation price rather than a named behavioral circuit.

## What is running now

The active work is a CPU-only compiler for the approved v2 design. It must turn the conditional experiment into an
exact auditable call graph: equal-length batches, sites, donor IDs, seeds, ranks, optimizer updates, forward/backward
counts, cached arrays, numeric dtypes and bytes, creation-only evidence paths, and an eight-hour hard runtime limit.
The worst-case logical schedule contains 60,000 optimizer updates, but that is not yet a physical call count.

The compiler will also include a task-conditioned local diagnostic. For donor residual difference $\delta_n$, output
gradient $g_n$, and causal direction sign $\sigma_n$, the symmetric operator

$$
A=\operatorname{mean}_n\frac{\sigma_n}{2}
\left(g_n\delta_n^{\mathsf T}+\delta_ng_n^{\mathsf T}\right)
$$

has a leading eigenvector that exactly maximizes the locally linear rank-one causal effect. This is not principal
component analysis: it uses the declared intervention and behavior, not activation variance. It will be compared with
finite DAS to test whether nonlinear curvature is doing important work. It cannot satisfy a success gate by itself.

After the compiler is frozen, a fresh independent audit must approve its exact calls and price. Only then can an
implementation be built and a managed GPU job considered. If the price is infeasible, we will write a prospective
successor rather than run a cheaper partial experiment and reinterpret it afterward.
