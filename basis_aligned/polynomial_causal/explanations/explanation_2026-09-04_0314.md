# Research update since 02:39 UTC: one real compression, one invalid circuit experiment, and the repairs now running

## The goal this work is serving

We are trying to replace bilin18 with a smaller, explicit program whose parts have circuit-level meanings. A proposed
part is useful only if it helps us say what information is read, what computation is performed, what is written, and
which later computations use it. It should predict held-out and shifted examples, compose with other recovered parts,
and support selective removals or swaps without disrupting unrelated behavior. Fewer dimensions or lower
cross-entropy loss alone is not enough.

This update has two distinct threads:

1. a broad analysis of how the late MLPs use a 768-dimensional input frame and its 384-dimensional complement; and
2. a circuit-specific induction experiment intended to separate an attention coefficient from the content it routes.

The first thread has now produced a genuine but generic compression. The second remains the higher-information circuit
test, but its first managed run correctly stopped on two defective validity checks before evaluating any hypothesis.

## Why 768 is an experimental resolution, not a natural boundary

The residual stream has width 1152. We formed the average centred covariance of the normalised late-MLP inputs,

$$
C=\frac{1}{|L|}\sum_{l\in L}\mathbb E[(x_l-\mu_l)(x_l-\mu_l)^\top],
$$

sorted its eigenvectors by decreasing eigenvalue, and tested preset widths. The tested sequence included 128, 256,
384, 512, and 768, followed by 896, 1024, and 1088 in later experiments. There was no eigengap or causal knee at 768.
We kept using it because it retains two thirds of the stream, leaves a large 384-dimensional complement, and gives a
measurable but still interpretable intervention.

The basis was fitted on documents 96–191 and evaluated on documents 0–63, but continued use of 768 was informed by
that sweep. Therefore every later 768/384 result is conditional on this chosen resolution. It does not establish that
the model has exactly 768 semantic dimensions.

## What the late-MLP compression actually does

For a normalised MLP input $x$, write

$$
c=Px,\qquad t=(I-P)x,
$$

where $c$ has 768 coordinates in the chosen frame and $t$ has 384 coordinates in its orthogonal complement. A
bilinear MLP

$$
M(x)=W_D[(W_Lx)\odot(W_Rx)]
$$

then splits exactly into a core-only term, a mixed term, and a tail-only term:

$$
M(c+t)=M_{cc}+M_{ct}+M_{tt}.
$$

The mixed term is linear in $t$ but its linear map depends on $c$. It can be written

$$
M_{ct}=J(c)t,
$$

where $J(c)$ is the input-dependent map from the 384 tail coordinates to the 1152-dimensional output. The recent
experiments approximate only the dependence of $J$ on $c$. They do not truncate the tail input, output, whole MLP, or
residual stream.

For each block, the dependence on $c$ was expanded in energy-ordered directions. Blocks 16 and 17 were unusually
concentrated: retaining 64 gate directions at block 16 and 16 at block 17 left approximately $0.0051$ and $0.0083$ nat
of CE damage when applied separately. Applied together, they cost $0.0149$ nat on documents 0–63 and $0.0175$ on the
disjoint documents 64–95. The earlier $0.015$ headline should therefore be read as approximately $0.015\pm0.003$, not
as a sharp threshold victory.

Extending the approximation to all ten late blocks produced this cumulative CE damage:

```text
compressed blocks       17   16–17  15–17  14–17  13–17  12–17  11–17  10–17   9–17   8–17
CE added (nat)         .008    .015   .022   .030   .038   .045   .051   .058   .064   .072
```

Each additional block costs about $0.006$–$0.009$ nat. Pairwise removals do not explain the larger joint interaction:
blocks 16+17 together cost only $0.93$ times the sum of their separate removals, and blocks 8+9 cost $1.18$ times the
sum. The stronger interaction appears only when many late reads are changed together.

This is a real executable approximation and it composes predictably over the tested natural-text documents. It is not
yet a semantic circuit decomposition: the retained directions are selected by energy, have no task-level names, and
have not passed selective swaps or unrelated-behavior controls. The full version also costs $0.072$ nat. We are
therefore keeping it as a priced program component and matched-capacity control, not making it the main discovery
direction.

## What happened to the induction selector/content experiment

The circuit-specific experiment asks whether an induction contribution can be separated into:

- an attention coefficient $e$, which says how strongly a source position is selected;
- a projected content vector $u$, which says what that source writes after the value and output maps; and
- their product $eu$.

For two semantic source roles at each of four head sites, the selected write is

$$
B(E,U)=\sum_{s,r}e_{s,r}u_{s,r}.
$$

Swapping $E$, $U$, or both between a recipient and donor gives an exact two-factor interaction decomposition. If the
separate swaps predictably change the intended answer while preserving active controls, they would identify a more
meaningful unit than an attention head: a selection variable, a routed-content variable, and their interaction.

R592 was the first attempt to run that full experiment with fixed counterfactuals and all 50,304 logits. The managed
runner stopped after exactly one batch: 32 endpoints, one model forward, zero backward passes or weight updates. No
factor swap, active control, score, bootstrap, held-out SELECT example, FINAL data, or out-of-distribution data was
opened. The terminal is therefore **invalid instrument**, not a positive result and not a null result.

Two checks were wrong:

1. The equality contribution and its complementary remainder were computed through separate float32 reductions. Due
   only to floating-point reduction order, their saved sum differed from the one-pass head output by
   $5.34\times10^{-5}$, above the registered $10^{-5}$ absolute threshold. The independent reconstruction of the
   complete nine-head attention output was exactly equal to the native output.
2. The code demanded that both semantic roles be equality-supported at every endpoint. The dataset was intentionally
   built so each positive endpoint has exactly one supported role and negative controls have none. Across FIT the
   endpoint counts are 1,440 with one role and 288 with zero; across SELECT they are 720 and 144. The observed support
   array was correct, while the `all roles are true` validity predicate was impossible.

The independent audit verified all 36 evidence files, 5.21 GB of bytes, the first batch's exact rows and token order,
and the fact that no scientific split or outcome was reached. The failed run is preserved and will not be rerun.

## R593: repairing the instrument without weakening it

R593 is now being built under a prospective amendment. It changes no scientific hypothesis, counterfactual,
threshold, bootstrap, or data split. It changes only the two defective validity checks.

The support check will compare the observed Boolean array to an expected array recomputed from the frozen token and
semantic-position authority. It also binds the exact FIT and SELECT counts above. Flipping either a true or false bit
must fail; filling the array with all true must fail.

The numerical check keeps the original $10^{-5}$ bound. From the already observed float32 attention pattern $p$, value
vectors $v$, and output weights $W_O$, it independently computes in float64

$$
\begin{aligned}
C^{64} &= W_O^{64}\sum_k m_kp_k^{64}v_k^{64},\\
R^{64} &= W_O^{64}\sum_k(1-m_k)p_k^{64}v_k^{64},\\
H^{64} &= W_O^{64}\sum_kp_k^{64}v_k^{64}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The remainder may not be defined as $H-C$, because that would make the check tautological. A model-free stress test at
larger-than-observed scale gives

$$
\max|C^{64}+R^{64}-H^{64}|=4.55\times10^{-13},
$$

while a planted $2\times10^{-5}$ error still fails the unchanged $10^{-5}$ bound. The native full-attention
reconstruction remains a separate float32 validity check. The wider evidence arrays add about 454 MB; the storage
gates are being recomputed rather than silently consuming the old safety margin.

No R593 GPU run is authorized yet. It needs model-free tests using real zero-role and one-role examples, exact source
hashes, a dry run, and a different-agent byte-level review before it can enter the managed queue.

## The reusable circuit-experiment compiler

In parallel, we are refactoring the repeated authority, batching, intervention, evidence, and publication logic into a
shared compiler. Its purpose is not convenience alone: the same classes of bugs have recurred when every circuit gets
a hand-written harness.

Claude is being used as an independent adversarial test writer and reviewer. One re-review found three ways the
otherwise green framework could still fail open: an invalid predicate action could let a failed instrument continue;
an invalid capitalization of an artifact type could disguise an outcome during a dry run; and a nested function could
use dynamic imports to read environment state while evading the purity check. The implementation owner added typed
checks and nested-code inspection at commit `100e4f3ff`. A fresh read-only review confirmed those repairs but found two
adjacent paths: projector functions could still obtain state through their global dictionary, bound object, or keyword
defaults, and predicate-evaluation functions were not subject to the same purity check at all. A further repair and
another fresh Claude review are now running. Passing ordinary tests is not being treated as approval until an
independent attack also passes.
