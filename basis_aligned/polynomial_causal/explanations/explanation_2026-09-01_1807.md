# Full research update since 15:18 — 2026-09-01 18:07 UTC

## Goal and current answer

Our goal is to replace bilin18 with a smaller, understandable tensor program that still:

1. predicts unseen and out-of-distribution text;
2. works when several replacements are installed together;
3. responds correctly to named removals, swaps, and causal edits; and
4. is actually smaller after counting every stored tensor, data type, operation, and runtime state.

For MLP0, the interpretation goal is more specific: explain its output as a token-only computation, a
token-by-context computation, a context-only computation, and a small normalization correction, then determine
whether those pieces admit a simpler program.

The main conclusions since the 15:18 update are:

- The exact native MLP0 calculation is now separated into those four roles. Token-only and token-by-context are
  jointly dominant; context-only is smaller but real; the normalization correction is lowest priority.
- Attention head 3 is the largest architectural carrier of the token-by-context role, but the other heads provide
  substantial redundant service. Head labels are therefore not the final explanatory basis.
- The rank-448 MLP0 approximation fails mainly on token-only and token-by-context calculations. Five alternative
  ways of choosing its input coordinates, simple routers, and two families of low-rank quadratic corrections all
  failed to beat ordinary covariance rank at equal storage price.
- The remaining rank-448 error can nevertheless be written exactly as a known quadratic tensor. This is a useful
  interpretation, but its input-side spectrum is too broad to compress economically with the tested methods.
- A physical 511,758,646-value, 1,023,517,292-byte whole-model tier is again fully validated after correcting its
  stored data types. A smaller 495,847,230-value tier is a strong physical near-miss, not an adopted tier.
- The old claim that all of layer17 was replaceable by a few quadratic functions was caused by overlapping fitting
  and evaluation rows. On clean data it fails catastrophically. Layer16's analogous small program remains real and
  layer-specific.
- The next attention interpretation should define “the same feature” by downstream indistinguishability. Similar
  output vectors are only a preliminary screen; the stronger object is a quotient or basis induced by what later
  layers can actually distinguish.

Cross-entropy, abbreviated CE, is the average negative natural logarithm of the probability assigned to the correct
next token. Lower is better. One **nat** is its natural-logarithm unit. When this document says a replacement has
`+0.01 nat` damage, it means its CE is 0.01 higher, hence worse, than the comparison model.

## 1. Exact MLP0 roles and their importance

Let `t` be the centered current-token contribution to MLP0's input, `c` the centered attention0 context write, and
let

`B(u,v) = Down((Left u) * (Right v))`

be MLP0's bilinear map. `Left` and `Right` project a 1,152-number residual vector into the layer's product
coordinates, `*` multiplies corresponding coordinates, and `Down` maps the products back to 1,152 output numbers.
The exact centered roles are, schematically,

- token-only: `T = B(t,t)` plus its centering terms;
- token-by-context: `I = B(t,c) + B(c,t)`;
- context-only: `C = B(c,c)` plus its centering terms;
- normalization: `S`, the change caused by the example-specific RMS normalization gain.

We physically evaluated all 16 subsets of `T/C/I/S`. This lets us distinguish three notions of importance:

- **deletion cost:** remove one role while keeping the other three;
- **singleton benefit:** add one role to a baseline containing none of the four;
- **Shapley benefit:** average the role's marginal benefit across every possible background subset.

On 96 held-out SELECT documents:

| role | deletion cost from the complete computation | singleton benefit | Shapley benefit | Shapley share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| token-only `T` | 0.081710 nat | 3.343782 nat | 1.498335 nat | 42.55% |
| context-only `C` | 0.054333 nat | 1.180933 nat | 0.417729 nat | 11.86% |
| token-by-context `I` | **0.125540 nat** | **3.375897 nat** | **1.537530 nat** | **43.67%** |
| normalization `S` | 0.000774 nat | 0.354463 nat | 0.067493 nat | 1.92% |

The branches were therefore not each about one nat when deleted. `T` and `I` are co-primary and strongly
overlapping: either one alone rescues more than 3.3 nats, yet deleting one from the complete computation costs only
0.08–0.13 nat. `C` is secondary but cannot be ignored. `S` is the first role to deprioritize.

This also explains why a single ablation number is insufficient. Two computations can each be nearly sufficient
alone while being partly redundant when both are present.

## 2. What the head result does and does not say

Attention0 has nine heads. If `y_h` is head `h`'s 128-number value result and `O_h` is that head's block of the
output-projection matrix, its residual-stream write is

`a_h = O_h y_h`, and `c = sum_h a_h` apart from a tiny floating-point rounding remainder.

Because `I` is linear in `c`, it splits exactly into nine architectural head terms:

`I_h = B(t,a_h) + B(a_h,t)`, so `I = sum_h I_h` plus the numerical remainder.

The 21-arm experiment measured every head alone and every head removed from the full set. Head 3, meaning the fourth
zero-indexed head, was largest: it recovered 79.3% of the full interaction endpoint gap alone, while removing it
from all heads lost 32.9% of the gap. The nine singleton percentages summed to 190.2%, while the nine leave-one-out
percentages summed to only 40.1%. Those totals cannot be read as fractions of a fixed pie; their mismatch is evidence
of redundancy and nonlinear downstream compensation.

The experiment did **not** evaluate all `2^9 = 512` head subsets, so it did not uniquely allocate every pairwise or
higher-order behavioral interaction. Inside `I` itself there is no head-by-head product: it is exactly additive over
heads. Products such as `B(a_3,a_8)` occur in `C`, because

`B(c,c) = sum_h B(a_h,a_h) + sum_{h<k} [B(a_h,a_k)+B(a_k,a_h)]`.

Later layers and CE are nonlinear, so the additive `I_h` terms can still have redundant or synergistic behavioral
effects after being inserted into the rest of the network.

## 3. The larger-document rank-448 audit

The deployed rank-448 MLP0 approximation stores one shared 448-coordinate input code. We decomposed its output error
relative to native MLP0 into the same `T/C/I/S` roles and physically evaluated every subset on 384 distinct source
documents, or 73,728 scored token positions.

| source of rank-448 error | Shapley CE damage | share of positive named damage |
|---|---:|---:|
| token-only `T` | 0.003344 nat | 38.0% |
| context-only `C` | 0.000673 nat | 7.6% |
| token-by-context `I` | 0.004791 nat | 54.4% |
| normalization `S` | -0.001670 nat | compensating rather than damaging |

`T+I` account for 92.4% of the positive named damage. `I` led on two 96-document waves and `T` led on the other two,
so the earlier interaction-only compression hypothesis was rejected. The stable target is the joint token-dependent
grammar.

Different roles are still allowed different internal formulas. `T` is a finite function on all 50,257 known token
inputs, `I` is bilinear in token and context, and `C` is quadratic in context. But they may share downstream output
directions. This distinction became important in the next result.

## 4. Separate computations, shared output directions

We projected the exact rank-448 error into 64 output directions learned only from fitting documents and evaluated
the correction on disjoint documents. These are oracle ceilings because the experiment reads the native error at
the evaluated position; they are not executable compressors by themselves.

| 64-direction correction | held-out CE damage above native |
|---|---:|
| rank-448 without correction | 0.007947 |
| separate token-32 plus interaction-32 bases | 0.005876 |
| one joint token+interaction rank-64 basis | 0.004838 |
| one rank-64 basis for total error | **0.004769** |
| ordinary covariance rank-640 | **0.002868** |

The joint token+interaction basis beat separate equal-budget bases by 0.001037 nat. Therefore different formulas do
not imply separate output dictionaries. The best present picture is **separate path computations feeding a shared
downstream output interface**. Any future path-specific method must still beat a global equal-price comparator.

## 5. Input metrics and routing were tested, not assumed

Several plausible ways of changing the rank-448 input basis failed:

- token/interaction derivative directions reconstructed the named branches more cleanly but worsened CE;
- a downstream CE-gradient, or Fisher, basis was reproducible but worse in every held-out wave;
- shuffled Fisher directions failed catastrophically, confirming that the real Fisher basis was aligned but not a
  good finite-error compressor;
- a two-expert document router remained worse and larger than one ordinary rank-768 program even when an impossible
  oracle used future losses to choose the expert;
- a learned causal router using position, token form, repetition distance, and training-only frequency selected only
  by position and performed worse than its constant expert.

This closes the tested metric and simple routing families. It does not prove that every possible nonlinear program
is impossible; it says that a new attempt should change the represented object rather than invent another local
weighting of the same 448-dimensional input subspace.

## 6. Exact quadratic error, but no cheap factorization

Let `z` be MLP0's 1,152-number normalized input and let `u_j` be one of the 64 shared output-error directions. The
coefficient of the exact native-minus-rank448 correction along that direction can be derived from the known weights:

`q_j(z) = z^T A_j z + beta_j`.

`A_j` is a 1,152 by 1,152 symmetric matrix. The expression means multiply `A_j` by `z`, dot the result with `z`, and
add the constant `beta_j`. The full matrices reproduce the directly measured coefficient with relative squared
error about `4e-12`. Thus the approximation error has an exact mathematical description and needs no future label or
native output at runtime.

The economical versions failed:

- 24 signed square terms per output coordinate recovered only 32.0% of the oracle gain and scored `+0.006929 nat`;
- one shared Tucker input subspace scored `+0.006989 nat` at its largest preregistered size;
- ordinary rank-640 scored `+0.002868 nat` while using only 145,856 more values than the first quadratic program;
- matched-price ordinary ranks 494, 552, and 638 beat Tucker ranks 96, 160, and 226 at every point, with the margin
  increasing rather than shrinking.

“Tucker” here means all 64 quadratic matrices share one smaller input basis but retain separate small core matrices.
The failure means the tensor is broad on its input modes even though it is narrow on its output mode. It is
interpretable as a quadratic tensor, but the tested decompositions do not make it a better compressor.

## 7. Whole-model storage correction and current frontier

A physical audit found that an earlier claimed all-two-byte artifact still stored the generated MLP0 and MLP4
programs in float32. Its behavior was measured correctly, but its honest byte price was 1,076,606,060, not the
published 1,023,517,292.

The artifact was rebuilt with all 26,544,384 generated MLP values physically stored in BF16. BF16, or bfloat16, is a
two-byte floating-point format; it is only a storage representation here, not an interpretability method. The hook
converts the stored values to float32 for computation.

The repaired whole program contains exactly **511,758,646 values and 1,023,517,292 bytes**. It has `+0.012331 nat`
census damage, retains 43 of 62 behavioral certificates, passes fresh and shifted-text tests, and preserves the
signed effect of the registered attention16 removal with cosine `0.986522`. This restores its predictive,
out-of-distribution, compositional, and named-intervention status.

A still smaller composite containing the layer16 quadratic program is physically valid at **495,847,230 values and
991,694,460 bytes**, or 0.92359 GiB. Its aggregate behavior is strong: `+0.052983 nat`, 17 of 62 certificates, and
near-perfect agreement with the independently predicted composition. It is not adopted because one preregistered
per-position precision test failed: its maximum CE change from the float32-source parent was 0.117641 nat against a
0.100 limit. We did not relax the bar after seeing the result.

## 8. Layer17 correction

The layer17 dossier contained an old result suggesting that the whole layer might be represented by a few quadratic
functions. We ported exactly that 14,984-value construction into the current physical harness and separated fitting
from evaluation.

The old overlapping-row CE reproduced exactly, but clean held-out and fresh local `R²` values were `-29.7` and
`-32.1`. `R²=1` would mean perfect prediction, `R²=0` would equal predicting the mean, and a negative number means
worse than that mean predictor. The program caused `+0.303 nat` census damage and retained 0 of 62 certificates.

The old whole-layer claim was therefore an overlap artifact. We do not tune its rank, compose it, or call layer17 a
four-quadratic-function layer. Layer16's clean held-out `R²` around 0.82 under the analogous construction remains a
real, layer-specific result.

## 9. Why head-output clustering is only the first screen

There are exact but limited gauge freedoms. A change of coordinates inside a head's private value space can be
undone by the corresponding output projection, leaving `a_h` unchanged. Heads can be permuted with all their
parameters. Query/key coordinates are more constrained because rotary position encoding fixes frequency planes; an
exact query/key transformation must commute with those rotations. Arbitrary continuous mixing of complete heads is
not a symmetry because different heads have different nonlinear attention patterns.

For that reason, output-projected writes `a_h` are safer than raw value coordinates. A rank-64 screen has been
preregistered to compare:

- modes with large pooled energy across all nine centered head writes;
- modes with large energy in the total attention write;
- modes associated with head 3;
- a random orthonormal control.

It will measure how many heads write each mode, whether the subspace repeats across fitting halves, how much of `I`
and `C` it reconstructs on held-out documents, and its physical singleton/removal CE benefit. This is a geometric
screen, not yet the final definition of a feature and not a compression claim.

## 10. Answer to the new question: define sameness by downstream discrimination

Yes—the proposed framework makes sense, and it is stronger than clustering head outputs by ordinary distance.

### 10.1 Two computations are the same only relative to a consumer

Let `u` and `v` be two candidate writes. Let `R` denote the downstream response map for a specified set of later
computations and interventions. In the simplest local form, `R` is the Jacobian that maps a small residual-stream
change into changes in attention1, MLP1, later residual states, or logits. Define

`distance_downstream(u,v) = average ||R(u-v)||²`.

If this distance is nearly zero on held-out data and under the registered interventions, later computation cannot
distinguish `u` from `v` for the purpose being measured. The equivalence relation is therefore

`u ~ v` when `R(u-v)` is approximately zero.

The unobservable directions form the approximate kernel of `R`; meaningful features live in the quotient space
“all writes divided by downstream-invisible differences.” This is an **interaction-determined basis**. It naturally
allows two copy heads to use different noise-resistant attention patterns but provide the same downstream service.

The consumer must be named. Two writes may be equivalent to attention1 but distinguishable to MLP1, or equivalent
for ordinary next-token CE but different under a copy-specific intervention. There is no single context-free answer
to “the same feature.” The project should retain a small family of consumer-defined quotients and test whether they
agree.

### 10.2 Writer equivalence and reader equivalence are dual questions

There are two complementary structures:

1. **Different producers, same downstream effect.** Several heads or MLP paths write different vectors whose
   difference lies in the downstream-invisible kernel. These should be grouped as redundant implementations of one
   service.
2. **Same input information, different downstream uses.** Several readers take information from the same residual
   subspace but send it into different outputs. These should share an input feature in the explanation while keeping
   their distinct output actions.

The first is found by comparing response vectors of writes. The second is found by comparing the input directions
read by downstream operators. If two downstream maps `L_1` and `L_2` have the same important right-hand, or input,
subspace but different left-hand output subspaces, the program should store one shared input vocabulary plus two
consumer-specific output maps.

This dual treatment handles the concern that output clustering alone misses “different outputs, same inputs.”

### 10.3 Bilinear layers, LL1 blocks, and gauge

A bilinear layer is a three-index tensor `W[o,i,j]`: output coordinate `o` receives products of left-input coordinate
`i` and right-input coordinate `j`. A canonical polyadic decomposition writes it as

`W = sum_r output_r ⊗ left_r ⊗ right_r`.

Individual factors are generally not unique: components can be permuted and reciprocally rescaled, and larger
degenerate subspaces can rotate. Therefore the explanation should not treat one fitted factor vector as canonical
unless an identifiability theorem and its assumptions apply.

An LL1, or rank-`(L,L,1)` block-term, decomposition groups an `L`-dimensional family of left/right input pairings
that all feed one output direction. That matches “different inputs, same output.” The dual structure—shared input
information feeding multiple distinguishable outputs—is represented by a block with one shared input factor or
input subspace and a multi-dimensional output mode, for example a rank-`(L,1,R)` or Tucker-style block depending on
which input half is shared.

There is one useful simplification: if the *exact same scalar product* always multiplies several fixed output
vectors, those vectors simply sum into one output vector. “Same inputs, different outputs” is only genuinely distinct
when there are multiple input coordinates in a shared subspace, different gates or partners, or downstream consumers
that preserve separate output directions.

The gauge-safe object is consequently not a particular LL1 factorization. It is the input subspace, output quotient,
and contraction restricted to them. We can choose a convenient orthonormal or sparse basis inside each identified
subspace after the subspace itself survives held-out causal tests.

### 10.4 Applying this to bilin18 attention

Bilin18 does not use a single query/key score. For head `h`, query position `i`, and source position `j`, its
pre-mask attention score is proportional to

`s_h(i,j) = <q_h(i), k_h(j)> * <q2_h(i), k2_h(j)>`.

Thus each head composes two bilinear matching functions. The proposed shared vocabulary can occur at several levels:

- several heads may share the first query/key bilinear form but combine it with different second forms;
- they may share the second form but use different first forms;
- they may use different score factorizations that produce the same attention pattern;
- different attention patterns may retrieve value writes that become equivalent after output projection and
  downstream computation.

Because rotary encoding depends on relative position, the exact query/key object is not merely `Q^T K`. For relative
offset `delta`, it is a family like `Q^T R_delta K`, where `R_delta` is the known rotary rotation. A cross-head
decomposition should therefore factor the tensor indexed by head, relative-position family, and the two score
halves—not compare raw query vectors in an arbitrary coordinate system.

One concrete model is a shared dictionary of first-half and second-half bilinear forms:

`score_h = sum_{r,s} core[h,r,s] * form1_r(query,key) * form2_s(query,key)`.

Sparsity or block structure in `core[h,r,s]` would say which common matchers each head composes. A shared `r` with
several different `s` values is precisely “one half shared across heads, paired with different other features.” The
decomposition must be compared with matched-rank controls and must predict held-out attention scores, value writes,
and physical CE; factor similarity alone is only a screen.

### 10.5 The revised experiment sequence

The best sequence is now:

1. **Geometric calibration at attention0.** Use output-projected head writes to verify stable multi-head residual
   subspaces and establish random, total-write, and dominant-head controls.
2. **Downstream-response quotient.** Apply controlled mode perturbations and record their signed effects on
   attention1, MLP1, later residual states, and logits. Find directions under the metric
   `G = average(R^T R)`, so two directions are close when downstream computation treats them alike.
3. **Producer grouping.** Express each head's write in that response basis and test whether head 3 and the tail are
   redundant producers of the same causal modes. Require held-out and intervention transport, not just covariance.
4. **Reader grouping.** Factor the input-side read maps of attention1 and MLP1 under the same metric. Identify shared
   residual subspaces read by multiple consumers, while preserving their distinct output maps.
5. **Double-QK block decomposition.** Build a gauge-respecting shared vocabulary for the two query/key bilinear
   factors across attention0 heads, score whether heads reuse one half with different partners, and connect those
   score components through values and output projection to the downstream quotient.
6. **Attention1 replication.** Repeat only the stable definitions at attention1. This checks whether the vocabulary
   is a real early-layer organizing principle rather than an attention0-specific coincidence.

The opposing outcomes are clear. If several architectural heads map to the same stable downstream-response modes,
then “copy service” is more fundamental than head number. If geometric modes disappear under the response metric,
then similar writes were not functionally equivalent. If shared query/key halves predict scores but not value writes
or downstream effects, they describe attention geometry but not the complete circuit. If the same input subspace is
read into several distinct response directions, the final program should share the reader vocabulary and keep
consumer-specific outputs rather than forcing those paths into one feature.

This is a broader and more principled target than clustering heads. It treats feature identity as an experimentally
testable property of the contraction graph: which differences later tensors can and cannot detect.
