# Explanation update — 2026-09-03 07:47 UTC

## The goal

We are trying to replace the model with a smaller, explicit tensor program whose parts are meaningful computations,
not merely compressed matrices. A proposed circuit part should tell us what information it reads, what it computes,
what it writes, and which later computations use that write. It should:

1. predict activations and behavioral effects on held-out and shifted text;
2. group pieces from different heads or MLPs when downstream computation treats them as the same variable, while
   splitting one head or MLP when its pieces do different jobs;
3. remain predictable when combined with other extracted parts;
4. support selective swaps, edits, and removals without damaging unrelated computations; and
5. remain stable across documents, fit seeds, and arbitrary rotations of an internal basis.

Fewer dimensions, lower reconstruction error, or fewer stored numbers do not establish those properties. Rung 522
uses rank four only as an equal-capacity comparison across the task-trained, random, recovery-only, and
label-randomized conditions.

## The exact computation being run

For a token, attention8 writes a vector `y` containing 1,152 floating-point numbers. A natural donor example has its
own write `d`. A learned matrix `Q` has shape `1152 x 4` and orthonormal columns. The intervention is

`y_changed = y + ((d-y) Q) Q^T`.

The inner product `(d-y)Q` extracts four coordinates of the donor difference. Multiplication by `Q^T` maps those
coordinates back to the 1,152-dimensional write space. Only `QQ^T` matters: rotating the four columns of `Q` changes
the coordinate names but does not change the intervention.

For each circuit-member token, the fit tries to reproduce the signed change in cross-entropy loss caused by swapping
all of attention8. For matched non-member tokens, it penalizes any projected change. In ordinary terms, the proposed
subspace must carry the known circuit effect without simply being a direction that perturbs everything.

The 103 fitted objects are:

- 15 projectors trained on two circuit identities and tested on the omitted third identity;
- 15 recovery-only controls that reproduce effects without the selectivity penalty;
- 20 single-circuit projectors, including five for the reserved fourth circuit, that measure whether the task is
  detectable at all;
- 48 projectors trained on 16 exact randomized circuit assignments for each omitted-circuit case; and
- 5 projectors trained on all three known circuits, from which a final candidate may be selected using only
  VALIDATION performance eligibility and projector geometry.

The registered maximum fitting cost is exactly 20,600 model forwards and 20,600 backwards. If VALIDATION passes,
the complete non-removal inference cost is exactly 9,422 forwards. Mean-centered removal is a separate conditional
36-forward sweep and occurs only if the combined held-out A/B gates and the fourth-circuit/fingerprint test pass.

## What TEST requires

Before TEST can open, the task-trained projector must predict its omitted circuit in every donor/direction cell for
at least four of five seeds. Its signed response must align with the full attention8 response, recover a positive
fraction of that response, produce at least `.02` nat root-mean-square change on member tokens, and be at least four
times stronger on members than matched controls. Row bootstraps must keep the four-times margin above zero.

The result must also beat:

- recovery-only fits, which reveal whether selectivity came from the penalty rather than generic response fitting;
- 20 random four-dimensional projectors;
- 16 circuit-label randomizations that preserve the overlap and matching structure; and
- same-seed single-circuit projectors, which must themselves be healthy and detectably active so a failed shared fit
  cannot be blamed on a dead target.

If those gates pass, the geometry-selected all-three projector is applied to the reserved fourth circuit and to all
32 circuits in the existing fingerprint battery. The four intended circuits must separate from the other 28, and
the intervention must not create broad loss damage outside the quartet. Only then is the projector mean-centered
and removed on TEST.

## The different mathematical follow-up now prepared

I also implemented and preregistered a conditional gradient-image test. It will run only if rung 522 passes all of
its finite prediction and removal gates.

Let `Y` be the complete attention8 write for a batch and let `S_c(Y)` be member-token CE minus matched-control CE for
circuit `c`. One backward pass gives the downstream gradient `G_c=dS_c/dY`. For a natural donor difference `Z`, the
full first-order response and the response carried by the fitted projector are

`b_c(Z) = <G_c,Z>` and `a_c(Z) = <G_c, Z QQ^T> = <G_c Q, Z Q>`.

This separates two claims that are easy to conflate:

- **gradient containment:** how much of the complete downstream gradient lies inside `Q`; and
- **natural-swap transport:** whether `Q` carries the gradient effect along the donor differences that actually
  occur, even if the complete gradient also has unrelated directions.

A planted CPU example confirms that natural-swap transport can be exact even when less than 10% of total gradient
energy lies inside the projector. Another planted example uses a four-dimensional `Q` but donor differences that
excite only one coordinate; the method correctly reports that only one direction is identifiable. All four tests,
including invariance to rotations inside `Q`, pass.

This follow-up is not a compression score. On fresh documents it would ask whether later layers literally use the
same variable, whether all four coordinates are naturally excited, whether the result beats the archived random and
label-randomized projectors, and whether the local derivative predicts the finite intervention. A failure would
limit the interpretation of an otherwise successful rung 522 to a finite donor-swap equivalence rather than a
general downstream reader.
