# What the late blocks' "tail" is for: a novel-token identity channel (2026-09-04, Claude lane, §2790–§2801)

Plain-language chapter expanding project shorthand. Sign convention (§2135): every cross-entropy (CE) number below is CE ADDED
ABOVE THE REAL MODEL on 64 held-out documents (docs 0–63 of the fixed natural-text set, 256 positions each, 16,384 predicted
tokens) — LOWER IS BETTER. Nothing in this chapter changes the strict explained fraction (5.348% / 10.923% / 4.727 nat / 0 of 68
installed tail items) or the §312 frontier; it is a description of one channel, with its predictions and misses.

## Terms

- **Residual stream**: the 1152-dimensional vector each block reads from and adds to. **Late blocks**: blocks 8–17 of 18.
- **Core / tail**: the earlier chapter (01:37) found that the late blocks' feed-forward parts (MLPs) all read the stream through
  one shared 768-dimensional frame — the **core** — obtained as the top eigenvectors of the average covariance of their
  (normalised) inputs. The remaining 384 dimensions are the **tail**. "The late tail write" = the part of each late MLP's
  output that lands in those 384 dimensions, with its average removed, accumulated block to block exactly as the model's own
  block-entry scaling (λ₀) carries it.
- **Withholding from a consumer**: the model runs unchanged, but one named reader (the later MLPs, the later attention, or the
  final readout = the unembedding matrix W_U that turns the stream into logits) is shown the stream MINUS the accumulated late
  tail write. The CE it adds is that consumer's use of the channel.
- **Target classes** (from Codex's audited `ops/target_token_classes.py`): at a position, the next token is **induction** if
  some earlier position had the same current token followed by the same next token; **repeat** if the next token occurs in the
  context but not as an induction match; **novel** if it does not occur in the context at all.

## What was measured, in order

1. **Who writes it** (§2796): the late MLPs themselves, from their core input — 73% of the tail write is the MLP evaluated on
   the core alone; it accumulates rather than being wired block to block (§2790).
2. **Who reads it** (§2797): withholding it from every consumer costs .1459; from the final readout alone .1130; from the later
   MLPs alone .0523; from later attention alone .0031. The readout is the majority consumer (marginal share .60). Blocks 15–17
   write 70% of what the readout uses.
3. **How many dimensions the readout uses** (§2798): many. Keeping only the top 8 / 32 / 128 of the tail's own principal
   directions recovers .18 / .32 / .62 of the .1130; the unembedding's own top directions do worse (.11 / .19 / .49). The
   effective rank is ~261. My preregistered "small interface" predictions (four of them) were falsified.
4. **How the late MLPs select it** (§2799): the tail is read through a gate that depends linearly on the 768 core coordinates;
   the exact weight-side operator family has effective rank ~600 of 768, and using only the top 64 / 128 / 256 gate modes
   leaves .089 / .063 / .033 of damage. No small gate exists.
5. **Which tokens pay** (§2800, this hour): 98% of the .1130 falls on NOVEL targets (per token: novel .188, induction .047,
   repeat −.009 — repeats get slightly BETTER without the channel). By frequency in a 24.6k-token reference corpus, the damage is
   monotone across all ten bins: targets never seen there pay .285 per token (31% of tokens, 78% of the damage), the most
   frequent targets GAIN .063 per token. It is not selective on the model's confidence (r = −.03 with the token's own baseline
   loss), and early positions pay slightly more, not less (a confound with class: fewer context tokens means more novel targets).
6. **Frequency signal or token identity?** (§2801): the one tail direction that most moves logits along "rare vs frequent"
   is worth 7–8% of the channel whichever way it is measured (keep only it: .071 recovered; deny only it: 8.1% lost; additive to
   .99). It is the unembedding's own leading tail direction (cosine .977). The other 92% needs the remaining 383 dimensions.
   Without the channel the model's predictions are flatter (mean entropy +.073 nat), not over-confident.

## The picture

The late blocks split their computation: a shared 768-dimensional quadratic core, plus a 384-dimensional output channel through
which they tell the unembedding WHICH out-of-context, infrequent token is coming. That is why the channel is high-rank in every
frame (token identity for a long vocabulary tail is not a few directions), why it has no small gate, and why withholding it helps
in-context and frequent targets (their rare competitors lose evidence). The three largest writers (blocks 15, 16, 17) all put their
value on novel targets, but on different tokens (token-level correlation .13–.25): three writers of the same kind of evidence,
not one shared circuit token by token.

## What this does and does not buy

It is a diagnostic attribution, not an executable compression: no parameter is saved, nothing installs into the frontier, and the
"rare-token gain" program item the last rung tested turned out to be worth 8%. What it buys is a functional label for 384 of the
late blocks' 1152 dimensions and a closed question: the search for a small dictionary or interface there is over, for a
mechanistic reason.

## Misses and process, preserved

Of 19 preregistered predictions in §2798–§2801, 11 were falsified. Three of the misses were errors of registration FORM (an
effective rank on an uncentred moment; a ratio whose denominator changed sign; an inequality written the wrong way for the arm as

## Your questions about the 768/384 split

### “The residual stream is 1152. Are you claiming that all late blocks read the same 768 dimensions and write to the rest?”

Not literally. That wording was too compressed.

The claim is specifically about the **late MLPs**, not the whole block. Let $P$ be the fitted projector onto the shared
768-dimensional frame and let $R=I-P$ project onto the remaining 384 dimensions. For the normalised input $x$ to a late
MLP, we write

$$
c=Px,\qquad t=Rx,\qquad x=c+t.
$$

The 768-dimensional part $c$ is the common frame that preserves most of what the late MLPs do. It is not an exact input
bottleneck: their weights still read $t$, and removing $t$ from all late MLP inputs adds about $0.125$ nat of CE loss.
The exact bilinear expansion makes this explicit. Ignoring the bias for readability,

$$
M(c+t)=W_D\left[(W_L(c+t))\odot(W_R(c+t))\right]
$$

splits into

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{cc} &= W_D[(W_Lc)\odot(W_Rc)],\\
M_{ct} &= W_D[(W_Lc)\odot(W_Rt)+(W_Lt)\odot(W_Rc)],\\
M_{tt} &= W_D[(W_Lt)\odot(W_Rt)].
\end{aligned}
$$

The mixed term $M_{ct}$ accounts for about 83% of the performance recovered by giving the late MLPs access to the
384-dimensional part. So a better summary is:

> The late MLPs share a useful 768-dimensional main frame, but they also make a high-rank, core-dependent linear read
> of the other 384 dimensions.

The output is not sent only to the 384-dimensional complement either. We can separately project an MLP's output into
$PM(x)$ and $RM(x)$. About 70% of the mixed-term output lands back in the 768-dimensional frame and about 30% lands in
the other 384 dimensions. The “late tail write” in this chapter refers only to $RM(x)$; every late MLP also writes into
the 768-dimensional frame.

### “Do the late blocks communicate with one another?”

Yes. The model still has its ordinary residual recurrence: each attention and MLP output is added to the stream seen by
later blocks. The 768/384 analysis does not cut those connections.

What the experiments found is a more particular communication pattern:

- Late MLPs do use earlier tail writes. Preventing later MLPs from seeing the accumulated late-MLP tail writes adds
  $0.0523$ nat of CE loss.
- A receiver usually gets most of that useful signal from the previous one or two blocks. Contributions from older
  writers become smaller in the receiver's normalised coordinates.
- This is generally not one vector being copied unchanged from block to block. For blocks 8–16, a linear model using
  the previous tail state predicts essentially none of the next MLP's tail write (median held-out $R^2\approx0.001$).
  Each MLP mostly computes a new tail write from its 768-dimensional core input.
- These separately computed writes interact downstream. Dropping writers one at a time accounts for only about 52% of
  the effect of dropping them jointly. Pairwise interaction terms explain the joint effect to within about 8%.

So the picture is not “no communication,” nor is it a simple 384-dimensional wire passed along unchanged. It is closer
to repeated **core $\rightarrow$ tail writes**, with later MLPs reading some of the accumulated tail and the final
readout reading much more of it.

### “Are only blocks 15–17 different?”

No. Blocks 8–17 all participate in the shared-frame and tail-write pattern. Blocks 15–17 are quantitatively important
because their writes account for about 70% of the final readout's measured use of this channel. That does not establish
a separate three-block circuit: they are also the largest and most recent writes before the final readout, so they have
less opportunity to be diluted by later residual additions.

Block 17 is a real outlier in several measurements. It reads the tail roughly three times more strongly than a typical
late block, its input/output maps have lower effective rank, and its tail output is more predictable from block 16's
tail than the other late blocks are from their predecessors. But the exact bilinear split still says that only about
10% of block 17's tail write is the direct core-by-tail mixed term; most of its write is computed from the core. Thus
“block 17 partly preserves earlier tail information” is supported, while “blocks 15–17 alone form the tail channel” is
not.

### “How does this relate to the attention modules?”

The 768/384 coordinates live in the residual stream, so attention and MLPs both receive vectors in those coordinates.
But the **shared 768-dimensional frame was inferred for the late MLP computation**; it is not a claim that every late
attention head has the same 768-dimensional input bottleneck.

We ran two relevant attention measurements:

1. When the entire 384-dimensional residual component is withheld from late attention, the cost is about $0.0153$ nat,
   much smaller than the late MLP cost of about $0.125$ nat. Roughly 72% of that attention effect comes from changing the
   attention pattern—mainly the query side—rather than from changing the value vectors.
2. When only the accumulated **late-MLP-written** tail component is withheld from late attention, the cost is just
   $0.0031$ nat. The same component costs $0.0523$ when withheld from later MLPs and $0.1130$ when withheld from the final
   unembedding/readout.

Those three costs are interventions on different consumers and need not add exactly because the consumers interact.
Their comparison says that this particular late-MLP tail write is primarily an MLP-to-readout channel, secondarily an
MLP-to-MLP channel, and only weakly an MLP-to-attention channel. Attention still has its own writes and its own uses of
the residual tail; those are not included when this chapter says “late-MLP tail write.”

### Correct one-sentence summary

The late MLPs share a 768-dimensional main computational frame, while using the other 384 dimensions through a
high-rank core-gated read and writing to both parts of the residual stream; late MLP tail writes communicate somewhat
to later MLPs, very little to late attention, and mostly to the final readout, with blocks 15–17 supplying most—but not
all—of the readout-relevant signal.

### “Is this true on data in general, or only for a specific circuit?”

This was a **corpus-wide analysis, not an analysis of one named circuit**. We did not select only induction examples,
numbered lists, brackets, or another one of the circuit datasets. The shared frame and the descriptive models were fit
on 96 ordinary natural-text documents (documents 96–191), with 256 positions per document: 24,576 token positions in
total. The causal CE-loss measurements used a disjoint set of 64 documents (documents 0–63), again with 256 positions
each: 16,384 predicted tokens.

That supports the narrower statement that the late-MLP structure generalises from its fitting documents to other
documents drawn from this fixed natural-text corpus. The results also include broad token categories such as whether a
target is repeated from its recent context or novel, rather than being conditional on one hand-picked task.

It does **not** yet establish that this is a universal decomposition on all possible data. We have not tested this
particular 768/384 frame on strongly shifted distributions such as code-only data, mathematics, synthetic circuit
prompts, or another language corpus. Those would be genuine out-of-distribution tests. The circuit-specific induction,
list, and equality studies elsewhere in the repository therefore provide different evidence and should not be
silently folded into this claim.

There are also two kinds of statement in this chapter:

- Weight identities and the exact $M_{cc}+M_{ct}+M_{tt}$ expansion are algebraic. Once $P$ is fixed, they are true for
  every possible input vector.
- Claims about how much CE loss changes, how much each block matters, and what kinds of tokens use the channel are
  empirical averages over the held-out natural-text documents above.

So the clean answer is: **general across the held-out documents and tokens in this natural-text dataset, not specific
to one circuit; not yet demonstrated to be distribution-general or universal.**
