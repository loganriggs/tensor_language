## Decision

The immediate scientific bottleneck is no longer finding another locally compact
factorization. It is identifying a **small causal state that predicts finite
composition**. Three concrete interfaces now support that route:

1. the 68-action observational program can measure whether compressed early-MLP
   states preserve downstream responses and edits;
2. the 8-by-8 cut-rank experiment can test whether replacement interactions cross
   layer 5 through one or two latent channels; and
3. the site-by-amplitude response matrix suggests a nearly separable nonlinear law,
   but only for the ideal empirical-token-mean intervention so far.

The correct definition of simplicity remains operational: a smaller program must
predict untouched compositions or interventions at lower bytes/calls, not merely
reconstruct one tensor in one gauge.

MLP4 supplies the counterexample: length-1 recovers 20.25% of the gap, while the
evaluation-context and fit-context rows recover -2.06% and -3.11%. Although the
context mean is much closer to the site's native output in squared distance, it is
causally worse downstream. Site reconstruction error is therefore empirically
disqualified as a sufficient simplicity/success metric. The useful object is a
fixed-cost table judged by downstream transport and then confirmed without selection
on fresh data.

There is an exact local explanation for why the Euclidean mean can fail. Let
(z_{s,t,c}) be a site's native output for token (t) in context (c), let (q_{s,t})
be a context-free replacement, and expand downstream negative log likelihood around
the native output. With gradient (g) and Hessian (H), the conditional quadratic
objective has minimizer

\[
q^*_{s,t}=
\left(\mathbb E[H\mid t]\right)^\dagger
\left(\mathbb E[H z-g\mid t]\right),
\]

up to null-space freedom. The ordinary token mean (\mathbb E[z\mid t]) follows only
under restrictive conditions such as a context-independent isotropic Hessian and
vanishing conditional gradient. MLP4 falsifies using the ordinary mean as a universal
rule; MLP5 shows it can nevertheless be an excellent approximation at a specific
site. The cheapest next model is not a larger table but a low-description correction
(q'_{s,t}=a_s q_{s,t}+b_s), or a low-rank Fisher/Gauss--Newton metric, fit only on the
fit role and judged by fresh CE plus composition. A scalar and one shared bias cost
almost nothing at inference, directly implementing the user's proposed “costless”
correction. This test must report CE; a current queued per-site table-choice run selects
on top-1 and therefore addresses practical prediction/extraction, not CE faithfulness.

## New mathematical result: a nearly rank-one response surface

For S1840 define

\[
D_{s,a}=\operatorname{CE}\!\left(
\mu_s(t)+\alpha_a(y_s-\mu_s(t))
\right)-\operatorname{CE}(y),
\]

over 34 sites and nontrivial amplitudes
\(\alpha_a\in\{0.9,0.75,0.5,0.25,0\}\). A singular-value decomposition gives:

- rank 1: 98.8355% of Frobenius energy;
- rank 2: 99.9693%;
- rank-1 normalized reconstruction error: 10.79%.

Thus a useful descriptive model is

\[
D_{s,a}\approx A_s f(\alpha_a),
\]

one site amplitude times one shared nonlinear curve. Calibrating a site at
\(\alpha=0.25\) ranks its full \(\alpha=0\) damage with Spearman 0.990, although full
magnitude error remains 27.5%. The rank-one energy is 99.84% for attention sites,
98.64% for MLP sites, and about 99.60% separately for early and deep sites.

The shared curve also admits a one-parameter approximation,

\[
f(\alpha)\approx(1-\alpha)^{2.8824},
\]

with 4.65% relative curve error. A fixed quadratic gives 22.94% response-matrix
error versus 11.60% for the learned shared curve. The exponent above two is consistent
with a cancelled/small first-order loss term plus nonlinear downstream propagation,
but that mechanism is not identified by the fit alone.

This is a compact response law, not yet a compiler explanation. It was discovered
post hoc, uses the empirical-token-mean rather than deployed length-1 table, and has no
heldout-document uncertainty. Its value is a sharp new prediction: the actual
length-1 response surface should retain at least 98% rank-one energy, and one frozen
\(\alpha=0.25\) calibration should rank full damage above 0.95 on heldout documents.
