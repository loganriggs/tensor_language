## Circuit interpretation targets

A useful decomposition must eventually provide all seven properties below.

1. **Computational specification:** say what information is read, what operation or composition is performed, what is
   written, and which later computations use it.
2. **Cross-module grouping and within-module splitting:** merge parts of different attention heads or MLPs when later
   computation treats them as one variable, and split one native module when its parts serve different computations.
3. **Held-out and out-of-distribution prediction:** predict activations and behavioral effects on unseen examples,
   task variants, and distribution shifts.
4. **Extraction or sufficiency:** an isolated executable circuit, or an explicit interface plus declared background,
   reproduces the target computation or signed causal effect.
5. **Selective manipulation:** removing, swapping, or editing the circuit changes the intended behavior while
   preserving unrelated behavior, including explicit tests for redundancy and interaction.
6. **Composition and reuse:** shared subcomputations can serve multiple tasks or modules and behave predictably when
   combined with task-specific branches.
7. **Stable identification:** the units survive data splits, fitting restarts, and valid changes of basis, or are
   defined by indistinguishability under their downstream uses.

The program-level goal remains a smaller explicit tensor program that is jointly predictive, composable, selectively
manipulable, and simpler in literal storage, computation, states, and edges. Lower rank, fewer parameters, explained
variance, reconstruction error, or cross-entropy are prices and controls. None is circuit interpretation by itself.

## Confound audit

- **Disconnected interventions:** every future manipulation must prove the changed state is used by the evaluated
  program and include a live control that moves the output. The frontier withdrawal makes this non-negotiable.
- **Local morphology versus complete syntax:** v2 requires absolute coordinated-plural alignment and bidirectional
  coordinated/ordinary causal transfer. Paired noun edits alone cannot pass.
- **Data leakage and post-selection:** discovery and validation are prompt-, group-, and head-role-disjoint; all C
  errors remain; family-only fits cannot see cross-syntax donors; validation cannot pick a replacement seed or site.
- **Missing causal ceilings:** low finite full-state effects are scientific site failures, not runtime errors. All
  mandatory discovery cells must have a live ceiling before a site is eligible.
- **Nonlinear composition and multiple mediators:** single-site necessity is not taken as exhaustive. The frozen
  top-two prediction-position test measures joint neutralization and its finite interaction; reader claims require
  ordered reset and rescue rather than adding marginal effects.
- **Normalization and sign errors:** output and activation distances remain in separate units; cell denominators,
  direction conventions, finite floors, reset baselines, overshoot, and subject-direction cells are explicit.
- **Precision and optimizer failure:** deterministic initialization, five healthy seeds, medoid/median/four-of-five
  gates, finite checks, and higher-rank matched controls are frozen. The physical compiler must still freeze dtype,
  exact calls, retained bytes, and a runtime ceiling.
- **Cost feasibility:** 60,000 logical updates may expand to substantially more model calls because current-projector
  normalization requires complete discovery reference cells. The exact compiler must calculate this before model
  access. An unaffordable plan requires a prospective successor, not a partial run interpreted after the fact.

## Independent alternatives and what would kill them

1. **Task-14 finite causal state and reader localization — highest information.** It advances computational
   specification, sufficiency, manipulation, interactions, and stable identification. Kill it if independent review
   finds another semantic shortcut; if no site has the registered full-state ceilings; if coordinated transfer fails;
   if rank two/four is necessary; or if exact physical pricing exceeds the approved bound.
2. **Task-conditioned local causal-response eigenvector — cheap diagnostic inside the same run.** From donor residual
   changes and output gradients, the leading eigenvector exactly maximizes the locally linear rank-one effect. Compare
   it with finite DAS to identify whether curvature is necessary. Kill it as an explanation if it fails finite held-out
   transfer or controls; never award it credit for small rank alone.
3. **Downstream-response signatures across the existing circuit battery.** Represent a candidate weight term or
   subspace by its signed effects across the registered tasks, then group pieces across heads/MLPs when later behavior
   cannot distinguish them and split a module when its signatures differ. This directly targets cross-module grouping.
   Kill it if signatures are unstable across FIT/TEST, collapse to generic token difficulty, or grouped interventions
   fail sufficiency and selective removal.
4. **Predictive-state or Hankel-style realization across task histories.** Infer the smallest state whose responses
   predict every future registered intervention, rather than choosing a covariance width. This targets reusable state,
   composition, and OOD prediction. Kill it if held-out response rank is unstable, the inferred states do not support
   finite interchange, or the required state grows with the number of examples rather than computations.
5. **Archetypal or structured sparse dictionaries anchored to observed computations.** Convex-hull atoms, block
   sparsity, or a directed hierarchy may improve stability, but only if the atoms predict held-out circuit behavior
   and enable selective swaps better than ordinary sparse autoencoders. Kill it if stability comes without causal
   transfer, downstream grouping, or OOD improvement.
6. **Weight-first bilinear decomposition — deferred until a causal input/read pair exists.** For an identified input
   state and downstream read direction, each bilinear product has an exact finite causal-response signature. This can
   split MLPs and group equivalent terms without treating product indices as semantic. Kill raw weight-only versions:
   existing evidence shows their ranking changes drastically with the chosen output axis, which the weights alone do
   not identify.
