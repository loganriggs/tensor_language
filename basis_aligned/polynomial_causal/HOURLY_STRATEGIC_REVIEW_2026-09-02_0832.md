## The interpretability goals that remain binding

A useful circuit description should jointly support:

1. a short human-readable computational specification, not merely fewer matrix parameters;
2. grouping components across module or head boundaries when they perform the same downstream computation;
3. splitting a head or MLP when its parts perform different computations;
4. prediction on held-out data and genuinely different input distributions;
5. extraction of the named circuit as a reusable executable object;
6. selective removal or editing with little effect on unrelated circuits; and
7. compositional reuse, so a discovered component can participate in more than one larger circuit without being
   relearned as an unrelated coordinate system.

Rank reduction, quantization, activation reconstruction, or a small parameter count alone establishes none of these.
Such quantities may be a price after a circuit is identified, or a control, but they are not the research target.
Rung480 serves the actual target because later circuit responses choose a gauge-covariant projector, circuit labels
test semantic stability, and a positive result must still pass exact held-out selective removal. It is not being
credited as compression.
