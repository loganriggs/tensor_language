# Whole blocks and reader nodes in the retained final-layer interaction

13September2026. Directly pruning whole blocks gives modest storage savings but no reader-node pruning in the tested frames. A numerical rank audit shows why strict-fidelity dense Tucker compression has little room. These are weights-only structural tests, not behavioral circuit identification.

## Object and fixed-block experiment

The existing mixed tensor is $T\in\mathbb R^{12\times1152\times128}$. It contracts a residual input and a head17.2 write-coordinate input into twelve individual token numerator readers after folding through MLP17. Normalizers, residual routes, final saturation and other retained interaction terms remain outside this tensor. The target uses arbitrary independent residual/head inputs; it does not exploit the coupled states generated by upstream attention.

Earlier entry pruning retained every reader node and almost every coordinate-product tile. Here blocks are selected directly, rather than grouped after scalar pruning. Test native coordinates and the established output/head spectral frame. Block widths are1/3/6/12along output and4/8/16along each input, giving72tilings. For each tiling all blocks have equal size, so retaining blocks with greatest squared coefficient norm is the exact best support at a given block count. The omitted block energies give exact coefficient Frobenius error. No nonlinear solver or convergence excuse applies to this fixed-frame support selection; learned frames remain outside its claim.

Charge FP32 block coefficients, a block-support bitmap and every nonidentity dense adapter. The controls preserve full tensor reconstruction and energy to numerical precision. The CPU calculation took0.93seconds.

| Coefficient error ceiling | Best block shape | Dense tensor storage saved | Reader nodes retained |
|---|---|---:|---|
|2%|3×8×8, native frame|0.049%|12/1152/128|
|5%|1×4×4, output/head frame|2.81%|12/1152/128|
|10%|1×4×4, output/head frame|10.17%|12/1152/128|

The10%error result passes the registered10%storage-saving bar but fails the10%input-node-pruning bar. Every residual/head block pair still has an output consumer. The earlier irregular scalar support stores4.90MB at10%error, versus6.36MB for the best whole-block representation and7.08MB dense. Larger regular blocks sacrifice much of the scalar storage saving. This does not determine runtime: dense small tiles could execute better than irregular sparsity, but no tile executor has been benchmarked here.

## Executed countercheck: necessary reader counts in any Tucker frame

Let $T_{(m)}$ be the matrix unfolding along mode $m$. Any multilinear representation using $r_m$ shared readers in that mode has unfolding rank at most $r_m$, regardless of its basis. Consequently its squared coefficient error is at least the sum of squared singular values of $T_{(m)}$ after the first $r_m$. This follows by projecting onto the best $r_m$-dimensional matrix column space. Evaluating the three spectra yields:

| Error ceiling | Necessary output readers | Necessary residual readers | Necessary head readers |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
|2%|12|1105|128|
|5%|12|1008|128|
|10%|12|869|125|

The2%residual bound reproduces the earlier LL1 total-rank bound, rather than claiming a new discovery there. The new all-mode accounting clarifies that neither output nor head reader nodes can be removed at2%or5%coefficient error. At10%, some residual-node reduction is possible; fixed block selection failing to find it is not a universal node-sparsity impossibility.

Separate necessary ranks are not jointly sufficient. Executed HOSVD truncation at the10%row gives13.36%actual error. Even this insufficiently accurate dense-core/dense-adapter representation is larger than the original tensor: about1.31times its scalar count. At2%and5%, the corresponding constructions are about1.68and1.53times larger. These prices omit unnecessary full-rank rotations when comparing the cheaper implementation. They are tested construction prices, not lower bounds on every implementation.

This restricts small Tucker-reader and LL1 rank budgets on the declared independent-port tensor. It does **not** rule out a simple full-rank sparse arithmetic graph, cheap structured transforms, or simpler functions on the actual producer-constrained input domain. It also does not constrain a behavioral objective that tolerates different coefficient errors.

## Decision

Do not launch another dense low-rank Tucker fit hoping for strict-fidelity storage savings in this setting. A useful next representation must avoid expensive arbitrary reader adapters or exploit the known joint QK/value producer constraints. The block result is a baseline for such a change, not a claim that the interaction lacks structure. The independently verified layer9–10 response simplification remains the stronger positive example of producer-aware compression.
