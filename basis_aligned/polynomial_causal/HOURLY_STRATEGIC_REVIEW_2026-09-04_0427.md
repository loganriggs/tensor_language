## Circuit interpretation targets

A useful circuit decomposition must eventually provide all of the following:

1. **Computational specification:** what information is read, what operation or composition is performed, what is
   written, and which downstream computations consume it.
2. **Grouping across modules and splitting within modules:** merge parts of different heads or MLPs when downstream
   computation treats them as one variable; split a native module when its parts serve different tasks.
3. **Held-out and out-of-distribution prediction:** predict activation and causal effects on unseen examples and shifted
   task forms.
4. **Extraction or sufficiency:** an isolated executable circuit or specified interface reproduces the target
   computation or signed causal effect.
5. **Selective manipulation:** removal, swap, or edit changes the intended behavior while preserving unrelated behavior,
   with redundancy and interactions measured explicitly.
6. **Composition and reuse:** shared subcomputations serve several tasks and their joint behavior is predictable.
7. **Stable identification:** the claimed units survive data splits, fitting restarts, and plausible gauges, or are
   defined operationally by indistinguishable downstream effects.

The full goal is a smaller transparent tensor program that predicts fresh and OOD text, composes when replacements are
installed together, supports targeted manipulation, and is simpler under literal storage, compute, edges, states, and
program description. Compression, rank, variance preserved, or low cross-entropy alone do not identify circuits.

- **Frame or gauge assumptions:** native heads and MLPs remain screening units. Final grouping must be determined by

## Genuinely different routes

1. **Strict behavior-first causal battery.** Discover writers and readers from valid task transformations, then compare
   their held-out causal-response profiles across tasks. This directly targets grouping, splitting, prediction,
   extraction, and selective manipulation.
2. **Weight translation after causal identification.** Once a reader interface is necessary and sufficient, expand the
   exact bilinear weights into left, right, and joint terms for the observed state change. This translates a validated
   state variable into parameter space without assuming hidden coordinates are canonical.
3. **Common-reader interaction algebra.** Fix one reader set before looking at outcomes and compute every physical subset
   intervention. The Möbius coefficients then identify additive, redundant, and synergistic combinations. Kill this
   route if the coefficients do not replicate on SELECT/OOD or change radically under matched controls.
4. **Predictive-state causal quotient.** Group internal directions when every registered downstream reader and
   intervention treats them equivalently. This is closer to a semantic basis than covariance PCA, but should begin only
   after several valid tasks provide response signatures.
5. **Exact tensor/realization route.** Use the bilinear contraction graph to seek minimal realizations or canonical
   forms. The current exact rational identity is a useful local result; it becomes a circuit result only when its
   row-dependent coefficients admit a shared predictive generator.
6. **Attention-5 liveness/clamp/injection route.** Measure which downstream inputs actually change after a defined
   attention-5 slice swap, then clamp and inject those changes. This is a deeper but much more expensive test and remains
   behind the first strict battery circuit.
